Fresh and Easy Veggie Spring Rolls with Crisp, Colorful Goodness

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These veggie spring rolls are super fresh and light, packed with crisp veggies wrapped in delicate rice paper. I love how easy they are to make—just five ingredients! Great for sharing at any party.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon veggie spring rolls years ago at a friend's gathering, and I was instantly hooked. The combination of crisp veggies wrapped in delicate rice paper was so refreshing and light. Ever since, I've experimented with different fillings, but this simple five-ingredient version is my favorite. I love how quick it is to put together, especially when you want a healthy snack or a colorful appetizer for guests. The trick I learned for perfect rolls is soaking the rice paper just right—too soft and it tears, too firm and it’s hard to roll. Also, layering fresh mint leaves adds a burst of flavor that really lifts the whole dish. These rolls look so pretty on the plate, with vibrant orange carrots and green herbs peeking through, making them almost too pretty to eat! They’re perfect for warm evenings or potlucks, and I promise, once you try this recipe, you’ll want to make them again and again.

What You'll Need

8 spring rolls, 4 servings

  • 8 rice paper wrappers
  • 1 cup shredded carrots, fresh
  • 1 cup thinly sliced cucumber
  • 1 cup fresh mint leaves
  • 1 cup cooked vermicelli noodles, cooled

How To Do It

Step 1
Fill a large shallow dish with warm water—just warm enough to soften the rice paper but not hot. Dip one rice paper wrapper into the water for about 10 seconds, letting it soften but not become too floppy. This step is crucial because if the wrapper gets too soft, it will tear when you roll. Watch for it to become pliable but still hold some structure.
⏱ Time: 10 seconds
✓ Doneness: Wrapper is soft and flexible but not sticky or falling apart
Step 2
Lay the softened rice paper flat on a clean surface. Place a small handful of vermicelli noodles near the bottom third of the wrapper. The noodles add body and texture, so be careful not to overfill. Spreading them out evenly helps create a balanced roll and prevents clumping.
⏱ Time: 1-2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Noodles are spread evenly and don’t spill over edges
Step 3
Add a small pile of shredded carrots over the noodles, followed by cucumber slices and fresh mint leaves. Layering ingredients in this order keeps the roll balanced and colorful. Using fresh, crisp veggies is key for that satisfying crunch.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Veggies are neatly stacked and vibrant in color
Step 4
Fold the sides of the rice paper inwards over the filling, then carefully roll from the bottom up, keeping the filling tucked tightly. Rolling snugly but gently prevents the roll from falling apart when you bite into it. Don’t rush this step—take your time to get a neat, tight roll.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Roll is tight and smooth with no gaps or tears
Step 5
Repeat the dipping and rolling process with the remaining rice papers and fillings. If the rice paper dries out while you work, lightly mist it with water to keep it pliable. Working quickly helps maintain the best texture.
⏱ Time: 10-15 minutes
✓ Doneness: All rolls are rolled and ready with no cracks
Step 6
Serve the spring rolls immediately with your favorite dipping sauce, like hoisin-peanut or a simple soy-lime dressing. Fresh rolls are best enjoyed right away for maximum crispness and flavor. If you need to store them, wrap tightly in plastic wrap and refrigerate.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Sauce is ready and rolls look fresh and inviting
Step 7
If prepping ahead, cover the rolls with a damp towel to prevent drying out. When ready to eat, bring to room temperature or serve chilled depending on preference. Freshness is key here—avoid making too far ahead to keep that tender rice paper texture.
⏱ Time: Up to 2 hours ahead
✓ Doneness: Rolls remain moist and pliable without drying
Step 8
For a fun twist, try adding sliced avocado or tofu for extra creaminess and protein. Just be mindful that adding extra moisture-rich ingredients can make the rice paper soggy if not eaten soon after rolling.
⏱ Time: Optional, during assembly
✓ Doneness: Add-ins integrated without sogginess
Step 9
Clean up your prep area promptly since wet rice paper can get sticky. Keeping your workspace organized makes rolling smooth and enjoyable. Plus, it helps avoid frustration if you want to make these again soon!
⏱ Time: After rolling
✓ Doneness: Workspace is tidy and ready for next use

Variations

Tofu and Avocado Spring Rolls

Add sliced firm tofu and creamy avocado for extra protein and richness. These ingredients add a satisfying texture contrast and make the rolls more filling, perfect for a light lunch or dinner.

Note: Slice tofu thinly and press to remove excess moisture. Add avocado last to prevent browning and roll quickly to maintain freshness.

Spicy Peanut Spring Rolls

Mix shredded carrots and cucumber with finely chopped chili or sriracha in the dipping sauce for a spicy kick. This adds heat and depth, great for those who like bold flavors.

Note: Adjust chili quantity to taste. Use a creamy peanut dipping sauce to balance the spice.

Shrimp and Herb Spring Rolls

Add cooked shrimp alongside fresh herbs like basil and cilantro for a classic Vietnamese flavor twist. These rolls are light yet packed with savory seafood goodness.

Note: Use peeled, deveined shrimp and slice lengthwise for easier rolling and bite-sized pieces.

Noodle-Free Veggie Rolls

Omit vermicelli noodles for a lower-carb option focusing purely on fresh veggies and herbs. The rolls are crisp and refreshing, perfect for keto or paleo diets.

Note: Increase the amount of veggies slightly to compensate for the missing noodles and maintain bulk.

Fruit and Mint Spring Rolls

Swap veggies for thin slices of mango, strawberry, and kiwi with mint for a sweet, refreshing appetizer or dessert. These rolls are colorful and bursting with fresh fruit flavors.

Note: Use firm fruit slices to avoid soggy rolls and serve immediately for best texture.

Pro Tips

Use warm water—not hot—to soak the rice paper.
Warm water softens the rice paper gently, making it pliable without tearing. Hot water can make it too fragile or sticky, causing it to break during rolling. Using the right temperature is crucial for perfect rolls every time.
Work quickly but carefully when assembling rolls.
Rice paper dries out fast once softened, becoming brittle and difficult to roll. Working efficiently keeps the wrappers moist and flexible, ensuring neat, tight rolls that hold together well.
Pat cucumber slices dry before rolling.
Excess moisture from cucumbers can make the rice paper soggy, ruining the texture. Drying them removes surface water, preserving the crispness and preventing the rolls from falling apart.
Layer mint leaves last before rolling.
Mint loses its flavor quickly when crushed or bruised. Adding it last preserves its fresh aroma and taste, giving each bite a refreshing herbal pop that brightens the entire roll.
Don’t overfill the rolls.
Too much filling causes the rice paper to tear or the roll to burst open. Keeping filling moderate helps maintain a balanced roll that’s easy to eat and visually appealing.
Cover rolls with a damp cloth if not serving immediately.
Rice paper dries out quickly when exposed to air, becoming brittle. A damp towel keeps the rolls moist and pliable, preserving texture and appearance until ready to serve.
Serve with a complementary dipping sauce.
A flavorful dipping sauce, like hoisin-peanut or tangy soy-lime, enhances the simple freshness of the rolls. It adds complexity and moisture, making each bite more satisfying.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Soaking rice paper too long

Why it happens Many think soaking longer makes the wrapper easier to roll, but it actually becomes too fragile and tears easily.

Fix Soak the rice paper just until it’s soft and flexible—usually 8-12 seconds in warm water. Remove promptly and place on a flat surface to assemble quickly.

Overfilling the rolls

Why it happens It’s tempting to pack in lots of veggies, but too much filling stretches the wrapper and causes tearing or bursting.

Fix Use a modest amount of filling, about a small handful of each ingredient. This ensures the roll stays tight and intact.

Not drying cucumber slices

Why it happens Wet cucumber slices release water inside the roll, making the wrapper soggy and prone to breaking.

Fix Pat cucumber slices dry with paper towels before assembling to keep the roll crisp and sturdy.

Rolling too loosely

Why it happens If rolls aren’t tight enough, they fall apart when picked up or bitten into.

Fix Roll firmly but gently, folding sides in first, to create a secure, compact roll that holds together well.

Leaving rolls uncovered

Why it happens Exposed rice paper dries out quickly and becomes brittle, ruining the texture.

Fix Cover finished rolls with a damp towel or plastic wrap until serving to keep them moist and pliable.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These spring rolls are best enjoyed fresh but can safely sit at room temperature for up to 1 hour if covered with a damp cloth to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container lined with a damp paper towel to keep moisture for up to 2 days. The rice paper may harden slightly; serve cold or let sit at room temperature for 10 minutes before eating.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as rice paper becomes brittle and soggy upon thawing. For best texture, prepare fresh rolls each time.

Reheating

Spring rolls are served cold or at room temperature and do not require reheating. If preferred warm, briefly steam for 1-2 minutes but expect softer texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare and slice all fillings up to 1 day ahead and store separately. Soak and roll the rice paper just before serving to maintain texture and freshness.

FAQ

Can I prepare the spring rolls the day before?
It’s best to prepare the fillings a day ahead but roll the spring rolls right before serving. Rice paper tends to dry out or get soggy if wrapped too early. If you must store rolled spring rolls, cover tightly with plastic wrap and a damp towel, and consume within 24 hours.
What can I use instead of vermicelli noodles?
If you want to skip noodles, try thinly sliced jicama or extra shredded veggies for crunch. Some people also use glass noodles or omit noodles entirely for a lighter, lower-carb option.
How do I prevent the rice paper from tearing?
Soak the rice paper just until it’s soft and flexible, about 8-12 seconds in warm water. Don’t soak too long or use hot water. Also, roll gently but firmly, folding the sides in first to secure the filling.
Can I freeze these spring rolls?
Freezing fresh spring rolls isn’t recommended because the rice paper becomes brittle and soggy after thawing. It’s best to enjoy them fresh or prepare fillings ahead and roll when ready.
What dipping sauces go well with veggie spring rolls?
Classic options include hoisin-peanut sauce, soy sauce with lime and chili, or a simple sweet chili sauce. These complement the fresh flavors and add moisture and depth to each bite.
How long do rice paper wrappers last?
Unopened rice paper wrappers can last up to a year in a cool, dry pantry. Once opened, store them in an airtight container to prevent moisture and use within a few months for best quality.
Can I add protein to these rolls?
Absolutely! Cooked shrimp, tofu, or grilled chicken are great additions. Just slice thinly and add with the veggies. Keep in mind that extra moisture from proteins can affect the wrapper texture, so roll tightly and serve soon after.
What if my spring rolls are sticky when rolling?
If the rice paper feels sticky or tears easily, it’s likely soaked too long or the surface is too wet. Try soaking for less time and patting the wrapper dry gently on a clean towel before filling.
