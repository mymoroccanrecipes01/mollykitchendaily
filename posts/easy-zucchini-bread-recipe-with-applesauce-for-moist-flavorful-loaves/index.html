Moist Zucchini Bread with Applesauce – A Cozy Classic Twist

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This zucchini bread is super moist thanks to applesauce replacing oil, giving it a tender crumb and subtle sweetness. It’s perfect for breakfast or a cozy afternoon snack. I love how simple and fuss-free it is!

Introduction

I've been making zucchini bread for years, but this version with applesauce quickly became my favorite. I first tried swapping oil for applesauce on a whim to cut back on fat, and wow, it turned out incredibly moist and tender without losing any flavor. My family loved it so much that it’s now a staple for weekend breakfasts and afternoon snacks. What makes this recipe different is how the applesauce not only adds moisture but also a subtle natural sweetness, which balances perfectly with the warm spices. I also squeeze the zucchini dry to avoid sogginess and keep the crumb just right. The trick I learned is to mix the dry and wet ingredients gently to keep the bread light and fluffy. The finished loaf has a golden crust with a soft, moist inside that’s packed with cozy cinnamon and nutmeg notes. It’s just the kind of comforting bread you want with a cup of tea or coffee. If you’ve ever struggled with dry or oily zucchini bread, this recipe will be a game-changer for you.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 cup unsweetened applesauce
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating your zucchini finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or several layers of paper towels and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step prevents your bread from becoming too wet and dense. The zucchini holds a surprising amount of water, so don’t skip this part. You want the bread moist but not soggy. Set the zucchini aside once drained.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini should feel damp but no longer dripping wet.
Step 2
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan or line it with parchment paper. This ensures your bread won’t stick and will come out cleanly. Having the oven fully heated before baking helps the bread rise evenly and develop a nice crust.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven temperature stable at 350°F and pan fully greased or lined.
Step 3
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg. Mixing these dry ingredients well distributes the leavening agents and spices evenly, so your bread rises properly and every bite has balanced flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients fully combined with no lumps or clumps.
Step 4
In a large bowl, beat the eggs with the sugars until just combined. Add the applesauce and vanilla extract, stirring gently. This wet mixture acts as the base for moisture and sweetness. Using applesauce instead of oil keeps the bread tender without heaviness, and the vanilla adds warmth.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients fully mixed, smooth, and slightly glossy.
Step 5
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture carefully, mixing just until no streaks of flour remain. Overmixing can develop gluten, making the bread tough. Gently fold in the zucchini and walnuts if using, so they’re evenly distributed without breaking down the batter.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter smooth with zucchini and nuts evenly spread, no dry flour.
Step 6
Pour the batter into your prepared loaf pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and develop a nice crust. Tap the pan gently on the counter to remove any large air bubbles that could cause holes in the bread.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter level and smooth in the pan with no large bubbles.
Step 7
Bake the bread in the preheated oven for 45 to 55 minutes. Check at 45 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Baking too long can dry it out, so watch closely near the end.
⏱ Time: 50 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust and toothpick comes out mostly clean.
Step 8
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes. This resting time helps the bread firm up so it slices cleanly. Then transfer it to a wire rack to cool completely, which prevents sogginess from steam trapped in the pan.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread slightly warm but firm enough to slice without crumbling.
Step 9
Slice and serve your zucchini bread plain or with a little butter or cream cheese. It’s delicious warm or at room temperature. Store leftovers in an airtight container to keep it fresh for several days.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Slices hold shape and have a moist, tender crumb.

Variations

Gluten-Free Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the bread moist and tender while accommodating gluten sensitivities. Expect a slightly different crumb texture but the same great flavor.

Note: Gluten-free flours absorb liquid differently, so you may need to adjust baking time or add a bit more applesauce for moisture.

Vegan Zucchini Bread

Replace the eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg) and use plant-based yogurt instead of applesauce if desired. This keeps the bread moist and flavorful without animal products.

Note: Flax eggs provide binding but can slightly change texture; be sure to let the flax mixture thicken before adding.

Chocolate Chip Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips to the batter for a sweet twist. The chocolate pairs wonderfully with the spices and zucchini for a dessert-like loaf.

Note: Fold chips in last to avoid melting during mixing; baking time remains the same.

Spiced Orange Zucchini Bread

Add zest of one orange and substitute half the cinnamon with ground ginger for a zesty, aromatic variation. This brightens the flavor and adds a fresh twist to the classic.

Note: Orange zest adds moisture and oils; be sure to incorporate it evenly to avoid clumps.

Walnut and Raisin Zucchini Bread

Stir in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts and 1/2 cup raisins for added texture and bursts of sweetness. This variation feels extra hearty and festive.

Note: Add nuts and raisins after folding in zucchini to distribute evenly without breaking them up.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from the zucchini before adding it to the batter.
Because zucchini contains so much water, failing to drain it properly results in a soggy, underbaked loaf. Removing moisture prevents the bread from becoming dense or gummy, ensuring a tender, fluffy crumb.
Use unsweetened applesauce to replace oil for a healthier, moist bread.
Applesauce adds natural sweetness and moisture without the fat of oil, keeping the bread lighter. Plus, it helps bind ingredients due to its pectin content, improving texture and shelf life.
Mix the batter gently and only until combined.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which makes quick breads tough and chewy. Folding ingredients carefully keeps the crumb soft and tender, perfect for this zucchini bread.
Check the bread for doneness starting at 45 minutes by inserting a toothpick.
Ovens vary, and overbaking dries out the bread. Testing early ensures you remove it at perfect doneness, with a moist crumb and golden crust.
Let the bread cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a rack.
This resting time firms up the bread so it slices cleanly without crumbling, and cooling on a rack prevents steam buildup that could make the crust soggy.
Add chopped nuts or raisins for extra texture and flavor.
Nuts give crunch and richness, while raisins add bursts of sweetness. Both complement zucchini bread well and make it more interesting without changing the core recipe.
Store the bread in an airtight container once cooled.
Proper storage keeps moisture inside the bread, preventing it from drying out while maintaining softness for several days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not draining zucchini thoroughly

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how much water zucchini holds and skip squeezing it out to save time.

Fix Use a clean towel to press and squeeze grated zucchini firmly until no more liquid comes out. This step is crucial for preventing a soggy bread texture.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Eager cooks often think mixing longer will improve batter smoothness, but it develops gluten.

Fix Fold ingredients gently just until combined. Stop mixing as soon as you see no dry flour streaks to keep the bread tender.

Using sweetened applesauce

Why it happens Some use whatever applesauce is on hand, not realizing added sugar can throw off balance.

Fix Always choose unsweetened applesauce to control sweetness and maintain the bread’s natural flavor.

Baking at wrong temperature

Why it happens Not preheating the oven or baking too hot/cold leads to uneven rise or dense crumb.

Fix Preheat oven fully to 350°F and use an oven thermometer if needed to ensure accurate temperature.

Skipping the cooling step

Why it happens Impatience leads to slicing bread too soon, causing it to crumble or feel gummy.

Fix Let the bread cool 10 minutes in the pan, then transfer to a rack to finish cooling before slicing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep zucchini bread at room temperature for up to 2 days if wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. This prevents it from drying out but avoid leaving it exposed to air to maintain freshness.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up slightly when chilled, so bring it to room temperature or warm briefly before serving to restore softness.

Freezer

Freeze the cooled bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil or in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave it at room temperature for a few hours or overnight. Reheat gently to refresh the crumb if desired.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven or microwave for 15-20 seconds until just warm. Avoid overheating which can dry out the bread. A light spread of butter or cream cheese after warming enhances moisture and flavor.

Make Ahead

You can grate and drain zucchini 1 day ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix the batter the day of baking for best texture and freshness. The baked loaf also freezes well for make-ahead convenience.

FAQ

Can I substitute applesauce with oil or butter?
Yes, you can replace applesauce with an equal amount of oil or melted butter if you prefer a richer bread. However, the texture will be a bit heavier and less naturally sweet. Applesauce keeps it moist without added fat, so it’s a healthier option.
How do I know when the zucchini bread is done baking?
The best way is to insert a toothpick or cake tester into the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the bread is done. The crust should be golden brown and spring back slightly when pressed. Avoid opening the oven too often early on to keep the temperature steady.
Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
You can use frozen zucchini that’s been fully thawed and drained, but fresh is best for texture. Frozen zucchini often releases more water, so be sure to squeeze out excess moisture thoroughly to avoid a soggy bread.
What if I don’t have walnuts? Can I leave them out?
Absolutely! Walnuts are optional and just add crunch and flavor. You can omit them or substitute with other nuts or seeds like pecans or sunflower seeds. The bread will still be delicious without any nuts.
How can I make this bread sweeter?
You can increase the sugar slightly or add mix-ins like chocolate chips or raisins for extra sweetness. Just be careful not to add too much sugar as it can affect texture and baking time.
Is it okay to use whole wheat flour instead of all-purpose?
You can substitute half the all-purpose flour with whole wheat for a nuttier flavor and more fiber. Using 100% whole wheat may make the bread denser, so balancing with all-purpose flour gives the best texture.
Can I make muffins instead of a loaf?
Yes, this batter works well for muffins. Fill muffin tins about 2/3 full and bake at 350°F for about 20-25 minutes. Check doneness with a toothpick as muffins bake faster than loaves.
How do I store leftover zucchini bread?
Wrap leftovers tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container at room temperature for 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze slices wrapped individually to keep them fresh.
