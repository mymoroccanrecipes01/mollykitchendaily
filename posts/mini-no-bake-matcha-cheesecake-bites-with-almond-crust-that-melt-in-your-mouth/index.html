Delicate No-Bake Matcha Cheesecake Bites with Almond Crust

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These no-bake matcha cheesecake bites are creamy and light, with a nutty almond crust that adds a lovely crunch. The subtle earthiness of matcha makes them a unique treat for afternoon snacks or elegant gatherings.

Introduction

I stumbled upon matcha cheesecake bites a few years ago, and ever since, they've been my go-to quick dessert when I want something a little fancy without the fuss. What I love about this recipe is how it’s no-bake — perfect for hot days or last-minute gatherings. The almond crust adds a subtle nuttiness that balances the smooth, earthy matcha filling so well. I used to try regular graham cracker crusts but found almonds give a richer flavor and a bit more texture. The trick I learned is to whip the cream separately and fold it gently into the matcha cream cheese mixture; this keeps the cheesecake filling light and airy instead of dense. When you bite into these, you get that melt-in-your-mouth creaminess paired with a slight crunch on the bottom. They’re perfect for afternoon tea or a small party appetizer. Honestly, the first time I made them, I was worried the matcha flavor might be too strong, but it’s just the right subtle earthiness that makes these special. Give these a try if you want an elegant dessert without turning on the oven!

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 bite-sized pieces

  • 1 1/2 cups almond flour, finely ground
  • 3 tablespoons melted coconut oil
  • 2 tablespoons maple syrup
  • 8 ounces cream cheese, softened
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 tablespoon matcha green tea powder
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream, chilled
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preparing the crust. In a medium bowl, combine the almond flour, melted coconut oil, and maple syrup. Mix until the mixture resembles wet sand and holds together when pressed. This step is crucial because the coconut oil acts as a binder, helping the crust hold its shape once chilled. Avoid adding too much oil or the crust will be greasy; too little and it won’t stick. Press this mixture firmly into the bottom of a lined mini muffin tin or silicone mold to form an even layer. Pressing firmly ensures the crust stays intact when you remove the bites later.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Crust looks compact and slightly glossy from the coconut oil, holds shape when pressed
Step 2
Place the crust in the fridge to chill while you prepare the filling. This helps the crust firm up and prevents it from mixing with the filling. Chilling also makes it easier to remove the bites later without crumbling. Don’t skip this step as it stabilizes the base for the creamy topping.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Crust feels firm and cool to the touch
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the softened cream cheese with powdered sugar and vanilla extract until smooth and creamy. The cream cheese must be softened to room temperature to avoid lumps and ensure a silky texture. Use a hand mixer or stand mixer for best results. Mixing well at this stage creates a smooth base that will blend perfectly with the matcha and cream.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, lump-free, and slightly fluffy
Step 4
Sift the matcha powder over the cream cheese mixture to avoid clumps and fold it in gently. Sifting ensures the matcha disperses evenly without forming bitter clumps. Folding gently preserves the airiness of the cream cheese mix and keeps the flavor balanced.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling has a uniform pale green color with no streaks
Step 5
In a separate chilled bowl, whip the heavy cream until soft peaks form. This means when you lift the whisk, the cream will hold its shape but the tip will curl slightly. Whipping the cream just right adds lightness to the cheesecake filling, preventing it from becoming too dense. Over-whipping can turn it grainy, so stop at soft peaks.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cream is fluffy and holds shape but still smooth
Step 6
Gently fold the whipped cream into the cream cheese and matcha mixture in batches. Use a spatula and fold carefully to keep as much air in the mixture as possible. This step is key to the light, mousse-like texture of the cheesecake bites. Rushing this or stirring too vigorously will deflate the mixture and make it dense.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling is smooth, airy, and well combined with no streaks
Step 7
Spoon or pipe the filling over the chilled almond crust in your molds. Fill each cavity almost to the top, smoothing the surface with the back of a spoon. Filling evenly ensures consistent bites and a balanced crust-to-filling ratio. Don’t overfill or the cheesecake may spill when chilled.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each mold is filled evenly with a glossy, smooth surface
Step 8
Cover the filled molds with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight. This chilling step allows the filling to set firmly so the bites hold their shape when unmolded. If you rush this, the cheesecake will be too soft and may collapse when removed.
⏱ Time: 240 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling is firm to the touch and holds shape when unmolded
Step 9
To serve, gently pop the cheesecake bites out of the molds. If they resist, briefly dip the mold bottom in warm water for a few seconds to loosen. Arrange on a platter and optionally dust with extra matcha powder or chopped almonds for garnish. These little bites are best served chilled and enjoyed within a day or two for freshness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bites release cleanly and hold their shape

Variations

Vegan Matcha Cheesecake Bites

Substitute cream cheese with vegan cashew cream and use coconut cream instead of heavy cream. Replace maple syrup with agave if preferred. These changes keep the texture creamy and the flavor fresh, perfect for dairy-free diets.

Note: Cashew cream needs to be soaked and blended smooth. The filling may be slightly less firm, so chill longer to set.

Matcha Cheesecake with Chocolate Almond Crust

Add 2 tablespoons cocoa powder to the almond crust mix for a chocolaty twist. The bitter cocoa complements the earthy matcha beautifully and adds depth to the crust flavor.

Note: Cocoa powder absorbs moisture; you may need to add a bit more coconut oil to keep crust bind.

Matcha Cheesecake Bites with White Chocolate Drizzle

Melt white chocolate and drizzle over the chilled cheesecake bites before serving. The sweetness cuts the matcha’s earthiness and adds a pretty finish.

Note: Allow drizzle to set in fridge before serving to avoid melting.

Spiced Matcha Cheesecake Bites

Add 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon and a pinch of cardamom to the filling for a warm spice note. This variation adds an unexpected cozy flavor that pairs well with green tea.

Note: Spices can overpower matcha if added too much; start small and adjust to taste.

Lemon Matcha Cheesecake Bites

Add 1 teaspoon lemon zest to the filling for a bright citrus contrast. The lemon enhances the matcha’s freshness and adds a zingy lift to the creamy bites.

Note: Use fresh zest only; dried zest lacks the brightness needed.

Pro Tips

Make sure the cream cheese is fully softened before mixing.
Softened cream cheese blends smoothly with the sugar and vanilla, preventing lumps that would ruin the silky texture of the filling. If it’s too cold, you’ll end up with clumps that affect the final mouthfeel.
Sift your matcha powder before adding it.
Matcha tends to clump due to its fine texture. Sifting ensures even distribution and prevents bitter clumps in the filling, which can throw off the flavor balance.
Fold the whipped cream gently into the cream cheese mixture.
Folding preserves the airiness of the whipped cream. Stirring vigorously would deflate the mixture, resulting in a dense, heavy filling rather than a light mousse-like texture.
Press the almond crust firmly into the molds.
A compact crust holds together better when unmolding the bites. If it’s too loose, the crust will crumble apart, making the bites hard to serve.
Chill the crust before adding the filling.
Chilling solidifies the coconut oil and stabilizes the crust, preventing it from mixing with the filling and ensuring a clean separation between layers.
Use a silicone mold or lined mini muffin tin for easy removal.
These molds help the cheesecake bites pop out easily without damage. Without a non-stick surface, the bites may break or crumble during removal.
Let the cheesecake chill overnight if possible.
Longer chilling time allows the filling to fully set, improving texture and flavor melding. Shorter chilling can result in a softer, less stable bite.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using cold cream cheese without softening.

Why it happens Many assume cream cheese can be mixed right from the fridge, but cold cream cheese resists blending and forms lumps.

Fix Always leave cream cheese out for 30-60 minutes to reach room temperature before mixing. If short on time, microwave in short bursts (5-10 seconds), stirring in between until soft but not melted.

Over-whipping the cream.

Why it happens It’s easy to whip past soft peaks to stiff or even butter stage if not paying close attention.

Fix Watch carefully and stop whipping as soon as soft peaks form. The cream should hold shape but still be smooth. Over-whipped cream will create a grainy texture and won’t fold in well.

Pressing the crust too lightly.

Why it happens Rushing or not applying enough pressure during crust formation leads to loose crumbs that fall apart.

Fix Use the back of a spoon or your fingers to firmly press the crust into the mold, compacting it fully. This helps it hold together once chilled.

Skipping the chilling step for the crust.

Why it happens Impatience or lack of planning can tempt cooks to fill the crust immediately.

Fix Always chill the crust for at least 15 minutes before adding filling to ensure it sets properly and keeps layers separate.

Using poor quality matcha powder.

Why it happens Matcha can be expensive, so some opt for cheaper, lower-grade powders.

Fix Invest in culinary-grade matcha for balanced flavor and color. Avoid dusty or bitter powders that can ruin the cheesecake taste.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These cheesecake bites should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to the dairy content. Keep them covered on the counter only briefly before serving.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The texture may firm up slightly when cold but will remain creamy. Serve chilled or let sit at room temperature for 10 minutes before eating for softer texture.

Freezer

Freeze cheesecake bites in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge before serving to maintain texture. Avoid refreezing.

Reheating

No reheating is necessary or recommended for these bites. Serve straight from the fridge or after brief room temperature rest. Reheating will ruin the texture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the crust and filling separately up to 2 days ahead. Store crust chilled and filling covered in the fridge. Assemble and chill the bites on the day of serving for best freshness and texture.

FAQ

Can I use regular flour instead of almond flour for the crust?
Regular flour won’t work well here because it lacks the natural oils and texture almonds provide. The crust would be denser and less flavorful. Almond flour’s oil content helps bind the crust when combined with coconut oil, giving the right crumbly yet firm texture.
What if I don’t have coconut oil? Can I substitute butter?
Butter can be used but it may make the crust softer and less stable at room temperature. Coconut oil solidifies when chilled, which helps keep the crust intact. If using butter, chill the crust longer and keep the cheesecake refrigerated until serving.
How long can I store these cheesecake bites in the fridge?
They keep well for up to 3 days in an airtight container. After that, the texture and flavor might start to degrade. It’s best to enjoy them fresh or within this timeframe for the best experience.
Can I make these bites ahead for a party?
Absolutely! Prepare the crust and filling a day ahead and assemble the bites on the day of the event, then chill for at least 4 hours. This makes hosting easier and keeps the bites fresh and firm.
Is it okay to use low-fat cream cheese?
Low-fat cream cheese tends to be grainier and less creamy, affecting the smooth texture of the filling. For best results, use full-fat cream cheese to achieve that rich, silky mouthfeel.
How do I avoid bitter clumps of matcha in the filling?
Always sift the matcha powder before adding it to the cream cheese mixture. Sifting breaks up clumps and distributes the powder evenly, preventing bitter pockets.
Can I use a hand whisk instead of a mixer for whipping cream?
You can, but it will take much longer and requires more effort. A mixer helps achieve soft peaks quickly and consistently, which is important for the light texture. If using a whisk, be patient and watch closely to avoid over-whipping.
What’s the best way to unmold the cheesecake bites?
Use silicone molds or line your mini muffin tin with parchment or paper liners. If bites stick, dip the bottom of the mold briefly in warm water to loosen them gently without breaking.
