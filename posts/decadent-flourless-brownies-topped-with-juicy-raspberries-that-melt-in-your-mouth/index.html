Rich and Fudgy Flourless Brownies with Fresh Raspberries for Weekend Treats

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Medium Difficulty

These flourless brownies are pure chocolate bliss—dense, fudgy, and perfectly rich. The secret lies in using top-notch dark chocolate and folding the batter gently to keep that melt-in-your-mouth texture. Fresh raspberries add a bright, tart contrast that’s just perfect for weekend dessert sharing.

Introduction

I first made these flourless brownies a few years ago when I wanted a gluten-free chocolate dessert that didn’t compromise on richness or texture. I was surprised at how indulgent and fudgy they turned out, even without flour! What sets this version apart is the use of really good dark chocolate and a gentle folding technique to keep the batter airy but thick. I’ve learned from past attempts that rushing the mixing or using lower quality chocolate can make the brownies dry or crumbly, which is a bummer. The finished brownies have this dense, melt-in-your-mouth feel balanced perfectly by fresh raspberries, which add a bright tartness that cuts through the richness. They’re great for weekend gatherings or any time you want a special treat that’s a little different but still super comforting. Honestly, once you try these, you’ll want to make them again and again!

What You'll Need

8 rich, fudgy brownies

  • 8 ounces high-quality dark chocolate, chopped
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, cut into pieces
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 cup fresh raspberries, for serving

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 325°F (160°C). Line an 8x8-inch baking pan with parchment paper, leaving some overhang to lift the brownies out easily once baked. This step is crucial because the lower temperature helps the brownies bake gently, preserving that fudgy texture rather than drying out. The parchment ensures easy removal without breaking the delicate brownies.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated and pan fully lined with parchment paper
Step 2
In a heatproof bowl set over simmering water, melt the dark chocolate and butter together, stirring occasionally until smooth and glossy. Using this gentle melting method prevents the chocolate from scorching, which can cause bitterness. Stirring helps the butter incorporate evenly, creating a silky base for the batter.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, fully melted, and shiny with no lumps
Step 3
Remove the chocolate mixture from heat and whisk in the sugar while it’s still warm. This helps dissolve the sugar slightly, so the brownies have a subtle sweetness without graininess. Don’t rush this step because well-incorporated sugar affects texture and chewiness.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Sugar fully incorporated; batter looks glossy and smooth
Step 4
Add the eggs one at a time, whisking well after each addition. This gradual addition allows the eggs to emulsify properly with the chocolate, creating a stable batter that rises just enough without becoming cakey. If you add eggs too quickly, the batter can seize or curdle.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, smooth, and shiny without streaks of egg
Step 5
Stir in the vanilla extract and salt to enhance the chocolate flavor and balance sweetness. Salt is a key ingredient that many overlook, but it really brings out the depth of the dark chocolate. Mix gently to keep the batter airy.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Ingredients fully combined with even color throughout
Step 6
Sift the cocoa powder over the batter and fold it in gently using a spatula. Folding instead of vigorous stirring preserves the air bubbles that give the brownies a light touch despite their richness. Overmixing here can make the brownies dense and heavy.
⏱ Time: 4
✓ Doneness: No visible streaks of cocoa powder; batter is uniform and thick
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top evenly with a spatula. Spreading the batter evenly ensures uniform baking and prevents dry edges. Avoid tapping the pan too hard as it can deflate the batter.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth, level, and glossy
Step 8
Bake for about 25-30 minutes or until the edges are set but the center still looks slightly soft and shiny. This timing is key; overbaking leads to dry brownies, while underbaking can make them too gooey to slice. The brownies will continue to firm up as they cool.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Edges look set and cracked; center jiggles slightly when nudged
Step 9
Let the brownies cool completely in the pan on a wire rack before lifting out and slicing. Cooling allows the texture to set properly and prevents crumbling. Serve topped with fresh raspberries for a bright contrast that cuts through the richness beautifully.
⏱ Time: 45
✓ Doneness: Brownies are firm to the touch and easy to cut cleanly

Variations

Nutty Flourless Brownies

Add 1/2 cup chopped toasted walnuts or pecans to the batter for a delightful crunch and nutty flavor. This adds texture contrast and a toasty aroma that complements the rich chocolate beautifully.

Note: Fold nuts in gently at the end to avoid deflating the batter. Baking time remains the same.

Spicy Chili Flourless Brownies

Stir in 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper and 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon to the batter for a subtle spicy kick that enhances the chocolate depth. Great if you want a grown-up twist on classic brownies.

Note: Be careful not to overdo the chili; start small and adjust next time based on preference.

Orange Zest Infused Brownies

Add 1 tablespoon freshly grated orange zest to the batter for a bright, citrusy aroma that pairs wonderfully with dark chocolate and raspberries. It freshens up the dessert beautifully.

Note: Mix zest in with vanilla and salt to distribute flavor evenly.

Vegan Flourless Brownies

Replace eggs with flax eggs (2 tablespoons ground flaxseed mixed with 6 tablespoons water) and use a vegan butter substitute. The texture will be a bit denser but still fudgy and delicious.

Note: Allow flax eggs to sit for 5 minutes before adding. Baking time may increase by 3-5 minutes.

Salted Caramel Flourless Brownies

Drizzle homemade or store-bought salted caramel sauce over the batter before baking for a gooey, sweet-salty surprise in every bite. This adds richness and complexity.

Note: Swirl caramel gently with a toothpick to avoid mixing fully into batter.

Pro Tips

Use a heatproof bowl over simmering water to melt chocolate and butter slowly.
Melting chocolate gently prevents it from burning or seizing, which would ruin the flavor and texture. The double boiler method allows precise temperature control, ensuring a smooth, glossy mixture essential for fudgy brownies.
Add eggs one at a time, whisking thoroughly after each addition.
Gradual incorporation helps eggs emulsify with the chocolate mixture, creating a stable batter that sets properly. Adding eggs too quickly can cause the batter to curdle or separate, leading to uneven texture.
Fold cocoa powder gently to preserve air bubbles.
Folding instead of stirring prevents deflating the batter, which keeps the brownies tender and light despite their dense appearance. Overmixing can make brownies tough and dense.
Bake at a lower temperature (325°F) for even cooking.
Lower heat allows the brownies to cook through without drying out the edges, preserving moisture and fudginess inside. High heat can cause cracking and dryness.
Cool brownies completely before slicing.
Allowing the brownies to cool fully helps them set firmly, making clean cuts easier and preventing crumbling. Warm brownies are too soft and fragile to slice neatly.
Use parchment paper with overhang for easy removal.
The parchment acts as a sling so you can lift the entire batch out without breaking. It also prevents sticking and makes cleanup faster.
Serve with fresh raspberries just before eating.
Raspberries lose their freshness quickly once cut or mixed in. Adding them last keeps their bright flavor and texture, balancing the richness of the brownies perfectly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overbaking the brownies

Why it happens Many assume brownies are done when they look fully set, but overbaking dries them out and ruins the fudgy texture.

Fix Remove brownies from the oven when the edges are set but the center still jiggles slightly. The residual heat will finish baking them as they cool, preserving moisture.

Using low-quality chocolate

Why it happens It’s tempting to use cheap chocolate or chips, but these often contain additives that affect flavor and melting.

Fix Invest in good-quality dark chocolate with high cocoa content. It makes a huge difference in taste and texture.

Mixing the batter too vigorously after adding cocoa powder

Why it happens Rushing to combine ingredients can deflate the batter and lead to dense, tough brownies.

Fix Fold cocoa powder gently with a spatula until just combined to keep air in the batter.

Not lining the pan properly

Why it happens Skipping parchment or greasing can cause brownies to stick and break apart when removed.

Fix Always line your pan with parchment paper with some overhang for easy removal and neat slicing.

Adding raspberries before baking

Why it happens Baking raspberries inside the batter can make them mushy and overly sweet, losing their fresh tartness.

Fix Add fresh raspberries only when serving to maintain their texture and bright flavor.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Brownies can be kept at room temperature for up to 24 hours if covered tightly with plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. This keeps them moist and fresh but they are best eaten within a day for optimal texture.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The brownies will firm up and become denser when cold. Warm slightly before serving to soften, or enjoy chilled for a firmer texture.

Freezer

Freeze brownies wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil for up to 3 months. To thaw, move to the fridge overnight or leave at room temperature for a few hours. Avoid microwaving frozen brownies directly as it can dry them out.

Reheating

Reheat individual brownie pieces in the microwave for 15-20 seconds with a small splash of milk or cream to restore softness. Alternatively, warm in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes wrapped in foil to prevent drying.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bring it to room temperature before baking. Alternatively, bake the brownies fully, cool, and store refrigerated; add fresh raspberries just before serving.

FAQ

Can I substitute the dark chocolate with milk chocolate?
Milk chocolate has more sugar and less cocoa solids than dark chocolate, which will make the brownies sweeter and less intense in chocolate flavor. Also, milk chocolate contains more milk solids, which can affect the fudgy texture. If you swap, reduce sugar slightly and expect a softer brownie.
Why don’t these brownies use any flour?
Flourless brownies rely on eggs and chocolate to provide structure and density. This makes them naturally gluten-free and intensely fudgy. Without flour, the texture is denser and more melt-in-your-mouth compared to cakey brownies.
Can I use cocoa powder instead of chocolate?
You can’t replace all the melted chocolate with cocoa powder because chocolate contains cocoa butter and sugar, which affect texture and sweetness. Cocoa powder can be added alongside chocolate but not as a full substitute.
How do I know when the brownies are done?
Look for set edges that start to crack and a center that still jiggles slightly when you gently shake the pan. The brownies will firm up as they cool, so slight softness is perfect.
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Yes! Nuts like walnuts or pecans add great crunch. Fold them in gently at the end to avoid deflating the batter. You can also add chocolate chips or swirl caramel for extra flavor.
What if my batter looks grainy after adding sugar?
Whisking the sugar into the warm chocolate and butter mixture helps it dissolve. If it looks grainy, keep whisking gently until smooth. Using superfine sugar can also help.
Why does the batter seize sometimes when adding eggs?
If eggs are added too quickly or the chocolate mixture is too hot, the eggs can cook instantly causing the batter to seize. Make sure to temper the eggs by adding them slowly and whisking continuously.
Can I make these brownies vegan?
Yes, by replacing eggs with flax eggs and using vegan butter or coconut oil. The texture will be slightly different—denser and less airy—but still delicious.
How should I store leftover brownies?
Keep brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 24 hours or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze tightly wrapped for up to 3 months.
How can I make these brownies less sweet?
Use dark chocolate with a higher cocoa percentage (75% or more) and reduce the granulated sugar slightly. The raspberries also help balance sweetness with tartness.
