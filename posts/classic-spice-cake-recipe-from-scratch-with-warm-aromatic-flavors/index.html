Cozy Homemade Spice Cake with Freshly Ground Cinnamon and Nutmeg

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 13, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Medium Difficulty

This homemade spice cake is such a comforting treat, loaded with warm cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves. Using freshly ground spices and buttermilk keeps it moist and tender. Perfect for cozy family dessert nights or afternoon tea!

Introduction

I first made this spice cake on a chilly autumn afternoon when I was craving something warm and comforting but didn’t want anything too complicated. What really sets this cake apart from other spice cakes is the use of freshly ground spices — the cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves just burst with flavor. Plus, the buttermilk adds this wonderful tang and keeps the cake incredibly moist and tender. I’ve tried spice cakes before that felt dry or overly dense, but this one strikes the perfect balance every time. The trick I learned was to melt the butter and mix it with the sugars before adding the buttermilk and eggs — it helps create a smooth batter and a tender crumb. Also, I always sift the dry ingredients together to avoid clumps and distribute the spices evenly. When it’s done, the cake has a beautiful golden color with a delicate crumb and a cozy aroma that fills the kitchen. It’s perfect for serving with a cup of tea or coffee, especially on a lazy weekend afternoon or for family dessert night. Honestly, it’s become my go-to recipe whenever I want something that feels like a warm hug in cake form.

What You'll Need

One 9-inch round cake, 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon, freshly ground
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg, freshly ground
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves, freshly ground
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1 cup buttermilk, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9-inch round cake pan, then line the bottom with parchment paper. This ensures the cake doesn’t stick and comes out cleanly. Also, preheating the oven gives you a consistent baking environment, which is key for an even rise and moist crumb. Don’t skip the parchment — it’s a simple step that makes a big difference when removing the cake.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan is evenly greased with parchment securely in place
Step 2
In a medium bowl, sift together the flour, baking soda, salt, and freshly ground spices (cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves). Sifting helps to aerate the flour and evenly distribute the spices, which results in a lighter texture and balanced flavor throughout the cake. Using freshly ground spices rather than pre-ground ensures the aroma and taste are much more vibrant and fresh.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no lumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, whisk the granulated sugar, brown sugar, and melted butter together until combined and slightly creamy. This step dissolves the sugars a bit and helps incorporate air, which contributes to a tender crumb. Make sure the melted butter has cooled slightly to avoid cooking the eggs when added next.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sugar and butter mixture is smooth and homogenous
Step 4
Add the eggs one at a time to the butter-sugar mixture, whisking well after each addition. This helps to emulsify the mixture and trap air, which promotes rise and a light texture. Then stir in the vanilla extract for flavor depth. Don’t rush this step — well-beaten eggs ensure the cake holds together nicely.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, glossy, and slightly thickened
Step 5
Alternate adding the dry ingredients and buttermilk to the wet mixture, starting and ending with the dry. Mix gently with a spatula or wooden spoon just until combined after each addition. Overmixing develops gluten and results in a tough cake, so be careful to fold the batter lightly. The buttermilk’s acidity reacts with the baking soda to give the cake a tender crumb and subtle tang.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no streaks of flour, but not overmixed
Step 6
Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan and smooth the top with a spatula. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any air bubbles trapped inside. This helps prevent large holes or tunnels in the cake and promotes even baking.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and even in the pan
Step 7
Bake in the preheated oven for 28-32 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through for even browning. Start checking at 28 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Avoid opening the oven too often to prevent the cake from sinking.
⏱ Time: 28-32 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown top and toothpick comes out clean or with moist crumbs
Step 8
Remove the cake from the oven and let it cool in the pan for about 10 minutes. Cooling in the pan helps the cake set and prevents it from breaking apart when you remove it. After 10 minutes, run a knife around the edges and invert onto a wire rack to cool completely.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake releases easily from the pan and cools on rack
Step 9
Once fully cooled, frost or dust with powdered sugar if desired. I love a simple cream cheese frosting or just a light sprinkle of powdered sugar to keep the warm spice flavors shining. Serving the cake at room temperature ensures the best texture and flavor.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Frosting is smooth or powdered sugar evenly dusted

Variations

Cream Cheese Frosted Spice Cake

Top your spice cake with a tangy cream cheese frosting for a rich, creamy contrast. The frosting’s slight tartness pairs beautifully with the warm spices, making the cake feel extra indulgent and festive.

Note: Make sure the cake is fully cooled before frosting to avoid melting. Chill frosting slightly if too soft.

Vegan Spice Cake

Replace eggs with flax eggs and use a plant-based butter substitute along with non-dairy milk plus vinegar instead of buttermilk. This version keeps the moistness and spice punch while fitting vegan diets.

Note: Flax eggs require a longer mixing time and may slightly change the crumb texture—expect a bit denser cake.

Spice Cake with Apples and Walnuts

Fold in diced apples and chopped walnuts into the batter for added texture and a fresh fruity note. This variation adds moisture and a pleasant crunch to the cake.

Note: Reduce sugar slightly to balance the sweetness from apples. Add apples last to prevent overmixing.

Gluten-Free Spice Cake

Use a gluten-free flour blend and add a teaspoon of xanthan gum to mimic the structure. This makes the cake accessible for gluten-sensitive eaters without sacrificing flavor.

Note: Gluten-free batters often need gentle folding and careful baking to avoid dryness.

Spice Cake with Orange Zest

Add a teaspoon of fresh orange zest to the batter for a bright citrus twist that complements the warm spices beautifully. It adds freshness and complexity.

Note: Zest should be finely grated to distribute flavor evenly without overpowering.

Pro Tips

Always sift your dry ingredients together.
Sifting aerates the flour and spices, ensuring an even distribution of leavening agents and spices. This prevents dense spots or uneven rising, resulting in a lighter, more uniform crumb.
Use room temperature eggs and buttermilk.
Cold ingredients can cause the melted butter to seize or the batter to curdle, which affects texture. Room temperature ingredients blend more smoothly, improving emulsification and final cake tenderness.
Don’t overmix the batter after adding flour.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, making the cake dense and chewy rather than tender and soft. Fold gently just until combined for the best texture.
Rotate your cake pan halfway through baking.
Home ovens often have hot spots. Rotating the pan ensures even baking and browning, preventing one side from overcooking or undercooking.
Let the cake cool in the pan before removing.
Allowing the cake to set in the pan reduces the risk of breaking or crumbling when you transfer it to a cooling rack. It helps the structure firm up gently.
Use freshly ground spices for best flavor.
Spices lose their potency over time. Grinding them fresh releases essential oils that give the cake its signature warm and vibrant flavor.
Measure your ingredients carefully, especially baking soda.
Too much baking soda can cause the cake to taste metallic and rise too fast, then collapse. Too little results in a dense cake. Accurate measurement ensures balanced leavening.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using old or pre-ground spices

Why it happens Many people use leftover pre-ground spices without realizing they lose potency over time, assuming all spices are the same.

Fix Always grind your cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves fresh before baking. This releases the essential oils that give the cake its signature warm aroma and flavor.

Skipping sifting of dry ingredients

Why it happens It’s easy to overlook sifting as an unnecessary step when in a hurry.

Fix Take the extra time to sift flour, spices, and baking soda together. This prevents lumps and ensures even distribution, which is key to a light, evenly flavored cake.

Overmixing after adding flour

Why it happens Sometimes, people want to make sure everything is combined and end up mixing too long.

Fix Mix just until you don’t see dry flour. Overmixing develops gluten, making the cake tough instead of tender.

Not letting the cake cool properly before frosting

Why it happens Impatience to decorate can lead to applying frosting on warm cake.

Fix Allow the cake to cool completely on a wire rack to prevent frosting from melting and sliding off.

Using cold ingredients

Why it happens Some forget to take eggs or buttermilk out ahead of time.

Fix Bring eggs and buttermilk to room temperature before mixing. This helps emulsify the batter and prevents curdling.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The spice cake can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 days if covered tightly with plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The cake may firm up slightly when cold, so bring to room temperature or warm gently before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil before freezing. It keeps well for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature to restore moisture.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk or covering with foil prevents drying.

Make Ahead

You can bake the cake a day ahead and keep it wrapped at room temperature. Frost or dust with powdered sugar on the day of serving for the freshest presentation.

FAQ

Can I use pre-ground spices instead of fresh?
Yes, but the flavor won’t be as vibrant or fresh. Pre-ground spices lose their essential oils and aroma over time. If using pre-ground, try to use recently purchased spices and increase the quantity slightly for stronger flavor.
What can I substitute for buttermilk?
If you don’t have buttermilk, mix 1 cup of milk with 1 tablespoon of lemon juice or white vinegar. Let it sit for 5 minutes before using. This mimics the acidity of buttermilk, which is important for tenderizing and leavening.
Why is my cake dense and heavy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter after adding flour, expired baking soda, or not using acidic buttermilk. Make sure to mix gently and check your leavening agents’ freshness.
How do I know when the cake is done?
Insert a toothpick into the center after 28 minutes of baking. It should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. The top should be golden brown and spring back lightly when touched.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes, use a gluten-free flour blend that includes xanthan gum or another binder. The texture may be slightly different, often a bit more crumbly, but the flavor remains wonderful.
Can I freeze the baked cake?
Absolutely! Wrap it tightly in plastic and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw in the fridge or at room temperature before serving. You can freeze the unfrosted cake or frosted cake, but frosting texture may change slightly.
How should I store leftover cake?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. Bring to room temperature before serving for best flavor and texture.
What frosting pairs best with this spice cake?
Cream cheese frosting is classic and complements the spices beautifully. Vanilla buttercream or a simple dusting of powdered sugar also work well if you prefer something lighter.
