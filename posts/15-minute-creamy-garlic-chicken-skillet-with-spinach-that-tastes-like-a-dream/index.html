Creamy Garlic Chicken Skillet with Fresh Spinach for Cozy Weeknights

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 30 min cook 4 servings

This skillet chicken dish is creamy, garlicky, and comes together in just about 30 minutes. The splash of cream and fresh spinach tossed in right at the end makes it feel special and comforting. Perfect for those busy nights when you want something warm and satisfying without a fuss.

Why This Works

This recipe works because it balances flavors, textures, and cooking techniques to deliver a comforting yet fresh dish. First, searing the chicken thighs over medium-high heat triggers the Maillard reaction, which creates that irresistible golden crust packed with savory umami flavor. This crust not only tastes great but also helps seal in the juices, keeping the chicken tender and moist.

Next, the garlic is gently sautéed to release its essential oils and mellow its sharpness without burning, which is crucial because burnt garlic turns bitter and can ruin the sauce. Deglazing the pan with chicken broth lifts the fond—the caramelized bits stuck to the skillet—and infuses the sauce with deep, roasted flavors that you can't get from plain broth alone.

Adding heavy cream at a gentle simmer creates a rich, velvety sauce by emulsifying fat and liquid. The cream’s fat coats the tongue, giving a luscious mouthfeel that complements the savory chicken perfectly. The fresh spinach tossed in at the end adds a slight crunch and a pop of color, plus a mild vegetal note that cuts through the richness, keeping the dish balanced.

Finally, seasoning at the end allows you to fine-tune the taste because the broth and cream dilute the initial saltiness. This step ensures the final dish is perfectly seasoned and satisfying. Each technique and ingredient interaction here is designed to maximize flavor and texture while keeping the recipe quick and approachable.

  • Chicken thighs are ideal for this recipe because they stay juicy and tender even with quick cooking. When buying, look for skinless, boneless thighs with a fresh, pink color and no unpleasant odor. The higher fat content compared to breasts helps keep the meat moist and flavorful, making them perfect for skillet cooking. Avoid frozen thighs if possible, or thaw thoroughly for even cooking.
  • Fresh garlic cloves provide the best aroma and flavor for this dish. When minced finely, garlic releases its essential oils more efficiently, creating a strong garlic flavor without bitterness. Avoid pre-minced garlic in jars as it tends to have a muted taste. Be careful to cook garlic gently over medium heat to prevent burning, which produces a bitter taste.
  • Heavy cream adds richness and smooth texture to the sauce. When selecting cream, choose one with at least 36% fat content for the best emulsification and mouthfeel. Lower-fat creams or milk can curdle when heated, so heavy cream is preferable. Adding cream slowly over low heat prevents separation and ensures a silky sauce.
  • Baby spinach is preferred for its tender leaves and mild flavor that complements the creamy sauce without overpowering. Fresh spinach wilts quickly, so add it just before serving to preserve its vibrant color and texture. Avoid frozen spinach here, as it releases too much water and dilutes the sauce.
  • Use a good-quality low-sodium chicken broth to control the saltiness of the sauce. The broth provides savory depth and moisture for the sauce base. Homemade broth is ideal, but store-bought works well if you pick one with clean ingredients. Avoid broth with added flavors or MSG to keep the sauce natural and balanced.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs boneless skinless chicken thighs, trimmed
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 6 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup chicken broth
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 3 cups fresh baby spinach
  • 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian herbs

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Season the chicken thighs evenly with kosher salt and black pepper on both sides. This step is key to building flavor right from the start. Make sure not to skip the salt—it helps to tenderize the meat and enhances the overall taste. If you under-season now, the dish can turn out bland. Pat the chicken dry before seasoning to ensure a better sear in the pan.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Chicken thighs seasoned and ready with no moisture on surface for good searing
  2. Step 2

    Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering but not smoking. Add the chicken thighs and cook undisturbed for about 5-7 minutes per side until golden brown and cooked through. This searing locks in juices and develops that delicious caramelized crust. Avoid flipping too often or pressing down on the chicken, which can make it dry.

    ⏱ 12-14 minutes✓ Golden-brown crust on chicken and internal temperature reaching 165°F (74°C)
  3. Step 3

    Remove the chicken from the skillet and set aside on a plate. Lower the heat to medium and add the minced garlic to the same pan. Stir frequently for 1-2 minutes until fragrant and lightly golden. Garlic burns easily and will turn bitter, so watch closely and don’t rush this step. This gentle cooking extracts maximum flavor to infuse the sauce.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Garlic turns lightly golden and smells aromatic but not burnt
  4. Step 4

    Pour in the chicken broth, scraping the bottom of the skillet with a wooden spoon to release any browned bits (fond). This deglazing step adds depth to the sauce and prevents bitterness. Bring the broth to a simmer and let it reduce by about half, concentrating the flavors and thickening the liquid slightly.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Broth reduces and thickens, pan bottom is clean with no stuck bits
  5. Step 5

    Stir in the heavy cream, Italian herbs, and red pepper flakes (if using). Return the chicken to the skillet, spooning some sauce over the top. Simmer gently for 5 minutes to let the chicken soak up the creamy sauce and finish cooking. Avoid boiling at this stage as cream can curdle and chicken can dry out.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Sauce thickens to coat the back of a spoon and chicken feels tender
  6. Step 6

    Add the fresh spinach to the skillet, folding it gently into the sauce until just wilted. This adds freshness, color, and a mild earthy flavor that balances the richness. Overcooking the spinach will make it soggy and dull the flavor, so stir until it just softens.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Spinach is bright green and slightly wilted but still vibrant
  7. Step 7

    Taste the sauce and adjust seasoning with extra salt or pepper if needed. This final tasting is important because the cream and broth dilute flavors slightly. A pinch more salt can really make the dish pop.

    ⏱ 1 minute✓ Balanced, creamy sauce with garlicky depth and seasoning to taste
  8. Step 8

    Serve the creamy garlic chicken skillet hot with your favorite side—rice, pasta, or crusty bread work beautifully to soak up the sauce. Garnish with a sprinkle of fresh parsley or parmesan if you like. Enjoy immediately for best texture and flavor.

    ⏱ 1 minute✓ Dish plated attractively with sauce coating chicken and spinach
  9. Step 9

    Clean the skillet promptly after cooking to avoid stuck-on bits hardening. Soak if necessary then wash with warm soapy water. Keeping your cookware well-maintained ensures even heating for future meals and better flavor development.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Skillet clean and ready for next use without residue

Variations

  • Add Mushrooms for Earthy Depth: Sauté 1 cup of sliced mushrooms with the garlic for an earthy twist. Mushrooms add umami and texture, making the dish heartier. This variation pairs well with rice or buttery noodles.
    Cook mushrooms until golden and their moisture evaporates to avoid watering down the sauce.
  • Spicy Kick with Red Pepper Flakes: Increase red pepper flakes to 1/2 teaspoon or add a dash of cayenne for a spicy version. The heat complements the creamy sauce and garlic beautifully for those who like a little zing.
    Add spices gradually and taste to avoid overpowering the dish.
  • Swap Spinach for Kale: Use chopped kale instead of spinach for a more robust texture and slightly bitter flavor. Kale holds up better if you want leftovers and adds extra nutrients.
    Cook kale a bit longer to soften, about 3-4 minutes, as it’s tougher than spinach.
  • Make it Dairy-Free: Replace heavy cream with full-fat coconut milk and use olive oil instead of butter for a dairy-free version. The sauce remains creamy with a subtle coconut flavor that pairs nicely with garlic.
    Simmer gently as coconut milk can separate if boiled too hard.
  • Serve Over Mashed Potatoes: Instead of rice or pasta, serve the creamy chicken over buttery mashed potatoes for a comforting, hearty meal that soaks up every bit of sauce.
    Prepare mashed potatoes ahead to save time; warm before serving.

Pro Tips

  • Pat chicken dry before seasoning and searing. — Removing surface moisture helps the chicken brown properly instead of steaming. This enhances flavor and texture by promoting the Maillard reaction, which is essential for a tasty crust.
  • Don’t rush cooking the garlic; cook it low and slow. — Garlic burns very easily, turning bitter and ruining the sauce’s flavor. Cooking it gently releases sweet, aromatic oils that build the sauce’s base without harshness.
  • Deglaze the pan with broth after searing chicken. — This step lifts the flavorful browned bits stuck to the pan, adding complexity and depth to the sauce that you can’t get otherwise.
  • Add spinach at the very end and fold gently. — Spinach wilts quickly and adding it last preserves its bright color and fresh texture, balancing the richness of the cream sauce.
  • Simmer sauce gently after adding cream. — High heat can cause the cream to curdle, ruining the sauce’s silky texture. Gentle simmering allows flavors to meld and sauce to thicken beautifully.
  • Season the sauce at the end, not before. — Cream and broth dilute saltiness, so adjusting seasoning last ensures the dish isn’t under- or over-salted.
  • Use boneless, skinless chicken thighs for juiciness. — Thighs have more fat than breasts, which keeps them tender and flavorful during quick cooking, preventing dryness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning the garlic during sautéing
    Why: Many cooks turn up the heat too high or leave garlic unattended, thinking faster cooking saves time.
    Fix: Cook garlic over medium or medium-low heat, stirring frequently. Remove from heat as soon as it turns golden and fragrant to avoid bitterness.
  • Skipping the deglazing step
    Why: Some might be in a rush or unfamiliar with fond, not realizing its flavor contribution.
    Fix: After searing chicken, pour broth into the pan and scrape the bottom with a wooden spoon to lift browned bits. This adds rich flavor to the sauce.
  • Adding spinach too early
    Why: Inexperience with spinach’s quick wilting leads to adding it with the broth or cream.
    Fix: Add spinach at the very end and fold gently until just wilted to keep it vibrant and fresh-tasting.
  • Using chicken breasts instead of thighs without adjusting cooking time
    Why: Breasts cook faster and can dry out if cooked like thighs.
    Fix: If using breasts, reduce cooking time and monitor closely to avoid dryness, or better yet, use thighs for this recipe.
  • Boiling the cream sauce
    Why: High heat can cause cream to curdle or separate if boiled aggressively.
    Fix: Simmer sauce gently after adding cream and avoid rapid boiling for a smooth, creamy texture.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: This dish can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely. Beyond that, refrigerate to prevent bacterial growth. Leaving it uncovered will dry out the sauce.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The sauce may thicken and chicken can firm up, so reheat gently with a splash of broth or cream to restore creaminess.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze in a sealed container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating. Texture may slightly change but flavor remains good.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat on the stovetop over low heat, stirring occasionally and adding a little broth or cream to loosen the sauce. Avoid microwaving on high to prevent curdling and uneven heating.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can cook the chicken and prepare the sauce up to 1 day ahead, then add fresh spinach just before serving. Store sauce and chicken separately from spinach to keep it fresh and vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use chicken breasts instead of thighs?
Yes, but chicken breasts cook faster and can dry out if overcooked. Use medium heat and reduce cooking time by a few minutes per side. Keep an eye on internal temperature to avoid dryness.
What can I substitute for heavy cream?
You can use half-and-half or full-fat coconut milk as alternatives. Keep in mind half-and-half is thinner, so sauce may be less rich and coconut milk adds a subtle flavor. Avoid low-fat milk as it can curdle.
How do I prevent the sauce from curdling?
Simmer the sauce gently after adding cream and avoid boiling. High heat causes the fat and liquid to separate. Stir frequently and keep temperature low to maintain a smooth sauce.
Can I make this recipe gluten-free?
Absolutely! This recipe is naturally gluten-free as long as you use gluten-free chicken broth. Always check labels on broth to be sure.
What sides go well with this chicken skillet?
Rice, pasta, mashed potatoes, or crusty bread are all great for soaking up the creamy sauce. A simple green salad also balances the richness nicely.
Can I prepare this recipe ahead of time?
Yes, you can cook the chicken and sauce a day ahead and reheat gently. Add fresh spinach just before serving to keep it vibrant and fresh.
How do I know when the chicken is fully cooked?
Chicken thighs should reach an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C). They should be opaque and juices run clear when pierced. Using a meat thermometer is the most reliable method.
Why does the sauce sometimes separate?
Sauce separation usually happens from boiling cream or overheating. To fix, reduce heat and stir in a small amount of cold cream or broth to bring it back together.
