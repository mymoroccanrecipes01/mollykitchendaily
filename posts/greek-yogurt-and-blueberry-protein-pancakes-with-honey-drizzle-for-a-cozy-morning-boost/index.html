Fluffy Protein-Packed Blueberry Pancakes with Greek Yogurt and Honey Drizzle

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These pancakes are super fluffy and packed with protein thanks to Greek yogurt. Fresh blueberries add a burst of sweetness, and a honey drizzle finishes them off perfectly. Great for a weekend brunch or a filling morning treat!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this recipe a few years ago when I wanted a breakfast that felt indulgent but was still packed with protein to keep me full until lunch. The secret ingredient here is Greek yogurt, which not only adds protein but keeps the pancakes incredibly moist and fluffy. I remember the first time I tried making these; I was worried the batter might be too thick or the blueberries would sink to the bottom, but with just the right balance of baking soda and powder, they puff up beautifully every time. Plus, folding in fresh blueberries right before cooking gives bursts of juicy sweetness in every bite. Topping them with a drizzle of honey adds a natural sweetness that’s never overpowering. These pancakes are perfect for slow weekend mornings or when you want to impress guests with something simple yet special. The texture is soft but airy, and the tang from the yogurt complements the sweetness of the fruit perfectly. Honestly, once you try this version, you might never go back to ordinary pancakes!

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 pancakes, 4 servings

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup vanilla Greek yogurt, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup milk, room temperature
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons honey, plus extra for drizzling
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup fresh blueberries
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter or oil

How To Do It

Step 1
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. This dry mix is crucial because the baking powder and soda work together to create the fluffy texture by releasing carbon dioxide bubbles during cooking. Make sure to sift or whisk well to distribute the leaveners evenly; otherwise, you risk uneven rising and dense spots in your pancakes.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no visible clumps
Step 2
In a separate bowl, combine the Greek yogurt, milk, egg, honey, and melted butter. Whisk these wet ingredients until smooth and slightly frothy. The yogurt should be at room temperature to blend easily without clumps, which helps keep the batter consistent and prevents lumps in the pancakes.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, creamy, and slightly thick
Step 3
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and gently fold them together using a spatula. It’s important not to overmix here; a few lumps are fine. Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, making pancakes tough instead of tender and fluffy.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is combined but still slightly lumpy
Step 4
Carefully fold in the fresh blueberries, distributing them evenly without breaking them up. Adding the blueberries last prevents them from bursting and staining the batter, which keeps your pancakes looking pretty and the blueberries intact for bursts of flavor.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Blueberries are evenly spread throughout the batter
Step 5
Heat a non-stick skillet or griddle over medium heat and lightly grease with butter or oil. The pan needs to be hot enough so the batter sizzles gently when poured, but not so hot that the pancakes burn before cooking through. Adjust heat as needed to maintain an even cooking temperature.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan is hot enough to make the batter sizzle softly on contact
Step 6
Pour about 1/4 cup of batter per pancake onto the skillet, spacing them apart. Cook without moving until bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set, about 2-3 minutes. This stage is key for the pancakes to rise and set properly before flipping.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bubbles appear on top and edges look firm and dry
Step 7
Flip the pancakes gently using a spatula and cook for another 2-3 minutes on the other side until golden brown and cooked through. Avoid pressing down on the pancakes, as that squeezes out the air and makes them dense rather than fluffy.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pancakes are golden on both sides and spring back to touch
Step 8
Transfer cooked pancakes to a warm plate and keep covered loosely with foil while you finish the rest. This keeps them warm and moist without steaming them soggy. Serving immediately is best, but this method helps if you’re making a big batch.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pancakes stay warm and fluffy, no condensation forming
Step 9
Serve the pancakes stacked with a generous drizzle of honey on top. The honey adds a natural sweetness that complements the tangy yogurt and fresh berries perfectly. You can also add a pat of butter or a sprinkle of nuts for extra texture if you like.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Honey glistens beautifully on the pancakes, ready to enjoy

Variations

Banana Blueberry Protein Pancakes

Add one mashed ripe banana to the wet ingredients for natural sweetness and extra moisture. This adds a lovely banana flavor and makes the pancakes even softer. The batter will be slightly thicker, so cook at medium-low heat to avoid burning.

Note: Banana increases moisture, so reduce milk by 2 tablespoons to keep batter consistency similar.

Lemon Blueberry Pancakes

Add 1 teaspoon of lemon zest to the batter and a splash of lemon juice to brighten the flavor profile. This pairs beautifully with blueberries for a fresh, tangy twist. Serve with a dusting of powdered sugar instead of honey for a lighter finish.

Note: Lemon juice reacts with baking soda, so reduce baking powder by 1/4 teaspoon to balance rise.

Gluten-Free Blueberry Pancakes

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free baking blend. Ensure it contains xanthan gum or add 1/4 teaspoon for structure. The texture will be slightly different—more tender but still fluffy with proper mixing and cooking.

Note: Gluten-free batters can be more delicate; handle gently and cook at slightly lower heat.

Vegan Blueberry Pancakes

Replace the egg with a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water) and use plant-based yogurt and milk. The pancakes will be a bit denser but still moist and flavorful with the right leavening and mixing.

Note: Let flax egg mixture sit 5 minutes before adding to wet ingredients for best binding.

Chocolate Chip Blueberry Pancakes

Add 1/3 cup mini chocolate chips along with the blueberries for a sweet treat that kids and adults love. The chocolate melts into the fluffy batter, creating pockets of gooey goodness balanced by the fresh berries.

Note: Use mini chips to prevent sinking; fold in gently to keep even distribution.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature ingredients for smooth batter
Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, leading to dense pancakes. Room temperature milk and yogurt blend better with dry ingredients, ensuring a uniform texture and consistent rise.
Don’t overmix the batter to keep pancakes tender
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which makes pancakes chewy and tough. Mixing just until combined leaves some lumps, producing a soft, fluffy texture once cooked.
Cook pancakes on medium heat for even browning
Too high heat burns the outside before the inside cooks, while too low heat results in pale, dense pancakes. Medium heat allows Maillard reactions for flavor and color without sacrificing texture.
Fold in blueberries gently at the end
Adding berries too early or stirring vigorously breaks them, releasing juice that can discolor the batter and make pancakes soggy. Folding preserves berry shape and flavor bursts.
Keep cooked pancakes warm under foil
Covering with foil traps heat but lets steam escape, preventing sogginess. This keeps pancakes soft and warm while finishing the batch.
Use a non-stick skillet or well-seasoned griddle
This prevents sticking and tearing when flipping pancakes, ensuring they stay intact and look appealing.
Serve immediately or reheat gently
Pancakes are best fresh but can be reheated in a warm oven or toaster. Avoid microwaving too long as it can dry them out.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Many cooks think a smooth batter means better pancakes, so they stir excessively to remove lumps.

Fix Mix the batter just until the dry ingredients are moistened. Some small lumps are normal and help keep the texture tender. Use a spatula to fold ingredients gently.

Using cold yogurt or milk

Why it happens For convenience, ingredients are often used straight from the fridge without warming.

Fix Bring dairy ingredients to room temperature before mixing. This helps them blend smoothly and prevents uneven cooking.

Cooking pancakes on too high heat

Why it happens Impatience or trying to speed cooking leads to higher heat.

Fix Maintain medium heat so pancakes cook evenly with a golden crust. Adjust burner as needed to avoid burning.

Pressing down pancakes with spatula

Why it happens Some think flattening speeds cooking or makes pancakes thinner.

Fix Resist pressing to keep the air bubbles intact, which create fluffiness. Flip carefully and let them cook undisturbed.

Adding blueberries too early or stirring them too much

Why it happens Trying to distribute fruit evenly or mixing vigorously.

Fix Fold blueberries in gently at the end to keep them whole and prevent bleeding color into the batter.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Pancakes can be safely left at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean towel to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store pancakes in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. They may stiffen slightly but reheat well in a toaster or oven to regain softness.

Freezer

Freeze pancakes in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer bag for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or reheat directly from frozen using a toaster or oven.

Reheating

Reheat pancakes in a toaster oven or on a skillet over low heat for a few minutes until warmed through. Avoid microwaving too long to prevent rubbery texture; add a small pat of butter or a splash of milk if needed for moisture.

Make Ahead

Mix the batter the night before and keep it covered in the fridge. Stir gently before cooking the next day. Alternatively, prepare pancakes fully and freeze for quick breakfasts.

FAQ

Can I use frozen blueberries instead of fresh?
Yes, but thaw and drain frozen blueberries well before folding them into the batter to avoid excess moisture that can make pancakes soggy. Frozen berries tend to break down more during cooking, so handle gently.
What if I don’t have Greek yogurt?
You can substitute with regular plain yogurt or sour cream, but the texture may be slightly less thick and the protein content lower. Adjust the milk quantity to keep batter consistency similar.
How do I make these pancakes dairy-free?
Use plant-based yogurt (like coconut or almond) and milk alternatives such as oat or soy milk. Make sure the yogurt is thick to mimic Greek yogurt’s moisture and protein for best results.
Why are my pancakes not fluffy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter, using expired leavening agents, or cooking at too high heat. Make sure to measure baking powder and soda correctly and mix gently.
Can I make the batter ahead of time?
Absolutely! Prepare the batter up to 24 hours ahead and keep it refrigerated. Give it a gentle stir before cooking. The batter may thicken slightly, so add a splash of milk if needed.
How do I store leftover pancakes?
Cool them completely, then store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days or freeze for longer storage. Reheat gently to maintain texture.
Can I add other fruits or mix-ins?
Yes! Chopped strawberries, raspberries, or chocolate chips work well. Add them at the same stage as the blueberries to avoid breaking them up.
Why should I avoid pressing pancakes while cooking?
Pressing squeezes out air bubbles that create fluffiness, resulting in dense, flat pancakes. Let them cook undisturbed until ready to flip.
