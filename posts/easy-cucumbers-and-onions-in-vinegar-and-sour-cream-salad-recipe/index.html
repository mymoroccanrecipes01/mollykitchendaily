Classic Creamy Cucumber and Onion Salad That Brightens Any Meal

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 4, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This crisp cucumber and onion salad tossed in a tangy vinegar and creamy sour cream dressing is my go-to summer side. It’s light, refreshing, and perfect for potlucks or BBQs.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this creamy cucumber and onion salad when my grandmother served it at a family cookout years ago. It was such a refreshing contrast to the heavier dishes on the table, and I’ve been hooked ever since. What makes this version special is the balance between the tangy vinegar and the rich sour cream, which coats the crisp cucumbers perfectly without being too heavy. I like to slice the cucumbers and onions very thinly to ensure the dressing seeps in well, giving every bite a burst of flavor. One trick I learned is to salt the cucumbers and let them sit for about 10 minutes before mixing—they release excess water, which keeps the salad from getting soggy. The finished salad looks vibrant and inviting, with the cool crunch of cucumbers and the slight bite of onion mellowed by the creamy, tangy dressing. It’s one of those simple dishes that feels both nostalgic and fresh, perfect for summer dinners or casual gatherings with friends. Honestly, sometimes I make a double batch just so I have leftovers for lunch the next day!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, 4 servings

  • 2 large cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 3 tablespoons white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 cup sour cream
  • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 1 tablespoon fresh dill, chopped (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing the cucumbers and red onion. Use a sharp knife or mandoline to get even, paper-thin slices. This is important because thin slices absorb the dressing better and create a delicate texture. After slicing, sprinkle the cucumbers with salt and toss gently. Let them sit in a colander for about 10 minutes to draw out excess moisture. This step prevents the salad from becoming watery and helps concentrate the cucumber's flavor. Avoid skipping this, or your salad might turn soggy.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers look slightly wilted and have released some liquid into the colander.
Step 2
While the cucumbers drain, whisk together the white vinegar, sugar, and sour cream in a large bowl. This mixture creates the creamy, tangy dressing that defines the salad. The vinegar adds brightness, the sugar balances acidity, and the sour cream brings smoothness. Whisk until smooth and well combined. If you rush this step, the sugar might not dissolve fully, leaving grainy spots in the dressing.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dressing is smooth, creamy, and slightly thickened with no sugar granules visible.
Step 3
Pat the salted cucumbers dry with paper towels to remove any remaining moisture. This ensures the dressing clings well and the salad stays crisp. Add the cucumbers and sliced onions to the bowl with the dressing. Gently toss everything together until the vegetables are evenly coated. Being gentle prevents the delicate slices from breaking apart, maintaining an appealing texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Vegetables are thoroughly coated with creamy dressing, and the salad looks fresh and glossy.
Step 4
Season the salad with freshly ground black pepper to taste. Pepper adds a subtle kick that balances the cool creaminess. If you like, stir in chopped fresh dill for an herbal note that complements the cucumbers beautifully. Dill is optional but highly recommended for a classic flavor profile.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pepper is evenly distributed, and dill pieces are visible throughout the salad.
Step 5
Cover the salad and refrigerate it for at least 30 minutes before serving. This chill time lets the flavors meld and the cucumbers absorb the dressing fully. It also enhances the refreshing quality of the salad. Avoid skipping this; serving immediately won’t let the flavors develop properly.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad tastes well-balanced, cool, and the flavors are harmoniously blended.
Step 6
Before serving, give the salad a gentle toss again. Check the seasoning and adjust salt or pepper if needed. Sometimes chilling dulls the flavors slightly, so a quick taste test is helpful. Serve chilled as a side dish to grilled meats, sandwiches, or as a light snack.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad looks fresh, vibrant, and tastes balanced with a creamy, tangy finish.
Step 7
To keep the salad fresh if making ahead, store it in an airtight container in the fridge. The cucumbers will continue to soften over time, so it’s best eaten within 24 hours for optimal texture. If you notice excess liquid, simply drain it off before serving to keep the salad crisp.
⏱ Time: Varies
✓ Doneness: Salad remains creamy and fresh without excessive water pooling.
Step 8
If you want a lighter version, you can substitute half the sour cream with Greek yogurt. This keeps the creaminess but adds a bit more tang and reduces fat. Whisk it the same way as the original dressing. This swap is great if you want a fresher, slightly tangier profile without losing texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dressing is smooth with a slightly tangy aroma and creamy consistency.
Step 9
For an extra crunch, consider adding a handful of toasted sunflower seeds or chopped walnuts just before serving. This adds texture contrast and a nutty flavor that pairs well with the creamy dressing. Sprinkle on top gently so the salad stays intact.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad has visible nutty pieces and a pleasant crunch when eaten.

Variations

Greek Yogurt Cucumber Salad

Swap sour cream for Greek yogurt for a tangier, lighter version. It adds protein and a fresh bite while keeping the creamy texture intact. Perfect if you want a healthier twist without sacrificing flavor.

Note: Greek yogurt is thicker and tangier; whisk it well with vinegar and sugar to maintain smoothness.

Spicy Cucumber and Onion Salad

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or chopped jalapeño to the dressing for a spicy kick. This variation adds heat that contrasts nicely with the cooling cucumbers and creamy dressing.

Note: Add spice gradually and taste as you go to avoid overpowering the salad.

Vegan Creamy Cucumber Salad

Use vegan sour cream or cashew cream instead of dairy sour cream. This keeps the salad creamy and tangy while making it suitable for plant-based diets.

Note: Ensure vegan sour cream is unsweetened and plain to maintain the salad’s traditional flavor.

Add Fresh Herbs Mix

Mix fresh parsley, chives, and dill for a herbal explosion. This adds complexity and freshness, making the salad more vibrant and aromatic.

Note: Chop herbs finely and stir in just before serving to preserve their brightness.

Crunchy Nutty Cucumber Salad

Top with toasted sunflower seeds or walnuts for added crunch and a nutty flavor that complements the creamy dressing. Great for adding texture contrast.

Note: Add nuts just before serving to keep them crisp.

Pro Tips

Salt the cucumbers and let them drain before mixing.
Salting draws out excess water from cucumbers, which prevents the salad from becoming watery and helps concentrate their natural flavor. If you skip this step, the dressing can become diluted, and the salad texture will suffer.
Use a sharp knife or mandoline for thin, even slices.
Thin, uniform slices absorb the dressing better and create a more elegant texture. Uneven slices can result in inconsistent flavor and texture, with some pieces overpowering others.
Chill the salad for at least 30 minutes before serving.
Allowing the salad to rest helps the flavors meld and the cucumbers soak up the dressing. Serving immediately results in a less flavorful dish and a less cohesive texture.
Adjust sugar to balance vinegar acidity carefully.
The sugar tames the sharpness of the vinegar, creating a balanced tang. Too little sugar makes the salad too sour; too much makes it overly sweet. Taste as you mix to find the perfect balance.
Add fresh dill for authentic flavor.
Dill adds a fresh, herbal note that brightens the salad. Without it, the salad can taste flat or overly creamy. Fresh dill is preferable to dried for vibrancy.
Pat cucumbers dry after salting.
Removing excess moisture ensures the dressing clings well and the salad remains crisp. Moisture left on the cucumbers dilutes the dressing and leads to a soggy texture.
Serve chilled but not frozen.
Chilling enhances flavor and texture, but over-chilling or freezing can cause the cucumbers to become mushy and watery. Keep it in the fridge until serving time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the salting step

Why it happens Many home cooks want to hurry and mix everything right away, not realizing that cucumbers hold a lot of water that can dilute the salad.

Fix Take the time to salt and drain cucumbers properly. Use a colander and let them sit for at least 10 minutes, then pat dry before mixing. This keeps the salad crisp and flavorful.

Using thick cucumber slices

Why it happens Without proper tools, people slice cucumbers unevenly or too thick, which affects texture and dressing absorption.

Fix Use a mandoline or sharp knife to get thin, even slices. This ensures good texture and that the dressing coats each piece well.

Not balancing vinegar and sugar

Why it happens Some assume more vinegar equals more flavor and add too much without sweetening, leading to an overly sour salad.

Fix Always add sugar to balance vinegar’s acidity. Taste the dressing before mixing and adjust gradually for a harmonious flavor.

Serving immediately

Why it happens Impatience or last-minute prep leads to serving the salad before flavors meld.

Fix Plan ahead to chill the salad at least 30 minutes. This resting time is key for flavor development and texture.

Using watery sour cream

Why it happens Some sour creams have excess liquid or are low-fat, which can thin the dressing.

Fix Choose full-fat, thick sour cream for the best texture. If watery, strain it through a fine sieve or use Greek yogurt as a substitute.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours as the dairy can spoil. Keep it covered in a cool spot if serving buffet-style.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 24 hours. The cucumbers will soften slightly, and flavors meld more, but the salad is best enjoyed fresh and cold. Avoid leaving it overnight if you prefer crisp texture.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers become watery and mushy upon thawing, ruining the salad’s texture.

Reheating

Do not reheat this salad. Serve chilled or at room temperature for best flavor and texture.

Make Ahead

You can slice cucumbers and onions a day ahead and store them separately in the fridge. Make the dressing and combine everything 30 minutes before serving for optimal freshness.

FAQ

Can I use regular white vinegar instead of apple cider vinegar?
Yes, white vinegar works perfectly in this recipe. It provides a clean, sharp acidity without altering the color or flavor too much. Apple cider vinegar can be used for a slightly fruitier taste but isn’t necessary.
How long can I store this salad in the fridge?
For best texture and flavor, eat the salad within 24 hours. The cucumbers release more water over time, which can make the salad soggy. If you need to store longer, keep cucumbers and dressing separate until ready to serve.
Can I substitute sour cream with mayonnaise?
Mayonnaise can be used but it changes the flavor and texture, making it richer and less tangy. I prefer sour cream or Greek yogurt for their fresh, slightly tart taste that balances the vinegar.
What if I don’t have fresh dill?
You can substitute dried dill, but use less because dried herbs are more concentrated. Fresh dill provides a brighter, fresher flavor that really lifts the salad.
How do I prevent the salad from getting watery?
Salting and draining the cucumbers before mixing is key to reduce excess water. Also, pat them dry thoroughly with paper towels. Avoid freezing or storing too long after mixing.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Definitely! Thinly sliced radishes or bell peppers add crunch and color. Just keep the slices thin so they blend well with the cucumbers and dressing.
Is this salad suitable for meal prep?
It’s best to prep ingredients separately and combine shortly before eating to keep cucumbers crisp. The salad doesn’t hold up well for more than a day mixed due to moisture.
Can I make this salad spicy?
Yes! Add red pepper flakes or a dash of hot sauce to the dressing for a spicy kick. Start with a small amount and adjust to taste.
