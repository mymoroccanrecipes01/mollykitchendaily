Moist and Chocolatey Zucchini Bread That Sneaks in Veggies

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 13, 2026
15 min Prep Time
45 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This chocolate zucchini bread is my go-to when I want a sweet treat that feels indulgent but sneaks in some veggies. The grated zucchini keeps it super moist and adds a subtle natural sweetness. Perfect for breakfast or an afternoon snack with coffee!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this chocolate zucchini bread recipe when I wanted to make something sweet but also sneak in some veggies for my kids. Honestly, I was skeptical at first—how good could a bread with zucchini really taste? But after a few trials, I found the perfect balance. The grated zucchini adds moisture and a subtle natural sweetness that pairs beautifully with cocoa. This version is better than the ones I tried before because I use both brown and granulated sugar for a richer flavor and add a touch of vanilla to enhance the chocolate notes. One trick I learned is to squeeze out some of the zucchini’s excess water before mixing it in. This keeps the bread from getting too soggy but still retains that tender crumb. The finished loaf is moist, chocolatey, and just sweet enough—not overpowering. It’s great for breakfast or as an afternoon pick-me-up with a cup of coffee. Plus, it’s a sneaky way to get some veggies into your day without anyone noticing. I love how versatile it is, and it always disappears fast at my house!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini)
  • 1/2 cup mini chocolate chips (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and greasing a 9x5 inch loaf pan. This ensures your bread won’t stick and will brown evenly. Greasing the pan well is key because the batter is pretty moist from the zucchini, and you want the loaf to release easily without tearing when you slice it.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated and pan ready with a light coating of oil or butter
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, and salt. Sifting these ingredients can help avoid lumps and ensures even distribution, which is important for a consistent rise and crumb in your bread.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no clumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, beat the granulated sugar, brown sugar, eggs, vegetable oil, and vanilla extract until smooth and well combined. The sugars dissolve slightly here, helping to create a moist texture and a deep flavor. Avoid overbeating to prevent a dense loaf.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet mixture is smooth, glossy, and homogenous
Step 4
Grate the zucchini finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze out some excess moisture. This step is crucial because zucchini holds a lot of water, and too much liquid can make your bread soggy and prevent it from baking through properly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet
Step 5
Add the wet ingredients to the dry ingredients and gently fold just until combined. Then fold in the grated zucchini and chocolate chips if using. Be careful not to overmix here; overmixing develops gluten and can make the bread tough instead of tender and soft.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, moist, and evenly mixed with no streaks of flour
Step 6
Pour the batter into your prepared loaf pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and rise nicely. Make sure the batter is spread evenly so it cooks uniformly and you avoid a lopsided loaf.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly distributed and level in the pan
Step 7
Bake for about 45 minutes, checking around 40 minutes with a toothpick inserted in the center. It should come out mostly clean with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Baking too long can dry out the bread, so start checking early to catch it at perfect doneness.
⏱ Time: 40-45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick comes out with a few moist crumbs, edges pull away slightly from pan
Step 8
Let the bread cool in the pan for 15 minutes before transferring it to a wire rack. This resting allows steam to escape slowly and prevents the bread from becoming soggy. Cooling completely before slicing ensures clean, neat slices.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is warm but firm enough to handle without breaking
Step 9
Slice and serve! This bread is delicious on its own or with a smear of butter or cream cheese. It keeps well for a few days stored airtight and even freezes beautifully — just thaw and warm before serving.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Slices hold shape and show moist crumb with chocolate flecks

Variations

Nutty Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans to the batter for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor that pairs beautifully with the chocolate and zucchini.

Note: Fold nuts in gently with the zucchini to distribute evenly without overmixing.

Spiced Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Mix in 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg to give the bread a warm, cozy spice profile that complements the chocolate.

Note: Add spices to the dry ingredients to ensure even distribution throughout the batter.

Gluten-Free Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the texture moist and tender without gluten.

Note: Check baking time and texture as gluten-free flours can vary; add a little extra moisture if needed.

Vegan Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a plant-based oil. This keeps it moist and delicious without eggs.

Note: Allow flax eggs to thicken before mixing; baking time may vary slightly.

Chocolate Zucchini Muffins

Divide the batter into a lined muffin tin for portable, individual treats. Great for grab-and-go breakfasts or lunchboxes.

Note: Bake at 350°F for 20-25 minutes, checking with a toothpick for doneness.

Pro Tips

Squeeze the grated zucchini well to remove excess moisture before adding.
Removing extra water prevents the batter from becoming too wet, which can lead to a gummy or undercooked center. It ensures the bread bakes evenly with a tender crumb instead of soggy spots.
Mix the batter just until the flour disappears.
Overmixing causes gluten development, making the bread tough and dense. Gentle folding keeps the crumb light and tender, which is especially important in quick breads with delicate ingredients like zucchini.
Use a light hand when measuring flour—spoon it into the cup and level off.
Packing flour too tightly adds too much, resulting in a dry, crumbly bread. Lightly measured flour maintains the right moisture balance for a soft, moist loaf.
Test doneness with a toothpick starting around 40 minutes.
Ovens vary, so checking early prevents overbaking, which dries out the bread. A toothpick with a few moist crumbs is perfect—avoid waiting for it to come out completely clean, which indicates dryness.
Let the bread cool in the pan before removing.
Cooling stabilizes the crumb and prevents it from breaking apart. Removing it too hot can cause the bread to fall apart or stick to the pan.
Add a handful of mini chocolate chips for extra indulgence.
Chocolate chips melt slightly during baking, adding gooey pockets of chocolate that complement the cocoa powder’s richness and make the bread feel more decadent.
Use room temperature eggs and oil for better mixing.
Cold ingredients don’t combine as smoothly, which can lead to uneven texture. Room temperature components blend more uniformly, resulting in a consistent batter and even crumb.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out zucchini moisture enough

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be added directly, but its high water content can make the batter too wet.

Fix Use a clean kitchen towel or paper towels to press and squeeze the zucchini firmly before folding it into the batter. This step is crucial to avoid soggy bread and ensure proper baking.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens In an attempt to combine ingredients thoroughly, it’s easy to overmix, especially with thick batters.

Fix Fold ingredients gently just until no flour streaks remain. Stop mixing as soon as the ingredients come together to keep the bread tender.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Some ovens run hot or people crank up the heat to speed baking, which can brown the crust too fast.

Fix Stick to 350°F and check doneness early. Lower temps allow the inside to cook fully without burning the outside.

Using too much flour

Why it happens Scooping flour directly from the bag packs it down, leading to excess flour in the batter.

Fix Spoon flour into measuring cups and level with a knife to avoid dense, dry bread.

Not allowing bread to cool before slicing

Why it happens It’s tempting to dig in immediately, but the bread is fragile when hot.

Fix Cool the loaf at least 30 minutes on a wire rack before slicing to let the crumb set and avoid crumbling.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cooled bread covered loosely with a clean kitchen towel or in a bread box for up to 2 days. This keeps the crust from becoming too soft while maintaining moisture inside.

Refrigerator

Keep bread in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up when chilled, so warm slightly before serving to restore softness.

Freezer

Wrap the bread tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature before warming.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a small pat of butter or a drizzle of milk before warming helps keep it moist and delicious.

Make Ahead

You can grate zucchini and measure dry ingredients a day ahead. Mix the batter and bake fresh to get the best rise and texture. The baked bread can be made 1-2 days ahead and stored wrapped.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this bread?
Yes, but thaw and squeeze out all excess moisture very well before using. Frozen zucchini tends to be more watery, so thorough draining is essential to avoid a soggy batter.
What if I don’t have cocoa powder?
You can omit cocoa powder for a plain zucchini bread or substitute with melted chocolate, but adjust sugar slightly. Keep in mind the texture and flavor will change significantly.
How do I know when the bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick in the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. The edges will also pull away slightly from the pan.
Can I make this bread gluten-free?
Yes, use a gluten-free flour blend with xanthan gum. Baking time may vary, so check doneness a bit earlier or later depending on your flour.
Why is my bread dense or heavy?
Overmixing the batter or adding too much flour can cause density. Be gentle when folding ingredients and measure flour carefully.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Absolutely! Fold in about 1/2 cup of chopped nuts or mini chocolate chips for extra texture and flavor.
How long does this bread keep fresh?
Stored properly at room temperature, it stays fresh for 2 days. Refrigerate for up to 5 days or freeze for 3 months.
Can I double the recipe?
Yes, double all ingredients and bake in two loaf pans. Baking time may be slightly longer; check doneness with a toothpick.
