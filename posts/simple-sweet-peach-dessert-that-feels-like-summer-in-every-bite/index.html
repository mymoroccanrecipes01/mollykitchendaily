Summer Peach Crumble with a Buttery Crumb Topping That Melts in Your Mouth

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 17, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This peach crumble is my go-to for easy summer desserts. Juicy fresh peaches get covered in a buttery, crumbly topping that crisps up beautifully. I love serving it warm with vanilla ice cream on weekend nights!

Introduction

I first made this peach crumble on a lazy summer afternoon when I had a basket of ripe peaches that begged to be used. I wanted something simple yet satisfying, and this recipe quickly became a family favorite. What sets my version apart is the combination of oats and flour in the crumb topping, which creates a perfect balance of crunch and tenderness. Plus, using cold butter cut into cubes helps the topping bake up into those delightful little crispy clusters instead of a flat crust. I've learned that letting the peaches sit with lemon juice and a bit of sugar before baking brings out their natural juices and keeps the filling wonderfully juicy but not soggy. When it’s done, the crumble is golden on top, with bubbling peach juices underneath. It’s the kind of dessert that feels like a warm hug, especially when paired with a scoop of vanilla ice cream. I love making this for weekend dinners or casual get-togethers because it’s fuss-free but always impresses. Honestly, the first time I made it, I forgot to add the vanilla extract and thought it tasted a bit flat—so don’t skip that part! This recipe is forgiving and straightforward, perfect for any home cook looking to celebrate summer’s best flavors.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 5 cups fresh peaches, peeled and sliced
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup rolled oats
  • 1/2 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, cold and cut into cubes

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). This temperature is ideal for gently baking the crumble topping to a golden crisp without burning it or undercooking the peaches. While the oven heats, peel, pit, and slice the peaches evenly so they cook at the same rate. Toss the peaches with lemon juice and granulated sugar in a large mixing bowl. The lemon juice not only adds brightness but also prevents the peaches from browning. Mixing in the sugar helps draw out the natural juices during baking, creating a luscious filling. Let the mixture sit for 10 minutes to macerate, which softens the peaches slightly and intensifies their flavor.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches should look juicy and slightly softened with sugar dissolved
Step 2
While the peaches macerate, prepare the crumble topping. In a separate bowl, combine the flour, rolled oats, brown sugar, cinnamon, and salt. These dry ingredients form the base of your crumb and add texture and warmth. Add the cold, cubed butter to the mixture. Using your fingers or a pastry cutter, work the butter into the dry ingredients until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces. The cold butter is key because as it melts during baking, it creates pockets of flakiness and crispness in the topping. Avoid over-mixing to keep that crumbly texture intact.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture should hold together when squeezed but still be crumbly
Step 3
Transfer the peach mixture to a buttered 8x8-inch baking dish, spreading it evenly. Pouring the peaches and their juices into the dish ensures even cooking and a juicy filling. Distribute the crumble topping evenly over the peaches, covering them completely. This layer seals in the juices and bakes into a crunchy, golden crust. Be sure not to press down on the topping; it should remain loose to achieve the best texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Even layer of crumble topping fully covering peach filling
Step 4
Place the baking dish on the middle rack of your preheated oven and bake for about 30 minutes. Baking at this position ensures even heat circulation around the crumble. The topping should turn golden brown and the peach filling should be bubbling around the edges. This bubbling is a sign that the filling is hot and the sugars have caramelized nicely. Avoid opening the oven door too often, as this can cause temperature fluctuations that affect baking.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown topping with bubbling, fragrant peach juices
Step 5
Once baked, remove the peach crumble from the oven and let it cool for 10-15 minutes. This resting time allows the filling to thicken slightly, so it won’t be too runny when served. It also helps the topping set just enough to hold its shape while still being tender. Serve warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or whipped cream for extra indulgence.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Slightly thickened peach filling and warm, crisp topping
Step 6
If you want to reheat leftovers, cover the crumble with foil and warm it in a 325°F oven for 15-20 minutes until heated through. Reheating slowly prevents the topping from burning and keeps the peaches juicy. Avoid microwaving if possible, as it can make the topping soggy.
⏱ Time: 15-20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Warm throughout with crisp topping restored
Step 7
For best results, use ripe but firm peaches. Overripe peaches tend to get mushy and watery, which can affect the texture of the crumble. If peaches are too tart, add a tablespoon more sugar to balance the flavor. Adjust sweetness based on your fruit’s ripeness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches should be fragrant, firm yet juicy
Step 8
If you want to add a little twist, sprinkle chopped nuts like pecans or walnuts over the crumble topping before baking. This adds a lovely crunch and nutty flavor that pairs beautifully with the sweet peaches.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Visible nuts on top, toasted golden after baking
Step 9
After baking and cooling, store any leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge. The crumble topping may soften over time, but reheating can bring back some crispness. Enjoy within 3 days for the best flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Stored properly without moisture buildup or sogginess

Variations

Peach Blueberry Crumble

Add 1 cup fresh blueberries to the peach filling for a burst of tartness and color. The blueberries complement the peaches and add extra juiciness. The topping remains the same, giving a lovely contrast to the mixed berries.

Note: Baking time may increase slightly because of the extra moisture from blueberries; watch for bubbling and golden topping.

Vegan Peach Crumble

Replace butter with coconut oil or vegan margarine and use maple syrup instead of brown sugar. This version is dairy-free but still has a rich, nutty crumble topping and juicy filling.

Note: Coconut oil melts differently, so mix quickly and bake immediately to maintain topping texture.

Peach Crumble with Almond Flour Topping

Swap half the flour for almond flour in the topping for a nutty flavor and softer texture. Almond flour adds moisture and richness, making the crumble more tender.

Note: Because almond flour browns faster, keep an eye on the topping to avoid burning.

Spiced Peach Crumble

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger and a pinch of nutmeg to the topping for a warming spice note. These spices pair beautifully with peaches and add complexity.

Note: Spices intensify during baking, so use sparingly to avoid overpowering the fruit.

Peach Crumble with Oatmeal Streusel

Increase oats in the topping and add a tablespoon of honey for a chewier, stickier crumble. This variation is heartier and pairs well with rustic, country-style desserts.

Note: Honey adds moisture, so bake a few extra minutes to ensure topping crispness.

Pro Tips

Use ripe but firm peaches for the best texture.
Ripe peaches provide sweetness and juiciness, but if they’re too soft, the filling becomes mushy and watery. Firm peaches hold their shape during baking, giving you that perfect balance between tender fruit and a thick filling.
Keep your butter cold and cut into small cubes before mixing.
Cold butter melts slowly in the oven, creating steam pockets that make the crumble topping flaky and crisp. If the butter is warm or melted, the topping can turn greasy and dense, losing that desirable crumbly texture.
Let the peaches macerate with sugar and lemon juice before baking.
This step draws out the natural juices and softens the fruit slightly, helping to build a rich, syrupy filling. It also enhances flavor by allowing the sugar and acid to penetrate the peaches evenly.
Don’t press down on the crumble topping before baking.
Leaving the topping loose helps it bake into crisp clusters instead of a flat crust. Pressing it down can create a dense texture and prevent the topping from crisping properly.
Bake the crumble in the middle rack of the oven.
This placement ensures even heat circulation around the dish, allowing the topping to brown nicely and the filling to bubble without burning the edges.
Let the crumble cool briefly before serving.
Resting allows the filling to thicken as it cools, preventing it from running when served. It also lets the topping set slightly for better texture.
Add chopped nuts to the topping for extra crunch.
Nuts like pecans or walnuts toast beautifully during baking, adding a wonderful texture contrast and a nutty flavor that complements the sweet peaches.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using overripe peaches that are too soft

Why it happens Sometimes you want to use peaches that look very ripe or feel soft, thinking they’ll be sweeter. But overly soft peaches break down too much during baking.

Fix Choose peaches that are ripe but still hold their shape when gently squeezed. If you only have soft peaches, reduce any added liquid and bake a bit less to prevent a soggy filling.

Mixing butter into the topping until fully combined

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix until smooth, but overworking the butter releases too much fat and prevents crumbly texture.

Fix Use a light touch and work the butter just until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces. This ensures a flaky, crisp topping.

Skipping the maceration step for the peaches

Why it happens In a hurry, some skip letting the peaches sit with sugar and lemon, but this step enhances flavor and juice release.

Fix Always let the peaches rest for at least 10 minutes after mixing with sugar and lemon juice before baking to develop a juicy filling.

Pressing down the crumble topping before baking

Why it happens Pressing the topping seems like it would hold it together better, but it results in a dense, cakey texture.

Fix Sprinkle the topping loosely over the peaches without pressing. This helps it bake into crisp, crunchy clusters.

Not baking long enough to brown the topping

Why it happens Removing the crumble too soon leads to a pale, soft topping instead of a golden, crisp one.

Fix Bake until the topping is golden brown and the peach juices are bubbling at the edges, usually around 30 minutes at 350°F.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep the peach crumble covered loosely at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Beyond that, the risk of spoilage increases, especially because of the fruit juices. Cover with foil or plastic wrap to prevent drying out during this short period.

Refrigerator

Store leftover crumble in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The topping may soften when chilled, but reheating in the oven can restore some crispness. Cold crumble is still safe to eat but less texturally appealing.

Freezer

Freeze the peach crumble in a freezer-safe container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly with plastic wrap and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating. The texture may be slightly softer after freezing but still delicious.

Reheating

Reheat crumble in a 325°F oven for 15-20 minutes, covered with foil to prevent over-browning. Add a splash of water or peach juice if the filling looks dry. Avoid microwaving since it tends to make the topping soggy.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the peach filling and crumble topping separately up to 1 day ahead. Store the filling in the fridge and keep the topping chilled. Assemble and bake just before serving for the freshest texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches instead of fresh?
Yes, but frozen peaches release more water, which can make the filling runnier. Thaw and drain them well before using, and consider adding a thickener like cornstarch to the filling to help it set properly.
What can I substitute for rolled oats in the topping?
You can use quick oats or chopped nuts, but quick oats may make the topping less crunchy. Nuts add great texture and flavor but will change the crumble’s character slightly.
How do I prevent the topping from burning before the filling is cooked?
If the topping browns too fast, cover it loosely with foil halfway through baking. This shields it while allowing the filling to cook fully.
Is it okay to skip the lemon juice?
Lemon juice helps balance sweetness and prevents browning. Skipping it might result in duller color and flavor, but the crumble will still taste good.
Can I make this crumble gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use gluten-free oats and a gluten-free flour blend instead of all-purpose flour. Make sure your oats are certified gluten-free to avoid cross-contamination.
Why is my crumble topping soggy?
Common reasons include using melted butter, pressing down the topping before baking, or underbaking. Always use cold butter, sprinkle topping loosely, and bake until golden brown.
How long does this crumble keep in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, it stays good for up to 3 days. The topping may soften, so reheat in the oven to restore some crunch.
Can I add other fruits to the peach crumble?
Yes! Berries, cherries, or apples work well. Adjust sugar levels slightly depending on the sweetness of added fruits to balance flavors.
