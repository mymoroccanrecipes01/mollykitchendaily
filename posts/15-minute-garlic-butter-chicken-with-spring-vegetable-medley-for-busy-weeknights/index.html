Quick Garlic Butter Chicken with Fresh Spring Veggies in 15 Minutes

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
10 min prep 15 min cook 4 servings

This garlic butter chicken is my go-to when I need a fast, flavorful dinner. The fresh spring veggies add a lovely crunch and color, making it both vibrant and satisfying. Perfect for busy weeknights!

Why This Works

This recipe works because it balances fast cooking techniques with flavor-building steps that maximize taste without long cook times. Searing the chicken in a hot pan creates a Maillard reaction, which is that delicious browning that adds complex savory notes and texture. It’s essential not to overcrowd the pan here, or the chicken will steam instead and lose that golden crust.

Using butter and garlic as the flavor base is a classic combination, but the timing matters — cooking garlic briefly in melted butter releases its sweet aroma without burning, which would create bitterness. The butter also emulsifies with the chicken broth and lemon juice added later, creating a silky sauce that clings to the chicken and veggies.

The quick sauté of fresh spring vegetables preserves their bright color, crispness, and natural sweetness, which contrasts beautifully with the richness of the butter and chicken. Adding lemon juice at the end introduces acidity that brightens and balances the dish, preventing it from feeling too heavy. This interplay of textures, flavors, and cooking methods makes the dish both satisfying and fresh.

Finally, seasoning in layers — from the chicken to the sauce and veggies — builds depth, ensuring the final dish is well-rounded and flavorful. The parsley garnish adds a fresh herbal note and visual appeal, rounding out this quick yet sophisticated meal.

  • Choose fresh, boneless skinless chicken breasts that are firm to the touch and pale pink with no odor. Thinly slicing the chicken ensures quick, even cooking and tender results. Avoid using frozen chicken unless fully thawed, as excess moisture can prevent proper searing.
  • Unsalted butter allows you to control the salt level precisely. It melts smoothly and carries the garlic flavor beautifully. Using good-quality butter with a high fat content enhances richness and mouthfeel. Avoid margarine or spreads as they lack the same flavor and texture.
  • Fresh garlic cloves provide a bright, pungent aroma that’s essential here. Mince finely to release the oils quickly during sautéing. Avoid pre-minced garlic in jars as it tends to be less vibrant and can impart a slightly bitter flavor when cooked.
  • Fresh asparagus, snap peas, and cherry tomatoes add crunch, color, and natural sweetness. Choose veggies that are crisp and bright, avoiding limp or wilted pieces. Their quick sauté preserves texture and freshness, balancing the richness of the chicken.
  • A quality low-sodium chicken broth adds moisture and a subtle savory depth to the sauce without overpowering the other flavors. Using broth instead of water enriches the dish and helps deglaze the pan, lifting flavorful browned bits.
  • Freshly squeezed lemon juice brightens the dish with acidity, balancing the butter’s richness and enhancing the veggies’ natural flavors. Avoid bottled lemon juice as it can taste flat or overly acidic.
  • Extra virgin olive oil is used to sear the chicken, providing a mild fruity flavor and a higher smoke point than butter alone. It helps create a crisp crust on the chicken without burning the butter.
  • Chopped fresh parsley adds a clean, herbaceous finish and vibrant green color. It complements the garlic butter flavors without overpowering them. Dried herbs won’t provide the same freshness or visual appeal.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb boneless skinless chicken breasts, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup asparagus, trimmed and cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 cup snap peas, trimmed
  • 1/2 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1/4 cup chicken broth
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • Fresh parsley, chopped for garnish

I’ve tweaked it over time by slicing the chicken thinly so it cooks evenly and quickly, and by adding a splash of chicken broth and lemon juice at the end to keep everything juicy and vibrant. How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by slicing your chicken breasts thinly and evenly. This ensures the chicken cooks quickly and stays tender rather than drying out. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering but not smoking. Adding the chicken in a single layer helps it sear nicely, creating a flavorful crust. Cook without moving it for about 3-4 minutes until the edges turn golden brown. Avoid overcrowding the pan, or the chicken will steam instead of sear. This step locks in juices and builds the base flavor for the dish.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Chicken edges are golden brown and the pieces easily release from the pan
  2. Step 2

    Flip the chicken pieces and cook the other side for another 2-3 minutes until cooked through but still juicy inside. Remove the chicken from the pan and set aside. In the same pan, reduce heat to medium and add butter. Once melted, stir in minced garlic and sauté for about 30 seconds to 1 minute until fragrant but not browned — you want that sweet garlic aroma without bitterness. This step infuses the butter with garlic flavor, which will coat the chicken and veggies beautifully.

    ⏱ 4 minutes✓ Garlic is aromatic and the butter looks slightly foamy but not browned
  3. Step 3

    Add the asparagus and snap peas to the skillet, tossing them in the garlic butter. Cook for about 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally until the veggies are crisp-tender — you want them bright green and still with a bit of snap, not mushy. This quick sauté preserves their fresh flavor and crunch, which contrasts nicely with the tender chicken.

    ⏱ 4 minutes✓ Veggies are vibrant green and slightly softened but still firm
  4. Step 4

    Return the chicken to the pan along with the halved cherry tomatoes. Pour in the chicken broth and lemon juice, then gently toss everything together to combine. The broth adds moisture and helps create a light sauce, while the lemon juice brightens the whole dish. Simmer for 2-3 minutes to warm through and allow the flavors to meld. Don’t overcook here or the veggies will lose their texture.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Sauce is slightly reduced and glossy, chicken is heated through
  5. Step 5

    Season the dish generously with salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste. Remember to season in layers throughout cooking to build depth of flavor. Taste before serving and adjust seasoning as needed. Finally, sprinkle chopped fresh parsley over the top for a fresh herbal note and a pop of color. Serve immediately while warm for the best texture and flavor.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Dish is well-seasoned and garnished with bright green parsley

Variations

  • Spicy Garlic Butter Chicken: Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or a splash of hot sauce when sautéing garlic to give the dish a spicy kick. The heat pairs wonderfully with the buttery, garlicky base and fresh veggies for a lively flavor twist.
    Be cautious with spice levels; start small and adjust to taste to avoid overpowering the delicate garlic and butter flavors.
  • Creamy Garlic Butter Chicken: Stir in 1/4 cup heavy cream or crème fraîche after adding the chicken broth and lemon juice to create a richer, creamier sauce. This makes the dish more indulgent and comforting, perfect for cooler nights.
    Simmer gently after adding cream to avoid curdling and keep the sauce smooth.
  • Herb-Infused Garlic Butter Chicken: Add fresh thyme or rosemary sprigs when melting the butter and garlic for an herbal aroma. Remove the sprigs before serving. This variation adds depth and a fragrant, garden-fresh note to the dish.
    Use fresh herbs and add them early enough to infuse the butter without overpowering the garlic.
  • Garlic Butter Chicken with Mushrooms: Sauté sliced mushrooms along with the spring veggies to add an earthy, umami dimension. Mushrooms soak up the garlic butter sauce beautifully and make the meal more robust.
    Cook mushrooms first until they release moisture and brown slightly before adding other veggies to avoid sogginess.
  • Lemon Herb Garlic Butter Chicken: Add extra lemon zest and a mix of fresh herbs like parsley, basil, and chives for a bright, fresh flavor profile. This variation feels lighter and more summery, great for outdoor meals.
    Add lemon zest at the end to preserve its bright citrus oils and avoid bitterness.

Pro Tips

  • Slice chicken thinly and evenly for quicker, uniform cooking. — Thin slices cook faster and more evenly, preventing the outside from drying out before the inside is done. Uneven pieces can result in some parts overcooked and others undercooked, affecting texture and flavor.
  • Don’t overcrowd the pan when searing chicken. — Crowding traps steam and prevents browning. Proper searing creates the Maillard reaction, which adds rich flavor and appealing color. Cook in batches if necessary to maintain heat and crispness.
  • Sauté garlic just until fragrant, not browned. — Garlic burns quickly and turns bitter. Cooking it briefly in melted butter releases its sweet aroma and flavor without bitterness, which is crucial for a balanced sauce.
  • Add lemon juice at the end of cooking. — Lemon juice loses brightness if cooked too long. Adding it last preserves its fresh acidity, which balances the richness and lifts the dish’s flavors.
  • Use fresh, crisp spring vegetables and cook them briefly. — Quick cooking preserves their color, crunch, and natural sweetness, providing a contrasting texture to the tender chicken and buttery sauce.
  • Season in layers throughout the cooking process. — Layered seasoning builds depth and complexity, ensuring every component is flavorful rather than just the finished dish.
  • Finish with fresh parsley for color and herbal brightness. — Fresh herbs add a pop of green and a fresh flavor that complements the richness, making the dish look and taste more vibrant.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the chicken until dry.
    Why: Many cooks leave the chicken in the pan too long trying to ensure it’s fully cooked, but this dries it out and makes it tough.
    Fix: Slice chicken thinly for fast cooking and watch carefully. Remove the chicken as soon as it’s opaque and firm but still juicy, then finish cooking with the sauce to keep it moist.
  • Burning the garlic.
    Why: Garlic cooks quickly and if left too long in hot butter, it can brown and taste bitter.
    Fix: Add garlic after butter melts and sauté only until fragrant, about 30 seconds to 1 minute, stirring constantly to prevent burning.
  • Overcrowding the pan when searing chicken.
    Why: Trying to cook all chicken at once lowers pan temperature, causing steaming instead of searing.
    Fix: Cook chicken in batches if needed, leaving space between pieces for proper browning and flavor development.
  • Cooking spring vegetables too long until mushy.
    Why: Overcooking destroys the veggies’ texture and bright color.
    Fix: Sauté veggies only until crisp-tender, about 3-4 minutes, to keep them fresh and vibrant.
  • Skipping the chicken broth or lemon juice.
    Why: Some may omit these to simplify but it results in a drier, less flavorful dish.
    Fix: Include broth and lemon juice to create a light sauce that keeps the dish juicy and brightens flavors.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: This dish can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered. Beyond that, bacteria growth can occur, so refrigerate leftovers promptly.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The veggies may soften slightly, but reheating gently on the stove or microwave with a splash of broth or water helps restore moisture.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cooked chicken and veggies in a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop over low heat or in short bursts in the microwave, adding a splash of chicken broth or water to keep the sauce from drying out. Avoid high heat which can toughen the chicken or overcook the veggies.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can slice the chicken and prep the veggies 1-2 days ahead and keep them refrigerated separately. Melt butter and sauté garlic fresh before cooking chicken and veggies on the day of serving for the best flavor and texture.

FAQ

Can I use chicken thighs instead of breasts?
Yes, boneless skinless chicken thighs work great and stay juicy due to their higher fat content. Slice them thinly as with breasts and adjust cooking time slightly if needed. Thighs may take a minute or two longer to cook through but add extra flavor.
What if I don’t have chicken broth?
You can substitute water, but the dish will be less flavorful. For a quick fix, add a small pinch of chicken bouillon powder or use vegetable broth. The broth helps create a light sauce and prevents dryness.
Can I make this recipe vegetarian?
To make it vegetarian, swap chicken for firm tofu or large mushrooms and use vegetable broth instead of chicken broth. Sear tofu or mushrooms similarly and follow the same steps for veggies and sauce.
How do I keep the veggies crisp?
Cook the veggies quickly over medium heat and avoid overcooking. They should be bright, tender-crisp, and still have some bite. Stir frequently and remove from heat as soon as they’re done.
Can I prepare this dish ahead of time?
You can prep ingredients ahead but it’s best to cook and combine everything fresh for optimal texture and flavor. Pre-cooked chicken and veggies tend to get soggy when reheated.
What if I don’t have fresh parsley?
You can substitute dried parsley but use it sparingly and add it earlier in cooking. Fresh parsley adds brightness and color that dried herbs can’t replicate as well.
Is this recipe gluten-free?
Yes, all ingredients are naturally gluten-free. Just ensure your chicken broth and any added seasonings are labeled gluten-free to avoid cross-contamination.
How can I make this recipe spicier?
Add red pepper flakes or a dash of cayenne pepper when sautéing garlic. Start with a small amount and adjust to your heat preference so it complements rather than overwhelms the garlic butter flavor.
