Warm Spiced Apple Crisp with Cinnamon and Clove for Cozy Nights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 19, 2026
15 min Prep Time
45 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This apple crisp is pure comfort—juicy apples baked with cinnamon and clove for a cozy, warming dessert. The secret? Toasting the spices before mixing. Perfect for chilly evenings or holiday get-togethers!

Introduction

I first made this spiced apple crisp on a chilly fall evening when I wanted something warm and comforting but easy to whip up. What makes this recipe stand out is the little trick I learned: toasting the cinnamon and clove briefly before mixing them into the apples. It really wakes up the spices and gives the whole dish a deeper, richer aroma. Plus, I like using tart apples because they hold their shape well and balance the sweetness perfectly. The topping is a simple mix of oats, brown sugar, and butter that crisps up beautifully in the oven. Whenever I serve this, my family always asks for seconds — it’s that cozy and satisfying. You’ll get a golden, bubbly apple filling with a crunchy, spiced crust that’s just begging for a scoop of vanilla ice cream. It’s the kind of dessert that feels like a hug in a bowl, perfect for those quiet nights or weekend gatherings. I’ve tried a few versions before, but this one nails the balance between warm spice and fresh apple every time.

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 servings

  • 6 cups peeled and sliced tart apples (about 6 medium)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 cup old-fashioned rolled oats
  • 1/3 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/3 cup cold unsalted butter, cut into small pieces
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). While it warms up, toss the sliced apples with lemon juice in a large bowl. The lemon juice prevents browning and adds a subtle tang that brightens the flavors. Make sure the apples are evenly coated so they stay fresh and flavorful throughout baking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Apples are evenly coated and look fresh without browning
Step 2
In a dry skillet over medium heat, toast the cinnamon and ground cloves for about 1 minute until fragrant. Be careful not to burn them as that will make the spices bitter. Toasting spices releases their essential oils, which deepens the aroma and flavor in your dish.
⏱ Time: 1-2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Spices smell warm and fragrant without turning dark brown
Step 3
Add the toasted spices and brown sugar to the apples, tossing gently to coat. The sugar will help draw out the juices from the apples, creating a natural syrup as it bakes. Stir just until combined to avoid bruising the fruit.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Apples glisten lightly with sugar and spice mixture
Step 4
In a separate bowl, combine the oats, flour, salt, and cold butter pieces. Using your fingers or a pastry cutter, work the butter into the dry ingredients until the mixture looks crumbly and the butter is pea-sized. This step is crucial for a crisp, flaky topping rather than a dense one.
⏱ Time: 7 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping resembles coarse crumbs with visible butter bits
Step 5
Transfer the spiced apple mixture to a greased 9x9-inch baking dish, spreading it out evenly. Then sprinkle the oat topping over the apples, covering them completely. This layering ensures the fruit bubbles underneath while the topping crisps up nicely.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Even layer of topping covers all apples without gaps
Step 6
Bake uncovered in the preheated oven for 40-45 minutes. You want the topping to turn golden brown and the apple juices to bubble around the edges. Avoid opening the oven door too often to keep the temperature steady for even cooking.
⏱ Time: 40-45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Top is golden and crunchy; filling is bubbly and tender
Step 7
Remove the crisp from the oven and let it cool for at least 10 minutes before serving. This resting time helps the juices thicken slightly so the filling isn’t too runny. It also makes it easier to slice and serve.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling thickens and topping remains crisp after cooling
Step 8
Serve warm, ideally with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream. The cold cream contrasts perfectly with the warm, spiced apples and crunchy topping, elevating the whole experience.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ice cream melting slightly atop warm crisp
Step 9
Store any leftovers covered in the refrigerator. Reheat gently in the oven or microwave before serving again to restore that fresh-baked warmth and crisp texture.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Reheated crisp smells fresh and topping remains crisp

Variations

Add Chopped Pecans for Crunch

Mix 1/2 cup chopped pecans into the oat topping for a nutty crunch that complements the warm spices. The nuts add texture and a toasty flavor that pairs beautifully with the apples.

Note: Add nuts just before baking to prevent them from burning. Monitor baking time slightly as nuts can brown faster.

Use Pears Instead of Apples

Swap out apples for firm pears like Bosc for a softer, sweeter fruit base. Pears add a floral sweetness that changes the flavor profile but still works wonderfully with cinnamon and cloves.

Note: Pears soften faster than apples, so reduce baking time by about 5-10 minutes and check for doneness early.

Make it Vegan with Coconut Oil

Replace butter with solid coconut oil in the topping for a dairy-free version. Coconut oil adds a subtle tropical note and keeps the topping crisp and flaky.

Note: Use chilled coconut oil to achieve the crumbly texture. The baking time remains the same.

Add a Streusel Layer with Brown Sugar and Nuts

Combine brown sugar, chopped nuts, and a bit of flour with butter to create a streusel topping instead of oats. This gives a richer, denser texture and a caramelized crunch.

Note: Press the streusel lightly over the fruit to ensure it bakes evenly and crisps up well.

Spice It Up with Ginger and Nutmeg

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger and 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg to the spice mix for a warmer, more complex flavor. These spices add brightness and depth to the classic cinnamon-clove combo.

Note: Adjust spice amounts to taste; too much ginger can overpower, so start with small quantities.

Pro Tips

Use cold butter for the topping.
Cold butter creates small pockets of fat in the topping that melt during baking, producing a flaky, crispy texture. If the butter is too warm or melted, the topping will turn dense and greasy instead of crumbly.
Toast your spices before adding them.
Toasting cinnamon and cloves releases their essential oils, making the flavors more vibrant and aromatic. Skipping this step can result in a flatter spice profile that doesn’t blend as well with the apples.
Don’t overmix the topping.
Overworking the oat and flour mixture with butter develops gluten and melts the butter prematurely, resulting in a tough or oily topping. Mixing just until crumbly ensures tender, crisp results.
Let the crisp rest before serving.
Resting allows the fruit juices to thicken slightly, preventing a runny filling and making it easier to serve. Cutting into the crisp immediately after baking can cause the filling to spill out and the topping to lose crunch.
Use tart apples for the best texture.
Tart apples like Granny Smith hold their shape during baking and provide a nice counterpoint to the sweet topping. Sweeter or softer apples might turn mushy and make the dessert overly sweet.
Bake uncovered for a crisp topping.
Baking uncovered allows moisture to evaporate, helping the topping brown and crisp. Covering the dish traps steam, which can make the topping soggy and the apples less concentrated in flavor.
Add a splash of lemon juice to the apples.
Lemon juice prevents the apples from browning during prep and adds a subtle brightness that balances the sweetness and spices, enhancing the overall flavor complexity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using overripe or soft apples

Why it happens People often use whatever apples they have on hand without considering texture, assuming all apples behave the same when baked.

Fix Choose firm, tart apples like Granny Smith or Braeburn. These hold their shape better and provide a balanced tartness that complements the sweet topping, avoiding mushy or overly sweet results.

Skipping spice toasting

Why it happens It’s easy to overlook this small step, thinking spices don’t need any prep before mixing.

Fix Toast cinnamon and cloves briefly in a dry pan to unlock their full aroma and flavor. This simple step makes a big difference in how fragrant and rich your crisp tastes.

Overmixing the topping dough

Why it happens Trying to fully combine ingredients can lead to overworking the butter and flour.

Fix Mix just until crumbly with visible butter pieces. This ensures a tender, crunchy topping. Use fingers or a pastry cutter to avoid warming the butter too much.

Baking the crisp covered

Why it happens Some think covering will keep moisture in and prevent drying out.

Fix Bake uncovered to let moisture evaporate, which crisps the topping and concentrates the fruit flavors. Covering traps steam and leads to sogginess.

Serving immediately after baking

Why it happens Impatience to enjoy the fresh dessert can cause cutting into it too soon.

Fix Let the crisp rest 10-15 minutes to thicken the filling and firm up the topping, making it easier to serve and enjoy.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep the apple crisp covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours safely. Beyond that, it’s best to refrigerate to prevent spoilage. Cover it tightly with foil or plastic wrap to keep moisture in and prevent the topping from drying out.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The topping may soften slightly but reheating restores some crunch. You can also enjoy it cold, which some prefer as the flavors meld more.

Freezer

Freeze the apple crisp for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly in plastic wrap and then aluminum foil or use a freezer-safe container. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating. The texture may be slightly softer after freezing but still delicious.

Reheating

Reheat in a 350°F oven for 15-20 minutes until warm and bubbly. Avoid microwaving if possible as it can make the topping soggy. If microwaving, add a splash of water and heat in short intervals to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

Prepare the apple mixture and topping separately up to 1 day ahead. Store the apple filling in the fridge and keep the topping chilled. Assemble just before baking to keep the topping crisp and fresh.

FAQ

Can I use frozen apples for this recipe?
Frozen apples can work in a pinch, but they tend to release more water and become mushy. If you use frozen, thaw and drain excess liquid before baking to avoid a soggy crisp. Fresh tart apples are best for texture and flavor.
What if I don’t have rolled oats?
You can substitute quick oats, but the topping will be less chewy and more crumbly. Avoid instant oats as they absorb too much moisture and can become mushy. Alternatively, add chopped nuts for texture.
How do I make this gluten-free?
Use gluten-free oats and swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free flour blend. Make sure your spices and other ingredients are certified gluten-free to avoid cross-contamination.
Why is my topping not crispy?
Common reasons include using melted butter instead of cold, baking covered, or not baking long enough. Use cold butter and bake uncovered until golden brown. Let the crisp rest to firm up the topping.
Can I prepare this in advance?
Yes! You can prepare the fruit mixture and the topping separately a day ahead. Keep both refrigerated and assemble just before baking to keep the topping crisp and fresh.
What’s the best apple variety for this?
Granny Smith is my top choice due to its tartness and firmness. Braeburn and Honeycrisp also work well. Avoid soft or overly sweet apples that turn mushy.
Can I add other fruits?
Absolutely! Pears, cranberries, or even berries can be mixed in for variation. Adjust sugar amounts accordingly since some fruits are sweeter or more tart.
How do I avoid the filling being too runny?
Use tart apples with firm texture and toss them with lemon juice and sugar to draw out moisture. Bake uncovered and allow the dish to rest before serving so juices thicken.
