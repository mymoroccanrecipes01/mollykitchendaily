Classic Rhubarb Bars with a Buttery Shortbread Crust and a Hint of Cinnamon

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 17, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Medium Difficulty

These rhubarb bars bring back cozy memories with their crisp buttery crust and tangy rhubarb filling. A sprinkle of cinnamon adds warmth, making them perfect for afternoon tea or a comforting dessert any day.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this rhubarb bar recipe during a rainy spring afternoon when rhubarb was in season and I was craving something both tart and sweet. These bars immediately reminded me of the kind of treats my grandma used to bake — simple, comforting, and made with love. What makes this version stand out is the buttery shortbread crust that’s crisp but tender, paired with a rhubarb filling that’s perfectly balanced by a touch of cinnamon. The cinnamon isn’t overpowering; it just adds a little warmth and depth that transforms the bars from ordinary to something special. One trick I learned after a few attempts is to keep the butter cold and work quickly when making the crust to get that lovely crumbly texture. Also, mixing the rhubarb with cornstarch ensures the filling sets just right without being soggy. When baked, these bars have a beautiful golden crust with a slightly tangy, fragrant filling that’s just right for afternoon tea or a cozy dessert. Honestly, they’ve become my go-to when I want something nostalgic yet fresh. I think you’ll love how easy they are to whip up, and how they make your kitchen smell like springtime.

What You'll Need

Approximately 8 bars, serves 8

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup unsalted butter, cold and cubed
  • 3 cups fresh rhubarb, chopped into 1/2-inch pieces
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 tablespoons cornstarch
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 large egg, beaten (for egg wash)
  • Optional: powdered sugar for dusting

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by making the shortbread crust. In a large bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, sugar, and salt. Add the cold, cubed butter and use a pastry cutter or your fingers to quickly cut the butter into the dry ingredients until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized bits. This technique helps create a tender, flaky crust because the butter pieces melt during baking, creating steam pockets. Be careful not to overwork the dough, or the crust will become tough.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: The dough should hold together when pressed but still feel crumbly and cool to the touch.
Step 2
Press about two-thirds of the dough evenly into the bottom of a greased 9x9-inch baking pan, forming the base crust. Make sure to press firmly but not too thickly, aiming for an even layer. The remaining dough will be used for the topping later. An even crust ensures it bakes through properly without sogginess.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The crust should look evenly compacted and smooth before baking.
Step 3
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Bake the crust alone for about 12 minutes or until it’s just starting to turn golden around the edges. This pre-baking step, called blind baking, prevents the crust from becoming soggy once the rhubarb filling is added. It also helps the crust hold its shape and crispiness.
⏱ Time: 12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges should be lightly golden and the surface firm to the touch.
Step 4
While the crust is baking, prepare the rhubarb filling. In a medium bowl, toss the chopped rhubarb with sugar, cornstarch, cinnamon, and vanilla extract. The cornstarch thickens the filling as it bakes, preventing watery bars. The cinnamon adds warmth and a subtle spice that complements the tartness of the rhubarb.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The rhubarb should be evenly coated and the mixture slightly glossy.
Step 5
Once the crust is partially baked, remove it from the oven and spread the rhubarb mixture evenly over the top. Distribute the rhubarb filling carefully to avoid breaking the crust. This layering ensures a nice balance of crust to filling in every bite.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling should cover the crust completely without spilling over the edges.
Step 6
Crumble the remaining dough over the rhubarb filling to create a streusel-like topping. It’s okay if the topping pieces are uneven; they’ll bake into a lovely, buttery crust. For extra shine, brush the top with beaten egg. This adds a golden color and a slight gloss to the finished bars.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping should cover the filling in small chunks with some gaps.
Step 7
Return the pan to the oven and bake for another 25-30 minutes until the topping is golden brown and the filling bubbles gently around the edges. Baking fully at this stage ensures the rhubarb softens and the flavors meld beautifully while the crust crisps up.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Look for bubbling filling and a golden, crisp topping.
Step 8
Remove the bars from the oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling allows the filling to set, so when you cut the bars, they hold their shape and don’t spill out. Patience here makes a big difference in the final texture.
⏱ Time: 1 hour
✓ Doneness: Bars should be cool to the touch and filling firm but tender.
Step 9
Once cooled, slice into squares with a sharp knife. For a pretty finish, dust lightly with powdered sugar before serving. These bars are perfect with a cup of tea or coffee and can be enjoyed at room temperature or slightly chilled.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Clean cuts with minimal filling sticking to the knife.

Variations

Strawberry Rhubarb Bars

Add 1 cup of chopped fresh strawberries to the rhubarb filling for a sweeter, more colorful twist. Strawberries complement rhubarb’s tartness beautifully and add a juicy texture. You might want to increase the sugar slightly to balance the tartness of both fruits.

Note: Because strawberries release more juice, increase the cornstarch by 1 tablespoon to prevent a runny filling.

Oat Crumble Topping

Swap the crumbly shortbread topping for an oat-based crumble by mixing rolled oats, brown sugar, butter, and a pinch of cinnamon. This adds a hearty texture and nutty flavor that pairs well with the rhubarb filling.

Note: Bake the oat topping until golden and crisp, watching closely to avoid burning due to the sugar content.

Gluten-Free Rhubarb Bars

Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend in place of regular flour to make these bars gluten-free without sacrificing texture. Choose a blend that includes xanthan gum for structure and use the same quantities as the original recipe.

Note: Gluten-free dough may be more delicate; chill it well before pressing into the pan to help it hold together.

Vegan Rhubarb Bars

Replace butter with vegan butter or coconut oil and use a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water) instead of the egg wash. This keeps the bars moist and flavorful while making them vegan-friendly.

Note: Coconut oil may slightly change the flavor and texture; chill the dough thoroughly to maintain crumbly consistency.

Spiced Rhubarb Bars with Ginger

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger along with the cinnamon for a spicier, warmer flavor profile. This variation gives the bars a subtle zing and is perfect for cooler seasons or when you want a little extra kick.

Note: Adjust sugar if needed to balance the added spice and prevent bitterness.

Pro Tips

Keep your butter cold until the last moment.
Cold butter creates small pockets of steam during baking, which makes the crust flaky and tender. If the butter melts too early, the dough becomes greasy and dense instead of crumbly and light.
Don’t skip the blind baking step for the crust.
Pre-baking the crust helps it set and prevents sogginess from the juicy rhubarb filling. It also starts browning, adding a richer buttery flavor.
Use cornstarch to thicken the filling.
Cornstarch absorbs excess moisture released by the rhubarb while baking, ensuring a firm filling that slices cleanly without being runny or watery.
Let the bars cool completely before slicing.
Cooling allows the filling to set and the crust to firm up, which means cleaner cuts and better texture. Cutting too soon can lead to a messy, falling-apart dessert.
Use a sharp knife dipped in hot water for slicing.
Wiping the knife blade clean between cuts prevents filling from sticking and tearing the bars, giving you neat, attractive squares.
Add a light dusting of powdered sugar before serving.
Powdered sugar adds a touch of sweetness and a pretty finish that contrasts nicely with the tart rhubarb and buttery crust.
Chill the dough if it feels too soft before pressing into the pan.
Chilling firms up the butter, making the dough easier to handle and preventing it from sticking to your hands or pan, which helps maintain the crumbly texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using warm butter in the crust

Why it happens Many home cooks don’t realize the importance of cold butter and use it straight from the fridge or slightly softened, thinking it’s easier to mix.

Fix Always cube and keep butter cold until just before mixing. Work quickly to cut it into the flour, stopping as soon as you see pea-sized chunks. This prevents the dough from becoming greasy and ensures a flaky crust.

Skipping the blind baking step

Why it happens Some think it’s unnecessary and want to save time, but this leads to a soggy crust because the filling releases moisture during baking.

Fix Bake the crust alone for about 12 minutes until lightly golden before adding the filling. This sets the base and prevents sogginess.

Not using enough thickener in the filling

Why it happens People often underestimate how much juice rhubarb releases and skip or reduce the cornstarch.

Fix Use the full amount of cornstarch and mix it well with the sugar and rhubarb. This ensures the filling thickens properly and holds its shape.

Cutting bars too soon after baking

Why it happens Impatience or wanting to serve warm can cause messy bars that fall apart.

Fix Allow bars to cool completely, ideally an hour or more, so the filling sets and the crust firms up for clean slices.

Unevenly chopped rhubarb

Why it happens In a rush, chopping sizes can vary widely, causing uneven cooking and texture.

Fix Take a few extra minutes to chop rhubarb into uniform 1/2-inch pieces. This ensures even cooking and a consistent texture throughout the bars.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These rhubarb bars can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 days if stored in an airtight container. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat sources to avoid the filling becoming too soft or the crust losing its crispness.

Refrigerator

Store bars in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The filling will firm up more when chilled, and the crust may soften slightly but remains tasty. Serve cold or let sit at room temperature for 15 minutes before eating.

Freezer

Freeze rhubarb bars wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then in a freezer-safe container for up to 3 months. To thaw, move bars to the refrigerator overnight. Reheat gently in a warm oven to restore some crispness in the crust.

Reheating

Reheat bars in a 350°F oven for 8-10 minutes to warm through and crisp the crust. Avoid microwaving as it can make the crust soggy. If needed, cover edges with foil to prevent over-browning.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the crust and rhubarb filling separately up to 1 day in advance. Keep the crust dough chilled and rhubarb filling refrigerated. Assemble and bake on the day you want to serve for the freshest texture.

FAQ

Can I use frozen rhubarb instead of fresh?
Yes, frozen rhubarb works well but tends to release more water. To compensate, slightly increase the cornstarch by a tablespoon or two to ensure the filling thickens properly. Thaw and drain excess liquid before mixing for best results.
What can I substitute for cornstarch if I don’t have any?
You can use arrowroot powder or potato starch as a 1:1 substitute for cornstarch. Flour can also be used but may make the filling less glossy and slightly denser. Avoid skipping the thickener as the filling will be too runny.
Why is my crust tough instead of crumbly?
This usually happens if the butter was too warm or the dough was overworked, developing too much gluten. To fix this, keep ingredients cold, handle the dough gently, and avoid overmixing.
Can I make this recipe vegan?
Absolutely! Replace butter with vegan butter or coconut oil and use a flax egg for the egg wash or skip the egg wash altogether. This will slightly change the texture but still yields delicious bars.
How do I store leftover bars?
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze them wrapped well. Let refrigerated bars come to room temperature or warm slightly before serving for best texture.
Can I add other fruits to the filling?
Yes! Strawberries, blueberries, or even chopped apples pair well with rhubarb. Adjust sugar and thickener as needed depending on the fruit’s juiciness to maintain the right consistency.
What’s the best way to cut these bars cleanly?
Use a sharp knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry between cuts. This prevents the filling and crust from sticking and tearing, giving you neat slices.
Why does the filling sometimes leak out during baking?
If the filling is too watery or the crust isn’t sturdy enough, juices can bubble over. Make sure to use enough thickener and blind bake the crust to set it firmly before adding filling.
