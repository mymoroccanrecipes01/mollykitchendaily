Moist and Easy Air Fryer Blueberry Cake for Cozy Afternoons

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 30, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This blueberry cake is a quick, moist treat made right in the air fryer—no oven needed! The fresh blueberries burst with flavor, making it perfect for a cozy afternoon or weekend dessert.

Introduction

I first tried making this blueberry cake in my air fryer on a lazy weekend when I didn’t feel like heating up the whole kitchen with the oven. Honestly, I was surprised at how moist and flavorful it turned out! Using fresh blueberries that burst with juices gives the cake a lovely natural sweetness and a pop of tartness that balances the batter perfectly. What sets this recipe apart is the simple yogurt addition, which keeps the crumb tender without making it dense. I also learned that folding the berries gently into the batter helps prevent them from sinking to the bottom, so you get a nice even distribution in each bite. The air fryer is a total game-changer here — it bakes the cake evenly and quickly, with a golden crust and soft interior. It’s perfect for an afternoon treat or a weekend dessert when you want something homemade but fuss-free. Plus, you don’t need any fancy equipment or ingredients. I think you’ll love how simple and satisfying this cake is!

What You'll Need

One 6-inch cake, approximately 6 servings

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup plain yogurt, room temperature
  • 1/4 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup fresh blueberries, washed and dried
  • Optional: powdered sugar for dusting

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by mixing all your dry ingredients — sift the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt into a large bowl. Sifting helps incorporate air and removes lumps, which gives your cake a lighter texture. Make sure everything is well combined so the rising agents are evenly distributed. Avoid skipping this step, or you might end up with uneven pockets of baking soda that can affect taste and rise.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients look evenly mixed with no clumps
Step 2
In a separate bowl, whisk together the yogurt, vegetable oil, egg, and vanilla extract until smooth. This wet mixture adds moisture and richness to the cake. The yogurt’s acidity also reacts with the baking soda to help the cake rise. Don’t rush this step—whisking well ensures everything blends smoothly, preventing lumps and ensuring a uniform batter.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients form a smooth, homogenous mixture
Step 3
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and gently fold them together with a spatula. Be careful not to overmix; overmixing develops gluten which can make the cake tough. Fold just until you no longer see streaks of flour. The batter should be thick but smooth.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is combined but still slightly thick and lumpy
Step 4
Gently fold in the fresh blueberries, taking care not to break them up too much. This keeps the berries intact so you get juicy bursts in the cake rather than a blue-tinted batter. If you mix too vigorously, the batter will turn purple and the texture can become gummy.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Blueberries are evenly distributed without crushing
Step 5
Lightly grease or line a 6-inch cake pan that fits inside your air fryer basket. Pour the batter into the pan and spread it evenly. This ensures even cooking and prevents sticking. If your pan is too small or the batter too thick, the cake might cook unevenly or spill over.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread with a smooth surface
Step 6
Preheat your air fryer to 320°F (160°C). This lower temperature allows the cake to cook through without burning the edges. Preheating is important for consistent cooking; skipping it can lead to uneven rise and texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer reaches and maintains 320°F
Step 7
Place the cake pan inside the air fryer basket and bake for 25-30 minutes. Avoid opening the air fryer too often during baking, as it can cause the cake to collapse. Check doneness by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean
Step 8
Once baked, carefully remove the cake pan and let it cool on a wire rack for at least 15 minutes. Cooling helps the cake set and makes it easier to remove from the pan. Trying to unmold it too soon can cause it to break apart.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake feels firm to the touch and slightly shrinks from pan edges
Step 9
Run a knife around the edges and gently invert the cake onto a plate. Dust with powdered sugar if you like for a pretty finish. Serve slightly warm or at room temperature with tea or coffee for a cozy treat.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake releases cleanly from pan and looks golden on top

Variations

Lemon Blueberry Cake

Add 1 tablespoon of fresh lemon zest to the batter for a bright citrus twist that complements the blueberries perfectly. You can also drizzle a simple lemon glaze on top after baking for extra zing.

Note: Zest the lemon finely and fold it in with the wet ingredients to distribute flavor evenly. Adjust sugar slightly if glaze is added.

Gluten-Free Version

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend designed for baking. This keeps the cake tender and moist while making it suitable for gluten-sensitive eaters.

Note: Use a gluten-free flour blend with xanthan gum included for best texture; baking time may vary slightly.

Vegan Blueberry Cake

Replace the egg with a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water) and use plant-based yogurt and oil. This makes the cake vegan-friendly without sacrificing moisture.

Note: Allow flax egg to thicken before mixing; expect a slightly denser texture but still delicious.

Blueberry Almond Cake

Add 1/4 cup almond flour to the batter and sprinkle sliced almonds on top before baking for a nutty flavor and crunchy texture contrast.

Note: Reduce all-purpose flour slightly to accommodate almond flour; watch closely for browning due to nuts.

Blueberry Muffins

Divide the batter into muffin cups and bake in the air fryer at 320°F for 15-18 minutes for quick, portable treats perfect for breakfast or snacks.

Note: Use silicone or metal muffin cups; check doneness earlier as muffins bake faster than cakes.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature ingredients for better mixing.
Room temperature eggs, yogurt, and oil blend more easily, creating a smooth batter without lumps. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, affecting texture and rise.
Fold the blueberries in gently to avoid color bleed.
Vigorous mixing breaks the berries, turning the batter purple and making the texture gummy. Gentle folding keeps the berries intact, resulting in pretty pockets of fruit and a balanced flavor.
Preheat your air fryer before baking.
Preheating ensures the cake starts cooking immediately at the right temperature, promoting even rise and preventing soggy spots. Skipping this can cause uneven baking and a dense texture.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes cakes tough and chewy rather than tender. Mixing just until combined keeps the crumb light and soft.
Use a 6-inch pan that fits comfortably in your air fryer basket.
A pan that’s too big or small affects cooking time and heat circulation. The right size ensures even cooking without batter spilling or undercooking.
Check doneness with a toothpick near the end of baking time.
Air fryer models vary, so testing early prevents overbaking, which dries out the cake. A clean toothpick indicates perfect bake, while wet batter means more time is needed.
Let the cake cool completely before removing from the pan.
Cooling allows the cake to set and prevents it from breaking apart when unmolding. Removing too soon can cause crumbling and a messy presentation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Many think mixing longer ensures better blending, but overmixing develops gluten which toughens the cake.

Fix Mix wet and dry ingredients just until no flour streaks remain. Use a spatula and fold gently rather than stirring vigorously.

Using frozen blueberries without thawing

Why it happens Frozen berries release excess water, which can make the batter soggy and affect rise.

Fix Always use fresh blueberries or thaw and drain frozen berries thoroughly before adding to batter.

Skipping air fryer preheat

Why it happens Some assume preheat isn’t needed for baking, but it’s crucial for consistent temperature.

Fix Preheat the air fryer at 320°F for 5 minutes before placing the cake inside to ensure even baking.

Using the wrong pan size

Why it happens Using a pan too large or too small can cause uneven cooking or overflow.

Fix Use a 6-inch round pan that fits comfortably in your air fryer basket for best results.

Removing the cake from the pan too early

Why it happens Eager to eat, some try to unmold the cake before it sets, causing it to crumble.

Fix Allow the cake to cool for at least 15 minutes on a rack before removing to ensure it holds together.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep the cake covered at room temperature for up to 1 day. Use a cake dome or airtight container to prevent it from drying out, but it’s best eaten fresh.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The cake may firm up and lose some moisture but can be warmed gently before serving to restore softness.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe bag. Freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge, then warm slightly before serving to refresh texture.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk before warming helps keep the cake moist. Avoid overheating which dries the cake out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a few hours ahead and keep it refrigerated. Bring it back to room temperature before baking to ensure even rise. Alternatively, bake the cake a day ahead and store it covered; freshen by warming before serving.

FAQ

Can I use frozen blueberries instead of fresh?
Frozen blueberries can be used but they tend to release extra moisture which can make the batter watery and affect baking. If using frozen, thaw and drain them well, then pat dry before folding gently into the batter to minimize sogginess.
Why does my cake turn out dense sometimes?
Dense cake often results from overmixing the batter or using too much flour. Mix just until combined to avoid gluten development, and measure flour accurately by spooning it into your cup and leveling off rather than scooping.
How do I know when the cake is done in the air fryer?
The best method is to insert a toothpick into the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the cake is done. Also, the cake should spring back lightly when pressed and have a golden top.
Can I double this recipe?
You can double the ingredients but you’ll need to bake in batches unless your air fryer is large enough. Overfilling can cause uneven cooking. Make sure your pan fits comfortably and adjust baking time slightly if needed.
What if I don’t have yogurt?
You can substitute yogurt with sour cream or buttermilk in equal amounts. These provide acidity to react with baking soda and keep the cake moist. Avoid using milk alone, as it lacks acidity and can affect rise.
Can I add other fruits or nuts?
Absolutely! Chopped strawberries, raspberries, or chopped nuts like walnuts work well. Just fold them in gently and adjust baking time slightly if adding extra moisture or volume.
Why does the cake sometimes stick to the pan?
Insufficient greasing or using the wrong pan can cause sticking. Lightly grease the pan or line it with parchment paper. Also, letting the cake cool before removing helps it release cleanly.
How do I make the cake more moist?
Using yogurt and oil already helps, but you can add a tablespoon of honey or applesauce to the batter for extra moisture. Make sure not to add too much liquid, or the cake may become too dense.
