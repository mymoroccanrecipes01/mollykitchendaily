Protein-Packed Spinach and Feta Egg Muffins for Busy Mornings

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 12, 2026
15 min Prep Time
20 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These egg muffins are a quick, protein-rich breakfast featuring fresh spinach and tangy feta. They’re super easy to make and perfect for busy mornings or a relaxed brunch with friends.

Introduction

I've been making these spinach and feta egg muffins for years, especially on mornings when I’m rushing out the door. The first time I tried a similar recipe, it was a bit bland, so I started adding fresh spinach and tangy feta to punch up the flavor. What makes this version stand out is the balance between creamy eggs and salty feta, plus the freshness from the spinach. I also like to sauté the onions lightly before mixing everything, which adds a subtle sweetness and depth. The trick I discovered is to whisk the eggs just enough to combine but not overbeat them, so the muffins stay light and fluffy rather than rubbery. When baked, they come out golden and firm yet tender inside, perfect for eating warm or cold. They’re great for a quick breakfast, a brunch spread, or even a protein-packed snack during the day. I love how easy they are to customize too—sometimes I toss in some chopped tomatoes or herbs. Honestly, once you make these, they’ll become a breakfast staple you’ll reach for again and again.

What You'll Need

6 muffins, 3 servings

  • 6 large eggs, beaten
  • 1 cup fresh spinach, chopped
  • 1/2 cup feta cheese, crumbled
  • 1/4 cup milk
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil or butter

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and lightly grease a muffin tin with olive oil or butter. This step is crucial to prevent the muffins from sticking and ensures they come out cleanly. Using oil also helps the muffins develop a slight golden crust around the edges, adding texture. Make sure to coat each cup evenly, or you might find some muffins harder to remove later.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffin tin is evenly greased with a slight sheen but no pools of oil
Step 2
In a skillet over medium heat, sauté the finely chopped onion in a little olive oil until soft and translucent, about 3-4 minutes. This step mellows the onion’s sharpness and brings out its natural sweetness, which adds subtle flavor to the muffins. Avoid browning the onions as that would change the taste profile and could overpower the delicate spinach and feta.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions are soft, translucent, and fragrant without browning
Step 3
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the beaten eggs and milk until combined but not too frothy. The milk adds moisture and helps keep the muffins tender. Over-whisking can incorporate too much air, making the muffins rise unevenly and become spongy, so mix just until blended.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth and uniform in color without bubbles on top
Step 4
Add the sautéed onions, chopped spinach, crumbled feta, salt, pepper, and garlic powder to the egg mixture. Stir gently to combine all the ingredients evenly. This ensures each muffin has a balanced distribution of flavors and avoids clumps of spinach or feta in just a few muffins.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ingredients are evenly mixed with no large clumps
Step 5
Pour the egg mixture evenly into the prepared muffin tin cups, filling each about 3/4 full to allow room for the muffins to puff up as they bake. Overfilling can cause spills and uneven cooking, while underfilling results in smaller, less satisfying muffins.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each cup is filled evenly and not overflowing
Step 6
Place the muffin tin in the preheated oven and bake for 18-20 minutes. Baking at a moderate temperature ensures the eggs cook through gently, preventing rubbery edges and a dry center. Avoid opening the oven door frequently, as this can cause temperature fluctuations and uneven baking.
⏱ Time: 18-20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffins are set, slightly golden on top, and a toothpick inserted comes out clean
Step 7
Once baked, remove the muffins from the oven and let them cool in the tin for 5 minutes. This resting period helps them firm up and makes them easier to remove without breaking. Use a small knife or spatula to loosen the edges gently before lifting them out.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffins hold their shape and come out cleanly from the tin
Step 8
Serve the egg muffins warm for the best texture and flavor, or let them cool completely before storing. They’re delicious on their own or paired with a fresh salad or toast. Warm muffins have a tender, fluffy inside and a slight crispness on the edges.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Muffins feel warm and slightly springy to the touch
Step 9
To store leftovers, place cooled muffins in an airtight container and refrigerate for up to 3 days. For longer storage, freeze them individually wrapped. When reheating, warm gently in the microwave or oven with a splash of water to maintain moisture. This helps preserve their soft texture and prevents drying out.
⏱ Time: Varies
✓ Doneness: Muffins are reheated thoroughly, moist, and tender

Variations

Add Cherry Tomatoes and Basil

For a fresh twist, add halved cherry tomatoes and chopped fresh basil to the egg mixture. This brings a bright, summery flavor and a pop of color. The tomatoes add juiciness while basil adds aromatic notes that complement the feta and spinach.

Note: Because tomatoes add moisture, pat them dry before adding and monitor baking time to avoid sogginess.

Use Goat Cheese Instead of Feta

Swap feta for creamy goat cheese for a milder, slightly tangier flavor. Goat cheese melts differently, creating pockets of creaminess inside the muffins. This variation softens the salty punch and adds richness.

Note: Goat cheese is softer, so crumble gently and avoid overmixing to keep texture contrast.

Add Cooked Bacon or Sausage

For a heartier breakfast muffin, stir in cooked, crumbled bacon or sausage. This adds smoky, savory depth and extra protein, making the muffins a filling meal on their own.

Note: Make sure meat is fully cooked and drained of excess fat to prevent greasiness in the muffins.

Make Vegan Egg Muffins

Replace eggs with a chickpea flour batter mixed with plant-based milk and nutritional yeast for a vegan version. Use vegan feta or tofu for creaminess and add sautéed veggies for flavor.

Note: Baking time may be longer and texture will be denser; monitor doneness carefully.

Add Red Pepper Flakes for Heat

If you like a bit of spice, add a pinch of red pepper flakes to the egg mixture. This adds a subtle kick that pairs well with the salty feta and mild spinach.

Note: Adjust spice level to taste and add gradually to avoid overpowering the delicate flavors.

Pro Tips

Whisk eggs gently just until combined, don’t overbeat.
Overbeating eggs incorporates too much air, which can cause the muffins to rise unevenly and become rubbery or spongy. Gentle whisking helps create a smooth custard that bakes into tender, fluffy muffins.
Sauté onions before adding to the egg mixture.
Cooking onions softens their sharpness and releases natural sugars, adding sweetness and depth. Raw onions would be too pungent and can detract from the delicate balance of flavors in the muffins.
Use fresh spinach and chop it finely.
Fresh spinach adds vibrant color and mild flavor without excess moisture. Chopping it finely ensures even distribution and prevents clumps that could release water and make the muffins soggy.
Grease the muffin tin well to prevent sticking.
Because eggs can stick easily, greasing the tin thoroughly helps muffins release cleanly and maintain their shape, avoiding breakage when removing.
Fill muffin cups about 3/4 full.
This allows the muffins room to rise without spilling over, ensuring even baking and a nice rounded top.
Bake at 350°F for even cooking.
Moderate oven temperature lets the eggs set slowly, preventing tough edges and undercooked centers. High heat risks drying out the muffins.
Let muffins cool in the pan for 5 minutes before removing.
Resting helps the muffins firm up and makes them easier to remove from the tin without breaking or crumbling.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overbeating the eggs

Why it happens Many assume more whisking makes a fluffier texture, but overbeating traps excess air, leading to rubbery muffins.

Fix Whisk eggs just until yolks and whites combine smoothly, avoiding vigorous beating. This ensures tender, custardy muffins instead of tough, spongy ones.

Using frozen spinach without draining

Why it happens Frozen spinach is convenient but often has excess water that can make muffins soggy.

Fix If using frozen spinach, thaw fully and squeeze out all moisture before adding. Alternatively, use fresh spinach for better texture.

Skipping the sautéing of onions

Why it happens Some may add raw onions to save time, but raw onion flavor is harsh and overpowering.

Fix Take a few minutes to sauté onions until translucent to mellow their sharpness and add natural sweetness.

Filling muffin cups too full

Why it happens Filling cups to the brim causes spills and uneven baking.

Fix Fill each cup about 3/4 full to allow muffins to rise without overflowing for even cooking and neat presentation.

Not greasing the muffin tin properly

Why it happens Eggs stick easily, and insufficient greasing leads to muffins breaking when removed.

Fix Grease each muffin cup thoroughly with oil or butter, or use silicone liners to ensure easy release.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These egg muffins can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean cloth to prevent drying out. Beyond that, bacteria growth risks increase, so refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftover muffins in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Flavor and texture may firm up when cold; reheat gently in the microwave or oven for best results.

Freezer

Freeze muffins individually wrapped in plastic wrap and placed in a freezer bag for up to 2 months. This prevents freezer burn and maintains freshness. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat muffins in the microwave for 30-45 seconds or in a 325°F oven for 10 minutes. Adding a splash of water before microwaving helps keep them moist. Avoid overheating which can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the egg mixture and chop ingredients 1 day ahead, storing separately in the fridge. Assemble and bake muffins fresh on the day of serving for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use frozen spinach instead of fresh?
Yes, but it’s important to thaw frozen spinach completely and squeeze out all excess moisture before adding it to the egg mixture. Frozen spinach tends to be watery, which can make the muffins soggy if not drained properly.
How do I know when the egg muffins are fully cooked?
They’re done when the tops are set and lightly golden, and a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean. The muffins should feel firm but still slightly springy to the touch.
Can I freeze these egg muffins?
Absolutely! Wrap each muffin tightly in plastic wrap and store them in a sealed freezer bag. They keep well for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently before serving.
What can I substitute for feta cheese?
You can use goat cheese for a milder, creamy flavor or ricotta for a softer texture. Avoid very mild cheeses like mozzarella, as they won’t provide the salty tang that feta brings to the muffins.
Can I add other vegetables to this recipe?
Yes! Chopped bell peppers, tomatoes, or mushrooms work well. Just make sure to cook any raw vegetables like mushrooms or onions first to remove excess moisture and prevent sogginess.
How long do these muffins keep in the fridge?
Store them in an airtight container for up to 3 days. After that, they may start to dry out or lose flavor. Reheat gently to restore moisture and texture.
Can I make these muffins dairy-free?
Yes, use a dairy-free milk alternative like almond or oat milk and substitute feta with a vegan cheese option or omit it entirely. The texture will be slightly different but still tasty.
Why do my egg muffins sometimes turn rubbery?
This usually happens if the eggs are overbeaten or baked at too high a temperature. Be sure to mix gently and bake at 350°F to get a tender, fluffy texture without toughness.
