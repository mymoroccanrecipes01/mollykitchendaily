Moist Banana Zucchini Bread with Melty Chocolate Chips You'll Love

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 8, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This banana zucchini bread is my go-to when I want something moist and cozy. The zucchini adds a fresh twist, and those melty chocolate chips make it feel like a treat. Perfect for mornings or a snack anytime!

Introduction

I first made this banana zucchini bread a few years ago when I had extra ripe bananas and zucchini from my garden. I wanted to create something that wasn’t the usual banana bread, so I threw in some grated zucchini for moisture and a subtle fresh flavor. The addition of chocolate chips? Pure magic. My family loved it instantly, and it quickly became a staple in our kitchen. What makes this version different is the balance between the sweetness of the bananas and the mild earthiness of the zucchini, plus the little pockets of melted chocolate that surprise you in every bite. I also learned that squeezing out excess moisture from the zucchini is key — otherwise, the bread can get too soggy. The result is a tender, moist loaf with a beautiful golden crust and a soft crumb. It’s perfect for breakfast, an afternoon snack, or even a cozy dessert. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes I keep coming back to because it’s so forgiving and delicious every time.

What You'll Need

1 loaf, approximately 8 slices

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 ripe bananas, mashed
  • 1 cup grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 3/4 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a standard 9x5-inch loaf pan. This ensures the bread doesn’t stick and bakes evenly. Preparing the pan ahead is crucial because once your batter is ready, you want to get it into the oven quickly for the best rise and texture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan is evenly greased and ready for batter
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon. Mixing these dry ingredients thoroughly helps distribute the leavening agent evenly, so your bread rises uniformly without any bitter pockets of baking soda.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no clumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, cream the softened butter and brown sugar together until light and fluffy. This step incorporates air, which contributes to a tender crumb and moist texture. Avoid rushing it; a good creaming can make all the difference.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is pale and fluffy
Step 4
Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. Then stir in the vanilla extract. This gradual mixing helps emulsify the batter, ensuring it doesn’t separate and that the texture stays smooth.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and glossy, without streaks
Step 5
Fold in the mashed bananas and grated zucchini. Make sure your zucchini is well-squeezed to avoid excess moisture, which can make the bread dense or gummy. Gently folding prevents overmixing, which can toughen the bread.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ingredients are just combined, with no large streaks of flour
Step 6
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients, folding carefully until just combined. Overmixing at this stage can activate gluten and make the bread chewy, so stop as soon as you see no dry flour.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick but smooth, no lumps of flour
Step 7
Fold in the chocolate chips evenly throughout the batter. Distribute them well so every slice has those yummy pockets of chocolate. Resist the urge to add too many, or the bread structure might weaken.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly distributed without clumps
Step 8
Pour the batter into your prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and develop a nice crust. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any air bubbles.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread in pan, surface smooth
Step 9
Bake in the preheated oven for 50-60 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Ovens vary, so start checking at 50 minutes. Avoid opening the oven door too often to prevent the bread from collapsing.
⏱ Time: 50-60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust with toothpick clean from center
Step 10
Let the bread cool in the pan for about 15 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. This resting time helps the bread set up so it slices cleanly without crumbling. Cutting too soon can cause it to fall apart.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm but not hot to the touch

Variations

Walnut Banana Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts to the batter for crunch and nutty flavor. This adds texture contrast and a toasty note that pairs beautifully with chocolate chips.

Note: Fold nuts in with chocolate chips to ensure even distribution without overmixing.

Spiced Banana Zucchini Bread

Boost the warm spice profile by adding 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg and 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves. This variation feels extra cozy, perfect for fall mornings.

Note: Adjust spices carefully; too much can overpower the banana and zucchini flavors.

Vegan Banana Zucchini Bread

Swap butter for coconut oil and use flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg). Use dairy-free chocolate chips to keep it vegan and still deliciously moist.

Note: Expect a slightly denser texture; bake time may vary by 5-10 minutes.

Gluten-Free Banana Zucchini Bread

Replace all-purpose flour with a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend. The bread remains moist but may have a slightly different crumb structure, still tasty and perfect for gluten-sensitive eaters.

Note: Add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t already contain it for better texture.

Chocolate Swirl Banana Zucchini Bread

Instead of mixing chocolate chips, swirl melted chocolate into the batter before baking for a marbled effect. It looks impressive and tastes indulgent without extra sweetness.

Note: Pour half the batter into the pan, drizzle melted chocolate, then top with remaining batter and swirl gently.

Pro Tips

Squeeze the zucchini well before adding it to the batter.
Zucchini holds a lot of water, and if not squeezed, it can release too much moisture during baking, resulting in a dense or gummy loaf. Removing excess liquid keeps the batter balanced and lets the bread bake with the perfect crumb.
Use very ripe bananas for natural sweetness and moisture.
Ripe bananas have higher sugar content and softer texture, which tenderizes the bread and adds rich banana flavor. Using underripe bananas can make the bread dry and less flavorful.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add the dry ingredients.
Overmixing activates gluten in the flour, leading to a tougher, chewier bread. Folding gently just until combined keeps the crumb tender and soft.
Cream butter and sugar thoroughly until light and fluffy.
This step incorporates air into the batter, helping the bread rise and creating a tender texture. Skipping or rushing this can make the bread dense.
Check the bread starting at 50 minutes to avoid overbaking.
Ovens vary, and overbaking can dry out the bread. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs, but not wet batter.
Let the bread cool partially before slicing.
Cooling allows the structure to set, preventing crumbling and making it easier to cut clean slices.
Use a 9x5-inch loaf pan for best shape and baking time.
Larger or smaller pans will affect baking times and bread thickness. This size ensures even cooking and a classic loaf shape.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing excess water from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can go straight into batter, but its water content can be surprisingly high.

Fix After grating, place zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove moisture. This prevents soggy texture and helps the bread hold together.

Using underripe bananas

Why it happens Sometimes bananas look fine but aren’t ripe enough to provide sweetness and moisture.

Fix Wait until bananas are very spotted or even slightly soft. If needed, ripen faster by placing them in a paper bag with an apple.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Eager cooks often mix too long hoping for smooth batter.

Fix Mix just until dry ingredients are incorporated. A few lumps are okay. This keeps the bread tender.

Not creaming butter and sugar properly

Why it happens Rushing or skipping this step leads to denser texture.

Fix Use softened butter and beat with sugar until pale and fluffy to trap air for lighter crumb.

Baking at the wrong temperature

Why it happens Oven temperature can be off or set incorrectly.

Fix Use an oven thermometer to ensure 350°F. Baking too hot or cold affects rise and crust.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The bread keeps well at room temperature for up to 2 days when wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. This helps maintain moisture and prevents it from drying out.

Refrigerator

Store the bread in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. Keep in mind refrigeration can dry out baked goods slightly, so warming slices before eating helps restore softness.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled loaf tightly in plastic wrap and then foil before freezing for up to 3 months. Freeze in individual slices for easy thawing. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature.

Reheating

Reheat slices in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5 minutes. Adding a small pat of butter or a splash of milk before warming can help retain moisture and flavor.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh the next day for the best texture and flavor. Alternatively, bake the loaf and freeze it; thaw and warm before serving.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
Yes, but make sure to thaw and squeeze out as much water as possible before adding it to the batter. Frozen zucchini tends to have more moisture, so proper draining is key to avoid a soggy loaf.
What if I don’t have chocolate chips? Can I skip them?
Absolutely! The bread will still be delicious without chocolate chips. You could also substitute with chopped nuts or dried fruit for a different twist.
How do I know when the bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick into the center; if it comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs, it's done. If there’s wet batter, bake for 5-10 more minutes and check again.
Can I make this bread in muffins instead of a loaf?
Yes! Bake the batter in a greased or lined muffin tin at 350°F for about 20-25 minutes. Check doneness with a toothpick. Muffins will be slightly less dense and perfect for grab-and-go snacks.
Why is it important not to overmix the batter?
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which makes the bread tough and chewy instead of tender. Mixing just until combined keeps the texture soft and moist.
How should I store leftover banana zucchini bread?
Wrap leftovers tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container. It keeps well at room temperature for 2 days or refrigerated for up to 5 days. Freeze for longer storage.
Can I substitute the butter with oil?
Yes, you can use an equal amount of vegetable or canola oil. The bread will be moist but may have a slightly different texture and flavor.
Is this recipe suitable for meal prep or freezing?
Definitely! It freezes well, making it a great make-ahead breakfast or snack. Slice and freeze individually for easy thawing.
