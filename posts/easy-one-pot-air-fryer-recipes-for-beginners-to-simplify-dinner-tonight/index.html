Crispy One-Pot Air Fryer Chicken and Veggies for Easy Weeknights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 4, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This one-pot air fryer chicken and veggies recipe is a total lifesaver for busy nights. It’s crispy, flavorful, and everything cooks together so cleanup is a breeze. I love how the air fryer locks in juiciness while getting that perfect golden crust.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 1.5 pounds boneless skinless chicken thighs, trimmed
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 cups baby potatoes, halved
  • 1 cup baby carrots
  • 1 cup broccoli florets
  • 1 lemon, sliced
  • Fresh parsley, chopped for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by patting the chicken thighs dry with paper towels—this step is key for achieving a crispy skin once air fried. Place the chicken in a large mixing bowl and drizzle with olive oil. Add smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, and black pepper, then toss everything together until the chicken is evenly coated. The oil helps the spices stick and promotes browning during cooking. Avoid overcrowding the bowl so each piece is well-seasoned. This initial seasoning builds the foundation for flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken pieces should look evenly coated with a slight sheen from the oil and spices.
Step 2
Add the halved baby potatoes, baby carrots, and broccoli florets to the bowl. Toss gently with the chicken and remaining oil and spices. Make sure the veggies are well coated but try not to break them up. This ensures even roasting and that each bite has a nice caramelized edge. If you’re in a hurry, don’t skip tossing the veggies with oil—it helps them crisp up instead of steaming in the air fryer.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Veggies should be glossy and evenly coated without clumps of seasoning.
Step 3
Preheat your air fryer to 380°F (193°C) for about 3-5 minutes. Preheating is important because it jumpstarts the cooking process, helping the chicken skin crisp up quickly and locking in juices. If you skip this step, you might get less crispiness and longer cook times. While the air fryer warms, arrange the chicken and veggies in the basket in a single layer, skin side up for the chicken. Avoid overcrowding to allow hot air to circulate freely for even cooking.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer should be warm and ready; chicken and veggies arranged neatly with space between pieces.
Step 4
Cook at 380°F for 25 minutes, shaking the basket or flipping the veggies halfway through. This flipping ensures even browning and prevents any pieces from burning on one side. Keep an eye on the potatoes as they take longer to cook than the other veggies. If you notice some pieces cooking faster, try to reposition them during shaking. The chicken skin should turn golden brown and crispy, and the veggies should be tender with a slight char.
⏱ Time: 25 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken skin is crisp and golden; potatoes pierced easily with a fork; veggies tender and slightly browned.
Step 5
In the last 3 minutes of cooking, add lemon slices on top of the chicken and veggies. The lemon adds a bright, fresh flavor and a little zing that balances the smoky paprika. You can also squeeze some lemon juice over the finished dish for extra brightness. Avoid adding lemon too early as it can burn and turn bitter.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Lemon slices slightly roasted but not charred, aroma fresh and citrusy.
Step 6
Once cooked, transfer everything to a serving platter. Sprinkle freshly chopped parsley over the top for a pop of color and fresh herbal note. This finishing touch adds visual appeal and a subtle flavor contrast to the smoky, savory chicken and veggies. Serve immediately while everything is hot and crisp.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dish looks vibrant with green parsley and lemon slices; aroma fresh and inviting.
Step 7
If you want extra crispy skin, you can air fry the chicken alone for an additional 3-5 minutes at 400°F after removing the veggies. This step is optional but can enhance texture. Just watch carefully to avoid burning. The higher temperature intensifies the Maillard reaction, creating a crunchier crust.
⏱ Time: 3-5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken skin crackles audibly and looks deeply golden brown.
Step 8
Let the chicken rest for 5 minutes before serving. Resting allows the juices to redistribute inside the meat, keeping it moist and flavorful. Cutting into it too soon causes the juices to run out, resulting in drier chicken. Cover loosely with foil while resting to retain warmth.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken feels warm but not steaming; juices settle when pressed gently.
Step 9
Serve with your favorite sides or a simple green salad for a complete meal. This recipe pairs well with crusty bread or rice to soak up any juices. Leftovers reheat nicely in the air fryer or oven for a quick next-day meal. Enjoy the balance of crispy, tender, and fresh flavors!
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dish plated attractively, ready to enjoy.

Variations

Spicy Cajun One-Pot Air Fryer Chicken

Swap smoked paprika for Cajun seasoning and add a pinch of cayenne pepper for a spicy kick. The veggies can stay the same or swap carrots for bell peppers. Expect a bolder, zestier flavor with a bit of heat that’s perfect for spice lovers.

Note: Spicy seasonings can burn faster, so watch the cooking time closely and shake the basket to prevent scorching.

Herb and Garlic Air Fryer Chicken

Use fresh chopped rosemary, thyme, and garlic powder instead of smoked paprika. Toss veggies with minced garlic and a drizzle of lemon juice before cooking. This version is aromatic and earthy, with a fresh garlic punch.

Note: Fresh herbs can brown quickly; add some halfway through cooking or sprinkle on after to avoid burning.

Mediterranean Style with Olives and Feta

Add pitted Kalamata olives and sprinkle crumbled feta over the cooked dish. Use oregano and lemon zest in the seasoning. This variation offers briny, tangy notes and a Mediterranean flair.

Note: Add olives after cooking to keep their texture, and add feta just before serving to prevent melting.

Sweet and Spicy Honey Glaze

Mix honey, soy sauce, and chili flakes to brush on the chicken halfway through cooking for a sticky, sweet-spicy glaze. It adds a glossy finish and a touch of Asian-inspired flavor.

Note: Brush glaze carefully to avoid excess dripping, which can cause smoke or burning in the air fryer.

Vegetarian One-Pot Air Fryer Meal

Replace chicken with firm tofu or chickpeas and season similarly. Use hearty veggies like cauliflower and sweet potatoes. This keeps the recipe plant-based while still crispy and flavorful.

Note: Press tofu well to remove moisture and cut evenly for best texture; cooking times may vary slightly.

Pro Tips

Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket.
Crowding the basket blocks airflow, which reduces the crisping effect and causes uneven cooking. Hot air needs to circulate freely around each piece to create that golden crust and roast veggies evenly. If your basket is small, cook in batches to get the best texture.
Pat chicken dry before seasoning.
Removing surface moisture from the chicken skin helps it crisp up instead of steaming in the air fryer. Wet skin prevents browning and can lead to soggy texture. Use paper towels to thoroughly dry the chicken before tossing with oil and spices.
Preheat your air fryer basket before cooking.
Preheating jumpstarts the Maillard reaction, helping chicken skin get crispy right away. Without preheating, the chicken might cook unevenly or take longer to brown, affecting texture and flavor. Most air fryers only take a few minutes to preheat.
Flip veggies halfway through cooking.
Turning the veggies ensures even browning and prevents burning on one side. Potatoes especially benefit from flipping because they take longer to cook. This simple step helps all pieces finish at the same time with perfect texture.
Add lemon slices at the very end.
Lemon can burn and turn bitter if cooked too long at high heat. Adding slices in the last few minutes keeps their bright flavor intact and adds a fresh aroma without overpowering the dish.
Let chicken rest before serving.
Resting allows juices to redistribute inside the meat, making the chicken more tender and juicy. Cutting into it immediately causes juices to escape, resulting in dry meat. Cover loosely with foil while resting.
Use bone-in thighs for even more flavor.
Bone-in chicken adds extra moisture and flavor during cooking, but requires slightly longer cook times. Make sure to adjust timing and check doneness carefully to avoid undercooked meat near the bone.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping preheating the air fryer.

Why it happens Many assume you can just toss ingredients into the air fryer without warming it up first.

Fix Take the extra 3-5 minutes to preheat the basket. This helps the chicken skin crisp immediately and promotes even cooking. Without preheating, you risk soggy skin and longer cook times.

Overcrowding the basket in one go.

Why it happens People want to save time and cook everything at once, but don’t realize airflow is key for crispiness.

Fix Cook in batches if needed, spreading ingredients out in a single layer. This ensures hot air circulates properly for even browning and roasting.

Not drying chicken skin before seasoning.

Why it happens Skipping drying to save time, unaware it affects texture.

Fix Pat chicken thoroughly with paper towels before adding oil and spices. Dry skin crisps better and yields a more appetizing crust.

Adding lemon too early in cooking.

Why it happens Assuming lemon can be cooked from the start for flavor infusion.

Fix Add lemon slices only in the last 3-5 minutes to avoid bitterness and burnt citrus flavor.

Not letting chicken rest after cooking.

Why it happens Eager to serve immediately, missing that resting improves juiciness.

Fix Let chicken rest 5 minutes loosely covered to keep it juicy and tender.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This dish can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely to prevent contamination. Beyond that, refrigerate promptly to avoid bacterial growth.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The chicken may lose some crispiness but reheating in the air fryer or oven will help restore texture. Veggies might soften but remain tasty.

Freezer

Freeze cooked chicken and veggies in a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat leftover chicken and veggies in the air fryer at 350°F for 5-8 minutes to restore crispiness. Alternatively, use the oven at 375°F. Avoid microwaving as it can make the skin soggy.

Make Ahead

Prep the chicken and veggies 1 day ahead by seasoning and storing covered in the fridge. When ready, air fry fresh to keep textures crisp and flavors bright. This saves time on busy nights.

FAQ

Can I use chicken breasts instead of thighs?
Yes, but chicken breasts tend to dry out faster in the air fryer. Use bone-in, skin-on breasts if possible, and reduce cooking time slightly. Keep a close eye to avoid overcooking for best results.
How do I know when the chicken is fully cooked?
Use a meat thermometer to check the internal temperature; it should reach 165°F (74°C). The chicken should also feel firm and juices run clear when pierced.
Can I add other vegetables to this recipe?
Absolutely! Just keep in mind different veggies cook at different rates. Hard veggies like carrots and potatoes need longer, while softer ones like zucchini or bell peppers should be added later or cut larger to avoid overcooking.
Is it necessary to flip the chicken during cooking?
For this recipe, flipping the veggies is important, but the chicken should be cooked skin side up the entire time to keep skin crispy. Flipping chicken can cause the skin to lose its crispness.
What if my air fryer basket is small?
Cook in batches to avoid overcrowding. Overcrowding prevents proper air circulation, leading to uneven cooking and soggy results. It’s better to cook in two rounds than sacrifice texture.
How can I make this recipe spicier?
Add cayenne pepper or chili powder to the seasoning mix, or sprinkle chili flakes on the chicken before cooking. You can also serve with a spicy dipping sauce for extra heat.
Can I prepare this recipe ahead and reheat?
Yes, you can prep ingredients and season a day ahead. Cook fresh in the air fryer for best crispiness. Leftovers reheat well in the air fryer or oven to restore texture.
What’s the best way to clean the air fryer after cooking?
Allow the basket to cool, then wash with warm soapy water or place it in the dishwasher if dishwasher safe. Wipe down the interior with a damp cloth. Avoid abrasive scrubbers to protect the non-stick coating.
