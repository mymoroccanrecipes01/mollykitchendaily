Quick and Easy 5-Ingredient Chocolate Cake That Everyone Will Love

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
10 min prep 20 min cook 8 servings

This chocolate cake is a total lifesaver when you're craving something sweet but short on time. Using just five ingredients and cocoa powder, it’s super simple yet rich. Great for spring gatherings or last-minute dessert needs!

Why This Works

This cake’s success lies in the perfect balance and interaction of just a few ingredients. The cocoa powder plays a dual role — it provides a deep chocolate flavor and also influences the cake’s texture because it’s acidic. This acidity reacts with the baking powder, helping the cake rise and develop a tender crumb. Without the right amount of cocoa and leavening, the cake could turn out dense or flat.

Using milk at room temperature is another key. Cold milk can shock the batter and inhibit proper mixing, while room temperature milk helps dissolve sugar evenly and hydrates the flour, promoting gluten development just enough for structure without toughness. The gradual mixing also prevents overworking the batter, which would cause a dense, chewy texture.

The baking powder used here is a single-acting leavening agent that activates with moisture and heat. That’s why it’s important to bake the cake soon after mixing — waiting too long can cause the batter to lose its lift. The oven temperature is moderate to encourage even cooking and Maillard browning, which adds flavor and a slightly crisp crust while keeping the inside moist.

Finally, the simplicity of the ingredients means there are no eggs or butter, which keeps the cake light but still satisfying. The absence of fat means the cocoa powder and sugar have to do most of the work flavor-wise, which makes choosing a good-quality unsweetened cocoa powder essential for the best taste.

  • Choose a good-quality all-purpose flour that is fresh and finely milled. This flour provides the structure for the cake by forming gluten when hydrated. Too old or coarse flour can result in a dry or crumbly cake. Sifting the flour before mixing helps aerate it and remove lumps, improving texture.
  • Regular granulated sugar is used here to provide sweetness and contribute to the cake’s moistness by attracting and holding water. Avoid substituting with coarse sugars as they won't dissolve as well, potentially leaving a grainy texture. Proper mixing ensures the sugar is evenly distributed for consistent sweetness.
  • Opt for a high-quality, unsweetened cocoa powder with a rich chocolate aroma. The cocoa’s acidity interacts with baking powder to help leaven the cake, so Dutch-processed cocoa (which is neutral) will alter the rise and texture. Sifting it with flour ensures no clumps, yielding a smooth batter.
  • Use fresh, single-acting baking powder for reliable leavening. It reacts once when wet and heated, so baking promptly after mixing is critical. Old or expired baking powder can cause the cake to be flat and dense. It’s the main rising agent here, replacing eggs’ leavening function.
  • Milk adds moisture and helps dissolve sugar and dry ingredients evenly. Room temperature milk is best to avoid shocking the batter and to promote even mixing. Whole milk adds richness, but you can substitute with 2% or plant-based milk for different textures. Avoid cold milk to prevent uneven batter consistency.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 cup milk, room temperature

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this chocolate cake recipe on a hectic weeknight when I wanted something sweet but didn’t have the energy or ingredients for a complicated dessert. What I love about it is how it uses only five simple ingredients you probably already have in your pantry, yet it delivers a rich, chocolatey flavor that feels indulgent. The secret is really in the cocoa powder and the balance of dry ingredients, which creates a wonderfully moist texture without any butter or eggs. Over time, I tweaked the mixing method so it whips up quickly without overworking the batter, which keeps the crumb tender and light. When it comes out of the oven, it’s soft, moist, and perfectly chocolatey — a real crowd-pleaser that’s just right for springtime celebrations or last-minute dessert needs. Honestly, it’s become one of my go-to recipes because it’s fuss-free and so reliable. Plus, it’s great for anyone who wants a comforting treat without the usual baking hassle.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and greasing an 8-inch round cake pan. This step is crucial because it ensures your cake won’t stick and will release cleanly after baking. Using a little butter or non-stick spray works well. While the oven heats, sift together the flour, cocoa powder, and baking powder into a large mixing bowl. Sifting removes any lumps and aerates the dry ingredients, which helps create a lighter, fluffier cake texture. Skip this step and you might end up with uneven pockets or a denser crumb, so take the extra minute to sift carefully.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Dry ingredients are well combined, fine, and lump-free
  2. Step 2

    Add the sugar to the dry mix and stir to combine evenly. Then pour in the milk gradually while stirring gently with a whisk or wooden spoon. Mix just until everything is incorporated – the batter should be smooth but slightly thick. Don’t overmix here, as that can develop gluten and make the cake tough. The milk adds moisture and helps dissolve the sugar, binding everything together for a tender crumb. If you rush this step or add milk too quickly, the batter might become lumpy or uneven, so pour slowly and stir steadily.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Smooth, slightly thick batter with no visible dry spots
  3. Step 3

    Pour the batter evenly into your prepared cake pan and gently smooth the top with a spatula. Even spreading helps the cake bake uniformly, avoiding thick spots that could stay gooey inside. Place the pan in the preheated oven on the middle rack to ensure consistent heat circulation. Baking on the middle rack prevents the bottom or top from cooking too fast, which could cause over-browning or undercooked centers.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Batter evenly spread in pan, smooth surface
  4. Step 4

    Bake for 18 to 20 minutes, but start checking at 18 minutes by inserting a toothpick or cake tester in the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs clinging, the cake is done. Baking too long will dry out the cake, while underbaking leaves it gooey and unstable. The cocoa powder also darkens the cake, so look for a matte, set surface rather than shiny or wet spots.

    ⏱ 18-20 minutes✓ Toothpick comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs
  5. Step 5

    Remove the cake from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes. This resting time allows the cake to set and makes it easier to remove from the pan without breaking. If you try to unmold it while too hot, it might crumble or stick. After 10 minutes, run a knife around the edges and invert onto a wire rack to cool completely.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Cake slightly firm and pulling away from pan edges
  6. Step 6

    Once the cake is completely cool, you can frost or dust it as desired. I like a light dusting of powdered sugar or a simple chocolate glaze. Cooling fully is important because warm cakes can melt frosting or cause it to slide off. This step also helps the flavors meld and the texture stabilize.

    ⏱ Varies✓ Cake cool to the touch, no warmth detected
  7. Step 7

    To make a quick chocolate glaze, melt 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips with 2 tablespoons butter in a microwave-safe bowl in 20-second intervals, stirring in between. Pour over the cooled cake and spread evenly. This adds a shiny, rich finish and extra chocolate flavor that pairs perfectly with the moist crumb.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Glaze smooth and shiny, fully coating cake surface
  8. Step 8

    Let the glaze set at room temperature for at least 15 minutes before slicing. This allows the glaze to firm up slightly so slices hold their shape. Cutting too soon may cause the glaze to drip or stick to the knife.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Glaze feels firm but not hard to the touch
  9. Step 9

    Slice with a sharp knife, wiping it clean between cuts for neat slices. Serve as is or with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or fresh berries for a simple dessert that feels special. Enjoy immediately for best texture and flavor.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Clean, even slices with moist crumb

Variations

  • Add Espresso Powder for a Mocha Twist: Stir in 1 teaspoon of instant espresso powder with the dry ingredients to amplify the chocolate flavor with a subtle coffee note. It gives the cake a richer, deeper taste without overpowering. Great if you love mocha desserts!
    Espresso powder dissolves quickly and enhances cocoa’s bitterness, so no texture changes are expected.
  • Swap Milk for Almond or Oat Milk: For a dairy-free version, replace milk with unsweetened almond or oat milk. The texture remains moist, though the flavor will be slightly nuttier or sweeter depending on the milk. Perfect for dairy intolerance or vegan tweaks.
    Plant milks have different fat content, so the cake might be a touch less rich but still tender.
  • Add Chocolate Chips for Extra Gooeyness: Fold in 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips into the batter before baking. This adds pockets of melted chocolate throughout the cake, making each bite extra indulgent and gooey.
    Chocolate chips may sink slightly; tossing them in flour before adding can help distribute evenly.
  • Top with Fresh Berries and Whipped Cream: Serve slices with fresh strawberries, raspberries, or blueberries and a dollop of whipped cream. It adds freshness and balances the rich chocolate with bright, light flavors.
    No changes to baking; add toppings just before serving.
  • Make It a Layer Cake with Simple Frosting: Double the recipe to bake two cakes and layer them with chocolate or vanilla frosting. This transforms the quick cake into a celebration-worthy dessert with more texture and flavor contrast.
    Bake each layer separately and cool completely before frosting.

Pro Tips

  • Always sift your dry ingredients together before mixing. — Sifting incorporates air and breaks up lumps, creating a lighter batter and a finer crumb in the finished cake. It also ensures even distribution of the cocoa, flour, and baking powder, which is crucial for uniform rise and texture.
  • Use room temperature milk instead of cold. — Room temperature milk mixes more evenly with dry ingredients, preventing lumps and ensuring the batter has consistent hydration. Cold liquids can slow down the baking powder reaction and cause uneven texture.
  • Don’t overmix the batter once liquid is added. — Overmixing develops too much gluten, which makes the cake tough and chewy instead of tender and moist. Mix just until combined for the best texture.
  • Bake the cake promptly after mixing. — Because baking powder reacts as soon as it’s wet, delaying baking can cause the batter to lose its rising power, resulting in a denser cake. Immediate baking ensures the best lift.
  • Grease and flour your cake pan well. — This prevents sticking and allows the cake to release cleanly without breaking apart. It also helps form a nice crust around the edges.
  • Check doneness with a toothpick starting at 18 minutes. — Ovens vary, so checking early prevents overbaking, which dries out the cake. The toothpick test is the most reliable method for ensuring moistness.
  • Let the cake cool before frosting or slicing. — Cooling allows the crumb to set, preventing crumbling or melting frosting. It also helps flavors develop fully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping sifting of dry ingredients
    Why: Many cooks assume sifting is unnecessary or too time-consuming, especially in simple recipes.
    Fix: Take the extra minute to sift flour, cocoa, and baking powder together. This prevents lumps and ensures even leavening, resulting in a smoother batter and better texture.
  • Using cold milk straight from the fridge
    Why: It’s easy to forget to bring milk to room temperature, especially when in a hurry.
    Fix: Warm milk slightly or leave it out for 30 minutes before mixing. This helps with even mixing and proper chemical reactions for rise.
  • Overmixing the batter after adding milk
    Why: Eager to get a smooth batter, some mix too vigorously or for too long.
    Fix: Mix gently and only until dry ingredients disappear. Overmixing develops gluten, resulting in a tough cake instead of a tender one.
  • Waiting too long to bake after mixing
    Why: Distractions or multitasking can cause delays after preparing the batter.
    Fix: Preheat your oven first and bake immediately after mixing to capture the full rising power of the baking powder.
  • Removing the cake from the pan while still hot
    Why: Impatience to serve or frost sometimes leads to unmolding too soon.
    Fix: Let the cake cool in the pan for at least 10 minutes before removing, which prevents breakage and sticking.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: The cake keeps well at room temperature for up to 2 days if covered tightly with plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. This prevents it from drying out and keeps the texture moist.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The cake may firm up and become denser when cold, so it’s best served at room temperature or warmed slightly before eating.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to freeze for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave it in the fridge overnight or at room temperature for a few hours. Avoid microwaving frozen cake directly to prevent sogginess.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes to restore softness. Adding a small dab of butter or a splash of milk before reheating can help maintain moisture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Mix the dry ingredients a day ahead and store in an airtight container. On the day of baking, simply add milk and mix. Alternatively, bake the cake a day ahead and store covered; this actually improves flavor as it rests.

FAQ

Can I use Dutch-processed cocoa instead of natural cocoa powder?
Dutch-processed cocoa is less acidic and won’t react properly with the baking powder in this recipe, leading to less rise and a denser cake. If you only have Dutch-processed cocoa, you might need to substitute baking powder with baking soda and add an acid like buttermilk for proper leavening.
What if I don’t have baking powder, can I use baking soda?
Baking soda requires an acid to activate, which this recipe lacks. Using baking soda alone will likely result in a flat cake. It’s best to stick with baking powder for this recipe or adjust the recipe to include an acid like vinegar or buttermilk if using baking soda.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes, you can substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free flour blend that includes xanthan gum. Be aware that texture may vary slightly, sometimes becoming more crumbly or dense depending on the blend used.
How do I know when the cake is done baking?
The best way is to insert a toothpick into the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the cake is done. The surface should look set and matte, not shiny or wet. Avoid relying solely on baking time as ovens vary.
Can I double the recipe for a larger cake?
Absolutely! Just double all ingredients and bake in a larger pan or two pans. Baking time may increase slightly, so start checking doneness at the original time and adjust as needed.
What can I use to frost this cake?
Simple options include a powdered sugar dusting, chocolate glaze, or buttercream frosting. For a lighter option, whipped cream or cream cheese frosting pairs nicely without overpowering the chocolate flavor.
Is it possible to make this cake vegan?
Since this recipe uses no eggs or butter, you can make it vegan by substituting the milk with plant-based milk like almond or oat milk. The texture might be slightly different but still delicious.
How should I store leftover cake?
Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze tightly wrapped for up to 3 months.
