Moist Strawberry Shortcake Layer Cake with Fluffy Whipped Cream Frosting

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
30 min prep 35 min cook 8 servings

This strawberry shortcake layer cake is bursting with fresh berries and topped with light whipped cream frosting. It’s super moist and perfect for springtime gatherings or a casual weekend treat when you want something sweet but not too heavy.

Why This Works

The secret to this strawberry shortcake layer cake’s moistness lies in the balance of ingredients and precise mixing technique. Using whole milk instead of water or skim milk adds richness and fat, which tenderizes the crumb and keeps it soft even after baking. The creaming method—beating butter and sugar until fluffy—incorporates air that helps the cake rise with a light texture.

Alternating the dry ingredients with milk during mixing prevents overworking the gluten in the flour, which can cause toughness. The gentle folding ensures a tender crumb that melts in your mouth. Macerating the strawberries with sugar releases their natural juices, which soak into the cake layers without making them soggy, adding bursts of fresh flavor throughout.

Whipping the cream to soft peaks and chilling the mixing tools beforehand helps stabilize the frosting. This light, airy whipped cream contrasts perfectly with the dense, moist cake layers. The combination of textures—the fluffy cream, juicy berries, and soft cake—creates a harmonious dessert experience that feels fresh and indulgent at once.

Finally, assembling the cake with even layers and letting it rest in the fridge for an hour allows the flavors to meld together. The strawberries’ juices slightly permeate the cake, enhancing the overall taste and keeping every bite juicy and delightful.

  • Choose a good quality all-purpose flour with moderate protein content around 10-11%. This balances gluten formation for structure without making the cake tough. Sifting the flour before use introduces air and removes lumps, resulting in a lighter batter and fluffier cake layers.
  • Use unsalted butter at room temperature for better control over salt content and a creamier batter. Softened butter creams better with sugar, trapping air bubbles that help the cake rise evenly. Avoid melted or cold butter as they affect texture and mixing consistency.
  • Pick ripe, firm strawberries that are bright red and fragrant for maximum flavor. Fresh berries hold their shape better when sliced and macerated. Avoid overripe or mushy berries as they can release too much liquid, leading to soggy cake layers.
  • Use cold heavy cream with at least 36% fat content for stable whipped cream that whips quickly and holds peaks well. Chilling your bowl and beaters prevents the cream from warming up too fast, which can cause it to become runny.
  • Regular granulated sugar is used both in the batter and to macerate strawberries. Sugar not only sweetens but also helps tenderize the cake by interacting with gluten and retains moisture, keeping the cake moist longer.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 2 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 3/4 cups granulated sugar
  • 4 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 cup whole milk, room temperature
  • 4 cups fresh strawberries, hulled and sliced
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar (for berries)
  • 2 cups heavy cream, cold
  • 1/4 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract (for whipped cream)

Introduction

I first made this strawberry shortcake layer cake a few springs ago when I wanted to bring a fresh twist to a classic dessert. Instead of the usual biscuits or sponge, I went for a tender, moist cake layered with juicy strawberries and a cloud-like whipped cream frosting. What makes this version special is how I keep the cake super soft by carefully folding in the flour and using whole milk for richness. Plus, letting the strawberries macerate with a bit of sugar brings out their natural sweetness and juiciness, which soaks into the cake layers just enough without making them soggy. I’ve tried other versions that ended up dry or too heavy, but this one strikes the perfect balance—light, fresh, and bursting with berry flavor. When you slice into it, you get that beautiful contrast between the airy whipped cream and the moist cake, with strawberries peeking out in every bite. It’s a dessert that never fails to get compliments, whether it’s a casual weekend treat or a centerpiece for a spring gathering. Trust me, once you make it, you’ll want to keep this recipe on hand for every berry season!

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and flour three 9-inch round cake pans to prevent sticking. This step ensures your cakes come out cleanly and keep their shape. Be sure to tap out excess flour so it doesn’t affect the texture of the cake. Properly preparing the pans is crucial because poorly greased pans can cause your cake layers to tear or crumble when you try to remove them.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Pans are evenly greased and floured with no clumps
  2. Step 2

    In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, and salt. Sifting adds air and helps avoid lumps, creating a lighter cake texture. Mixing dry ingredients separately also ensures even distribution of the leavening agents, which is essential for even rising.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dry ingredients are combined with no visible clumps
  3. Step 3

    In a large bowl, beat the softened butter and sugar with an electric mixer on medium speed until light and fluffy, about 4-5 minutes. This creaming step is key to incorporating air into the batter, which helps the cake rise and become tender. Don’t rush this step—if you under-cream, your cake will be dense.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Butter and sugar mixture is pale and fluffy
  4. Step 4

    Add eggs one at a time to the butter-sugar mixture, beating well after each addition. This gradual incorporation prevents the batter from curdling and ensures a smooth texture. Once all eggs are in, mix in the vanilla extract. The vanilla adds warmth and depth to the flavor, balancing the sweetness.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Batter is smooth and glossy without streaks
  5. Step 5

    Alternately add the dry ingredients and whole milk to the batter in three parts, starting and ending with the dry ingredients. Mix gently on low speed and stop as soon as combined to avoid overmixing, which can make the cake tough. The milk adds moisture and richness, making the crumb tender and soft.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Batter is smooth with no flour pockets but not overworked
  6. Step 6

    Divide the batter evenly between the prepared pans and smooth the tops with a spatula. Bake in the preheated oven for 30-35 minutes or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Even batter distribution ensures uniform layers, which makes stacking easier and the cake more stable.

    ⏱ 30-35 minutes✓ Cake tops are golden and spring back when lightly pressed
  7. Step 7

    While the cakes bake, toss the sliced strawberries with 2 tablespoons of sugar and let them sit at room temperature for 20-30 minutes. This maceration draws out the juices and sweetens the berries naturally, making them juicier and more flavorful to complement the cake layers.

    ⏱ 20-30 minutes✓ Strawberries are slightly glossy and have released some juice
  8. Step 8

    For the whipped cream frosting, chill your mixing bowl and beaters in the freezer for 15 minutes. Then whip the cold heavy cream with powdered sugar and vanilla extract on medium-high speed until soft peaks form. Chilling the equipment helps the cream whip faster and hold its structure better for a stable, fluffy frosting.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Whipped cream forms soft peaks that hold shape but are still smooth
  9. Step 9

    Once cakes are completely cool, level the tops if needed. Place one cake layer on your serving plate, spread a layer of whipped cream, then sprinkle a generous amount of macerated strawberries. Repeat for the next layers, finishing with whipped cream on top and a decorative strawberry arrangement. This layering technique ensures every bite has cake, cream, and berry goodness.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Cake is evenly layered with visible cream and strawberry filling

Variations

  • Lemon-Infused Strawberry Shortcake: Add lemon zest to the cake batter and a splash of lemon juice to the macerated strawberries for a bright citrus twist. This variation adds freshness and a subtle tang that pairs beautifully with the sweet berries and cream.
    Zest finely and fold gently into the batter; avoid overmixing to keep texture tender.
  • Strawberry Shortcake with Mascarpone Frosting: Swap whipped cream for a mascarpone-based frosting mixed with a little powdered sugar and vanilla. It’s richer and tangier, offering a sophisticated flavor profile while still complementing the fresh strawberries.
    Chill mascarpone well and beat gently to avoid curdling.
  • Vegan Strawberry Shortcake Cake: Use plant-based butter and milk alternatives like oat milk, and replace eggs with flax eggs. Whip coconut cream for the frosting. This version keeps the cake moist and flavorful while accommodating vegan diets.
    Ensure coconut cream is well chilled before whipping to achieve stiffness.
  • Chocolate Strawberry Shortcake: Add 1/4 cup cocoa powder to the flour mixture for a chocolate twist. The rich chocolate flavor contrasts nicely with the sweet strawberries and light whipped cream, creating a decadent treat.
    Reduce flour by 2 tablespoons to balance the added cocoa powder.
  • Strawberry Shortcake Cupcakes: Portion the batter into cupcake liners and bake for 18-22 minutes. Top each cupcake with whipped cream and a strawberry slice for an individual-sized dessert perfect for parties or picnics.
    Adjust baking time and check doneness early to avoid overbaking.

Pro Tips

  • Always bring eggs and milk to room temperature before mixing. — Room temperature ingredients blend more easily into the butter-sugar mixture, creating a smoother batter. Cold ingredients can cause the butter to seize and the batter to curdle, resulting in a dense or uneven cake texture.
  • Don’t overmix the batter once flour is added. — Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which makes the cake tough and chewy instead of tender. Mix just until the flour disappears to keep the crumb soft and light.
  • Let the strawberries macerate for at least 20 minutes. — This step draws out the natural juices and sweetens the berries, enhancing their flavor and moisture without overwhelming the cake layers. Skipping this can result in less juicy berries and a drier cake.
  • Chill your mixing bowl and beaters before whipping cream. — Cold equipment helps the cream whip faster and hold its structure longer. Warm tools cause the cream to loosen and lose volume, making it hard to frost the cake smoothly.
  • Level cake layers with a serrated knife before assembling. — Cutting off domed tops creates flat layers that stack evenly, preventing the cake from leaning or collapsing. Uneven layers can make frosting tricky and affect the cake’s appearance.
  • Use an offset spatula for spreading whipped cream. — An offset spatula provides better control and smoother application of frosting, especially on layered cakes. It helps avoid tearing the cake layers or creating lumps in the cream.
  • Refrigerate the assembled cake for at least an hour before serving. — Chilling sets the whipped cream and allows the strawberry juices to meld with the cake layers, improving flavor and texture. Serving immediately can cause the frosting to be too soft and the cake to crumble.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using cold eggs and milk directly from the fridge
    Why: Many assume cold ingredients speed up baking or don’t realize it affects batter texture.
    Fix: Always let eggs and milk sit at room temperature for 30 minutes before mixing. This ensures smooth blending and prevents curdling, resulting in a tender cake.
  • Overmixing the batter after adding flour
    Why: It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to remove lumps, but this develops gluten.
    Fix: Mix just until no streaks of flour remain. Stop early to keep the cake soft and avoid toughness.
  • Skipping maceration of strawberries
    Why: Rushing or forgetting this step reduces berry juiciness and sweetness.
    Fix: Toss berries with sugar and let sit at room temp for 20-30 minutes. This enhances flavor and moisture in the cake layers.
  • Not chilling bowl and beaters before whipping cream
    Why: People often overlook this prep step, especially if in a hurry.
    Fix: Pop your tools in the freezer for 15 minutes before whipping. It helps the cream hold peaks better and prevents runniness.
  • Stacking cake layers before they fully cool
    Why: Impatience or distraction leads to assembling warm cake layers.
    Fix: Cool layers completely on wire racks to prevent the whipped cream from melting and sliding off.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: The assembled cake can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with a cake dome or large bowl to prevent drying out. Beyond that, refrigerate to keep the whipped cream fresh.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store the cake in an airtight container or cover it tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for up to 3 days. Note that the whipped cream might weep slightly and the cake may firm up, so bring it to room temperature before serving for best texture.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze the unassembled cake layers wrapped tightly in plastic and foil for up to 2 months. Thaw in the refrigerator overnight. Whipped cream frosting is best made fresh, so assemble the cake after thawing.
  • ♨️ Reheating: This cake is best served chilled or at room temperature. If desired, bring to room temp for about 30 minutes before serving. Avoid microwaving as it will melt the whipped cream and alter texture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Bake the cake layers 1-2 days in advance, wrap well and refrigerate. Macerate the strawberries the day of serving to keep them fresh. Whip the cream and assemble the cake a few hours before serving for optimal freshness.

FAQ

Can I use frozen strawberries for this cake?
Frozen strawberries tend to release more water when thawed, which can make the cake soggy. If you must use frozen berries, thaw and drain them well, then pat dry before macerating. Fresh strawberries are best for texture and flavor.
How do I prevent the whipped cream from melting too fast?
Use heavy cream with at least 36% fat and chill your mixing bowl and beaters before whipping. Also, keep the cake refrigerated until serving and avoid warm environments to help the whipped cream hold its shape longer.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes, substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend that includes xanthan gum for structure. Baking times may vary slightly, so watch the cake closely for doneness.
What’s the best way to level cake layers?
Use a serrated knife or a cake leveler to slice off any domed tops once the cake is fully cooled. This creates flat, even layers for stacking and prevents the cake from leaning or sliding.
How long can I store leftover cake?
Store leftover cake covered in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. The whipped cream may soften and the cake may dry out slightly, so bring it to room temperature before eating for best texture.
Can I use a different frosting instead of whipped cream?
Absolutely! Cream cheese frosting or mascarpone frosting are delicious alternatives that pair well with strawberries. Just keep in mind they are richer and heavier than whipped cream.
Why is my cake dense instead of fluffy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter, using cold ingredients, or not creaming the butter and sugar long enough. Follow the mixing steps carefully to incorporate air and avoid toughness.
Can I make this cake ahead and freeze it assembled?
It’s best to freeze cake layers separately and assemble fresh. Freezing the assembled cake with whipped cream frosting can cause the cream to lose texture and become watery upon thawing.
