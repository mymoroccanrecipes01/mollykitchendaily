Fudgy Flourless Chocolate Brownies with Fresh Raspberry Twist

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 25 min cook 8 servings

These rich, flourless brownies are incredibly fudgy and naturally gluten-free, made with just chocolate and eggs. The fresh raspberries add a lively tartness that balances the deep chocolate flavor perfectly. Great for dessert or an indulgent afternoon treat!

Why This Works

Flourless brownies rely heavily on the balance between eggs and chocolate to achieve their signature fudgy texture. Eggs provide structure and moisture, but without flour, the protein network is less rigid, allowing for a dense, almost truffle-like crumb. The key is to whisk the eggs well to incorporate some air, which lightens the texture slightly without making it cakey. The melted chocolate acts as both flavor and fat, creating richness and smoothness.

The absence of flour means the brownies won’t rise much, so even baking temperature and timing are crucial to avoid overcooking. The shiny, crackly top forms due to the sugar and chocolate crystallizing at the surface, adding a delightful textural contrast to the soft interior. Folding in fresh raspberries adds bursts of acidity that cut through the richness, preventing the dessert from feeling too heavy.

Using good quality dark chocolate with a cocoa content around 60-70% ensures a deep chocolate flavor without excessive bitterness. The gentle melting process preserves the chocolate’s smoothness, which is vital since it’s the main structural ingredient. Finally, letting the brownies cool completely before cutting allows the fats to solidify and the texture to set perfectly.

In essence, this recipe succeeds because it balances moisture, fat, and acidity without relying on flour, resulting in a naturally gluten-free dessert that’s indulgent but not overwhelming, with a fresh fruit twist that keeps each bite interesting.

  • Look for high-quality dark chocolate with 60-70% cocoa content for the best flavor balance—too low and it’ll be too sweet, too high and bitterness dominates. Good chocolate melts smoothly and gives the brownies a rich, glossy texture. Avoid chocolate chips with stabilizers as they may not melt as well.
  • Use large eggs at room temperature to help the batter emulsify and achieve the right structure. Cold eggs can cause the chocolate to seize when combined. Eggs provide moisture and protein, which sets the brownies without flour, so fresh eggs yield the best texture.
  • Regular granulated sugar helps create the crackly top by dissolving into the batter and caramelizing during baking. It also balances the bitterness of the dark chocolate. Avoid substituting with coarse sugars unless dissolved first.
  • Fresh raspberries add a natural tartness and moisture that balances the dense chocolate. Choose firm, ripe berries to avoid excess juice that could make the batter too wet. Frozen raspberries can be used but will release more liquid and may affect texture.
  • Vanilla enhances the chocolate flavor and rounds out the taste profile. Use pure vanilla extract rather than imitation for the best aroma and depth. It’s a subtle but important ingredient that lifts the entire dessert.

Ingredients

  • 200g dark chocolate, chopped
  • 3 large eggs, at room temperature
  • 100g granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt
  • 100g fresh raspberries

Introduction

I stumbled upon this flourless brownie recipe a few years ago when I wanted a decadent chocolate treat but needed to avoid gluten. It’s been a favorite ever since because it’s so simple and delivers that ultra-fudgy texture that’s hard to beat. What sets this version apart is the addition of fresh raspberries, which cut through the richness with a lovely tartness. I usually mix the raspberries gently into the batter right before baking, so they stay whole and juicy, creating pockets of brightness in every bite. The trick I learned is to melt the chocolate gently over a double boiler to keep it silky smooth and avoid graininess. When they come out of the oven, the brownies have a shiny, crackly top and a luxuriously dense center that feels almost like a chocolate truffle. They’re perfect for serving at a casual dinner party or simply indulging in with a cup of coffee on a quiet afternoon. I’ve tried some flourless brownies before that turned out cakey or too eggy, but this one nails the fudgy texture every time, and the raspberries make it feel fresh and special.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preheating your oven to 325°F (160°C) and lining an 8-inch square baking pan with parchment paper. This prevents sticking and helps you lift the brownies out easily later. Make sure the parchment overhangs the sides for a simple removal. A well-prepared pan is crucial because flourless brownies are delicate and can stick if you skip this step.

    ⏱ 10✓ Oven preheated and pan fully lined with parchment, no wrinkles
  2. Step 2

    Melt the chopped dark chocolate gently using a double boiler or microwave in short bursts. Stir frequently to avoid overheating, which can cause the chocolate to seize or become grainy. The goal is a smooth, glossy melted chocolate base that will give the brownies their rich texture. Avoid direct heat to maintain the chocolate’s silky consistency.

    ⏱ 5✓ Chocolate is completely melted, shiny, and smooth with no lumps
  3. Step 3

    In a separate bowl, whisk the eggs and sugar together until the mixture is light and slightly frothy. This aerates the batter a bit to help achieve a tender crumb despite the lack of flour. Add the vanilla extract and a pinch of salt to enhance the chocolate flavor. Don’t rush this step; well-whisked eggs make a big difference in texture.

    ⏱ 5✓ Egg mixture is pale, slightly thickened, and frothy on top
  4. Step 4

    Slowly pour the melted chocolate into the egg mixture while whisking continuously. This gradual combining tempers the eggs and prevents them from cooking or scrambling. The batter should become smooth and glossy with a deep chocolate hue. If you add the chocolate too fast, you risk curdling the eggs.

    ⏱ 3✓ Batter is homogeneous, thick, and shiny with no lumps
  5. Step 5

    Gently fold in the fresh raspberries using a spatula. Be careful not to overmix; you want the berries to stay mostly intact so they burst with flavor during baking. This step adds a fresh, tart contrast that brightens the dense chocolate. If you mix too vigorously, the raspberries will break down and turn the batter pink.

    ⏱ 2✓ Raspberries are evenly distributed but still whole or in large pieces
  6. Step 6

    Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top with an offset spatula. Spreading it evenly ensures uniform baking and that the brownies have consistent thickness. Uneven batter can cause some parts to overcook while others remain underbaked.

    ⏱ 2✓ Batter is level and smooth across the pan surface
  7. Step 7

    Bake in the preheated oven for about 20-25 minutes. Start checking around 18 minutes by gently shaking the pan; the edges should be set but the center still slightly soft and fudgy. Overbaking will dry out the brownies and lose that signature fudgy texture, so keep a close eye.

    ⏱ 25✓ Edges are firm and pull away slightly from the pan, center jiggles slightly
  8. Step 8

    Remove the brownies from the oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling allows the brownies to set and makes cutting cleaner. If you try to cut them warm, they’ll be too soft and likely fall apart.

    ⏱ 30✓ Brownies are firm to the touch and cooled to room temperature
  9. Step 9

    Lift the brownies out using the parchment overhang and cut into squares with a sharp knife. Wiping the knife between cuts prevents sticking for clean edges. Serve as is or with a dusting of powdered sugar or a scoop of vanilla ice cream for extra indulgence.

    ⏱ 5✓ Brownies cut cleanly with moist, dense interior and visible raspberries

Variations

  • Add chopped nuts for crunch: Mix in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans along with the raspberries for a delightful crunchy texture that contrasts the fudgy brownies. Nuts add a toasty flavor and extra bite, making the brownies even more satisfying.
    Ensure nuts are evenly distributed and avoid overmixing to keep batter smooth; nuts may slightly increase baking time.
  • Swap raspberries for fresh cherries: Use pitted fresh cherries instead of raspberries for a sweeter, juicier burst in your brownies. Cherries pair beautifully with dark chocolate and offer a slightly different flavor profile that’s equally delicious.
    Cherries have more moisture, so fold in carefully and monitor baking closely to avoid sogginess.
  • Make it vegan with flax eggs and dairy-free chocolate: Replace eggs with flaxseed eggs (1 tbsp flaxseed meal + 3 tbsp water per egg) and use vegan dark chocolate to make this recipe vegan-friendly. The texture will be slightly different—more dense but still rich and fudgy.
    Flax eggs don’t aerate as well as real eggs, so expect a denser brownie; bake time may need slight adjustment.
  • Add espresso powder for deeper flavor: Stir in 1 tsp instant espresso powder with the sugar to enhance the chocolate’s richness and add a subtle coffee note. This elevates the flavor complexity without overpowering the raspberries.
    Espresso powder dissolves quickly; no extra liquid needed, just mix well into the egg and sugar mixture.
  • Top with a dusting of powdered sugar or cocoa: After cooling, sift powdered sugar or unsweetened cocoa powder over the brownies for a pretty finish that adds a touch of sweetness or bitterness contrast. It’s an easy way to dress up the brownies for guests.
    Apply just before serving to keep the topping fresh and visually appealing.

Pro Tips

  • Use a double boiler to melt the chocolate gently. — Melting chocolate slowly over simmering water prevents it from overheating and seizing, which can ruin the texture. Direct heat can cause chocolate to become grainy or burnt, affecting both taste and the final brownie texture.
  • Whisk eggs and sugar until frothy before adding chocolate. — Incorporating air into the eggs creates a lighter batter texture, even without flour. This step helps the brownies avoid being too dense or rubbery and ensures a tender crumb with a fudgy center.
  • Fold in raspberries gently to keep them intact. — Overmixing will break down the berries, releasing too much juice and turning the batter pink. Keeping the raspberries whole gives bright bursts of tartness and a pleasant texture contrast.
  • Let brownies cool completely before cutting. — Cooling allows the fats and chocolate to solidify, making clean cuts possible and preventing the brownies from falling apart. Warm brownies are too soft and fragile to slice neatly.
  • Line your baking pan with parchment paper with overhang. — This makes it easy to lift the brownies out in one piece, preventing damage to the edges and enabling cleaner slicing. It also prevents sticking without extra greasing.
  • Avoid overbaking to keep fudgy texture. — Flourless brownies can dry out quickly if baked too long. Watch for a slight jiggle in the center when done; the residual heat will finish setting the brownies during cooling.
  • Use a sharp knife wiped clean between cuts. — Chocolate can stick to the knife, causing ragged edges. Cleaning the knife between slices ensures smooth, attractive brownie squares.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overheating the chocolate when melting.
    Why: Many cooks rush melting or use direct heat, which can cause chocolate to seize or burn.
    Fix: Use a double boiler or microwave in short bursts, stirring frequently. Stop heating as soon as the chocolate is mostly melted and stir to finish melting with residual heat.
  • Skipping whisking the eggs and sugar properly.
    Why: Some assume just mixing is enough, but without aeration, texture suffers.
    Fix: Whisk until the eggs are pale and frothy to incorporate air, which lightens the batter and improves texture.
  • Overmixing raspberries into the batter.
    Why: Raspberries are fragile and break easily, releasing juice that changes batter consistency.
    Fix: Fold raspberries in gently and briefly to keep them intact and maintain the batter’s texture.
  • Cutting brownies before they’ve cooled.
    Why: Impatience leads to warm brownies that are too soft and crumbly.
    Fix: Allow brownies to cool fully at room temperature before slicing for clean cuts and proper texture.
  • Not lining the pan with parchment paper.
    Why: Skipping this step causes brownies to stick and break apart when removing from pan.
    Fix: Always line the pan with parchment with an overhang to lift brownies out easily without damage.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Store the brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Covering them well prevents drying out. They maintain their fudgy texture best when not refrigerated but consume within this time frame for freshness.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Keep brownies in an airtight container for up to 5 days. Refrigeration can firm them up and intensify the chocolate flavor but may dry the edges slightly. Bring to room temperature before serving or warm briefly for softness.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap brownies tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe bag or container. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and then bring to room temperature or warm gently before serving to restore texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm brownies in a microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F/150°C) for about 5 minutes. Add a small splash of milk or cream if they seem dry. Avoid overheating as it can make them crumbly.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the batter and fold in raspberries a few hours ahead, keeping it covered in the fridge. Bake just before serving for the freshest texture. Alternatively, bake fully and store as above, reheating gently when ready to serve.

FAQ

Can I use frozen raspberries instead of fresh?
Yes, but frozen raspberries release more moisture and can make the batter wetter, which might affect texture and baking time. Thaw and drain them well before folding in to reduce extra liquid.
Why are my brownies crumbly instead of fudgy?
Overbaking or under-whisking the eggs can cause crumbly texture. Make sure to watch the baking time closely and whisk eggs and sugar until frothy to incorporate air for the right fudginess.
Can I substitute the dark chocolate with cocoa powder?
Flourless brownies rely on melted chocolate for structure and fat, so substituting with cocoa powder alone won’t work well. You’d need to add extra fat like butter and adjust the recipe significantly.
How do I store leftover brownies?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze tightly wrapped for up to 3 months.
Can I make these brownies nut-free?
Absolutely! This recipe is naturally nut-free unless you add nuts as a variation. Just ensure your chocolate and other ingredients are processed in nut-free facilities if allergy is a concern.
What if my chocolate seizes while melting?
If chocolate seizes, it becomes grainy and thick. Adding a small spoon of warm vegetable oil or butter and stirring gently can help smooth it out. Always melt chocolate slowly to prevent seizing.
Why do my brownies stick to the pan?
Not lining the pan with parchment paper or insufficient greasing causes sticking. Always use parchment with overhang for easy removal and clean edges.
Can I add other fruits besides raspberries?
Yes, fresh cherries, blueberries, or blackberries can be great alternatives. Just be mindful of moisture content and fold them in gently to keep the batter balanced.
