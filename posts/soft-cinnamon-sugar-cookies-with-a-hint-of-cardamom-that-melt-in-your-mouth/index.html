Soft Cinnamon Sugar Cookies with a Hint of Cardamom for Cozy Snacking

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 12 min cook 24 servings

These soft cinnamon sugar cookies bring a cozy twist to a classic favorite. The subtle cardamom adds warmth and depth, making them perfect for afternoon tea or a comforting treat anytime you need a little sweetness.

Why This Works

The magic behind these soft cinnamon sugar cookies lies in the balance of ingredients and technique. The combination of butter and baking powder plays a crucial role. Butter provides richness and moisture, while baking powder acts as a leavening agent that creates tiny air pockets, giving the cookies a tender, slightly puffy texture instead of being flat and hard. The key is not to overmix the dough after adding the flour because overworking the gluten in flour can make cookies tough. Mixing just until combined ensures a soft crumb.

Rolling the dough balls in cinnamon sugar before baking is more than just decoration—it creates a slight caramelized crust that contrasts with the soft interior. This happens because the sugar melts and caramelizes on the surface in the oven’s heat, while the cinnamon infuses a warm flavor throughout. The addition of cardamom is a subtle but impactful twist; it brings an exotic, aromatic note that complements cinnamon beautifully, making the flavor profile more complex and interesting.

Temperature and timing are also critical. Baking at 350°F (175°C) allows the cookies to cook evenly without drying out. Baking just until the edges are set and the centers remain soft ensures the cookies stay chewy and moist. Letting them cool on the baking sheet first helps them firm up gently without becoming hard. Altogether, these steps and ingredients work harmoniously to produce cookies that are soft, flavorful, and delightfully cozy.

  • Choose a good quality all-purpose flour with moderate protein content (around 10-12%) for the best texture. This flour provides enough gluten structure to hold the cookie together but not so much that it becomes tough. Avoid using cake flour, which is too soft, or bread flour, which can make cookies chewy and dense.
  • Use unsalted butter softened to room temperature for better control over salt content and to ensure even creaming with sugar. Butter’s fat content contributes to tenderness and flavor. Avoid melted butter here to keep the dough’s texture right; softened butter traps air when creamed, which helps create a light cookie.
  • A leavening agent that releases carbon dioxide when heated, baking powder helps the cookies rise and become soft and slightly puffy. Using fresh baking powder is important because old or expired baking powder can lose potency, resulting in flat, dense cookies.
  • Cinnamon is the star spice in this recipe. Use a fresh, aromatic ground cinnamon for the best flavor — preferably Ceylon cinnamon if you can find it, as it has a sweeter, more delicate profile. It adds warmth and that classic cinnamon sugar cookie taste.
  • Cardamom is a fragrant spice with citrusy and floral notes that pairs beautifully with cinnamon. Use it sparingly (1/4 teaspoon) to add subtle complexity without overpowering the cookie. Ground cardamom should be fresh for the best aroma; whole pods can be ground at home for maximum flavor.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom
  • 3/4 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar (for rolling)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon (for rolling)

I first made these soft cinnamon sugar cookies on a chilly autumn afternoon when I wanted something comforting but a little different from the usual. The idea to add a pinch of cardamom came from a spice blend I’d been experimenting with, and it instantly elevated the classic cinnamon sugar flavor into something a bit more exotic and cozy. What sets this recipe apart is how soft and tender the cookies stay even after cooling, thanks to the balance of butter and a touch of baking powder. Plus, rolling the dough balls in a cinnamon-sugar mix before baking adds that perfect crunchy, sweet coating that makes every bite feel special. I've had my fair share of cinnamon sugar cookies that turn out dry or crumbly, but this one nails the texture every time — soft edges, a gentle chew, and just enough spice to keep you coming back for more. They’re great with a cup of tea or coffee, especially when you want to slow down and savor a little homemade comfort. I think you’ll love how easy they come together and how everyone always asks for the recipe once they try them!

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. This ensures even baking and prevents sticking. Preparing your baking sheets ahead is key because once the dough is ready, you want to bake the cookies immediately to keep their soft texture.

    ⏱ 10✓ Oven is fully preheated and baking sheets are ready before dough shaping
  2. Step 2

    In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, salt, ground cinnamon, and cardamom. Sifting helps aerate the flour and evenly distributes the spices, which prevents clumping and ensures every cookie has a consistent flavor.

    ⏱ 5✓ Dry ingredients are combined into a smooth, uniform mixture with no lumps
  3. Step 3

    In a large bowl, beat the softened butter and granulated sugar with an electric mixer on medium speed until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. This aeration creates a tender crumb in the cookies and helps the sugar dissolve properly, giving a nice texture and sweetness.

    ⏱ 4✓ Butter mixture looks pale, fluffy, and noticeably increased in volume
  4. Step 4

    Add the egg and vanilla extract to the butter mixture and beat until fully incorporated. The egg adds moisture and binds the dough, while vanilla enhances the overall flavor. Make sure to scrape down the sides to mix everything evenly.

    ⏱ 2✓ Mixture is smooth and homogenous with no streaks of egg or butter
  5. Step 5

    Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients, mixing on low speed just until combined. Overmixing here can develop gluten and make the cookies tough, so stop as soon as you see no streaks of flour.

    ⏱ 3✓ Dough comes together in a soft, slightly sticky ball without dry patches
  6. Step 6

    In a small bowl, combine the 1/4 cup granulated sugar and 1 teaspoon cinnamon for rolling. This coating adds a sweet, crunchy crust that contrasts beautifully with the soft interior of the cookies, giving them that classic cinnamon sugar cookie appeal.

    ⏱ 2✓ Cinnamon sugar mixture is evenly blended and ready for rolling
  7. Step 7

    Using a tablespoon or small cookie scoop, form dough balls and roll each in the cinnamon sugar mixture until fully coated. This step not only adds flavor but creates a lovely texture on the cookie surface after baking.

    ⏱ 5✓ All dough balls are evenly coated with cinnamon sugar and ready to bake
  8. Step 8

    Place the coated dough balls on the prepared baking sheets about 2 inches apart to allow for spreading. Baking with enough space prevents the cookies from merging and ensures even heat circulation for uniform baking.

    ⏱ 3✓ Dough balls are spaced evenly without crowding the pan
  9. Step 9

    Bake in the preheated oven for 10-12 minutes, or until the edges look set and slightly golden but the centers still appear soft. This timing keeps the cookies tender and chewy rather than crisp. Remove from oven and let cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack.

    ⏱ 12✓ Edges are lightly golden and centers look set but soft to the touch

Variations

  • Chocolate Chip Cinnamon Sugar Cookies: Add 1 cup of mini chocolate chips to the dough for a melty chocolate surprise that pairs perfectly with the cinnamon and cardamom flavors. The chips add bursts of richness and a fun twist on the classic cookie.
    Fold chips gently into dough after mixing dry and wet ingredients to avoid overmixing and breaking chips.
  • Gluten-Free Soft Cinnamon Sugar Cookies: Swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the cookies soft and chewy while making them safe for gluten-sensitive eaters.
    Adjust baking time slightly if needed and check texture; gluten-free doughs can behave differently.
  • Vegan Cinnamon Sugar Cookies: Replace butter with vegan margarine and use a flax egg (1 tbsp flaxseed meal + 3 tbsp water) instead of a regular egg. These changes keep the cookies soft and flavorful without animal products.
    Chill dough slightly before baking to help it hold shape better without eggs.
  • Spiced Orange Cinnamon Sugar Cookies: Add 1 teaspoon of orange zest to the dough for a bright, citrusy note that complements the warm spices beautifully. It adds freshness and a lovely aroma to the cookies.
    Use finely grated zest and mix into the wet ingredients for even distribution.
  • Pumpkin Cinnamon Sugar Cookies: Incorporate 1/2 cup pumpkin puree into the dough along with an extra 1/4 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg for a fall-inspired treat that’s moist and tender.
    Reduce butter slightly to balance moisture and bake a few minutes longer if needed.

Pro Tips

  • Always cream the butter and sugar until light and fluffy. — Creaming incorporates air into the butter, which helps create a tender and light crumb in the cookie. If you skip this or don’t cream long enough, cookies can turn out dense or greasy. The texture difference is noticeable.
  • Don’t overmix the dough after adding flour. — Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, making cookies tough and chewy instead of soft. Mix just until no flour streaks remain to keep the texture tender and delicate.
  • Use room temperature butter rather than melted butter. — Softened butter traps air when creamed, which contributes to cookie rise and softness. Melted butter results in denser cookies with a different texture because it lacks that aeration.
  • Roll dough balls generously in the cinnamon sugar before baking. — This coating caramelizes during baking to create a sweet, crunchy crust that contrasts deliciously with the soft inside, enhancing both texture and flavor.
  • Space cookies well on the baking sheet. — Leaving at least 2 inches apart allows cookies to spread without merging, ensuring even baking and a nice round shape without overcrowding.
  • Let cookies cool on the baking sheet before transferring to a rack. — Cooling on the sheet lets cookies set gently, preventing them from breaking apart. It also helps preserve their soft texture by allowing residual heat to finish the baking.
  • Use fresh spices for maximum flavor. — Old cinnamon or cardamom loses potency and aroma, resulting in bland cookies. Fresh spices ensure your cookies have that warm, inviting flavor that makes them special.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using butter that’s too cold or melted
    Why: Some recipes call for melted butter, or people rush and use cold butter straight from the fridge, thinking it will speed up prep.
    Fix: Always let butter soften at room temperature until it’s pliable but not oily or melted. This allows for proper creaming with sugar, which is essential for texture and rise.
  • Overmixing the dough after adding flour
    Why: It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid lumps, but this activates gluten, toughening the cookies.
    Fix: Mix just until combined with no visible flour. Stop as soon as the dry ingredients are incorporated to keep cookies tender.
  • Not preheating the oven fully
    Why: Sometimes you start baking before the oven reaches temperature to save time.
    Fix: Wait for the oven to preheat completely. Baking in a cold oven affects how cookies spread and cook, often leading to uneven texture.
  • Crowding the baking sheet
    Why: Trying to bake more cookies at once can be tempting to save time.
    Fix: Space cookies at least 2 inches apart so they bake evenly and don’t stick together.
  • Skipping the cinnamon sugar roll step
    Why: Some skip this step thinking it’s unnecessary or extra work.
    Fix: Don’t skip it! It adds a delightful crunchy coating and extra cinnamon flavor that makes these cookies stand out.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat sources to maintain softness and prevent drying out.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Cookies can be refrigerated in an airtight container for up to 7 days. They may firm up in the fridge but will soften again at room temperature or with a quick 10-second microwave zap.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe bag or container for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature for 30 minutes before serving to restore soft texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm cookies in a microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5 minutes to refresh softness. Avoid overheating, which can dry them out. A light mist of water on a paper towel in the microwave can help retain moisture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the dough up to 24 hours ahead and refrigerate it tightly wrapped. When ready, roll, coat, and bake fresh to keep cookies soft and flavorful.

FAQ

Can I use salted butter instead of unsalted?
Yes, but reduce or omit any added salt in the recipe to avoid an overly salty taste. Unsalted butter gives you better control over seasoning, but salted butter works fine if that's what you have.
What if I don’t have cardamom?
You can omit cardamom or substitute with a small pinch of ground nutmeg or cloves for a similar warm spice note. The cookies will still be delicious but less complex in flavor.
Why are my cookies turning out flat?

How do I keep cookies soft for longer?
Store them in an airtight container with a slice of bread or apple wedge to maintain moisture. Avoid storing in the fridge long term, as it can dry them out.
Can I make the dough ahead of time?
This also helps develop flavors.
Can I freeze baked cookies?
Yes, freeze fully cooled cookies in a single layer, then transfer to a sealed bag or container. Thaw at room temperature before eating. Reheat briefly to regain softness if desired.
What’s the best way to measure flour for this recipe?
Accurate flour measurement is key for soft texture.
Can I substitute brown sugar for white sugar?
You can replace half the granulated sugar with light brown sugar for a deeper, caramel-like flavor and slightly moister texture. Too much brown sugar can change the texture, so keep it balanced.
