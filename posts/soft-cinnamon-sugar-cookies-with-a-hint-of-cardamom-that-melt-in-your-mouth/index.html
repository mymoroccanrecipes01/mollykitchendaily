Soft Cinnamon Sugar Cookies with a Hint of Cardamom for Cozy Snacking

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 17, 2026
15 min Prep Time
12 min Time
24 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These soft cinnamon sugar cookies bring a cozy twist to a classic favorite. The subtle cardamom adds warmth and depth, making them perfect for afternoon tea or a comforting treat anytime you need a little sweetness.

Pro Tips

Always cream the butter and sugar until light and fluffy.
Creaming incorporates air into the butter, which helps create a tender and light crumb in the cookie. If you skip this or don’t cream long enough, cookies can turn out dense or greasy. The texture difference is noticeable.
Don’t overmix the dough after adding flour.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, making cookies tough and chewy instead of soft. Mix just until no flour streaks remain to keep the texture tender and delicate.
Use room temperature butter rather than melted butter.
Softened butter traps air when creamed, which contributes to cookie rise and softness. Melted butter results in denser cookies with a different texture because it lacks that aeration.
Roll dough balls generously in the cinnamon sugar before baking.
This coating caramelizes during baking to create a sweet, crunchy crust that contrasts deliciously with the soft inside, enhancing both texture and flavor.
Space cookies well on the baking sheet.
Leaving at least 2 inches apart allows cookies to spread without merging, ensuring even baking and a nice round shape without overcrowding.
Let cookies cool on the baking sheet before transferring to a rack.
Cooling on the sheet lets cookies set gently, preventing them from breaking apart. It also helps preserve their soft texture by allowing residual heat to finish the baking.
Use fresh spices for maximum flavor.
Old cinnamon or cardamom loses potency and aroma, resulting in bland cookies. Fresh spices ensure your cookies have that warm, inviting flavor that makes them special.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using butter that’s too cold or melted

Why it happens Some recipes call for melted butter, or people rush and use cold butter straight from the fridge, thinking it will speed up prep.

Fix Always let butter soften at room temperature until it’s pliable but not oily or melted. This allows for proper creaming with sugar, which is essential for texture and rise.

Overmixing the dough after adding flour

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid lumps, but this activates gluten, toughening the cookies.

Fix Mix just until combined with no visible flour. Stop as soon as the dry ingredients are incorporated to keep cookies tender.

Not preheating the oven fully

Why it happens Sometimes you start baking before the oven reaches temperature to save time.

Fix Wait for the oven to preheat completely. Baking in a cold oven affects how cookies spread and cook, often leading to uneven texture.

Crowding the baking sheet

Why it happens Trying to bake more cookies at once can be tempting to save time.

Fix Space cookies at least 2 inches apart so they bake evenly and don’t stick together.

Skipping the cinnamon sugar roll step

Why it happens Some skip this step thinking it’s unnecessary or extra work.

Fix Don’t skip it! It adds a delightful crunchy coating and extra cinnamon flavor that makes these cookies stand out.

Variations

Chocolate Chip Cinnamon Sugar Cookies

Add 1 cup of mini chocolate chips to the dough for a melty chocolate surprise that pairs perfectly with the cinnamon and cardamom flavors. The chips add bursts of richness and a fun twist on the classic cookie.

Note: Fold chips gently into dough after mixing dry and wet ingredients to avoid overmixing and breaking chips.

Gluten-Free Soft Cinnamon Sugar Cookies

Swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the cookies soft and chewy while making them safe for gluten-sensitive eaters.

Note: Adjust baking time slightly if needed and check texture; gluten-free doughs can behave differently.

Vegan Cinnamon Sugar Cookies

Replace butter with vegan margarine and use a flax egg (1 tbsp flaxseed meal + 3 tbsp water) instead of a regular egg. These changes keep the cookies soft and flavorful without animal products.

Note: Chill dough slightly before baking to help it hold shape better without eggs.

Spiced Orange Cinnamon Sugar Cookies

Add 1 teaspoon of orange zest to the dough for a bright, citrusy note that complements the warm spices beautifully. It adds freshness and a lovely aroma to the cookies.

Note: Use finely grated zest and mix into the wet ingredients for even distribution.

Pumpkin Cinnamon Sugar Cookies

Incorporate 1/2 cup pumpkin puree into the dough along with an extra 1/4 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg for a fall-inspired treat that’s moist and tender.

Note: Reduce butter slightly to balance moisture and bake a few minutes longer if needed.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat sources to maintain softness and prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Cookies can be refrigerated in an airtight container for up to 7 days. They may firm up in the fridge but will soften again at room temperature or with a quick 10-second microwave zap.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe bag or container for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature for 30 minutes before serving to restore soft texture.

Reheating

Warm cookies in a microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5 minutes to refresh softness. Avoid overheating, which can dry them out. A light mist of water on a paper towel in the microwave can help retain moisture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the dough up to 24 hours ahead and refrigerate it tightly wrapped. When ready, roll, coat, and bake fresh to keep cookies soft and flavorful.

FAQ

Can I use salted butter instead of unsalted?
Yes, but reduce or omit any added salt in the recipe to avoid an overly salty taste. Unsalted butter gives you better control over seasoning, but salted butter works fine if that's what you have.
What if I don’t have cardamom?
You can omit cardamom or substitute with a small pinch of ground nutmeg or cloves for a similar warm spice note. The cookies will still be delicious but less complex in flavor.
Why are my cookies turning out flat?
Also, chilling the dough briefly before baking can help the cookies hold their shape better.
How do I keep cookies soft for longer?
Store them in an airtight container with a slice of bread or apple wedge to maintain moisture. Avoid storing in the fridge long term, as it can dry them out.
Can I make the dough ahead of time?
This also helps develop flavors.
Can I freeze baked cookies?
Yes, freeze fully cooled cookies in a single layer, then transfer to a sealed bag or container. Thaw at room temperature before eating. Reheat briefly to regain softness if desired.
What’s the best way to measure flour for this recipe?
Accurate flour measurement is key for soft texture.
Can I substitute brown sugar for white sugar?
You can replace half the granulated sugar with light brown sugar for a deeper, caramel-like flavor and slightly moister texture. Too much brown sugar can change the texture, so keep it balanced.

What You'll Need

Approximately 24 cookies

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom
  • 3/4 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar (for rolling)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon (for rolling)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. This ensures even baking and prevents sticking. Preparing your baking sheets ahead is key because once the dough is ready, you want to bake the cookies immediately to keep their soft texture.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Oven is fully preheated and baking sheets are ready before dough shaping
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, salt, ground cinnamon, and cardamom. Sifting helps aerate the flour and evenly distributes the spices, which prevents clumping and ensures every cookie has a consistent flavor.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are combined into a smooth, uniform mixture with no lumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the softened butter and granulated sugar with an electric mixer on medium speed until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. This aeration creates a tender crumb in the cookies and helps the sugar dissolve properly, giving a nice texture and sweetness.
⏱ Time: 4
✓ Doneness: Butter mixture looks pale, fluffy, and noticeably increased in volume
Step 4
Add the egg and vanilla extract to the butter mixture and beat until fully incorporated. The egg adds moisture and binds the dough, while vanilla enhances the overall flavor. Make sure to scrape down the sides to mix everything evenly.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth and homogenous with no streaks of egg or butter
Step 5
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients, mixing on low speed just until combined. Overmixing here can develop gluten and make the cookies tough, so stop as soon as you see no streaks of flour.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Dough comes together in a soft, slightly sticky ball without dry patches
Step 6
In a small bowl, combine the 1/4 cup granulated sugar and 1 teaspoon cinnamon for rolling. This coating adds a sweet, crunchy crust that contrasts beautifully with the soft interior of the cookies, giving them that classic cinnamon sugar cookie appeal.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Cinnamon sugar mixture is evenly blended and ready for rolling
Step 7
Using a tablespoon or small cookie scoop, form dough balls and roll each in the cinnamon sugar mixture until fully coated. This step not only adds flavor but creates a lovely texture on the cookie surface after baking.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: All dough balls are evenly coated with cinnamon sugar and ready to bake
Step 8
Place the coated dough balls on the prepared baking sheets about 2 inches apart to allow for spreading. Baking with enough space prevents the cookies from merging and ensures even heat circulation for uniform baking.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Dough balls are spaced evenly without crowding the pan
Step 9
Bake in the preheated oven for 10-12 minutes, or until the edges look set and slightly golden but the centers still appear soft. This timing keeps the cookies tender and chewy rather than crisp. Remove from oven and let cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack.
⏱ Time: 12
✓ Doneness: Edges are lightly golden and centers look set but soft to the touch

Final Thoughts
