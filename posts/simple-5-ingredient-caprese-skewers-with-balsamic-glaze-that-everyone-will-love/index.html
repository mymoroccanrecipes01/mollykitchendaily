Fresh and Easy Caprese Skewers with Balsamic Glaze Drizzle

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 9, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These Caprese skewers are a fresh, no-fuss appetizer with juicy cherry tomatoes, creamy mozzarella, and fragrant basil. The balsamic glaze adds a sweet tang, making them perfect for summer parties or quick snacks.

Introduction

I first made these Caprese skewers at a summer potluck a few years ago, and they were an instant hit. What I love about this recipe is how simple and fresh it is—just a handful of quality ingredients that come together effortlessly. Unlike traditional Caprese salads that need plating, these skewers are perfect for grabbing and munching, making them a fun, casual appetizer. I like to use small mozzarella balls instead of slicing larger cheese because it’s less messy and looks so cute on each skewer. The balsamic glaze is my secret weapon here—it adds a lovely sweetness and depth without overpowering the fresh flavors. Plus, it’s so quick to assemble that I can whip these up in about 15 minutes, which is fantastic when I need a last-minute snack or party dish. They’re light but satisfying, with juicy tomatoes, creamy cheese, and fragrant basil all in one bite. If you haven’t tried making skewers instead of a salad, I highly recommend it—so much less fuss and just as delicious!

What You'll Need

Approximately 20 skewers, serves 4

  • 20 cherry tomatoes, ripe and firm
  • 20 small fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini), drained
  • 20 fresh basil leaves, whole
  • 2 tablespoons balsamic glaze
  • 20 wooden skewers or toothpicks

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by rinsing the cherry tomatoes under cold water and patting them dry with a kitchen towel. Drying is important because excess moisture can make the skewers soggy and the balsamic glaze won’t stick well. Choose tomatoes that are firm but ripe to ensure they hold up on the skewer and burst with flavor when you bite into them.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomatoes are clean, dry, and firm to the touch
Step 2
Drain the fresh mozzarella balls thoroughly using a fine mesh sieve or paper towels. Removing excess liquid prevents the cheese from making the skewers watery and helps the balsamic glaze cling nicely. If your mozzarella is too wet, it can dilute the flavors and make the presentation less appealing.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mozzarella balls feel slightly firm and not dripping
Step 3
Lay out your wooden skewers or toothpicks on a clean surface. Thread one cherry tomato onto each skewer first. Starting with the tomato helps anchor the skewer and creates a nice balance of flavors and textures with the following ingredients.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomatoes are securely on skewers without slipping
Step 4
Next, add a folded fresh basil leaf onto each skewer. Folding the basil helps it fit nicely and releases its aroma. Fresh basil is key here—it should be vibrant and not wilted, as it adds a bright herbal note that complements the tomato and cheese perfectly.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Basil leaves are fresh and firmly placed on skewers
Step 5
Follow the basil with one mozzarella ball per skewer. Make sure the cheese is secure but not squished, so it retains its shape and creamy texture. The mozzarella provides a mild, milky contrast to the tart tomato and fragrant basil.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mozzarella balls are intact and evenly spaced on skewers
Step 6
Repeat the pattern if your skewers are long enough or leave as is for bite-sized snacks. The layering order helps each bite have a balanced mix of all ingredients, ensuring every mouthful is flavorful.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers have a pleasing, balanced arrangement
Step 7
Arrange the skewers on a serving platter. Drizzle the balsamic glaze evenly over the top just before serving. The glaze adds a glossy finish and a sweet tang that elevates the entire dish. Avoid drizzling too early to keep the basil fresh and prevent sogginess.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balsamic glaze lightly coats skewers without pooling
Step 8
Optional: sprinkle a little flaky sea salt or freshly cracked black pepper over the skewers for an extra flavor boost. These simple touches can really enhance the taste and make the appetizer feel a bit more special.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Seasoning is evenly distributed without overpowering
Step 9
Serve immediately or chill for up to an hour before guests arrive. Serving chilled keeps the ingredients fresh and refreshing, but avoid letting them sit too long or the basil may wilt and the mozzarella soften excessively.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Skewers are fresh, vibrant, and ready to enjoy

Variations

Add Prosciutto for a Savory Twist

Wrap a small piece of prosciutto around the mozzarella before skewering to add a salty, savory layer. This variation adds richness and pairs beautifully with the balsamic glaze, creating a more indulgent appetizer experience.

Note: Use thinly sliced prosciutto and avoid overcrowding to keep the skewer stable. The saltiness of prosciutto balances the sweet glaze nicely.

Use Pesto Instead of Basil Leaves

Swap fresh basil with a small dollop of pesto on the mozzarella or tomato for a more intense herb flavor and creamy texture. This changes the flavor profile to a richer, garlicky taste while keeping the fresh vibe.

Note: Apply pesto sparingly to avoid sogginess. Serve immediately to maintain fresh textures.

Make It Vegan with Marinated Tofu

Replace mozzarella with small cubes of marinated tofu for a vegan-friendly version. Use a simple marinade of olive oil, lemon, and herbs to mimic the creamy texture and mild flavor of cheese.

Note: Press tofu well before marinating to reduce moisture. This helps firm up the cubes and prevents watery skewers.

Add Fresh Fruit Like Strawberries

Include a slice of strawberry on each skewer for a sweet, fruity contrast that complements the balsamic glaze. This adds a surprising pop of color and flavor, making the appetizer even more refreshing and summery.

Note: Use firm, ripe strawberries and slice thinly to keep the skewer balanced and easy to eat.

Drizzle with Honey and Sprinkle Cracked Pepper

For a sweeter take, drizzle honey instead of balsamic glaze and add freshly cracked black pepper. This creates a unique sweet-spicy combo that’s unexpected but delicious.

Note: Apply honey lightly to prevent stickiness and serve immediately for best texture.

Pro Tips

Use ripe but firm cherry tomatoes to avoid soggy skewers.
Ripe tomatoes bring sweetness and juiciness, but if they’re too soft, they’ll release excess liquid, making the skewers soggy and messy. Firm tomatoes hold their shape better and provide a pleasant pop when bitten.
Drain mozzarella balls thoroughly before assembling.
Mozzarella can hold a lot of moisture, which if not drained, will drip and dilute the balsamic glaze. Proper draining keeps the cheese creamy but not watery, preserving the overall texture and flavor balance.
Fold basil leaves instead of piercing them flat.
Folding basil helps release its essential oils and aromas, enhancing flavor. It also fits better on the skewer, preventing leaves from tearing or slipping off, which keeps the presentation neat.
Drizzle balsamic glaze just before serving.
Applying the glaze too early can cause the basil to wilt and the mozzarella to soften excessively. Drizzling at the last moment preserves freshness and keeps the glaze looking glossy and appetizing.
Serve skewers slightly chilled for the best texture.
Chilling keeps the tomatoes firm, the mozzarella cool and creamy, and the basil fresh. However, avoid serving too cold as it can dull flavors; room temperature for 10-15 minutes before serving works well.
Use toothpicks or short skewers for easy snacking.
Smaller skewers make these bite-sized and convenient for parties. They also prevent ingredients from sliding off and make handling easier for guests.
Add a sprinkle of flaky sea salt to enhance flavors.
Salt brings out the natural sweetness of the tomatoes and balances the creaminess of mozzarella. Flaky salt adds a nice texture and bursts of flavor in every bite.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using underripe or overly soft tomatoes

Why it happens Sometimes we grab whatever tomatoes are on hand without checking ripeness, assuming all cherry tomatoes are the same. This can lead to soggy or bland skewers.

Fix Choose firm, ripe cherry tomatoes with a bright color and slight give. If tomatoes are too soft, they’ll release too much juice and make the skewers messy. Avoid green or very soft tomatoes for the best texture and flavor.

Skipping draining mozzarella

Why it happens It’s easy to overlook draining fresh mozzarella, especially when in a hurry, assuming it’s dry enough to use directly.

Fix Always drain mozzarella balls on paper towels or in a sieve for at least 10 minutes before assembling. This removes excess whey that can water down the dish and prevent the balsamic glaze from sticking.

Drizzling balsamic glaze too early

Why it happens Sometimes we add dressing or glaze before guests arrive to save time, not realizing it affects freshness.

Fix Wait to drizzle the balsamic glaze right before serving. This keeps the basil vibrant and prevents the glaze from soaking into the cheese and tomatoes, preserving texture and appearance.

Using wilted or bruised basil leaves

Why it happens Basil is delicate and wilts quickly, so sometimes older leaves are used without checking freshness.

Fix Pick fresh, bright green basil leaves without dark spots or bruises. Store basil properly wrapped in a damp paper towel in the fridge or use it the day you buy it to maintain flavor and appearance.

Overstuffing skewers with too many ingredients

Why it happens Trying to make skewers look fuller can lead to overcrowding, which makes them hard to eat and less visually appealing.

Fix Keep it simple: one tomato, one basil leaf, one mozzarella ball per skewer works best. This balance ensures each bite is flavorful and easy to handle.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Caprese skewers can be safely kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours, especially in cooler environments. Keep them covered loosely with plastic wrap or a clean towel to prevent drying out or contamination.

Refrigerator

Store skewers in an airtight container lined with paper towels to absorb moisture, for up to 1 day. The basil may wilt and mozzarella soften slightly, so it’s best to drizzle balsamic glaze just before serving again if refrigerated.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended for Caprese skewers as the fresh ingredients, especially tomatoes and basil, lose texture and flavor upon thawing. Mozzarella also changes consistency after freezing.

Reheating

These skewers are best served cold or at room temperature. Reheating is not necessary and may negatively impact the fresh flavors and textures.

Make Ahead

You can prep the ingredients (wash tomatoes, drain mozzarella, wash basil) 1-2 days ahead and store separately. Assemble the skewers and drizzle balsamic glaze just before serving to keep them fresh and vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use regular tomatoes instead of cherry tomatoes?
You can, but cherry tomatoes are preferred because of their bite-sized shape and sweetness. If using larger tomatoes, cut them into small, uniform pieces to maintain easy-to-eat skewers. Larger tomato pieces may release more juice, making the skewers soggier.
What if I don’t have balsamic glaze? Can I use regular balsamic vinegar?
Regular balsamic vinegar is more liquid and acidic, so it won’t provide the same sweet, syrupy coating. You can simmer balsamic vinegar with a bit of sugar until it thickens to make your own glaze, or drizzle a small amount of vinegar just before serving for some acidity.
How long can I prepare these skewers in advance?
It’s best to assemble the skewers no more than a few hours before serving. The basil tends to wilt and the mozzarella can soften if kept too long. If prepping early, store ingredients separately and assemble just before guests arrive.
Can I substitute mozzarella with another cheese?
Fresh mozzarella is ideal for its creamy texture and mild flavor. You could try bocconcini or even small balls of fresh goat cheese, but harder cheeses won’t give the same soft bite. Keep in mind some cheeses have stronger flavors that may overpower the other ingredients.
What’s the best way to store leftover skewers?
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 24 hours. The texture of basil and mozzarella may change, so it’s best to consume them quickly. Avoid drizzling balsamic glaze until ready to serve again to keep freshness.
Can I add other herbs or greens instead of basil?
Basil is traditional and adds a sweet, peppery aroma. However, you could try fresh mint or oregano for a different twist. Just be sure the herb complements the tomatoes and cheese rather than overpowering them.
Is it necessary to use wooden skewers?
Wooden skewers or toothpicks are ideal for easy serving and eating. Metal skewers can be used but may be less convenient for guests and can conduct heat if served warm. Wooden skewers also add a rustic touch to presentation.
How do I make balsamic glaze at home?
Simmer 1 cup of balsamic vinegar with 2 tablespoons sugar over medium-low heat until it reduces by half and thickens to a syrupy consistency, about 15-20 minutes. Let it cool before drizzling. This homemade glaze tastes fresh and has a nice balance of sweet and tangy.
