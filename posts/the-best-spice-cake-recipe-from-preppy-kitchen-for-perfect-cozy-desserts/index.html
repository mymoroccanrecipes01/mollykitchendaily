Warm and Cozy Spice Cake with Cinnamon and Nutmeg

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 13, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This spice cake is my go-to when I crave something comforting and cozy. The cinnamon and nutmeg blend gives it a special warmth. Perfect for afternoon tea or holiday treats!

Introduction

I first made this spice cake a few years ago after stumbling upon a recipe from Preppy Kitchen, and it quickly became one of my favorite go-to treats when I want something warm and comforting. What really sets this cake apart is the perfect balance of cinnamon and nutmeg — it’s like a cozy hug in every bite. I’ve tried a few spice cakes before, but this one has that lovely moist texture and just the right amount of spice that’s not overpowering but still so flavorful. The trick I learned is to sift the dry ingredients together and to use room temperature butter and eggs; it helps the batter come together smoothly and results in a tender crumb. Plus, using buttermilk adds a subtle tang that lifts the flavors and keeps the cake from feeling too heavy. When it’s done, you’ll have a golden-brown cake with a tender, slightly dense texture that’s perfect with a cup of tea or coffee, especially on a chilly afternoon. My family loves it with a simple dusting of powdered sugar or a light glaze, but honestly, it’s great on its own too. If you’re looking for a crowd-pleaser that’s easy to whip up and fills your kitchen with that irresistible spice aroma, this cake is definitely worth a try.

What You'll Need

One 9-inch round cake, 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and greasing a 9-inch round cake pan with butter or non-stick spray. This step ensures your cake won’t stick and will come out cleanly after baking. Also, line the bottom with parchment paper for extra insurance. Proper oven temperature is crucial here — too hot and the cake might brown too fast on the outside while staying undercooked inside, too cool and it won’t rise properly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated to 350°F and pan greased and lined
Step 2
In a large bowl, sift together the all-purpose flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves. Sifting helps evenly distribute the spices and leavening agents, which means your cake will rise evenly and have a consistent flavor throughout. If you skip this, you might end up with clumps of spices or uneven texture in your cake.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients fully combined and free of lumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, cream the softened butter with the brown sugar until light and fluffy. This usually takes about 3-4 minutes using an electric mixer. Creaming incorporates air into the butter which helps create a tender crumb and lifts the cake. If you rush this step, your cake might turn out dense and heavy.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Butter and sugar mixture is pale, smooth, and fluffy
Step 4
Add the eggs one at a time to the creamed butter and sugar, beating well after each addition. This ensures the eggs are fully incorporated and the batter remains smooth. Adding eggs slowly helps maintain the emulsion and prevents the batter from curdling, which can negatively affect the cake’s texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and slightly thickened without streaks
Step 5
Mix in the vanilla extract to the wet ingredients, stirring until just combined. Vanilla adds depth to the spice flavors and balances the sweetness. Avoid overmixing here to keep the batter light.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Vanilla evenly distributed without overworked batter
Step 6
Alternately add the dry ingredients and buttermilk to the wet mixture, beginning and ending with the dry ingredients. Mix gently between additions just until combined. This alternating method helps maintain the batter’s structure and prevents overmixing, which can make the cake tough. The buttermilk adds moisture and a subtle tang that balances the spices.
⏱ Time: 6 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth, thick, and fully combined without streaks
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan and smooth the top with a spatula. Tap the pan gently on the counter a few times to release any trapped air bubbles. This helps prevent large holes or tunnels in the finished cake and ensures an even crumb.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread and free of air pockets
Step 8
Bake the cake in the preheated oven for 28-32 minutes. Start checking around 28 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center. It’s done when the toothpick comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Avoid opening the oven door too often during baking as it causes temperature fluctuations affecting rise and texture.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown top and clean toothpick test
Step 9
Let the cake cool in the pan for 10-15 minutes before transferring it to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling in the pan allows the cake to set up and prevents it from breaking apart when removed. Once cooled, you can dust with powdered sugar or glaze as desired.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake cool enough to handle and retains its shape

Variations

Maple Glazed Spice Cake

Add a simple maple glaze made from maple syrup and powdered sugar to drizzle over the cooled cake. This adds a sweet, rich layer that complements the spices beautifully. It’s perfect for fall gatherings or brunch.

Note: Glaze should be thin enough to drizzle but thick enough to hold; adjust powdered sugar quantity accordingly.

Spice Cake with Chopped Walnuts

Fold in 1/2 cup of toasted chopped walnuts into the batter before baking for added crunch and nuttiness. This variation adds texture and a lovely contrast to the tender cake.

Note: Toast nuts lightly to enhance flavor; avoid overmixing batter after adding nuts.

Vegan Spice Cake

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a non-dairy milk with a tablespoon of vinegar instead of buttermilk. Use vegan butter or coconut oil for fat. Expect a slightly denser but still flavorful result.

Note: Mix flax eggs well and let sit before adding; monitor baking time as it may vary slightly.

Spice Cake with Orange Zest

Add 1 tablespoon of fresh orange zest to the batter to brighten the warm spices with a citrusy note. This twist adds freshness and a subtle complexity to the flavor profile.

Note: Add zest with the vanilla extract to distribute evenly.

Gluten-Free Spice Cake

Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend with xanthan gum to replace regular flour. The cake will be slightly denser but still moist and flavorful. Perfect for those avoiding gluten without sacrificing taste.

Note: Ensure blend is suitable for baking; avoid overmixing to prevent tough texture.

Pro Tips

Always use room temperature ingredients like butter, eggs, and buttermilk.
Room temperature ingredients blend more evenly, creating a smooth batter that traps air better, resulting in a lighter, fluffier cake. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, impacting texture.
Sift your dry ingredients together before adding to the wet mixture.
Sifting ensures the spices and leavening agents are evenly distributed, preventing clumps and ensuring uniform rising and flavor throughout the cake.
Don’t overmix after adding the flour mixture.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which toughens the cake and results in a dense texture rather than tender and moist.
Use fresh spices or freshly ground for maximum flavor.
Spices lose potency over time. Freshly ground spices release more essential oils, resulting in a more aromatic and flavorful cake.
Tap the pan on the counter before baking.
Tapping releases trapped air bubbles in the batter, preventing large holes and ensuring an even crumb.
Check doneness with a toothpick starting at 28 minutes.
Ovens vary, so testing early prevents overbaking, which dries out the cake.
Cool the cake in the pan before removing.
This prevents the cake from breaking apart and allows it to set properly for cleaner slices.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using cold butter and eggs straight from the fridge

Why it happens Many assume cold ingredients are fine or forget to prep ahead, but cold butter doesn’t cream properly and cold eggs don’t incorporate well.

Fix Plan ahead to take butter and eggs out 30-60 minutes before baking. This ensures smooth creaming and better batter consistency, resulting in a lighter cake.

Skipping sifting of dry ingredients

Why it happens It feels like an unnecessary step to some, especially when in a hurry.

Fix Always sift spices and leavening agents with flour to avoid clumping and uneven rising, ensuring a uniform texture and flavor.

Overmixing the batter after adding dry ingredients

Why it happens Eager to finish, some mix quickly and thoroughly, not realizing this toughens the cake.

Fix Mix gently until just combined. Stop as soon as you don’t see flour streaks to maintain a tender crumb.

Opening the oven door frequently during baking

Why it happens Curiosity or impatience to check doneness.

Fix Check doneness only near the end of baking to avoid temperature drops that cause uneven rising or sinking.

Not greasing and lining the pan properly

Why it happens Sometimes skipped to save time or effort.

Fix Grease and line the pan with parchment paper to ensure the cake releases easily and keeps its shape.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cake covered loosely with a cake dome or plastic wrap at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Keep the cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. It may firm up slightly but will stay moist. Bring to room temperature before serving or warm gently in the microwave for 10-15 seconds.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and then in aluminum foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and then bring to room temperature before serving for best texture.

Reheating

Reheat slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or warm in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes, covered loosely with foil to prevent drying. Avoid overheating to keep moistness.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bring to room temperature before baking to ensure even rising. The baked cake can be made 1-2 days ahead and stored covered at room temperature or refrigerated.

FAQ

Can I substitute regular milk for buttermilk?
Yes! You can make a quick buttermilk substitute by adding 1 tablespoon of lemon juice or white vinegar to 1 cup of regular milk. Let it sit for 5 minutes until it curdles slightly. This acidity is important to react with baking soda for the cake to rise properly and stay tender.
What if I don’t have all the spices?
If you’re missing some spices like cloves or nutmeg, you can use just cinnamon or a combination you have on hand. The cake will still taste great, though the flavor will be less complex. Adjust amounts to your taste and avoid overpowering with just one spice.
How do I know when the cake is done baking?
Use a toothpick inserted into the center of the cake. When it comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs, the cake is done. Also, the top should be golden brown and spring back lightly when touched. Avoid opening the oven too soon to prevent sinking.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend that includes xanthan gum for structure. The texture might be slightly denser, but the flavor will remain delicious. Make sure to mix just until combined to avoid toughness.
Why is my cake dense or heavy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter, using cold ingredients, or inaccurate measuring of leavening agents. Be gentle when combining ingredients, use room temperature butter and eggs, and measure flour correctly by spooning into the cup and leveling off.
Can I add nuts or fruit to the cake?
Yes! Adding chopped toasted nuts like walnuts or pecans adds great texture. Dried fruit like raisins or chopped dates also work well. Fold them in gently at the end of mixing to avoid overworking the batter.
How should I store leftover cake?
Store leftover cake in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze tightly wrapped for up to 3 months. Bring to room temperature before serving for best taste.
Is this cake suitable for freezing?
Yes, this spice cake freezes well. Wrap it tightly in plastic wrap and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge and let it come to room temperature before serving. You can also freeze individual slices for convenience.
