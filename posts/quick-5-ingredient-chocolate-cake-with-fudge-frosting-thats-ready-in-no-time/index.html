Simple 5-Ingredient Chocolate Cake with Fudge Frosting for Any Occasion

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 30 min cook 8 servings

This chocolate cake is a lifesaver when you crave rich, fudgy goodness without the fuss. Made with just five ingredients and topped with a luscious fudge frosting, it’s perfect for quick celebrations or cozy nights at home.

Why This Works

This simple chocolate cake recipe succeeds because of the balance between ingredients and the techniques used. The all-purpose flour provides structure through gluten development, but by mixing gently and not overworking the batter, the cake remains tender rather than tough. The cocoa powder adds deep chocolate flavor and also contributes to the cake's moist texture by absorbing some moisture.

The use of vegetable oil instead of butter keeps the cake moist and soft since oil remains liquid at room temperature, unlike butter which can solidify. Eggs act as a binding agent and provide richness, while the baking powder helps the cake rise, creating a light crumb. The key to a tender crumb is not overmixing the batter, which can make the cake dense.

The fudge frosting is a simple emulsion of butter, cocoa powder, powdered sugar, and milk. Melting the butter and stirring in cocoa powder creates a smooth chocolate base, and gradually adding powdered sugar and milk helps achieve a creamy texture. This frosting complements the cake perfectly by adding moisture and richness without overpowering the chocolate flavor of the cake. Cooling the cake completely before frosting prevents the frosting from melting and ensures a neat presentation.

Overall, the recipe balances ease with flavor by focusing on ingredient quality and basic baking principles. The result is a moist, tender cake with a rich chocolate flavor and a luscious fudge frosting that feels special but requires minimal effort.

  • Choose a good-quality all-purpose flour for structure. It contains enough protein to develop gluten that holds the cake together but not so much as to make it tough. Sifting the flour removes lumps and aerates it, which helps create a lighter cake crumb. Avoid using self-rising flour here as the recipe already includes baking powder.
  • Use natural unsweetened cocoa powder for deep chocolate flavor. It’s important to sift it with the flour to prevent clumps in the batter. Cocoa powder also slightly acidifies the batter, which can react with baking powder to help the cake rise. Dutch-processed cocoa can be used but may require adjusting leavening agents.
  • Vegetable oil keeps the cake moist and tender because it remains liquid at room temperature. This contrasts with butter, which solidifies and can make cakes firmer. Neutral-flavored oils like canola or sunflower work well. Avoid strongly flavored oils as they can affect the chocolate taste.
  • Eggs provide structure, moisture, and richness. They act as emulsifiers, helping the wet and dry ingredients combine smoothly. Using large eggs at room temperature ensures they mix evenly and help trap air for a light texture. Cold eggs can cause uneven mixing and denser cakes.
  • Baking powder is a chemical leavening agent that releases carbon dioxide bubbles during baking, which makes the cake rise and become fluffy. Using fresh baking powder is important — expired powder can result in a dense, flat cake. The right amount balances rise without leaving a metallic taste.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • For the fudge frosting:
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter
  • 1/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 2 cups powdered sugar, sifted
  • 2 tablespoons milk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and greasing an 8-inch round cake pan. This ensures your cake won’t stick and will come out cleanly. Greasing and lightly flouring the pan or lining it with parchment paper works well. Preheating is important so the cake bakes evenly from the start, giving it a nice rise and tender crumb.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Oven fully preheated and pan ready with no dry spots
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, sift together the flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, salt, and sugar. Sifting helps to combine the dry ingredients evenly and breaks up any lumps, which is crucial for a smooth cake batter and even texture. Mix gently but thoroughly to avoid pockets of dry ingredients.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dry ingredients are well combined with no clumps visible
  3. Step 3

    In a separate bowl, whisk together the eggs, vegetable oil, and milk until smooth. This wet mixture adds moisture and richness to the cake. Whisking well ensures the eggs incorporate air, helping the cake rise and become fluffy. If you add the wet ingredients too quickly to the dry, the batter can become lumpy, so pour slowly.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Wet ingredients are fully combined and smooth
  4. Step 4

    Pour the wet ingredients into the dry mix and gently fold together using a spatula or wooden spoon. Avoid overmixing here — stir just until combined to keep the batter light. Overmixing can develop gluten and make the cake dense or tough. The batter should be thick but pourable.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Batter is homogenous with no streaks of flour or dry spots
  5. Step 5

    Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan and smooth the surface with a spatula. This helps the cake bake evenly and rise uniformly. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any large air bubbles that could cause holes or cracks.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Batter evenly spread with no air pockets
  6. Step 6

    Bake the cake for 28-30 minutes, checking for doneness by inserting a toothpick into the center. It should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Baking too long can dry the cake, while underbaking leaves it gooey. The edges will start to pull away slightly from the pan when done.

    ⏱ 28-30 minutes✓ Toothpick comes out mostly clean with moist crumbs
  7. Step 7

    While the cake bakes, start the fudge frosting by melting butter in a saucepan over low heat. Stir in cocoa powder until smooth, then remove from heat. Slowly whisk in powdered sugar, alternating with milk, until thick and creamy. Add vanilla extract last. The key is to add milk gradually to get the right frosting consistency — too much makes it runny, too little makes it stiff.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Frosting is smooth, glossy, and spreadable
  8. Step 8

    Once the cake is baked, let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling fully before frosting is essential; if the cake is warm, the frosting will melt and slide off. Patience here ensures a neat, attractive finish.

    ⏱ 40 minutes✓ Cake is completely cool to the touch
  9. Step 9

    Spread the fudge frosting evenly over the cooled cake using a spatula or knife. You can add swirls or keep it smooth — either way, the frosting adds rich flavor and a beautiful finish. Chill briefly if you want the frosting to set before slicing.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Frosting evenly coated with a glossy finish

Variations

  • Gluten-Free Chocolate Cake: Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. The texture will be slightly different — a bit denser but still moist and delicious. Perfect for gluten-sensitive friends!
    Gluten-free blends absorb moisture differently; you might need to add a tablespoon of milk or oil for the right batter consistency.
  • Vegan Chocolate Cake: Replace eggs with flaxseed meal mixed with water (1 tbsp flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg). Use plant-based milk and vegan butter for the frosting. The cake will be a little denser but still rich and chocolaty.
    Flax eggs don’t trap as much air, so the cake may rise less; be gentle when mixing to maintain tenderness.
  • Espresso-Infused Chocolate Cake: Add 1 tablespoon of instant espresso powder to the dry ingredients for a mocha twist. It enhances the chocolate flavor without making the cake taste like coffee.
    Dissolve espresso powder thoroughly to avoid gritty texture; it blends well with cocoa powder.
  • Chocolate Cake with Raspberry Filling: Slice the cooled cake horizontally and spread raspberry jam or fresh raspberries mixed with sugar between layers before frosting. The tartness brightens the rich chocolate flavor.
    Ensure the filling isn’t too wet to avoid soggy layers; a thicker jam or drained berries work best.
  • Nutty Chocolate Cake: Fold chopped toasted walnuts or pecans into the batter before baking for added crunch and flavor. It adds texture contrast and a nutty aroma.
    Toast nuts lightly to enhance flavor but avoid burning; fold gently to keep batter airy.

Pro Tips

  • Sift your dry ingredients together before mixing. — Sifting breaks up clumps and aerates the flour and cocoa powder, which helps create a lighter, more even crumb. It also ensures the baking powder is evenly distributed, preventing uneven rising or dense spots.
  • Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients combine. — Overmixing develops too much gluten, which makes the cake tough and chewy instead of tender and soft. Stir just until no dry flour remains to keep the crumb light and fluffy.
  • Cool the cake completely before frosting. — Applying frosting to a warm cake causes it to melt and slide off, ruining the finish. Cooling allows the crumb to firm up, making it easier to spread frosting evenly for a polished look.
  • Use room temperature eggs and milk for best mixing. — Cold ingredients can cause the batter to seize or mix unevenly, leading to lumps or dense texture. Room temperature ingredients blend smoothly and help trap air for a lighter cake.
  • Tap the cake pan on the counter before baking. — This releases large air bubbles trapped in the batter that can cause holes or uneven texture in the cake. It helps the cake bake evenly and look smooth on top.
  • Use a toothpick to check doneness near the end of baking. — This simple test prevents overbaking, which dries out the cake. The toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter for perfect moistness.
  • Gradually add milk when making the fudge frosting. — Adding milk slowly helps control frosting consistency. Too much milk makes it runny, while too little makes it stiff and hard to spread. Adjust to get a creamy, spreadable texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping sifting dry ingredients
    Why: Many cooks want to save time and skip sifting, assuming it’s optional.
    Fix: Always sift flour, cocoa powder, and baking powder together to avoid lumps and ensure even mixing. It makes a big difference in texture and rise.
  • Overmixing batter after adding wet ingredients
    Why: It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to get a smooth batter, but this develops gluten.
    Fix: Mix just until combined. Stop when you see no dry streaks. This keeps the cake tender and prevents toughness.
  • Applying frosting on warm cake
    Why: Impatience or not allowing enough cooling time.
    Fix: Wait until the cake is completely cool before frosting to avoid melting and sliding frosting.
  • Using expired baking powder
    Why: Baking powder loses potency over time and is often forgotten in the pantry.
    Fix: Test baking powder by adding a little to hot water—if it bubbles vigorously, it’s fresh. Replace if not.
  • Baking at incorrect oven temperature
    Why: Ovens can be off or preheating steps skipped.
    Fix: Use an oven thermometer to ensure accurate temperature. Preheat fully before baking for even rise and texture.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Store the frosted cake covered loosely with a cake dome or plastic wrap at room temperature for up to 2 days. This keeps the cake moist but avoid warm or humid environments which can cause frosting to melt.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store the cake in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days. The cake may firm up and the frosting harden slightly; allow it to come to room temperature before serving for best flavor and texture.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap the unfrosted cake layers tightly in plastic wrap and aluminum foil, then freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and bring to room temperature before frosting and serving to maintain moisture and texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: If desired, warm individual slices gently in the microwave for 10-15 seconds to soften the cake and frosting. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out. Adding a small splash of milk before warming can help retain moisture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Bake the cake layers a day ahead and store wrapped at room temperature or refrigerated. Prepare the frosting separately and assemble the cake the day of serving for the freshest texture and appearance.

FAQ

Can I use butter instead of vegetable oil?
Yes, you can substitute melted butter for vegetable oil in equal amounts, but the cake will be slightly denser and have a richer flavor. Oil keeps cakes moister because it stays liquid at room temperature, so using butter might make the cake firmer once cooled.
Why is my cake dense and not fluffy?
Dense cake often results from overmixing the batter, using old baking powder, or baking at the wrong temperature. Mix just until combined, check your leavening agent’s freshness, and make sure your oven is properly preheated for the best rise.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend with xanthan gum for best results. The texture might be a bit different—usually denser—but it will still be moist and delicious.
How do I store leftover cake?
Keep leftover cake in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. If refrigerated, let it come to room temperature before eating to soften the frosting and crumb.
Can I freeze the frosted cake?
It’s better to freeze the cake layers unfrosted to avoid texture changes in the frosting. Wrap layers tightly and freeze for up to 3 months, then thaw before frosting and serving.
What’s the best way to check if the cake is done?
Insert a toothpick into the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. If it’s clean or wet, bake a few minutes longer and check again.
Can I use a different type of cocoa powder?
Natural unsweetened cocoa powder is best for this recipe. Dutch-processed cocoa can be used but may affect the rise due to its neutral pH, possibly requiring adjustments to the baking powder or adding baking soda.
How do I make the frosting less sweet?
Reduce the powdered sugar slightly or add a pinch of salt to balance the sweetness. You can also add a bit more cocoa powder for a richer, less sweet chocolate flavor.
