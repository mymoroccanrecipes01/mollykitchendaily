Rich and Creamy Vegan Chocolate Cheesecake That’s Perfectly Chill and Indulgent

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 12, 2026
15 min Prep Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This vegan chocolate cheesecake is a dreamy, no-bake delight made creamy with cashews and coconut cream. It’s wonderfully rich yet light, making it perfect for warm days or anytime you want a cool, indulgent dessert.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this vegan chocolate cheesecake recipe when I was craving something rich but didn’t want to heat up the kitchen with the oven. I’d tried other vegan cheesecakes before, but they often felt grainy or lacked depth. What makes this one stand out is the creamy blend of soaked cashews and chilled coconut cream, which creates an unbelievably smooth and indulgent texture without any dairy. The trick I learned was soaking the cashews long enough—at least 8 hours—to soften them completely, so the blender can whip them into a velvety base. I also like using maple syrup as a natural sweetener because it adds a subtle complexity that’s missing in plain sugar. When it’s chilled, this cheesecake feels light and refreshing, perfect for summer or anytime you want a dessert that’s both decadent and kind to your stomach. It’s become my go-to for gatherings because it looks impressive but is so simple to throw together. Plus, the chocolate cookie crust adds a lovely crunch that balances the creamy filling beautifully. I hope you enjoy making and sharing it as much as I do!

What You'll Need

One 9-inch cheesecake, 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups raw cashews, soaked overnight and drained
  • 1 cup full-fat coconut cream, chilled
  • 1/2 cup maple syrup
  • 1/4 cup cocoa powder, unsweetened
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups vegan chocolate cookie crumbs (for crust)
  • 4 tablespoons coconut oil, melted (for crust)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preparing your crust. In a medium bowl, combine the vegan chocolate cookie crumbs with the melted coconut oil. Mix thoroughly until the crumbs are evenly coated and start to clump together—this helps the crust hold its shape once pressed into the pan. Press this mixture firmly into the bottom of a 9-inch springform pan, creating an even layer. You want to press down hard enough so the crust is compact but not so much that it’s rock solid. This crust sets the foundation for texture contrast, so don’t skip or rush this step. Once pressed, place the pan in the fridge to chill while you prepare the filling.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Crust is firmly packed and smooth on top, with no loose crumbs
Step 2
Drain the soaked cashews and add them to a high-speed blender along with the chilled coconut cream, maple syrup, cocoa powder, vanilla extract, lemon juice, and salt. Blend on high until completely smooth and creamy, stopping to scrape down the sides as needed. This step is crucial because any grit or lumps will affect the cheesecake’s texture. The lemon juice brightens the flavor and helps balance the sweetness, while the salt enhances the chocolate notes. Take your time blending until the mixture is silky and thick, almost like a mousse.
⏱ Time: 8
✓ Doneness: Filling is smooth, thick, and glossy with no visible cashew bits
Step 3
Pour the filling over the chilled crust and spread evenly with a spatula. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any air bubbles and smooth the top. Cover the cheesecake tightly with plastic wrap or a lid and place it in the fridge to set for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight. This chilling time allows the filling to firm up and the flavors to meld perfectly. Don’t rush this step; a rushed cheesecake will be too soft and won’t slice neatly.
⏱ Time: 240
✓ Doneness: Filling is firm to the touch and holds shape when sliced
Step 4
Once set, carefully remove the cheesecake from the springform pan. Use a sharp knife dipped in hot water to slice clean pieces without cracking the filling. Wipe the knife between cuts for the best results. Serving chilled enhances the creamy texture and flavor, making it a refreshing dessert on warm days. If you want to add a little flair, top with fresh berries or shaved vegan chocolate before serving.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Slices hold their shape and have clean edges
Step 5
If you want a firmer crust, you can bake the crust alone at 350°F (175°C) for 8-10 minutes before adding the filling. This adds a toasty flavor and extra crunch but is optional. Just let the crust cool completely before pouring in the filling. Baking the crust also helps it hold together better if you plan to store the cheesecake for several days.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Crust is golden brown and crisp after baking
Step 6
For best blending results, use a high-power blender or food processor. If your blender isn’t very strong, blend the cashews with a little bit of coconut cream first to loosen them before adding the rest of the ingredients. This prevents the motor from overheating and ensures a smooth texture without lumps.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth and free of grainy bits
Step 7
Adjust the sweetness and cocoa level to your taste. If you like it more chocolatey, add an extra tablespoon of cocoa powder, or if you prefer it sweeter, add a bit more maple syrup. Blend again briefly to incorporate. Taste the filling before pouring it over the crust so you can tweak it perfectly.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Filling tastes balanced and rich, not too bitter or sweet
Step 8
If you want to make this cheesecake nut-free, you can substitute cashews with silken tofu, but the texture will be less creamy and more custard-like. The method remains the same; just blend the tofu with the other filling ingredients until smooth. This is a great option for those with nut allergies but expect a slightly different mouthfeel.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Filling is smooth but less thick than cashew version
Step 9
Before serving, let the cheesecake sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes to soften slightly. This step enhances the creamy texture and makes slicing easier. Too cold and the filling can feel firm and less flavorful; too warm and it may lose shape, so timing here is key.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Cheesecake is soft but holds shape when cut

Variations

Peanut Butter Chocolate Cheesecake

Swirl in 1/2 cup creamy peanut butter into the filling before pouring it over the crust. This adds a rich, nutty flavor that pairs wonderfully with chocolate and gives the cheesecake a marbled effect.

Note: Blend peanut butter thoroughly to avoid clumps and pour immediately to prevent separation.

Berry Topped Vegan Chocolate Cheesecake

Top the cheesecake with fresh raspberries, blueberries, or sliced strawberries right before serving. The tartness of berries balances the rich chocolate and adds a fresh, colorful touch.

Note: Add berries just before slicing to prevent them from releasing too much moisture and softening the crust.

Mint Chocolate Vegan Cheesecake

Add 1 teaspoon peppermint extract to the filling for a refreshing twist. Garnish with chopped vegan dark chocolate and fresh mint leaves for a festive look and flavor.

Note: Peppermint extract is strong—start with less and adjust to taste to avoid overpowering the chocolate.

Nut-Free Silken Tofu Version

Replace soaked cashews with 1 1/2 cups silken tofu blended until smooth. This makes the cheesecake suitable for nut allergies but results in a slightly lighter, custard-like texture.

Note: Silken tofu blends easily but yields a softer set, so chill longer for best firmness.

Gluten-Free Crust Option

Use gluten-free vegan cookies or crushed nuts mixed with coconut oil for the crust. This keeps the dessert free of gluten while maintaining a crunchy base.

Note: Make sure the crust mixture is pressed firmly and check for binding since some gluten-free crumbs are drier.

Pro Tips

Soak cashews overnight for the creamiest filling.
Soaking softens the cashews, allowing your blender to break them down into a smooth, silky texture. Without soaking, you risk a gritty filling that ruins the cheesecake’s mouthfeel.
Use chilled full-fat coconut cream, not milk.
Coconut cream has higher fat content, which solidifies when chilled, helping the cheesecake set without baking. Using milk will result in a runnier filling that won’t hold its shape.
Press the crust firmly into the pan to avoid crumbling.
A compact crust provides a sturdy base that contrasts nicely with the creamy filling. If it’s loose, it will fall apart when sliced, making serving messy.
Blend filling ingredients thoroughly for a silky texture.
Stopping to scrape the blender sides ensures all cashews are fully pureed, preventing graininess and lumps. A smooth filling is key for that classic cheesecake mouthfeel.
Chill the cheesecake at least 4 hours, preferably overnight.
The chilling time allows the filling to firm up and flavors to meld. Cutting too soon results in a soft, unstable cheesecake that won’t slice cleanly.
Warm your knife before slicing for clean cuts.
Dipping the knife in hot water and wiping it between slices prevents sticking and cracking, giving you neat, beautiful pieces.
Add a splash of lemon juice to brighten flavors.
Lemon juice enhances the chocolate and balances the sweetness, preventing the dessert from tasting flat or overly sweet.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not soaking cashews long enough

Why it happens Many underestimate the time needed to soften cashews, assuming a quick rinse is enough.

Fix Plan ahead and soak cashews at least 8 hours or overnight. If short on time, soak in hot water for 1-2 hours but expect less creamy results.

Using coconut milk instead of coconut cream

Why it happens They’re often confused since they come in similar cans, but coconut milk is thinner and less fatty.

Fix Use only full-fat coconut cream, chilling it overnight. This ensures the cheesecake sets properly and has the right richness.

Skipping the chilling time

Why it happens Impatience or underestimating the time it takes to firm up the filling.

Fix Chill the cheesecake for a minimum of 4 hours, preferably overnight, to get a sliceable texture and fully developed flavor.

Pressing the crust too lightly

Why it happens Not realizing how important a firm crust is for structural support.

Fix Press the crust firmly and evenly into the pan, using the bottom of a glass or measuring cup for even pressure.

Using a dull knife for slicing

Why it happens A dull knife drags and cracks the filling, ruining presentation.

Fix Use a sharp knife warmed in hot water and wipe clean between cuts for smooth, clean slices.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cheesecake should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours as it contains perishable ingredients. Always cover it loosely with plastic wrap if serving for a short period.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container or cover tightly with plastic wrap for up to 5 days. The flavor actually improves after a day. Serve chilled for best texture, but let it sit 10-15 minutes at room temp before slicing.

Freezer

Freeze the cheesecake wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then in foil for up to 2 months. To thaw, place in the refrigerator overnight. Avoid thawing at room temp to prevent condensation and soggy crust.

Reheating

No reheating needed. Serve chilled or slightly softened at room temperature. If too firm from the fridge, let it sit out 10-15 minutes before slicing.

Make Ahead

Prepare the crust and blend the filling 1-2 days ahead. Assemble and chill overnight for best flavor and texture. This also saves time on the day of serving.

FAQ

Can I use almond milk instead of coconut cream?
Almond milk is too thin and lacks the fat content needed for a creamy cheesecake filling. Coconut cream provides the richness and structure that almond milk can’t replicate. If you want a lighter version, consider using silken tofu instead of cashews but still keep the coconut cream.
What if I don’t have a high-speed blender?
If your blender isn’t powerful, soak the cashews longer and blend them with a small amount of coconut cream first to loosen them. You may need to blend in batches and scrape down the sides frequently to get a smooth texture.
How do I make sure the cheesecake sets properly?
Chilling time is key. Make sure to let the cheesecake set in the fridge for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight. Also, using full-fat coconut cream and soaked cashews helps the filling firm up naturally without baking.
Can I make this cheesecake nut-free?
Yes! Replace the cashews with silken tofu for a nut-free version. The texture will be a bit lighter and less creamy, but it’s still delicious. Just blend the tofu with the other filling ingredients until smooth.
How long does the cheesecake keep in the fridge?
Stored properly in an airtight container, it keeps well for up to 5 days. The flavor and texture actually improve after sitting a day. Always keep it chilled and covered to prevent it from drying out or absorbing fridge odors.
Can I freeze the cheesecake?
Yes, you can freeze it for up to 2 months. Wrap it tightly in plastic wrap and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw in the fridge overnight before serving. Note that freezing might slightly change the texture but it remains tasty.
What’s the best way to slice the cheesecake?
Use a sharp knife dipped in hot water and wiped clean between cuts to get smooth, clean slices without cracking the filling. Let the cheesecake sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes before slicing to soften slightly.
Can I add flavors like coffee or mint?
Absolutely! Add a teaspoon of espresso powder or peppermint extract to the filling for interesting flavor twists. Just blend well and taste before pouring over the crust to get the balance right.
