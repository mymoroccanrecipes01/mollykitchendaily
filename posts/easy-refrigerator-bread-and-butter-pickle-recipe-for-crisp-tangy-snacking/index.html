Simple Tangy Bread and Butter Pickles Ready in Hours

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 31, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These bread and butter pickles are a quick, tangy snack with just the right touch of sweetness and a subtle mustard seed kick. Perfect for sandwiches or munching anytime you want a little zing!

Introduction

I first tried bread and butter pickles years ago at a summer BBQ, and I was hooked by their perfect sweet and tangy combo. Since then, I've experimented to make my own version that comes together quickly but still has that authentic, slightly spicy kick you expect. What makes my recipe stand out is using fresh cucumbers sliced thin and soaking them briefly in salt to draw out excess water — this keeps the pickles crunchy and prevents sogginess. I also love adding just the right amount of mustard seeds and turmeric to get that classic flavor without overpowering the cucumbers. The whole process only takes a few hours, so you don’t have to wait days to enjoy them. When they’re ready, they’re crisp, flavorful, and perfect on sandwiches or straight from the jar as a snack. Honestly, the first time I tried making these, I forgot to salt the cucumbers, and the pickles turned out mushy — lesson learned! Now I always make sure to soak them properly. This recipe is a great intro to pickling if you’re new to it, and it’s a fun way to elevate your sandwich game or add a little zing to your snack time.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, 4 servings

  • 4 cups thinly sliced cucumbers
  • 1 cup thinly sliced onions
  • 1/4 cup kosher salt
  • 2 cups white vinegar
  • 1 1/2 cups granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon mustard seeds
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1/2 teaspoon celery seeds

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing your cucumbers and onions evenly. This ensures they pickle at the same rate and absorb the brine flavors uniformly. Place them in a large bowl and sprinkle the kosher salt over the slices. Toss everything gently to coat and let it sit for about 1 hour. This step draws out excess moisture from the cucumbers, which is crucial for keeping them crisp during pickling. Avoid skipping this or rushing it because cucumbers that retain too much water end up soggy and lose their crunch.
⏱ Time: 60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers will release water and feel slightly limp but still firm
Step 2
While the cucumbers are salting, combine the white vinegar, granulated sugar, mustard seeds, turmeric, and celery seeds in a saucepan. Bring this mixture to a gentle boil over medium heat, stirring occasionally to dissolve the sugar completely. This hot brine is what infuses the pickles with their signature sweet and tangy flavor. Boiling helps the spices release their oils and meld with the vinegar and sugar, creating a balanced, flavorful pickling liquid.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sugar is fully dissolved and the mixture is aromatic and simmering gently
Step 3
After the cucumbers have sat for an hour, drain the excess water but don’t rinse them; the salt helps flavor and preserve the pickles. Transfer the cucumbers and onions into a clean jar or container. Pour the hot brine over the vegetables, making sure everything is submerged. Sealing the jar now helps the flavors meld quickly. Let it cool to room temperature before sealing the jar tightly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Brine fully covers cucumbers and onions, no floating pieces
Step 4
Place the jar in the refrigerator and let the pickles chill for at least 3 hours before tasting. The cold temperature slows bacterial growth but allows the cucumbers to soak up the brine flavors. The longer they sit, the more intense the flavor becomes. For the best crunch and tang, I recommend eating them within a week.
⏱ Time: 3 hours minimum
✓ Doneness: Pickles have a bright color and a balanced tang when tasted
Step 5
Before serving, give the pickles a gentle stir or shake to redistribute the spices and flavors. This also helps coat every slice evenly. If you notice the pickles are too salty or sour, try rinsing a few slices lightly before eating. The balance of sweet, sour, and spice should be harmonious, not overwhelming.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Spices are evenly distributed and pickles taste balanced
Step 6
Use these bread and butter pickles to brighten up sandwiches, burgers, or even as a tangy snack on their own. Their crisp texture adds a refreshing contrast to rich or savory dishes. Keep in mind that these are refrigerator pickles, so they won’t last as long as canned ones but are easier and faster to make.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Pickles maintain crispness and flavor when used as a topping or snack
Step 7
If you want to store the pickles longer, use sterilized jars and keep them refrigerated. Always use a clean fork or tongs to remove pickles to prevent contamination. This simple habit keeps your pickles fresh and safe to eat for up to two weeks.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Pickles remain fresh without signs of spoilage
Step 8
Feel free to experiment by adding sliced jalapeños for a spicy kick or a cinnamon stick for warmth. Just add these extras to the brine before boiling so the flavors infuse properly. Adjusting spices lets you customize the pickles to your taste.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Additional spices are fully infused and complement the base flavor
Step 9
Always taste your pickles before serving and adjust sweetness or acidity by adding a bit of sugar or vinegar if needed. This final tweak lets you perfect the balance based on your preferences or the cucumbers you used, as freshness can vary.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Pickles have a balanced sweet and sour flavor tailored to your liking

Variations

Spicy Jalapeño Bread and Butter Pickles

Add thinly sliced jalapeños to the cucumber and onion mix before salting. The spicy heat complements the sweet and sour brine, giving your pickles a lively kick. Perfect if you love a little fire with your tang!

Note: Since jalapeños add moisture, ensure proper salting to maintain crispness. The heat will intensify as pickles sit.

Dill and Garlic Bread and Butter Pickles

Mix in fresh dill sprigs and crushed garlic cloves with your cucumbers and onions for a savory twist. This variation blends the classic dill pickle flavor with the sweet tang of bread and butter pickles for a unique taste experience.

Note: Add garlic and dill before pouring hot brine to infuse flavors evenly.

Vegan-Friendly Low-Sugar Bread and Butter Pickles

Reduce sugar by half and replace with a natural sweetener like maple syrup or agave. This version keeps the tang but lowers sweetness, ideal for those watching sugar intake or vegan sweetener preferences.

Note: Adjust brine heating time slightly to fully dissolve alternative sweeteners.

Apple Cider Vinegar Bread and Butter Pickles

Swap white vinegar for apple cider vinegar to add a fruity, mellow acidity. This subtle change brings warmth and complexity to the pickles, pairing well with fall-inspired meals.

Note: Apple cider vinegar may darken the pickles slightly; flavor will be less sharp but more nuanced.

Quick Refrigerator Bread and Butter Pickles with Honey

Use honey instead of sugar for a natural sweetness and slightly floral note. This quick pickle is ready in hours and perfect for those who want a healthier, all-natural ingredient version.

Note: Heat the brine gently to preserve honey’s delicate flavors without caramelizing.

Pro Tips

Always slice cucumbers uniformly for even pickling.
Uniform slices ensure that each piece absorbs the brine at the same rate, preventing some slices from becoming mushy while others remain under-flavored. Uneven slices can also affect texture and overall bite, so taking the extra time to slice evenly pays off in the final product.
Don’t skip the salting step before pickling.
Salting draws out excess water from the cucumbers, which is critical to prevent sogginess. This step also firms up the cucumbers by tightening their structure. Skipping it can result in watery, limp pickles that don’t have that satisfying crunch we all love.
Use freshly ground spices or whole mustard seeds for best flavor.
Whole or freshly ground spices release their essential oils during heating, adding depth and complexity to the brine. Pre-ground spices that have been sitting for a long time lose potency and can make your pickles taste flat or dull.
Pour hot brine over cucumbers to help flavor absorption.
The heat from the brine helps soften the cucumbers slightly and allows the spices and sugar to dissolve fully into the vegetables. Pouring cold brine would slow flavor infusion and might result in uneven seasoning.
Store pickles in the refrigerator and consume within two weeks.
Refrigeration slows bacterial growth and fermentation, keeping pickles safe and crisp. Homemade refrigerator pickles don’t have preservatives like canned ones, so they won’t last indefinitely. Eating them within two weeks ensures optimal flavor and texture.
Use clean utensils every time you remove pickles from the jar.
Introducing contaminants from dirty utensils can cause spoilage or mold growth. Using clean forks or tongs preserves the pickles’ shelf life and keeps them safe to eat.
Adjust sugar and vinegar balance after tasting if needed.
Cucumbers vary in freshness and water content, which can affect the final flavor. Tasting and tweaking the sweetness or acidity lets you customize the pickles perfectly to your palate, ensuring a balanced sweet-sour profile.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the salting step

Why it happens Sometimes cooks rush or assume salting isn’t necessary, especially when short on time, not realizing its critical role.

Fix Make sure to salt the cucumbers for at least an hour to draw out moisture. This step ensures crispness and better flavor absorption. If pressed for time, use thinner slices to speed up salting.

Using low-acidity vinegar

Why it happens People sometimes use milder vinegar types or homemade vinegar without checking acidity, risking unsafe pickles.

Fix Always use vinegar with at least 5% acidity for proper preservation and flavor. White distilled vinegar is best for consistent results.

Not dissolving sugar completely

Why it happens Rushing the brine heating step can leave sugar grainy or undissolved, leading to uneven sweetness.

Fix Heat the brine until the sugar is fully dissolved and the mixture is simmering gently. Stir often and check for any sugar crystals before pouring.

Overpacking the jar

Why it happens Trying to fit too many cucumbers can prevent brine from reaching all pieces properly.

Fix Leave some space in the jar and ensure all slices are submerged in brine to pickle evenly. Use a utensil to press down gently if needed.

Using dull or uneven knife for slicing

Why it happens A dull knife crushes cucumbers instead of cleanly slicing, damaging texture.

Fix Use a sharp knife or mandoline slicer for thin, even slices. This preserves cucumber structure and results in crispier pickles.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Bread and butter pickles should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours. After preparing, keep them covered and refrigerated promptly to prevent spoilage and maintain crispness.

Refrigerator

Store the pickles in an airtight container in the fridge for up to two weeks. Over time, the flavor intensifies, but the texture may soften slightly. Best enjoyed chilled or at room temperature after a quick rinse if too salty.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as it damages the cucumbers’ texture, causing them to become mushy upon thawing. It’s better to consume pickles fresh within the refrigeration time frame.

Reheating

No reheating is necessary or recommended. Serve pickles cold or at room temperature to maintain their crisp texture and fresh flavor. Avoid microwaving as it will ruin the crunch.

Make Ahead

You can slice and salt the cucumbers and onions a day ahead, storing them covered in the fridge. Prepare the brine fresh the day you plan to pickle. This saves time and ensures the best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use regular cucumbers instead of pickling cucumbers?
You can, but regular slicing cucumbers have higher water content and thinner skins, which can lead to softer, less crisp pickles. If using them, slice thinner and salt a bit longer to draw out more moisture and improve crunch.
How long do bread and butter pickles last in the fridge?
Stored properly in an airtight container, these pickles last about two weeks in the refrigerator. After that, they may become too soft or develop off flavors. Always use clean utensils to prevent contamination and extend shelf life.
Can I make these pickles without sugar?
Sugar balances the acidity and enhances flavor, but you can reduce it or substitute with natural sweeteners like honey or maple syrup. Keep in mind this may change the taste and preservation slightly, so monitor flavor and texture closely.
Why are my pickles soggy?
Sogginess usually happens if you skip or shorten the salting step, or if the cucumbers are very watery to begin with. Proper salting draws out moisture and firms cucumbers. Also, avoid overpacking the jar, which can prevent brine circulation.
Is it necessary to boil the brine?
Yes, boiling dissolves sugar and activates the spices’ flavors, ensuring a well-balanced brine. Pouring hot brine over the cucumbers helps them absorb flavors faster and improves texture.
Can I can these pickles for shelf storage?
This recipe is designed as a quick refrigerator pickle and isn’t safe for canning as is. For shelf-stable pickles, follow tested canning recipes with appropriate acidity and processing times to ensure safety.
What if I don't have mustard seeds?
Mustard seeds add a subtle spice and texture but can be omitted if unavailable. You might add a pinch of ground mustard powder instead, but use less to avoid overpowering the brine.
How do I keep pickles crunchy longer?
Use fresh, firm cucumbers, salt properly, and keep pickles refrigerated. You can also add a grape leaf or a pinch of calcium chloride (pickle crisp) to the jar to help maintain crunch.
