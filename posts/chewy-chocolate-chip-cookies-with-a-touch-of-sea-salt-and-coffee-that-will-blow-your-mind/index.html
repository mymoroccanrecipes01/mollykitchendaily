Chewy Chocolate Sea Salt Cookies with a Hint of Coffee

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
20 min prep 15 min cook 24 servings

These cookies strike the perfect chewy balance with rich chocolate and a subtle coffee twist. Topped with sea salt for that little pop, they're great for sharing or a cozy afternoon snack.

Why This Works

What makes these cookies so special is the balance of ingredients and techniques that create a chewy yet tender texture with deep chocolate flavor. First, melting the butter instead of creaming it with sugar changes the fat structure in the dough. Melted butter coats the flour proteins more completely, which limits gluten development, resulting in a denser, chewier cookie instead of a fluffy one. The use of both brown and granulated sugars also plays a key role. Brown sugar contains molasses, which adds moisture and acidity, helping to keep the cookies soft and tender while also encouraging the Maillard reaction for rich flavor and color.

The addition of instant espresso powder is a subtle but powerful enhancer. Coffee compounds intensify the chocolate's bitterness and complexity without adding liquid, which would alter dough consistency. This ingredient is often overlooked but can transform a good chocolate cookie into something extraordinary. Refrigerating the dough before baking is another essential step. Chilling solidifies the butter, preventing excessive spreading in the oven, and allows flavors to meld, resulting in more depth and a better texture.

Finally, the sprinkle of flaky sea salt on top right after baking creates a delightful contrast. Salt enhances sweetness and chocolate flavor while adding a burst of texture and surprise in every bite. Baking just until the edges are golden but the centers remain soft prevents overbaking and keeps the cookies chewy rather than dry and crumbly. Altogether, these techniques and ingredients work harmoniously to create cookies that are rich, chewy, and irresistibly flavorful.

  • Choose a good-quality, unbleached all-purpose flour for the best texture. Bleached flours can sometimes produce a softer cookie but with less flavor. Sifting the flour before use aerates it and removes lumps, which ensures even mixing and a tender crumb. Avoid using cake flour as it has less protein and can make the cookies too delicate and cakey.
  • Using unsalted butter lets you control the salt content precisely, which is important for balancing sweetness and enhancing chocolate flavor. Melted butter is key here—it coats the flour proteins differently than softened butter, limiting gluten development and making the cookies chewy. Make sure to cool the melted butter before mixing with eggs to prevent cooking them prematurely.
  • Brown sugar contains molasses, which adds moisture and acidity to the dough, helping keep cookies soft and flavorful. Dark brown sugar has more molasses and a stronger flavor than light brown sugar, so choose based on your preference. Packing the brown sugar tightly ensures you’re using the correct amount for the recipe’s balance.
  • Instant espresso powder is ideal because it dissolves quickly into the dough without adding liquid. It intensifies the chocolate flavor by enhancing the bitter notes and complexity. Avoid brewed coffee as it would change the dough’s moisture content and texture. A little goes a long way—too much can make the cookies taste bitter.
  • Flaky sea salt sprinkled on top adds a wonderful flavor contrast and texture. It enhances the sweetness and chocolate richness by balancing the flavors on your palate. Use a finishing salt like Maldon or fleur de sel rather than fine table salt to get the best effect. Adding salt before baking can cause it to dissolve too much, so sprinkle it immediately after baking.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/4 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon fine sea salt, plus extra for sprinkling
  • 3/4 cup unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 1 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon instant espresso powder
  • 1 large egg plus 1 egg yolk, at room temperature
  • 2 teaspoons pure vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups semisweet chocolate chips

Adding a touch of espresso powder to bring out the chocolate's depth without overpowering it. One secret I learned is melting the butter instead of creaming it—it keeps the cookies dense and chewy rather than cakey. Also, using both an egg and an extra yolk adds richness and tenderness that you can really taste. When they come out of the oven, they’re soft, gooey in the center, and perfectly crisp at the edges. How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted all-purpose flour, baking soda, and 1 teaspoon sea salt. This dry mix ensures even distribution of the leavening agent and salt, which are crucial for texture and flavor balance. Sifting removes lumps and aerates the flour, helping the cookies bake evenly without dense spots. Avoid skipping this or mixing dry ingredients directly with wet, as it can cause uneven rising and texture.

    ⏱ 5✓ Dry ingredients are well combined with no visible clumps or pockets of baking soda.
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, whisk the melted butter with both sugars until smooth and glossy. The melted butter creates a denser, chewier cookie compared to creamed butter, while the sugars provide moisture and sweetness. Make sure the butter is cooled slightly to prevent cooking the eggs in the next step, which would ruin the texture. Whisking well also helps dissolve the sugar crystals for a smoother dough.

    ⏱ 5✓ Mixture looks smooth and shiny, with no gritty sugar texture.
  3. Step 3

    Add the instant espresso powder to the butter and sugar mixture and stir until fully dissolved. This step is key to infusing a subtle coffee flavor that enhances the chocolate without overpowering. Instant espresso powder dissolves quickly and evenly, unlike brewed coffee which adds liquid and could change dough consistency.

    ⏱ 2✓ No visible granules of espresso powder; mixture is uniform in color.
  4. Step 4

    Beat in the egg and extra egg yolk, followed by the vanilla extract. The extra yolk adds richness and chewiness, while the egg binds everything together. Beat just until combined to avoid incorporating too much air, which can make the cookies cakier instead of chewy.

    ⏱ 3✓ Mixture is smooth and slightly thickened, with no streaks of egg white.
  5. Step 5

    Gradually fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture using a spatula. Folding gently preserves some air but doesn’t overwork the gluten, which keeps the cookies tender. Overmixing will develop gluten and result in tougher cookies. Stop mixing as soon as you see no more flour streaks.

    ⏱ 4✓ Dough is uniform in color and texture, slightly sticky but not wet.
  6. Step 6

    Fold in the chocolate chips evenly throughout the dough. This ensures every cookie gets a good amount of chocolate. Use a spatula to distribute them gently so they don’t break or melt prematurely.

    ⏱ 2✓ Chocolate chips are evenly dispersed in the dough with no clumping.
  7. Step 7

    Cover the dough and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes or up to 24 hours. Chilling solidifies the butter, which prevents cookies from spreading too much in the oven and deepens the flavor as the ingredients meld together. Don’t skip this step—it’s what gives these cookies their signature texture.

    ⏱ 30✓ Dough is firm but scoopable after chilling.
  8. Step 8

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Line baking sheets with parchment paper or a silicone mat. Use a cookie scoop to portion dough balls about 2 tablespoons each, spacing them 2 inches apart. Uniform size helps them bake evenly. Gently press a few extra chocolate chips on top of each dough ball for a pretty finish.

    ⏱ 10✓ Oven is fully preheated and dough balls are evenly spaced and ready.
  9. Step 9

    Bake for 12-15 minutes until the edges are golden but centers still look soft and slightly underbaked. The cookies will continue to set as they cool, so pulling them out too late makes them hard. Watch the edges carefully—the golden color signals caramelization and perfect chewiness. Sprinkle with a pinch of flaky sea salt immediately after removing from the oven for that flavor pop.

    ⏱ 15✓ Edges are golden and centers look just set but soft.
  10. Step 10

    Let cookies cool on the baking sheet for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. This resting time allows them to firm up without breaking apart. Moving them too soon can cause them to crumble, while leaving them too long on a hot pan can overcook the bottoms.

    ⏱ 10✓ Cookies hold shape when gently lifted and have a slightly crisp bottom.

Variations

  • Double Chocolate Sea Salt Cookies: For an extra chocolate hit, swap half the flour for cocoa powder and add white chocolate chips along with semisweet ones. These cookies are richer and more decadent with a beautiful dark color. Expect a slightly drier texture due to the cocoa powder but still chewy and delicious.
    Because cocoa powder absorbs moisture, reduce flour slightly or add a tablespoon of milk to keep dough pliable.
  • Nutty Chocolate Sea Salt Cookies: Add 1 cup chopped toasted walnuts or pecans for crunch and nutty flavor. Nuts add texture contrast and deepen the cookie’s complexity. Perfect if you like a bit of bite and earthiness alongside rich chocolate.
    Fold nuts in with the chocolate chips to distribute evenly without breaking them up.
  • Vegan Chewy Chocolate Cookies: Replace butter with coconut oil and use a flax egg instead of real eggs. Use dairy-free chocolate chips. These changes keep the chewiness and rich flavor while making the recipe plant-based. The coconut oil adds a slight tropical note that pairs well with chocolate.
    Chill the dough longer (up to 2 hours) to firm up coconut oil and improve texture.
  • Spiced Chocolate Sea Salt Cookies: Add 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper for subtle warmth and spice. This unexpected twist complements the coffee and chocolate beautifully, making the cookies more complex and interesting.
    Add spices with the dry ingredients to distribute evenly without clumping.
  • Gluten-Free Chocolate Sea Salt Cookies: Use a 1:1 gluten-free flour blend designed for baking. The texture will be slightly different—less chewy but still tender and delicious. Adding a teaspoon of xanthan gum can improve structure and chewiness in gluten-free doughs.
    Watch baking time closely; gluten-free cookies can brown faster and dry out if overbaked.

Pro Tips

  • Chill the dough for at least 30 minutes before baking. — Chilling solidifies the butter, which prevents cookies from spreading too thin and helps develop richer flavors. It also allows the flour to hydrate fully, improving texture. Skipping this step often leads to flat, greasy cookies.
  • Use a cookie scoop for uniform dough balls. — Evenly sized cookies bake at the same rate, preventing some from burning while others remain undercooked. A scoop also makes portioning faster and cleaner, ensuring consistent results every time.
  • Don’t overmix the dough once you add flour. — Overmixing develops gluten, which makes cookies tough and cakey instead of tender and chewy. Fold gently until just combined to keep the perfect texture.
  • Use both an egg and an extra yolk. — The whole egg provides structure and moisture, while the extra yolk adds richness and chewiness. This combination creates a soft, tender crumb with a slightly dense texture that’s hard to beat.
  • Add a sprinkle of flaky sea salt right after baking. — Salt enhances sweetness and balances the rich chocolate, plus the flakes add a delightful crunch. Adding it post-bake prevents it from melting away and keeps the texture intact.
  • Melt the butter and cool it before mixing. — Melted butter coats flour proteins differently, reducing gluten formation and creating chewiness. Cooling prevents cooking the eggs in the next step, which can ruin dough consistency.
  • Use instant espresso powder instead of brewed coffee. — Instant espresso powder dissolves without adding liquid, intensifying chocolate flavor subtly. Brewed coffee would add moisture and could make the dough too wet or change baking times.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the chilling step
    Why: Many assume cookie dough can go straight to the oven, wanting a quick bake.
    Fix: Always chill the dough at least 30 minutes. It solidifies fats to control spreading and improves flavor development, preventing flat, greasy cookies.
  • Overmixing the dough after adding flour
    Why: It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid flour pockets, but overmixing develops gluten.
    Fix: Fold the flour in gently with a spatula just until no streaks remain. This keeps the texture tender and chewy.
  • Using too much espresso powder
    Why: Thinking more coffee flavor will be better, some add extra powder.
    Fix: Stick to the recipe amount. Too much espresso powder causes bitterness and can overpower the chocolate flavor.
  • Not watching the baking time carefully
    Why: Ovens vary, and it’s easy to leave cookies in too long.
    Fix: Pull cookies when edges are golden but centers still look soft. They’ll firm up as they cool for perfect chewiness.
  • Using salted butter without adjusting salt in recipe
    Why: Some use salted butter thinking it’s interchangeable.
    Fix: Use unsalted butter and add salt separately to control flavor precisely. Salted butter can make cookies too salty or unevenly seasoned.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Cookies keep well at room temperature for up to 2 days when stored in an airtight container. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat to prevent melting chocolate chips and drying out. Adding a slice of bread in the container can help maintain softness.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store cookies in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. They may harden slightly when cold, so let them come to room temperature before serving or warm briefly in the microwave for 10 seconds to soften.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe container or ziplock bag for up to 3 months. Wrap well to prevent freezer burn. Thaw at room temperature for 30 minutes before eating or warm in the oven at 300°F for 5 minutes.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat cookies in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes to restore softness and bring out chocolate gooeyness. Avoid microwaving too long or at high power as this can make them rubbery. Adding a small piece of butter on top before warming can enhance moisture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Prepare the cookie dough 1-2 days ahead and keep it refrigerated. This resting time improves flavor and texture. On baking day, scoop and bake fresh. You can also freeze dough balls individually for later use—bake straight from frozen adding a minute or two to baking time.

FAQ

Can I use regular brewed coffee instead of espresso powder?
It’s best to use instant espresso powder because it dissolves completely without adding extra liquid. Brewed coffee adds moisture that can alter dough consistency, making cookies spread too much or bake unevenly.
Why do my cookies spread too much and become thin?
Usually this happens if the dough isn’t chilled or the butter is too warm. Chilling solidifies fat and prevents excessive spreading. Also, using too much sugar or low-protein flour can cause spreading.
Can I substitute brown sugar with white sugar?
You can, but brown sugar adds moisture and depth due to molasses, which keeps cookies chewy and flavorful. Using only white sugar may result in crisper, less moist cookies.
How do I keep cookies chewy instead of cakey?
Use melted butter, an extra egg yolk, and avoid overmixing flour. Also, baking just until edges are set but centers remain soft helps maintain chewiness.
What’s the best way to store these cookies to keep them fresh?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature with a slice of bread to keep moisture. Refrigeration is fine but can harden cookies; warm briefly before serving.
Can I make the dough ahead of time?
Absolutely! Refrigerate the dough for up to 24 hours or freeze dough balls for up to 3 months. This resting enhances flavor and texture.
Why do my cookies sometimes come out dry or crumbly?
This can happen if you overbake or use too much flour. Measure flour carefully and remove cookies from oven when centers still look slightly underdone.
Can I use salted butter instead of unsalted?
It’s best to use unsalted butter so you can control salt levels precisely. Salted butter can make cookies too salty or unevenly seasoned.
How important is the sea salt topping?
Sea salt adds a wonderful flavor contrast and crunch that elevates the cookies. You can skip it, but the cookies won’t have that same pop of flavor.
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Yes! Chopped nuts, different types of chocolate chips, or dried fruit all work well. Just fold them in with the chocolate chips before chilling.
