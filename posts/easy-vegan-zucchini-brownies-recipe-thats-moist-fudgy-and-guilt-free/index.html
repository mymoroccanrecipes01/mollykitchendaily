Fudgy Vegan Zucchini Brownies That Everyone Will Love

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 29, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These vegan zucchini brownies are super moist and fudgy thanks to the hidden zucchini. They’re dairy-free, egg-free, and perfect for a cozy snack or sharing with friends on a chill afternoon.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon zucchini brownies years ago when I was looking for ways to sneak veggies into desserts without sacrificing flavor. At first, I was skeptical—brownies with veggies? But the zucchini adds moisture without any veggie taste, which is a total win. This version is my favorite because it’s super fudgy and uses simple pantry staples. The trick I learned is to grate the zucchini finely and squeeze out excess water so the batter isn’t too wet, which keeps the brownies from turning out soggy. Also, using oil instead of vegan butter keeps them moist and tender. When baked, they have this rich, fudgy texture with a subtle hint of earthiness that just works. These brownies are perfect for an afternoon pick-me-up or sharing with friends who might not even realize they’re eating veggies. I love how versatile they are—you can dress them up with nuts or chocolate chips or keep them simple. Honestly, these brownies have become a staple in my kitchen and a crowd-pleaser every time I make them.

What You'll Need

Approximately 8 brownies

  • 1 cup grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup cocoa powder, unsweetened
  • 3/4 cup sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup almond milk (or any plant milk)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchini finely using a box grater. Once grated, place the zucchini in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much moisture as possible. This step is crucial because excess water can make the brownies too wet and prevent them from baking properly. You want the zucchini to add moisture but not sogginess. If you skip this, the texture will suffer and the brownies might be gummy or undercooked in the center.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet after squeezing
Step 2
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease or line an 8x8 inch baking pan with parchment paper. This ensures easy removal of the brownies later and prevents sticking. Proper oven temperature is important for even baking and that classic fudgy texture. Rushing this step or baking at too low a temperature can make the brownies dense or underbaked.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven reaches temperature and pan is fully greased or lined
Step 3
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, sugar, baking powder, and salt until well combined. This dry mix forms the base of your brownies. Make sure to sift or break up any cocoa lumps to avoid clumps in the batter. Mixing thoroughly helps with even rising and consistent texture throughout the brownies.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are evenly mixed with no visible clumps
Step 4
In a separate bowl, combine the vegetable oil, vanilla extract, and almond milk. Whisk these wet ingredients together to create a smooth liquid base. Using oil keeps the brownies tender and moist, while the almond milk helps bind everything without dairy. Vanilla adds subtle flavor depth.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are fully combined and smooth
Step 5
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and stir gently with a spatula or wooden spoon. Mix just until combined; overmixing can develop gluten and make the brownies tough. The batter should be thick but spreadable. Fold in the grated zucchini evenly so it’s distributed throughout the batter.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform in color with zucchini pieces evenly mixed
Step 6
Transfer the batter into the prepared baking pan and spread it out evenly with your spatula. Leveling the batter helps it bake uniformly and prevents thin spots. Smooth the top gently without compressing it too much to maintain a light crumb.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth and even in the pan
Step 7
Bake the brownies in the preheated oven for 25-30 minutes. Keep an eye on them starting at 25 minutes—insert a toothpick in the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but not wet batter. This ensures fudginess without being raw. Baking too long will dry them out, too short and they’ll be underdone.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick comes out with moist crumbs, edges pull away slightly from pan
Step 8
Remove the brownies from the oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling allows the brownies to set and makes cutting easier. If you try to cut them warm, they might crumble or stick.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Brownies are firm to touch and cooled to room temperature
Step 9
Once cooled, lift the brownies out using the parchment paper and cut into squares with a sharp knife. Wipe the knife clean between cuts for neat edges. These brownies keep well in an airtight container for several days or can be frozen for later treats.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Clean, even brownie squares with fudgy texture

Variations

Chocolate Chip Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 cup vegan chocolate chips folded into the batter before baking for extra melty pockets of chocolate. This adds richness and texture without changing the moist fudgy base.

Note: Fold chips gently to avoid overmixing. Bake as usual but watch closely as chips can brown quickly.

Nutty Zucchini Brownies

Stir in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans for a crunchy contrast to the fudgy brownies. Nuts add a lovely earthy flavor that complements the zucchini and cocoa.

Note: Add nuts with the zucchini for even distribution. Toast nuts lightly beforehand for deeper flavor.

Spiced Zucchini Brownies

Sprinkle 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper into the dry ingredients for a warm, spicy twist that balances the chocolate and veggie flavors beautifully.

Note: Mix spices thoroughly with dry ingredients to ensure even flavor throughout the brownies.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Brownies

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend 1:1. Expect a slightly different texture—still fudgy but a bit more crumbly. Great for gluten-sensitive friends!

Note: Make sure your gluten-free flour blend contains xanthan gum for structure or add 1/4 teaspoon separately.

Peanut Butter Swirl Zucchini Brownies

Drop dollops of peanut butter on top of the batter and swirl gently with a knife before baking. This adds creamy pockets of peanut flavor that pair wonderfully with chocolate.

Note: Swirl lightly so you keep the marbled effect without overmixing the batter.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the zucchini well to remove excess water before adding it to the batter.
Excess moisture can make the brownies soggy and prevent them from baking properly. Removing water ensures the batter consistency is right and the final texture stays fudgy but not wet.
Use room temperature ingredients for better mixing and texture.
Cold ingredients can cause uneven mixing and clumps, affecting texture. Room temperature oil and milk combine smoothly with dry ingredients, creating a uniform batter.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make brownies tough and chewy instead of soft and tender.
Line your baking pan with parchment paper for easy removal.
This prevents sticking and helps lift the brownies out cleanly without breaking, preserving their fudgy edges.
Check the brownies a few minutes before the minimum baking time.
Ovens vary; checking early prevents overbaking which dries out the brownies and ruins the fudgy texture.
Let the brownies cool completely before cutting.
Cooling allows the brownies to set fully. Cutting warm brownies can cause them to crumble or stick to the knife.
Experiment with mix-ins like nuts or vegan chocolate chips for added texture.
Adding nuts or chips adds crunch and flavor contrast, making the brownies more interesting without affecting the core fudgy texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not removing enough moisture from the zucchini

Why it happens People often want to skip this step to save time or underestimate how much water zucchini holds.

Fix Grate the zucchini finely and use a clean towel or cheesecloth to squeeze out as much water as possible. This step is essential for the right brownie texture.

Overbaking the brownies

Why it happens It’s tempting to bake until the toothpick is completely clean, but this can dry out fudgy brownies.

Fix Remove brownies when a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with moist crumbs, not completely dry. This keeps them soft and fudgy.

Using the wrong type of fat

Why it happens Some may substitute butter or coconut oil without considering texture changes.

Fix Use neutral vegetable oil for the best moist and tender crumb. Butter or coconut oil can change texture and flavor.

Mixing batter too vigorously

Why it happens Trying to get a smooth batter quickly can lead to overmixing.

Fix Mix gently until just combined to avoid tough brownies caused by gluten development.

Not preheating the oven properly

Why it happens Starting with a cold oven affects rising and texture.

Fix Always preheat the oven fully to 350°F before baking for even cooking and proper texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These brownies keep well at room temperature for up to 2 days if stored in an airtight container away from direct sunlight. Covering them prevents drying out and preserves their fudgy texture.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The brownies may firm up and lose some moisture but still taste great. You can eat them cold or warm briefly to soften.

Freezer

Freeze brownies for up to 3 months. Wrap tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe container or bag to prevent freezer burn. Thaw at room temperature or overnight in the fridge before serving.

Reheating

Reheat brownies in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes to restore softness. Avoid overheating as it can dry them out. Adding a small splash of plant milk can help if they seem dry.

Make Ahead

You can grate the zucchini and prepare the batter 1 day ahead, then store it in the fridge covered tightly. Bake fresh the next day for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I substitute zucchini with another vegetable?
Yes, grated carrots or pumpkin puree can work as substitutes, but they will slightly change the flavor and moisture. Carrots add sweetness while pumpkin adds earthiness. Always squeeze out excess water if using fresh veggies.
What if I don’t have almond milk?
You can use any plant-based milk like soy, oat, or coconut milk. Just choose unsweetened varieties to control the sweetness. Water can be used in a pinch, but milk adds richness and improves texture.
How do I make these brownies gluten-free?
Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend with xanthan gum included, or add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t contain it. This helps mimic the structure gluten provides and prevents crumbling.
Can I use coconut oil instead of vegetable oil?
Yes, but coconut oil has a distinct flavor and solidifies when cold, which can change the texture. If using coconut oil, melt it first and cool slightly before mixing to keep the batter consistent.
How do I know when the brownies are done?
Insert a toothpick in the center; it should come out with moist crumbs but no wet batter. The edges will start to pull away slightly from the pan. This indicates fudgy brownies without underbaking.
Can I add chocolate chips or nuts?
Absolutely! Fold in 1/2 cup vegan chocolate chips or chopped nuts for extra texture and flavor. Add them after mixing the wet and dry ingredients but before pouring the batter into the pan.
Why do my brownies turn out dry sometimes?
Dry brownies usually mean they were overbaked or the zucchini wasn’t moist enough. Make sure to follow baking times closely and squeeze zucchini properly but not too much, as a little moisture is needed.
Can I double this recipe?
Yes, you can double the ingredients and bake in a larger pan or two standard pans. Adjust baking time by checking at 25 minutes and then every 5 minutes until done.
