Simple 5-Ingredient Chocolate Cake with Silky Ganache Frosting

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This chocolate cake is surprisingly easy but feels fancy thanks to a smooth ganache frosting. Just five ingredients create a moist, tender crumb that's perfect for birthdays or any time you want an effortless treat.

Introduction

I remember the first time I tried making this chocolate cake—it was a last-minute decision when I ran out of fancy ingredients but still wanted something delicious. What I love about this recipe is how it uses just five main ingredients, yet the cake turns out incredibly moist and tender every single time. The real game-changer is the ganache frosting—a simple mix of heavy cream and bittersweet chocolate that creates this silky, rich topping that feels so indulgent without much effort. I've tried more complicated chocolate cakes before, but this one wins for simplicity and flavor. Plus, the texture is just right—not too dense, not too crumbly, but perfectly soft and luscious. I usually bake it for birthdays or casual get-togethers, and it’s always a crowd-pleaser. The trick I learned is to melt the butter and let it cool before mixing it with the other ingredients; it keeps the batter smooth and prevents the eggs from cooking. Also, gently warming the cream before pouring it over the chocolate helps the ganache come together silky and glossy. Honestly, this cake looks like you spent hours on it, but it’s a breeze to make. I think you’ll love how simple and satisfying it is!

What You'll Need

One 8-inch round cake, 8 servings

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 cup milk, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 1 large egg
  • For Ganache:
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 8 ounces bittersweet chocolate, chopped

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease an 8-inch round cake pan. This ensures the cake won’t stick and will bake evenly. Greasing and lightly flouring the pan or lining it with parchment paper helps the cake release cleanly after baking. Don’t skip this step, or you might struggle to get the cake out intact.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Pan is evenly greased with no dry spots
Step 2
In a large bowl, sift together the flour, cocoa powder, sugar, and baking powder. Sifting removes lumps and aerates the dry ingredients, which helps create a lighter cake crumb. Mixing these dry ingredients first ensures even distribution so your cake bakes uniformly.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients look smooth and evenly combined
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk the egg, melted butter (cooled to avoid cooking the egg), and milk until combined. The melted butter adds richness and moisture, while the milk helps hydrate the dry ingredients. Whisking the egg well incorporates air, helping the cake rise nicely.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are smooth with no lumps
Step 4
Gradually pour the wet ingredients into the dry mixture, folding gently with a spatula until just combined. Overmixing can develop gluten, making the cake tough, so stir just until there are no streaks of flour. The batter should be smooth and slightly thick.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter is homogenous but not overworked
Step 5
Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the cake bake evenly and have a nice flat surface for frosting. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any air bubbles trapped inside.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread with small bubbles released
Step 6
Bake for 28-30 minutes or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out with a few moist crumbs. Baking time can vary depending on your oven, so start checking around 25 minutes. Avoid opening the oven too often as it may cause the cake to sink.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Toothpick test with moist crumbs, not wet batter
Step 7
Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then run a knife around the edges and invert onto a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling in the pan traps steam and can make the cake soggy. Transferring to a rack prevents condensation and keeps the crumb tender.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Cake easily releases from pan and is fully cooled
Step 8
To make the ganache, heat the heavy cream in a small saucepan just until it begins to simmer. Avoid boiling as it can scald the cream. Pour the hot cream over the chopped chocolate and let sit for 2-3 minutes to melt the chocolate gently.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Chocolate looks melted and cream is hot but not boiling
Step 9
Stir the chocolate and cream mixture slowly with a whisk or spatula until smooth and glossy. This emulsifies the fat and liquid into a silky ganache perfect for frosting. Let it cool slightly before spreading to avoid melting the cake.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Ganache is shiny, smooth, and thickened slightly
Step 10
Spread the ganache evenly over the cooled cake using an offset spatula or the back of a spoon. You can pour it on for a drippy effect or carefully smooth it for a clean finish. Chill briefly to set if desired, then serve and enjoy!
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Ganache evenly coats cake and sets slightly on cooling

Variations

Add Espresso for Depth

Stir 1 teaspoon instant espresso powder into the milk before adding to the batter. It intensifies the chocolate flavor without making the cake taste like coffee. This small tweak creates a richer, more complex dessert.

Note: Espresso powder dissolves easily in warm milk; ensure it’s fully mixed to avoid graininess.

Use Coconut Milk for Dairy-Free

Swap regular milk with full-fat coconut milk and replace butter with coconut oil. This gives the cake a subtle tropical twist and keeps it moist while accommodating dairy-free diets.

Note: Coconut oil solidifies when cold, so melt it gently before mixing.

Whipped Ganache Frosting

After ganache cools, whip it with a mixer until fluffy for a lighter, mousse-like frosting. It’s easier to spread and adds an airy texture contrast to the dense cake.

Note: Ganache must be fully cooled but not set hard before whipping.

Add Chopped Nuts or Chocolate Chips

Fold 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or chocolate chips into the batter for added texture and flavor. Nuts add a crunchy bite, while chips give bursts of melted chocolate throughout.

Note: Gently fold in to avoid deflating the batter.

Make Mini Cupcakes

Divide batter into a lined muffin tin and bake for 15-18 minutes for individual cupcakes. Perfect for parties or portion control, with the same moist texture and ganache topping.

Note: Adjust baking time and check doneness with toothpick.

Pro Tips

Always sift your dry ingredients before mixing.
Sifting removes lumps and aerates the flour and cocoa powder, which helps create an even texture and a lighter crumb. Ignoring this step can result in dense pockets or uneven baking.
Cool the melted butter before combining with eggs.
If the butter is too hot, it can scramble the eggs, causing a lumpy batter and a tough cake. Cooling the butter prevents this and ensures a smooth, homogenous batter.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes the cake dense and chewy. Stir gently until just combined to keep the crumb tender and soft.
Use room temperature eggs and milk.
Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or not mix properly, affecting rise and texture. Room temperature ingredients blend more evenly for consistent results.
Let the cake cool completely before frosting.
If the cake is warm, the ganache will melt and slide off, ruining the finish. Cooling allows the ganache to set nicely and keeps the frosting thick and shiny.
Warm the cream gently when making ganache.
Heating cream just to a simmer melts the chocolate smoothly without burning it. Boiling cream can scald and affect the flavor and texture of the ganache.
Tap the cake pan on the counter after pouring batter.
This releases trapped air bubbles that could cause holes or uneven texture. It helps the cake bake uniformly and creates a smooth surface.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using hot melted butter directly with eggs

Why it happens Sometimes you melt butter and immediately mix it with eggs without waiting, thinking it saves time.

Fix Always let melted butter cool to room temperature before combining with eggs to prevent scrambling. If you accidentally add hot butter, whisk vigorously and add a little flour to stabilize, but best to start over if curdled.

Overmixing the batter after adding wet to dry

Why it happens Eager to combine everything, cooks often stir vigorously, believing it improves texture.

Fix Mix gently until just combined. Stop as soon as no dry streaks remain to avoid gluten development that leads to toughness.

Not greasing or lining the pan properly

Why it happens In a rush, some skip this step or do it unevenly thinking it’s optional.

Fix Grease the pan thoroughly and dust with flour or use parchment paper. This prevents sticking and ensures a clean release.

Using cold ingredients

Why it happens Some assume cold eggs and milk won’t affect the batter.

Fix Bring eggs and milk to room temperature for smooth mixing and better rise.

Pouring ganache over warm cake

Why it happens Impatience leads to frosting before cake cools, causing melting.

Fix Wait until the cake is completely cool before frosting to get a clean, shiny ganache finish.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cake can stay safely at room temperature for up to 2 days when covered with a cake dome or airtight container. The ganache will remain soft but should be protected from direct sunlight or heat to avoid melting.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container or cake dome for up to 5 days. The ganache may firm up and the cake can become slightly denser when chilled. Bring to room temperature before serving for best texture and flavor.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and then bring to room temperature before frosting or serving to restore moisture and texture.

Reheating

If desired, warm individual slices gently in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) wrapped in foil for 10 minutes to soften ganache and warm the cake. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

Bake the cake layers a day ahead and store wrapped at room temperature or refrigerated. Prepare ganache on the day of serving for the freshest texture and flavor, then frost just before guests arrive.

FAQ

Can I use cocoa powder instead of melted chocolate for the ganache?
Ganache requires chocolate to melt and emulsify with cream, so cocoa powder alone won't work as a frosting. You need chopped chocolate or chips to create the smooth, glossy texture. For a cocoa-based frosting, try a buttercream recipe instead.
What if my ganache is too thick or too thin?
If ganache is too thick, gently warm it in short bursts and stir to loosen. If too thin, chill it in the fridge for 10-15 minutes until it thickens. The right consistency spreads easily but holds shape.
Can I substitute vegetable oil for butter?
You can swap melted butter for oil, but the flavor and texture will differ slightly. Butter adds richness and a tender crumb, while oil makes the cake moist but less flavorful. Use a neutral oil like canola if substituting.
How do I prevent the cake from sinking in the middle?
Make sure not to open the oven door during baking, and measure baking powder accurately. Overmixing the batter or underbaking can also cause sinking. Follow timing closely and test doneness with a toothpick.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes, use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend that includes xanthan gum for structure. The texture may be slightly different but will still be moist and tender. Avoid overmixing to keep it light.
Why is my cake dry despite following the recipe?
Dry cake can result from overbaking or using too much flour. Use a kitchen scale for accuracy and check the cake starting at the minimum baking time. Also, ensure butter is melted but not overheated.
Can I make the ganache ahead and store it?
Yes, ganache can be made a day ahead and refrigerated in an airtight container. Rewarm gently before using to restore a spreadable consistency. Whisk if needed to smooth out after chilling.
What’s the best way to store leftover frosted cake?
Keep leftovers covered with a cake dome or in an airtight container at room temperature for 2 days or refrigerated for up to 5 days. Let refrigerated cake come to room temp before serving for best taste.
