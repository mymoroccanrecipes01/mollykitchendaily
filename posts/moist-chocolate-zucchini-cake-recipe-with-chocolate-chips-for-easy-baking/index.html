Moist Chocolate Zucchini Cake with Melty Chocolate Chips for Any Occasion

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 31, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This chocolate zucchini cake is my go-to when I want something sweet but a little sneaky with veggies. The melted chocolate chips throughout make every bite luscious and moist. Perfect for afternoon snacks or easy family dessert nights!

Introduction

I first made this chocolate zucchini cake a few years ago when I wanted to hide some veggies in a dessert my kids would actually enjoy. Honestly, I was skeptical at first—would the zucchini make the cake weird? But the result blew me away. This cake is so moist and chocolatey, you’d never guess it’s got veggies in it! What sets this version apart is the generous amount of chocolate chips that melt into the batter, giving pockets of gooey chocolate with every bite. Plus, I use sour cream to keep it extra tender and rich. The trick I learned is to grate the zucchini finely and squeeze out as much moisture as possible so the cake doesn’t turn out soggy. When it’s done, the cake has a deep chocolate flavor with a subtle freshness from the zucchini, making it perfect for afternoon treats or casual family dinners. I love making it because it feels like a little secret indulgence—delicious and a bit healthier. If you’re looking for a dessert that’s easy, comforting, and a bit special, this one’s for you!

What You'll Need

One 9-inch cake, 8 servings

  • 2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup cocoa powder, unsweetened
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchini finely using the small holes of a box grater. Once grated, place the zucchini in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step is crucial because zucchini contains a lot of water, and removing excess moisture prevents the cake from becoming soggy. If you skip this, your cake might be too wet and dense, so take your time here.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet when squeezed.
Step 2
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and flour a 9-inch cake pan or line it with parchment paper. This ensures your cake bakes evenly and releases easily from the pan without sticking.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven reaches consistent 350°F and pan is evenly coated.
Step 3
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, baking powder, and salt. Sifting the dry ingredients together helps to evenly distribute the leavening agents and cocoa, preventing clumps and ensuring a uniform texture in your cake.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry mixture is smooth and lump-free.
Step 4
In a large bowl, beat the melted butter and sugar until combined but not fluffy. Then add the eggs one at a time, mixing well after each addition. Stir in the vanilla extract. Be careful not to overbeat once eggs are added, or your cake can become tough.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth and glossy but not overly aerated.
Step 5
Mix the sour cream into the wet ingredients until fully combined. The sour cream adds moisture and acidity, which helps tenderize the cake crumb and balance the chocolate’s bitterness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter looks creamy and smooth.
Step 6
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture in batches, mixing gently just until no flour streaks remain. Overmixing here can develop gluten and make your cake dense, so be gentle and patient.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick but uniform, no visible flour.
Step 7
Fold in the grated zucchini and chocolate chips evenly throughout the batter. The chips will melt during baking, creating those irresistible pockets of gooey chocolate.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and chips are distributed evenly without overmixing.
Step 8
Pour the batter into the prepared pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. Bake for about 30 minutes or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Avoid opening the oven too early to prevent the cake from sinking.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick test passes; cake springs back lightly when touched.
Step 9
Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling fully helps the cake set and makes slicing cleaner. Serve warm or at room temperature for best flavor.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake is firm to touch and cooled enough to slice cleanly.

Variations

Walnut Chocolate Zucchini Cake

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts to the batter for a crunchy texture contrast. The nuts pair perfectly with chocolate and add a nutty depth. Expect a slightly heartier cake with delightful crunch in every bite.

Note: Fold walnuts in with chocolate chips to distribute evenly. Toasting nuts first enhances flavor but is optional.

Spiced Chocolate Zucchini Cake

Add 1 tsp cinnamon and 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper to the dry ingredients for a warm, spicy twist. The cinnamon complements chocolate beautifully, while the cayenne adds a subtle kick that wakes up the flavors.

Note: Add spices with the dry ingredients to ensure even mixing. Adjust cayenne to taste for milder or bolder spice.

Vegan Chocolate Zucchini Cake

Replace eggs with flax eggs (2 tbsp ground flax + 6 tbsp water) and use coconut oil instead of butter. Swap sour cream for coconut yogurt. The cake will be slightly denser but still moist and delicious for vegan diets.

Note: Mix flax eggs and let sit before adding. Baking time may increase by 5-7 minutes; test doneness carefully.

Chocolate Zucchini Bundt Cake

Bake the batter in a greased bundt pan for a pretty presentation and slightly different texture. The ring shape helps the cake bake evenly and makes it perfect for sharing at gatherings.

Note: Increase baking time to about 40-45 minutes due to pan shape; check doneness with toothpick.

Gluten-Free Chocolate Zucchini Cake

Swap all-purpose flour for a 1:1 gluten-free baking blend. The texture may be slightly crumblier but still moist. Great for gluten-sensitive folks craving chocolate cake with a veggie twist.

Note: Ensure your gluten-free blend contains xanthan gum or add 1/2 tsp for structure.

Pro Tips

Squeeze zucchini very well before mixing to avoid soggy cake.
Zucchini contains a lot of water, and if not removed, it can make the cake wet and dense. Properly squeezing out moisture helps the batter hold structure and results in a tender crumb rather than a gummy one.
Don’t overmix the batter when combining wet and dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which makes the cake tough and chewy instead of soft and tender. Folding gently just until combined preserves air bubbles that keep the cake light.
Use room temperature eggs and sour cream for better batter consistency.
Cold ingredients can cause the melted butter to seize or the batter to curdle, leading to uneven texture. Room temperature ingredients blend smoothly, resulting in a consistent batter that bakes evenly.
Test cake doneness with a toothpick inserted near the center.
Since moisture from zucchini can make the cake seem wet, checking with a toothpick helps ensure it’s baked through without overbaking. A few moist crumbs are okay, but no wet batter should remain.
Cool the cake completely before slicing for clean cuts.
Cutting warm cake can cause it to crumble or stick. Cooling allows the crumb to set and chocolate chips to firm up slightly, making slicing easier and prettier.
Use good-quality cocoa powder for richer chocolate flavor.
Lower quality cocoa can taste flat or bitter. A high-quality cocoa powder gives depth and brightness, elevating the cake’s flavor and balancing the sweetness of the chips.
Fold chocolate chips in last to prevent sinking.
Adding chocolate chips right before baking and folding gently keeps them suspended throughout the batter, creating even distribution so every bite has melty chocolate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture from the zucchini.

Why it happens Many think just grating is enough, but zucchini’s high water content can make the batter too wet if not pressed well.

Fix Use a clean kitchen towel to squeeze tightly after grating. Aim for zucchini that feels damp but not dripping. This prevents soggy texture and helps the cake hold its shape.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour.

Why it happens In a hurry, cooks often stir vigorously to combine everything quickly, unaware it develops gluten.

Fix Fold the dry ingredients gently into the wet just until you see no flour streaks. Stop mixing as soon as combined to keep the crumb tender.

Skipping the sour cream or substituting with milk.

Why it happens Some assume any dairy works the same, but milk lacks acidity and fat content.

Fix Use sour cream or full-fat Greek yogurt to maintain moisture and activate baking soda. This keeps the cake tender and helps it rise properly.

Using the wrong baking powder or baking soda amounts.

Why it happens Inexperience with leavening agents can cause imbalance, resulting in dense or collapsed cake.

Fix Measure carefully and use both baking soda and baking powder as stated. This ensures proper rise and texture.

Removing cake from oven too early.

Why it happens Impatience or misjudging doneness leads to underbaked cake that’s gooey inside.

Fix Use a toothpick test near center: it should come out with moist crumbs but no wet batter. Let the cake finish baking fully to set the crumb.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Keep the cake covered tightly with plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. This prevents it from drying out while maintaining softness and flavor.

Refrigerator

Store the cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The cake may become firmer and less moist when cold, so bring it to room temperature or warm slightly before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to freeze for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave it in the fridge overnight or at room temperature for a few hours. Avoid freezing chocolate chips separately as they may bloom or lose texture.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk or cream before warming can help restore moisture. Avoid overheating which can dry out the cake.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze zucchini up to 1 day ahead and store it wrapped in the fridge. The batter can be mixed and kept in the fridge for a few hours before baking. Bake the cake the day of serving for best freshness.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this cake?
You can use frozen zucchini if fresh isn’t available, but be sure to thaw and squeeze out all excess water thoroughly. Frozen zucchini tends to be more watery, so extra care in drying is important to avoid soggy cake.
What if I don’t have sour cream? Can I substitute it?
Greek yogurt is the best substitute for sour cream here, providing similar moisture and acidity. Regular yogurt or buttermilk can also work but may slightly alter texture and flavor. Avoid using milk alone as it lacks acidity.
Why does my cake sometimes turn out dense?
Dense cake often results from overmixing the batter or not squeezing out enough moisture from the zucchini. Also, inaccurate measurements of leavening agents can affect rise. Be gentle when folding and measure ingredients carefully.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes! Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend that contains xanthan gum for structure. The texture may be a bit different—slightly crumblier—but still delicious. Adjust baking time slightly and test with a toothpick.
How do I know when the cake is done baking?
Insert a toothpick into the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Because of the zucchini’s moisture, don’t expect it to come out completely dry. Also, the cake should spring back lightly when pressed.
Can I use dark chocolate chips instead of semi-sweet?
Absolutely! Dark chocolate chips will add a richer, slightly more bitter chocolate flavor which pairs well with the zucchini. Adjust sugar slightly if you prefer it sweeter, but it’s a great swap.
What’s the best way to store leftover cake?
Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. Reheat gently before serving to restore moisture and softness.
Can I double this recipe for a larger cake?
Yes, doubling works well. Use two pans or a larger pan and adjust baking time accordingly—usually adding 10-15 minutes. Keep an eye on doneness with a toothpick test to avoid overbaking.
