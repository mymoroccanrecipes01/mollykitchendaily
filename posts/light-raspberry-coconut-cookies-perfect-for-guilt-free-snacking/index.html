Light Raspberry Coconut Cookies That Stay Soft and Delicious

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 3, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These raspberry coconut cookies are a light, sweet treat that stays soft and packed with flavor. Made with fresh raspberries and shredded coconut, they’re perfect for an afternoon pick-me-up or a guilt-free dessert!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this recipe last summer when I was craving something sweet but didn't want to feel weighed down by heavy desserts. These raspberry coconut cookies quickly became a favorite in my house because they’re delightfully soft and packed with fresh fruit flavor. What makes this version different? I use fresh raspberries folded gently into the batter instead of dried or frozen ones, which keeps the cookies moist and bursting with juicy pockets. Plus, the shredded coconut adds a lovely chewy texture without overpowering the delicate berry taste. I remember my first batch didn’t turn out quite right—I overmixed and the cookies came out tough. Since then, I’ve learned to handle the batter gently and bake just until the edges are golden, keeping them soft and tender. The end result is a batch of cookies that feel like a cozy hug—light, fruity, and perfect with a cup of tea or coffee any time of day.

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 cookies

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened shredded coconut
  • 1/3 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/4 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup fresh raspberries, gently rinsed and patted dry

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment paper. This setup ensures even baking and prevents sticking. Mixing the dry ingredients first—flour, baking powder, salt, and shredded coconut—helps distribute everything evenly, so every cookie has a consistent texture and flavor. Don’t skip sifting the flour; it keeps the cookies light and prevents clumps.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are combined evenly without lumps or streaks
Step 2
In a separate bowl, cream the softened butter with both sugars until the mixture is light and fluffy. This process incorporates air, which helps the cookies rise and become soft. If you rush this step or don’t cream well, your cookies might turn out dense. Use a hand mixer or stand mixer on medium speed for about 3-4 minutes.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks pale, fluffy, and smooth with no visible sugar grains
Step 3
Beat in the egg and vanilla extract to the creamed butter mixture. Adding these ingredients now helps bind the dough and adds moisture and flavor. Make sure to mix just until combined to avoid overworking the dough, which can make cookies tough.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is uniform and glossy without streaks of egg or butter
Step 4
Gently fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture using a spatula. The key here is to fold, not stir vigorously. Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, leading to tougher cookies. Stop mixing as soon as the flour disappears and the dough looks cohesive.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: No visible flour patches, dough looks soft and slightly sticky
Step 5
Carefully fold in the fresh raspberries. Be gentle to avoid smashing the berries, which can turn the dough pink and watery. The raspberries add natural sweetness and moisture, so handle delicately to keep the texture balanced.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Berries are evenly distributed without too much juice released
Step 6
Scoop the dough onto the prepared baking sheet using a tablespoon or cookie scoop, spacing them about 2 inches apart. This spacing allows the cookies to spread slightly without merging. If the dough feels too sticky, chill it for 10 minutes to firm up.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Even mounds of dough spaced well, slightly rounded tops
Step 7
Bake the cookies for 12-15 minutes until the edges turn golden but the centers still look soft. Baking just until this point ensures the cookies remain tender and moist inside. Overbaking will dry them out and make them crumbly.
⏱ Time: 12-15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges are golden, centers look set but soft when touched lightly
Step 8
Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the cookies cool on it for 5 minutes. This resting time allows the cookies to firm up slightly, making them easier to transfer without breaking. Then move them to a wire rack to cool completely.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cookies hold their shape and are slightly firm when picked up
Step 9
Once cooled, store the cookies in an airtight container at room temperature. They’ll stay soft for several days. If you want to enjoy them warm, just pop them in the microwave for 10-15 seconds before serving to revive that fresh-baked softness.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Cookies remain soft and flavorful after storage

Variations

Chocolate Chip Raspberry Coconut Cookies

Add 1/2 cup of mini chocolate chips to the dough for a chocolatey twist that pairs beautifully with the tart raspberries and chewy coconut. Expect a richer flavor and slightly denser texture with melty chocolate pockets.

Note: Fold in chocolate chips gently with raspberries to avoid breaking the berries; baking time remains the same.

Gluten-Free Raspberry Coconut Cookies

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free flour blend made for baking. This keeps the cookies soft and tender without gluten. The coconut adds extra chewiness which helps mimic traditional texture.

Note: Make sure the gluten-free flour blend includes xanthan gum or add 1/4 tsp for structure.

Vegan Raspberry Coconut Cookies

Replace butter with coconut oil and the egg with a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water). These swaps keep the cookies moist and flavorful while making them dairy-free and vegan-friendly.

Note: Chill the dough longer before baking to compensate for the softer texture from coconut oil.

Lemon Raspberry Coconut Cookies

Add 1 tbsp lemon zest to the batter for a bright citrus note that complements the raspberries and coconut. This variation feels fresher and more vibrant, perfect for spring or summer days.

Note: Mix lemon zest with sugars during creaming to release oils and enhance flavor.

Spiced Raspberry Coconut Cookies

Include 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg to add warm spice flavors that deepen the cookie’s complexity. Great for cozy fall baking with a soft, aromatic finish.

Note: Add spices to the dry ingredients and mix thoroughly before folding into wet ingredients.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature butter for easier creaming and better texture.
Butter that’s too cold won’t cream properly with sugar, leading to dense cookies. If butter is too soft or melted, the dough can become greasy and spread too much. Room temperature butter helps trap air during creaming, which creates a lighter, softer cookie.
Be gentle when folding in fresh raspberries to keep them intact.
Raspberries are delicate and can release juice if crushed, making the dough too wet. Folding carefully preserves their shape and prevents the dough from becoming soggy, ensuring pockets of fresh berry flavor in the finished cookies.
Don’t overbake; remove cookies when edges are golden but centers still look soft.
Overbaking dries out cookies and makes them crumbly. Taking them out early ensures they retain moisture and stay tender. The cookies will continue to set slightly as they cool on the baking sheet.
Sift your dry ingredients to avoid lumps and improve dough texture.
Sifting aerates the flour and combines baking powder and salt evenly. This helps the cookies rise uniformly and ensures a smooth, tender crumb without pockets of dry flour.
Chill the dough if it feels too sticky before scooping.
Chilling firms up the dough, making it easier to handle and preventing cookies from spreading too much during baking. This results in thicker, chewier cookies with better shape.
Use parchment paper or a silicone mat to prevent sticking and promote even baking.
These liners create a non-stick surface, so cookies bake evenly without burning on the bottom. They also help with easy cleanup and maintaining cookie shape.
Store cookies in an airtight container to keep them soft longer.
Exposure to air dries out cookies quickly. Airtight storage traps moisture and preserves the soft, chewy texture. Adding a slice of bread inside the container can help maintain moisture balance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the dough after adding flour

Why it happens Many cooks think mixing longer makes dough smoother, but overmixing develops gluten, making cookies tough.

Fix Mix just until the flour disappears and the dough looks combined. Use a spatula to fold gently instead of stirring vigorously.

Using frozen or thawed raspberries

Why it happens Frozen berries release more water, causing soggy dough and flat cookies.

Fix Always use fresh raspberries, rinse and dry them carefully before folding into the dough.

Baking cookies too long

Why it happens Waiting for cookies to look completely firm leads to dryness.

Fix Take cookies out when edges are golden but centers still look slightly soft; they will firm up as they cool.

Not creaming butter and sugar properly

Why it happens Rushing this step or using cold butter prevents air incorporation, resulting in dense cookies.

Fix Use room temperature butter and beat with sugars until mixture is pale and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes.

Skipping sifting of dry ingredients

Why it happens Lumps in flour or uneven baking powder can cause uneven texture and rise.

Fix Sift flour, baking powder, and salt together before adding to wet ingredients for even distribution.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat to prevent drying out. Covering helps maintain softness and flavor.

Refrigerator

You can refrigerate cookies in an airtight container for up to 7 days. Refrigeration may firm them up slightly, so let them come to room temperature or warm briefly before eating to restore softness.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 3 months. Wrap well to avoid freezer burn. Thaw at room temperature before serving.

Reheating

Warm cookies in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5 minutes to restore softness. Avoid overheating which can dry them out or make them crumbly.

Make Ahead

Prepare the dough up to 2 days in advance and keep it tightly wrapped in the fridge. Scoop and bake fresh when ready. This helps develop flavors and keeps the dough manageable.

FAQ

Can I use frozen raspberries instead of fresh?
Frozen raspberries release more liquid when thawed, which can make the dough too wet and cause flat, soggy cookies. If you must use frozen, thaw them completely and pat dry very well, but fresh raspberries are best for texture and flavor.
How do I keep my cookies soft for longer?
Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature and add a slice of bread to the container. The bread helps maintain moisture. Avoid refrigeration if you want to keep them soft, but warming before serving helps if they’ve firmed up.
What can I substitute for shredded coconut?
If avoiding coconut, try oat flakes or chopped nuts, but expect a less chewy cookie.
Why did my cookies turn out flat?
Flat cookies usually mean the dough was too warm or overmixed. Chilling the dough before baking helps it hold shape. Also, make sure your baking powder is fresh, as expired leavening won’t provide enough lift.
Can I make these cookies dairy-free?
Yes! Substitute butter with coconut oil or vegan butter and replace the egg with a flax or chia egg. Dough may be softer, so chill it longer before baking to maintain shape and texture.
How do I prevent raspberries from bleeding into the dough?
Fold raspberries in gently at the end and avoid crushing them. Using firm, ripe berries helps. Also, don’t overmix after adding berries to keep them intact and prevent excess juice from coloring the dough.
Can I double the recipe?
Definitely! Just make sure to mix in batches if needed to avoid overmixing, and bake cookies in batches without crowding the baking sheet. Adjust baking time slightly if cookies are larger or smaller.
What’s the best way to scoop sticky dough?
Use a cookie scoop or two spoons dipped in cold water to prevent sticking. Alternatively, chill the dough for 10-15 minutes to firm it up, making it easier to handle and shape.
