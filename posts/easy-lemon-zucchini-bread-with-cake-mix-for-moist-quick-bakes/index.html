Moist Lemon Zucchini Bread Made Easy with Cake Mix

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 2, 2026
15 min Prep Time
40 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon zucchini bread is incredibly moist thanks to the cake mix base, with fresh zucchini and zesty lemon brightening every slice. It’s a quick treat that’s perfect for breakfast or a cozy afternoon snack.

Introduction

I first stumbled across this lemon zucchini bread recipe on a busy weeknight when I wanted something fresh but didn’t have hours to bake. Using a yellow cake mix as the base was a game changer—it keeps the bread incredibly moist and tender without fussing over measuring flour or mixing from scratch. What really makes this bread pop is the fresh lemon zest and juice paired with grated zucchini, which adds moisture and a subtle earthiness. I like to squeeze the zucchini to remove excess water so the bread doesn’t get soggy but stays perfectly soft. Over the years, I’ve tweaked this recipe slightly, like adding a touch of baking powder for a better rise and balancing the lemon flavor so it’s bright but not overpowering. The finished loaf has a beautiful golden crust with a tender crumb studded with flecks of green zucchini and bursts of lemon. It’s perfect for breakfast with coffee or sliced as an afternoon snack. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes I keep coming back to because it’s simple, reliable, and always gets compliments from friends and family.

What You'll Need

One 9x5-inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 1 box yellow cake mix
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/4 cup lemon juice, fresh
  • 2 teaspoons lemon zest
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan with butter or nonstick spray to ensure the bread doesn’t stick while baking. This step is important because a properly greased pan helps the bread release easily and keeps the crust intact. While the oven heats, prepare your zucchini by grating it and then squeezing out the excess moisture using a clean kitchen towel or paper towels. Removing the water is crucial to prevent the bread from becoming soggy and dense.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan is evenly greased, zucchini is moist but not dripping wet
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the cake mix, baking powder, and salt to evenly distribute the leavening agents and salt. This ensures a consistent rise and balanced flavor throughout the bread. Then add the eggs, vegetable oil, lemon juice, lemon zest, and vanilla extract. Mix thoroughly until the batter is smooth and uniform. Be careful not to overmix as that can make the bread tough. The batter should be thick but pourable.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, thick batter with no lumps
Step 3
Fold in the grated zucchini gently with a spatula. This step adds moisture and texture but folding carefully prevents the batter from deflating, which keeps the bread light. Make sure the zucchini is evenly distributed to avoid clumps that might cause uneven baking.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is incorporated evenly without overmixing
Step 4
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and rise uniformly. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles that could cause holes or cracks.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Even batter surface with no large air pockets
Step 5
Bake in the preheated oven for 40-45 minutes. Start checking at 40 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center—if it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the bread is done. Avoid opening the oven door too often, as temperature fluctuations can affect the rise and texture.
⏱ Time: 40-45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden crust and toothpick inserted in center comes out clean
Step 6
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for about 10 minutes. This resting time allows the bread to firm up so it’s easier to slice without crumbling. Then transfer it to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling on a rack prevents the bottom from getting soggy from trapped steam.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is warm but firm to the touch and easily removable from pan
Step 7
Once cooled, slice the bread with a serrated knife for clean cuts. Serve plain or spread with butter or cream cheese. The lemon zest really shines when the bread is at room temperature, making each bite bright and fresh.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Clean slices with visible zucchini flecks and moist crumb
Step 8
Store any leftovers wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. This keeps the bread moist and fresh without drying out. For longer storage, refrigerate or freeze (see storage section).
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread remains soft and fresh after storing
Step 9
If freezing, slice the bread first and wrap individual pieces in plastic wrap, then place in a zip-top freezer bag. This prevents freezer burn and lets you thaw only what you need. To thaw, leave slices at room temperature for 30 minutes or warm gently in the microwave.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes prep, thaw time varies
✓ Doneness: Bread thaws soft and moist without being soggy

Variations

Add Blueberries for a Fruity Twist

Fold 1 cup of fresh or frozen blueberries into the batter along with the zucchini for bursts of juicy sweetness. Blueberries complement the lemon flavor beautifully and add extra color and texture.

Note: If using frozen blueberries, toss them in a bit of flour before adding to prevent them from sinking to the bottom of the loaf.

Swap Lemon for Orange Zest and Juice

Replace the lemon zest and juice with the same amount of fresh orange zest and juice for a sweeter, milder citrus flavor. This variation pairs wonderfully with the moist zucchini base and is great for those who prefer less tartness.

Note: Orange juice is less acidic, so consider adding an extra 1/2 teaspoon baking powder to help the bread rise well.

Make It Gluten-Free

Use a gluten-free yellow cake mix to make this bread suitable for gluten-sensitive diets. The zucchini adds moisture that works well with gluten-free flours, keeping the bread tender and soft.

Note: Gluten-free batters often benefit from resting 10 minutes before baking to hydrate the flours fully.

Add Spices for Warmth

Stir in 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg to the dry ingredients for a cozy, spiced version. These warm spices pair nicely with the fresh lemon and zucchini for a comforting loaf.

Note: Spices do not affect baking time but enhance aroma and flavor complexity.

Top with a Lemon Glaze

Mix powdered sugar with fresh lemon juice to make a simple glaze and drizzle over the cooled bread. This adds sweetness and a shiny finish that makes the loaf extra special for serving guests.

Note: Apply glaze only when the bread is completely cooled to prevent it from melting off.

Pro Tips

Squeeze zucchini well to remove excess water before adding.
Excess moisture from zucchini can make the bread dense and soggy. By squeezing out water, you keep the batter balanced and ensure a tender crumb without a heavy, wet texture.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice for the best flavor.
Fresh lemon zest contains essential oils that provide a bright, aromatic citrus flavor that dried zest can't replicate. The juice adds acidity, which helps activate the leavening agents for better rise and balances sweetness.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add wet ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make the bread tough and chewy instead of soft and tender. Mix just until combined for the best texture.
Bake at 350°F and avoid opening the oven door frequently.
A steady oven temperature ensures even baking and proper rise. Opening the door lets heat escape, which can cause the bread to sink or bake unevenly.
Let the bread cool in the pan before removing.
Cooling briefly in the pan allows the bread to set and reduces the chance it will break apart when you remove it. Then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely and prevent sogginess.
Use a serrated knife to slice the bread cleanly.
A serrated knife cuts through the tender crumb without compressing or tearing the loaf, preserving its texture and appearance.
Store wrapped tightly to keep moisture in.
Wrapping the bread in plastic or foil prevents it from drying out and keeps it soft for several days at room temperature.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing zucchini enough

Why it happens Many people assume grated vegetables can be added as-is without considering extra moisture. This leads to soggy, dense bread.

Fix After grating, place zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove as much liquid as possible. This keeps the bread’s texture light and prevents it from becoming mushy.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly for smoothness, but overmixing develops gluten, toughening the bread.

Fix Mix just until ingredients are combined and no large dry patches remain. Fold in zucchini gently at the end.

Using expired baking powder

Why it happens Baking powder loses potency over time, which reduces the bread’s rise.

Fix Check expiration dates and test by mixing a small amount in warm water—if it bubbles vigorously, it’s still good.

Opening oven door frequently

Why it happens Impatience or curiosity leads to checking too often, causing temperature drops.

Fix Set a timer and resist the urge to open the oven until the minimum baking time has passed to ensure even cooking.

Not greasing the pan properly

Why it happens Skipping or uneven greasing can cause the bread to stick and break apart when removed.

Fix Use butter or nonstick spray evenly on all sides and bottom of the pan before pouring in batter.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat sources to maintain moisture and freshness.

Refrigerator

You can refrigerate the bread wrapped well or in an airtight container for up to 7 days. The bread may dry out slightly, so bring to room temperature before serving or warm briefly in the microwave for a softer texture.

Freezer

Freeze the bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. To prevent freezer burn, remove as much air as possible from the bag. Thaw slices overnight in the fridge or at room temperature for 30 minutes before warming.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small pat of butter or a sprinkle of water before reheating helps retain moisture and freshness.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh the next day for the best rise and texture. Alternatively, bake the loaf in advance and freeze slices individually for quick snacks anytime.

FAQ

Can I use yellow squash instead of zucchini?
Yes, yellow squash works well as a substitute because it has a similar moisture content and mild flavor. Just grate and squeeze it out the same way you would with zucchini to avoid excess moisture in the bread.
What if I don’t have fresh lemons for zest and juice?
You can use bottled lemon juice but the flavor won’t be as bright or fresh. For zest, it’s best to use fresh since dried lemon zest lacks the essential oils that give the bread its aromatic citrus note.
Why do I need to add baking powder if the cake mix already has leavening?
Adding a little extra baking powder helps the bread rise better because the zucchini adds moisture and weight to the batter, which can weigh down the rise. It ensures a lighter, fluffier loaf.
How do I prevent the bread from being soggy?
The key is squeezing out as much water as possible from the grated zucchini before mixing. Also, avoid overmixing the batter and bake fully until a toothpick comes out clean.
Can I make muffins instead of a loaf?
Absolutely! Use a muffin tin and fill each cup about 2/3 full. Bake at 350°F for about 20-25 minutes, checking with a toothpick. Muffins will bake faster and have a slightly different texture but just as delicious.
Is this recipe suitable for freezing?
Yes, it freezes very well. Slice the bread first, wrap each slice in plastic wrap, and store in a freezer bag. Thaw slices as needed to enjoy fresh bread anytime.
Can I reduce the sugar in the cake mix?
Reducing sugar can affect texture and moisture since the cake mix is formulated for balance. If you want less sweetness, try a low-sugar or sugar-free yellow cake mix instead.
What’s the best way to grate zucchini?
Use the medium holes on a box grater or a food processor with a grating disk. Grate just before using to keep it fresh and prevent browning.
