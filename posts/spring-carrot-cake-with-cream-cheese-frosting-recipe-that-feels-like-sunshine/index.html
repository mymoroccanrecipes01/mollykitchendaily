Moist and Spiced Carrot Cake with Tangy Cream Cheese Frosting Perfect for Spring

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 19, 2026
20 min Prep Time
40 min Time
8 Servings
Medium Difficulty

This carrot cake is a fresh spring favorite—moist, tender, and bursting with warm spices. The silky cream cheese frosting adds just the right tang. Perfect for Easter brunch or an afternoon coffee break!

Introduction

I've been making carrot cake for years, but this particular version has become my go-to for springtime celebrations. The first time I baked it, I was aiming for a cake that felt fresh and light, yet still packed with the cozy spices that make carrot cake so special. What sets this recipe apart is the balance between the moist, tender crumb and the rich cream cheese frosting that’s just tangy enough to cut through the sweetness. I like to grate the carrots finely—not too chunky—so the texture is soft but still has that wonderful carrot presence. Also, tossing in walnuts adds a nice crunch, but that’s totally optional if you prefer it nut-free. The trick I learned is to mix the wet and dry ingredients just until combined; overmixing can make the cake dense, and nobody wants that. When baked, this cake comes out golden with a subtle spice aroma that fills the kitchen and makes it impossible to wait before slicing. It’s perfect for Easter brunch or a sunny afternoon tea, and honestly, it’s the kind of dessert that feels like a warm hug. If you’re a carrot cake fan or just want to try something new for spring, this recipe won’t disappoint.

What You'll Need

One 9-inch two-layer cake, 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup packed light brown sugar
  • 1 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 3 cups finely grated carrots (about 4-5 medium carrots)
  • 1 cup chopped walnuts (optional)
  • For the frosting:
  • 8 oz cream cheese, softened
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 3 cups powdered sugar, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and flour two 9-inch round cake pans or line them with parchment paper. This prep ensures the cake layers release easily after baking, preventing any sticking that can ruin the perfect edges.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pans are evenly greased with no dry spots
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves. Sifting aerates the flour and spices, helping the cake rise evenly and distributing the spices so every bite has balanced flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no lumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, beat the eggs with the granulated and brown sugar until pale and slightly thickened, about 3 minutes. This step incorporates air, which adds lightness to the cake crumb and helps dissolve the sugars for a smooth batter.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Egg mixture is lighter in color and slightly thick
Step 4
Gradually whisk in the vegetable oil and vanilla extract until fully combined. Adding the oil slowly ensures the mixture emulsifies properly, which keeps the cake moist and tender rather than dry or crumbly.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth and glossy with no separation
Step 5
Fold the dry ingredients gently into the wet mixture just until no streaks remain. Avoid overmixing here because it can develop gluten, making the cake tough instead of soft and delicate.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform in color with no visible flour patches
Step 6
Fold in the grated carrots and walnuts (if using) carefully to keep some texture but avoid overworking the batter. The carrots add moisture and natural sweetness, while walnuts provide a satisfying crunch that contrasts nicely with the tender crumb.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Carrots and nuts are evenly distributed throughout the batter
Step 7
Divide the batter evenly between the prepared pans and smooth the tops with a spatula. This helps the layers bake evenly and avoids domed or uneven cakes that can be tricky to frost.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and smooth in both pans
Step 8
Bake for 35-40 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Don’t open the oven door too early to prevent the cake from sinking. The smell of warm spices will fill your kitchen when it’s nearly done.
⏱ Time: 35-40 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick comes out mostly clean with no wet batter
Step 9
Let the cakes cool in the pans for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling in the pan briefly prevents the cakes from breaking apart, while finishing on a rack avoids sogginess from trapped steam.
⏱ Time: 20-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cakes are cool to the touch and hold their shape
Step 10
For the frosting, beat the softened cream cheese and butter together until smooth and fluffy. Gradually add the powdered sugar and vanilla, beating until light and creamy. This method ensures a silky frosting without lumps or graininess.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Frosting is smooth, spreadable, and holds soft peaks
Step 11
Spread a thick layer of frosting on the first cake layer, then top with the second layer and frost the top and sides evenly. Use a spatula or knife to create swirls or smooth it out for a clean finish. Chill briefly if you want firmer frosting before slicing.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Frosting is evenly spread with no crumbs mixed in

Variations

Pineapple Carrot Cake

Add 1 cup of crushed pineapple (drained) to the batter for extra moisture and a tropical twist. The pineapple pairs beautifully with the warm spices and cream cheese frosting, giving a subtle tangy sweetness that brightens every bite.

Note: Drain pineapple well to avoid excess moisture that could make the batter too wet and affect baking time slightly.

Gluten-Free Carrot Cake

Swap all-purpose flour with a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This adaptation keeps the cake tender and moist while making it suitable for gluten-sensitive eaters without sacrificing flavor.

Note: Mix gluten-free flour blend thoroughly and monitor baking time as it may vary slightly.

Vegan Carrot Cake

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use vegan cream cheese and butter alternatives. This version stays moist and flavorful with a slightly denser texture but still delicious.

Note: Let flax eggs gel for at least 10 minutes before mixing; texture will be denser so don’t overbake.

Spiced Orange Carrot Cake

Add 1 tablespoon orange zest to the batter and swap half the oil for melted butter. The citrus zest adds fresh brightness that complements the spices, making this cake feel even more festive and spring-ready.

Note: Use fresh organic oranges for best zest flavor; avoid the white pith which can add bitterness.

Carrot Cake Cupcakes

Pour the batter into cupcake liners for individual portions. Bake for 20-25 minutes and frost once cooled. Great for parties or easy grab-and-go treats!

Note: Check doneness early with a toothpick as cupcakes bake faster than full cakes.

Pro Tips

Grate carrots finely using the small holes of a box grater.
Finely grated carrots release their moisture evenly and blend into the batter without creating large, wet pockets. It also helps maintain the cake’s soft texture without too much chewiness.
Sift the dry ingredients together before mixing.
Sifting aerates the flour and spices, preventing clumps and helping the cake rise evenly. It also ensures spices are evenly distributed, so every bite has balanced flavor.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add flour.
Mixing too long develops gluten, making the cake dense and tough instead of tender. Folding gently until just combined yields a light, soft crumb.
Use room temperature eggs and cream cheese.
Room temp ingredients incorporate more smoothly, creating an even batter and silky frosting without lumps or curdling.
Cool the cake layers completely before frosting.
If the cake is warm, frosting will melt and slide off, ruining the appearance and texture. Patience pays off here for a clean, beautiful finish.
Add chopped walnuts for texture but toast them first.
Toasting walnuts brings out their flavor and crunch, adding a delicious contrast to the soft cake and creamy frosting.
Chill the frosted cake for 30 minutes before slicing.
Chilling firms up the frosting, making it easier to cut clean slices without smudging or tearing the cake layers.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens Many home bakers think mixing longer makes batter smoother and better combined, but it actually develops gluten, leading to dense, tough cake.

Fix Mix the flour in gently with a spatula or wooden spoon, folding until just combined. Stop as soon as you don’t see dry streaks.

Using cold cream cheese for frosting

Why it happens Some rush to frost the cake and don’t let cream cheese soften, resulting in lumpy, uneven frosting and difficulty spreading.

Fix Allow cream cheese to sit at room temperature for at least 30 minutes before beating. This ensures smooth, creamy frosting.

Not measuring flour correctly

Why it happens Scooping flour directly with a cup can pack it tightly, adding too much flour and drying the cake.

Fix Spoon flour lightly into measuring cups and level with a knife, or weigh for best accuracy.

Skipping the parchment paper or greasing pans unevenly

Why it happens Some assume greasing a pan alone is enough, but uneven greasing can cause the cake to stick and break on removal.

Fix Grease pans thoroughly and line with parchment paper for easy release.

Frosting warm cake layers

Why it happens Impatience leads to frosting a cake that’s still warm, causing the frosting to melt and slide off.

Fix Wait until the cake is completely cool before frosting to keep the frosting stable.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the frosted cake covered loosely with a cake dome or plastic wrap at room temperature for up to 2 days. Avoid direct sunlight or heat to prevent frosting melting.

Refrigerator

Keep the cake in an airtight container or covered tightly with plastic wrap in the fridge for up to 5 days. The frosting firms up and the cake stays moist, but bring to room temperature before serving for best flavor.

Freezer

Wrap the cake layers tightly in plastic wrap and then foil, freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge wrapped, then frost once fully thawed to avoid sogginess.

Reheating

Carrot cake is best served at room temperature, so no reheating needed. If preferred warm, microwave a slice for 10-15 seconds but avoid heating the frosting directly to prevent melting.

Make Ahead

Bake the cake layers 1-2 days ahead and store wrapped in plastic wrap at room temperature or fridge. Prepare the frosting the day you plan to serve and assemble shortly before guests arrive for freshest texture.

FAQ

Can I make this carrot cake dairy-free?
Absolutely! Swap the cream cheese and butter in the frosting for dairy-free alternatives like vegan cream cheese and margarine. For the cake, use a plant-based milk and oil as usual. The texture and flavor will still be delicious, just slightly different in richness.
How do I know when the carrot cake is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick into the center of the cake. It should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Also, the cake edges will start to pull away from the pan slightly, and the top should be golden brown.
Can I use pre-shredded carrots for this recipe?
I recommend grating fresh carrots yourself. Pre-shredded carrots can be dry or clump together, which affects moisture and texture. Freshly grated carrots release moisture evenly and blend better into the batter.
How long does the cream cheese frosting last?
When refrigerated, the frosting stays fresh for up to 5 days. Keep it covered tightly to prevent it from drying out or absorbing fridge odors. Bring to room temperature before spreading or serving for best texture.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes! Use a high-quality gluten-free flour blend that includes xanthan gum to mimic gluten’s structure. The texture will be slightly different but still moist and flavorful. Adjust baking time as needed and test doneness carefully.
What’s the best way to store leftover carrot cake?
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. Let the cake come to room temperature before eating for best flavor. You can also freeze slices wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Can I add nuts or raisins to the cake?
Yes, chopped walnuts or pecans are a classic addition and add nice crunch. Raisins also work well if you like a bit of chewiness. Add about 1 cup and fold them in with the carrots during the batter mixing step.
How do I avoid the frosting melting or sliding off?
Make sure the cake layers are completely cooled before frosting. Also, use room temperature cream cheese and butter to get a stable frosting. Chilling the frosted cake for 30 minutes before slicing helps it set and stay in place.
