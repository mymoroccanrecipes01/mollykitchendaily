Creamy Berry Chia Seed Pudding with Coconut Milk – Easy & Healthy Treat

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 8, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This berry chia pudding blends creamy coconut milk with fresh berries for a naturally sweet, guilt-free treat. Super easy to whip up and perfect for breakfast or a light dessert anytime.

Pro Tips

Shake the coconut milk can vigorously before opening.
Coconut milk naturally separates, with cream rising to the top. Shaking ensures an even, creamy base for the pudding. If you skip this, the pudding texture can be inconsistent, with watery spots and clumps.
Let the pudding chill overnight.
Chia seeds need several hours to fully absorb the liquid and swell properly. Rushing this step results in a thin, runny pudding that lacks the desired creamy texture.
Stir the pudding well after chilling.
Chia seeds can clump during soaking. Stirring breaks up these clumps and evens out the texture, giving you a smooth, spoonable pudding.
Use full-fat coconut milk for richness.
The fat in coconut milk gives the pudding a creamy mouthfeel and helps carry the flavors. Using light coconut milk or plant milks with less fat results in a thinner, less satisfying pudding.
Add a pinch of sea salt to the mixture.
Salt enhances sweetness and balances flavors, making the pudding taste richer and more complex without adding more sugar.
Serve with fresh berries just before eating.
Adding berries just before serving keeps them fresh and prevents them from releasing too much juice, which can thin the pudding.
If you dislike the seed texture, blend the mixture before chilling.
Blending breaks down the chia seeds, creating a smoother pudding without the crunchy texture some people find off-putting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not shaking the coconut milk before use.

Why it happens Many assume canned coconut milk is uniform, but it separates naturally, leading to uneven texture if not mixed.

Fix Always shake the can vigorously before measuring to ensure the cream and liquid are combined, giving the pudding a smooth consistency.

Skipping the chilling time or not chilling long enough.

Why it happens Impatience or forgetting to prepare the pudding ahead means the chia seeds don’t absorb enough liquid.

Fix Plan ahead and chill the pudding at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, so the seeds fully swell and thicken the mixture.

Not stirring the pudding after chilling.

Why it happens Some believe the pudding is ready straight from the fridge, but chia seeds tend to clump during soaking.

Fix Give the pudding a good stir after chilling to break up any clumps and ensure even texture.

Using low-fat or carton coconut milk instead of full-fat canned.

Why it happens Some opt for lighter options for fewer calories but don’t realize the impact on texture.

Fix Use full-fat canned coconut milk for the best creaminess and flavor; lighter versions produce a watery pudding.

Adding berries too early or thawing frozen berries directly into the pudding.

Why it happens Berries release juice that can thin the pudding if added too soon or thawed inside it.

Fix Add fresh berries just before serving. If using frozen, thaw and drain them before adding.

Variations

Chocolate Berry Chia Pudding

Add 2 tablespoons of unsweetened cocoa powder to the coconut milk mixture for a chocolate twist. The cocoa adds richness and pairs beautifully with the berries for a dessert-like feel.

Note: Mix cocoa powder thoroughly with coconut milk to avoid lumps before adding chia seeds.

Tropical Mango Chia Pudding

Swap the berries for diced fresh mango and add a tablespoon of shredded coconut on top. This variation brings a sunny, tropical vibe and a sweet, juicy flavor profile.

Note: Use ripe mango for best sweetness and texture; add coconut flakes just before serving to keep them crunchy.

Vanilla Almond Chia Pudding

Replace coconut milk with almond milk and increase vanilla extract to 2 teaspoons. Top with sliced almonds and a drizzle of honey for a nutty, fragrant pudding.

Note: Almond milk produces a lighter pudding, so expect a thinner texture compared to coconut milk.

Spiced Chia Pudding

Add 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg to the pudding base. These warm spices add depth and pair nicely with the berries for a cozy flavor perfect for fall mornings.

Note: Mix spices well with the liquid before adding chia seeds to distribute flavor evenly.

Green Matcha Chia Pudding

Whisk 1 teaspoon of matcha powder into the coconut milk mixture for a vibrant green pudding with a subtle earthy flavor. Top with berries for a colorful and antioxidant-rich breakfast.

Note: Sift matcha powder to avoid clumps and whisk thoroughly for smooth color and flavor.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Chia pudding should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours to avoid spoilage. Keep it covered if you need to leave it out briefly.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The pudding thickens as it sits, so stir in a splash of coconut milk before serving if it becomes too dense. It tastes best chilled and does not require reheating.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the texture changes and the seeds may separate upon thawing. If you must freeze, store in a sealed container for up to 1 month and thaw overnight in the fridge, then stir well.

Reheating

This pudding is best served cold or at room temperature. Avoid reheating as heat breaks down the gel structure and alters texture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the pudding base up to 2 days ahead and keep it refrigerated. Add fresh berries just before serving to keep them fresh and vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use almond milk or other plant milks instead of coconut milk?
Yes, you can substitute almond, oat, or soy milk, but expect a thinner pudding since these milks have less fat. To compensate, you might add a bit more chia seeds or reduce liquid slightly. Coconut milk provides the creamiest texture.
How long does chia pudding last in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, chia pudding lasts up to 4 days in the fridge. The texture thickens over time, so stir in a splash of coconut milk before serving if it becomes too dense.
Why is my chia pudding too runny?
Chia pudding needs enough time to soak—at least 4 hours, preferably overnight. Also, make sure you use the right ratio of chia seeds to liquid (about 1:6). Not shaking the coconut milk well can also affect texture.
Can I add sweeteners other than maple syrup?
Absolutely! Honey, agave, or coconut sugar work well. Just adjust the amount to your taste. Avoid artificial sweeteners as they don't blend well with the creamy texture.
Is it normal to see some liquid on top after chilling?
Sometimes a bit of liquid separates on top—just stir it back in. This happens if the pudding sits for a long time or if the chia seeds didn't absorb fully. Stirring restores the creamy texture.
Can I make chia pudding without vanilla extract?
Yes, but vanilla adds a lovely depth and warmth to the pudding’s flavor. If you skip it, consider adding a pinch of cinnamon or nutmeg for extra flavor.
How can I avoid chia seed clumps?
Whisk the chia seeds vigorously into the liquid right at the start. Stir again after 10 minutes during soaking to break up any forming clumps. This ensures an even, smooth pudding.
Can I add protein powder to this pudding?
Yes, adding a scoop of your favorite protein powder is a great way to boost nutrition. Mix it thoroughly with the liquid before adding chia seeds to avoid lumps.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 1/4 cup chia seeds
  • 1 1/2 cups canned coconut milk, full fat, well shaken
  • 2 tablespoons pure maple syrup or honey
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup mixed fresh berries (blueberries, raspberries, strawberries)
  • Optional: a pinch of sea salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by shaking your can of coconut milk really well. This is crucial because the cream tends to separate and settle at the top. You want a smooth, even texture in your pudding, so mixing it well ensures the chia seeds soak in the right consistency. Pour 1 1/2 cups of the coconut milk into a mixing bowl. This step sets the creamy base, so don’t skip the shaking—otherwise, your pudding might be uneven or grainy.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: The liquid should be smooth and homogenous with no visible separation.
Step 2
Add 1/4 cup of chia seeds, 2 tablespoons maple syrup (or honey), and 1 teaspoon vanilla extract to the coconut milk. Whisk everything together thoroughly to avoid clumps. The chia seeds will start absorbing the liquid and swell, creating the pudding texture. Mixing well now helps prevent clumps from forming later, giving you a smooth pudding instead of lumpy spots.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks evenly combined with chia seeds suspended and no clumps.
Step 3
Cover the bowl with plastic wrap or transfer the mixture into a jar with a lid. Place it in the refrigerator and let it chill for at least 4 hours, but preferably overnight. This resting time is key because it allows the chia seeds to fully absorb the coconut milk, swell, and create that thick, creamy pudding texture. Rushing this step results in a thinner, less satisfying pudding.
⏱ Time: 4+ hours
✓ Doneness: Pudding is thick, spoonable, and no longer liquid.
Step 4
Once the pudding has set, give it a good stir to break up any clumps and even out the texture. This step ensures your pudding is creamy and smooth. If it’s too thick for your liking, you can stir in a splash of coconut milk to loosen it slightly. The texture should be creamy but still hold its shape on a spoon.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth pudding with no lumps and creamy consistency.
Step 5
Prepare your fresh berries by rinsing and gently patting them dry. You can use a mix of blueberries, raspberries, and chopped strawberries for a nice balance of flavors and colors. Fresh berries add a lovely burst of freshness and slight tartness that contrasts beautifully with the creamy pudding.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Berries are clean, fresh, and ready to add.
Step 6
Serve the chia pudding in bowls or jars topped generously with the fresh berry mix. You can add a few extra drizzles of maple syrup or a sprinkle of toasted coconut flakes if you want some crunch. This finishing touch makes it feel extra special and adds texture contrast.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pudding looks inviting with fresh berries on top.
Step 7
If you prefer a smoother texture, you can blend the pudding before chilling it. Use a blender to pulse the mixture briefly after adding the chia seeds. This breaks the seeds down and creates a more pudding-like consistency rather than a gel. It’s a nice option if you’re not a fan of the tiny seed texture but still want all the benefits.
⏱ Time: Optional, 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks creamy and smooth without whole seeds visible.
Step 8
For extra flavor, try adding a pinch of sea salt to the mixture before chilling. Salt enhances the sweetness and balances the flavors, making the pudding taste richer and more complex. It’s a small trick that really elevates the final result.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Mixture tastes balanced, not overly sweet.
Step 9
Store any leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 4 days. The pudding will thicken further as it sits, so you might need to stir in a little coconut milk before serving again. This pudding is versatile and keeps well, making it a great make-ahead option for busy mornings.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Pudding remains fresh and creamy after storage.
