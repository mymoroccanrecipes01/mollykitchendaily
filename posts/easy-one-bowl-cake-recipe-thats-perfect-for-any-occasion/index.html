One Bowl Moist Vanilla Cake That’s Perfect for Any Occasion

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 30 min cook 8 servings

This one bowl vanilla cake is a total lifesaver when you want something moist and delicious without the fuss. Minimal cleanup and simple ingredients make it ideal for quick celebrations or a cozy treat anytime.

Why This Works

The magic of this vanilla cake lies in its simplicity and the careful balance of ingredients and technique. Using just one bowl minimizes overmixing and reduces the chance of developing too much gluten, which is what can make cakes tough. The creaming of butter and sugar is a critical step that traps air bubbles, providing the lift and light crumb we desire.

Alternating the addition of dry ingredients and milk helps maintain a smooth batter without overworking it. The room temperature ingredients play a big role here too — cold eggs or milk can cause the batter to seize, leading to uneven texture. The baking powder acts as the leavening agent, releasing carbon dioxide during baking to help the cake rise.

Proper oven temperature and timing are also key. Baking at 350°F allows the cake to cook through evenly while developing a tender golden crust. Checking doneness with a toothpick prevents overbaking, which dries out the cake. Finally, the gentle cooling and removal from the pan prevents the cake from becoming soggy and helps maintain its light texture.

Overall, this recipe combines classic baking science with practical shortcuts to deliver a moist, flavorful, and beautifully textured vanilla cake with minimal effort and cleanup.

  • Choose a good quality all-purpose flour with moderate protein content (10-12%). This balance provides enough gluten to structure the cake without making it tough. Sifting removes lumps and aerates the flour, helping create a lighter crumb. Avoid using bread flour as it’s too high in protein and will yield a dense cake.
  • Use fresh, double-acting baking powder to ensure proper rise. It reacts first when wet and again in the oven’s heat. Old or expired baking powder can cause the cake to be flat and dense. Measure carefully because too much can cause a bitter taste.
  • Softened unsalted butter allows you to control the salt level while providing richness and moisture. Creaming butter with sugar incorporates air, which is crucial for a fluffy cake. Avoid melted butter here as it won’t trap air as effectively.
  • Room temperature eggs blend more easily into batter, creating a smooth, stable emulsion. They provide structure and moisture, as well as richness. Adding them one at a time prevents curdling and keeps the batter smooth.
  • Whole milk adds fat and moisture, contributing to the cake’s tender crumb. Using room temperature milk prevents the batter from seizing and helps ingredients blend evenly. You can substitute with buttermilk for a slightly tangier flavor and more tender texture.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 2 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 3/4 cups granulated sugar
  • 4 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 tablespoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 cup whole milk, room temperature

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9-inch round cake pan with butter or non-stick spray. This step ensures your cake won’t stick and will come out cleanly after baking. Also, line the bottom with parchment paper for extra insurance against sticking. Proper oven temperature is crucial here to get a nicely risen cake with a golden crust without drying it out.

    ⏱ 10✓ Pan is evenly greased with no dry spots, parchment paper fits snugly at the bottom
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, and salt until evenly combined. Sifting helps aerate the flour and remove lumps, which leads to a lighter cake texture. Mixing these dry ingredients first ensures even distribution of leavening agents so your cake rises uniformly without dense spots.

    ⏱ 5✓ Dry ingredients appear well blended, no clumps visible
  3. Step 3

    Add the softened butter and sugar to the dry ingredients. Using an electric mixer, beat on medium speed until the mixture looks creamy and pale—about 3-4 minutes. This creaming step is what traps air into the batter, contributing to the cake’s light, fluffy crumb. Don’t rush this part; under-creaming can cause a dense cake.

    ⏱ 4✓ Mixture is pale, smooth, and fluffy
  4. Step 4

    Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. This gradual incorporation helps keep the batter smooth and prevents it from curdling. If you add eggs too quickly, the batter may split, resulting in a less tender crumb.

    ⏱ 5✓ Batter is smooth and glossy after all eggs are incorporated
  5. Step 5

    Pour in the vanilla extract and mix briefly to combine. Vanilla adds warmth and depth to the flavor, making the cake taste richer and more inviting. Be sure to use pure vanilla extract for the best results.

    ⏱ 1✓ Vanilla evenly mixed with no streaks
  6. Step 6

    Alternately add the flour mixture and milk to the batter, starting and ending with the dry ingredients. Mix on low speed just until combined after each addition. Overmixing at this stage develops gluten, which can make the cake tough, so keep it gentle and brief.

    ⏱ 5✓ Batter is smooth, no visible streaks of flour or milk
  7. Step 7

    Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the cake bake evenly and rise uniformly. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles trapped in the batter.

    ⏱ 3✓ Batter surface is even and free of large bubbles
  8. Step 8

    Bake the cake in the center of the oven for 30-35 minutes. Check doneness by inserting a toothpick into the center—if it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, it’s done. Avoid opening the oven door too early to prevent the cake from sinking.

    ⏱ 30✓ Golden top, toothpick inserted comes out clean or with moist crumbs
  9. Step 9

    Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then run a knife around the edges and invert onto a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling in the pan too long can cause sogginess, but flipping it too soon risks breaking the cake. This step ensures the perfect texture and easy removal.

    ⏱ 15✓ Cake releases easily from pan and cools evenly on rack

Variations

  • Lemon One Bowl Cake: Add 2 tablespoons of fresh lemon juice and 1 teaspoon lemon zest to the batter for a fresh, tangy twist. This brightens the flavor and pairs beautifully with a light glaze or whipped cream.
    Reduce the milk by 2 tablespoons to compensate for added lemon juice and avoid thinning the batter.
  • Chocolate Swirl Cake: Stir in 1/3 cup cocoa powder with 1/4 cup milk and swirl it gently into half the batter for a marbled effect. This adds a rich chocolate flavor without overpowering the vanilla base.
    Do not overmix the swirl to maintain a pretty pattern and light texture.
  • Gluten-Free Version: Swap all-purpose flour for a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend. The texture will be slightly denser but still moist and delicious, perfect for those avoiding gluten.
    Add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t contain it, to improve structure.
  • Buttermilk Vanilla Cake: Replace whole milk with buttermilk for a tangier flavor and extra moist crumb. The acidity tenderizes the gluten and gives a subtle richness to the cake.
    Reduce baking powder to 2 teaspoons and add 1/2 teaspoon baking soda to balance acidity.
  • Vanilla Almond Cake: Add 1/2 teaspoon almond extract along with vanilla and fold in 1/3 cup sliced almonds for a nutty crunch. This adds complexity and a lovely texture contrast.
    Toast almonds lightly before folding to enhance flavor and prevent sogginess.

Pro Tips

  • Always use room temperature ingredients. — Room temperature eggs and milk blend more smoothly into the batter, preventing curdling and helping the cake rise evenly. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to seize and result in a dense, uneven crumb.
  • Don’t overmix after adding flour. — Overmixing develops gluten, which makes the cake tough and chewy rather than tender. Mix just until dry ingredients are incorporated to keep the crumb soft and light.
  • Cream butter and sugar thoroughly. — This step traps air into the batter, which is essential for a fluffy texture. Under-creaming can lead to a denser cake, so beat until the mixture is pale and fluffy.
  • Use fresh baking powder. — Baking powder loses potency over time, which can cause your cake to not rise properly. Always check the expiration date for best results.
  • Line the bottom of your pan with parchment paper. — Even when greased, cakes sometimes stick to the bottom. Parchment ensures easy release and a clean bottom crust.
  • Let the cake cool before frosting or slicing. — Cutting or frosting a warm cake can cause it to crumble or melt the frosting. Cooling helps it set and hold its shape better.
  • Tap the pan gently before baking. — This releases large air bubbles trapped in the batter, preventing holes or tunnels in the cake crumb.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using cold ingredients
    Why: Many people forget to take eggs and milk out ahead of time, or use refrigerated butter, which can cause uneven mixing.
    Fix: Plan ahead and leave eggs and milk on the counter for at least 30 minutes before baking. Soften butter by letting it sit at room temperature until it’s easily indented but not melted.
  • Overmixing batter after flour is added
    Why: It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid lumps, but this develops gluten.
    Fix: Mix just until you no longer see dry flour. A few small lumps are okay and will bake out, ensuring a tender crumb.
  • Opening the oven door too early
    Why: Impatience or curiosity can cause this, but it lets heat escape.
    Fix: Wait at least 25 minutes before checking doneness. Opening early can cause the cake to sink or bake unevenly.
  • Not greasing the pan properly
    Why: Skipping greasing or using insufficient butter can cause sticking.
    Fix: Grease the pan thoroughly and line the bottom with parchment paper for easiest release.
  • Using expired baking powder
    Why: Baking powder loses effectiveness over time, especially if stored improperly.
    Fix: Test baking powder by adding a teaspoon to hot water—it should bubble vigorously. Replace if it doesn’t.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Store the cooled cake covered loosely with a clean kitchen towel or cake dome for up to 2 days. This keeps it moist but avoid leaving it uncovered to prevent drying out.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Keep the cake in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap for up to 5 days. The texture firms up slightly when chilled; bring it to room temperature before serving for best flavor and softness.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge, then bring to room temperature before eating to restore softness.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm slices gently in a microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) wrapped in foil for 10 minutes. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out. A small splash of milk or butter can refresh moisture if needed.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the batter a few hours ahead and keep it refrigerated tightly covered. Bring it to room temperature and give a gentle stir before baking. Alternatively, bake the cake a day ahead and store it wrapped; this actually helps flavors develop.

FAQ

Can I use oil instead of butter in this cake?
Yes, you can substitute an equal amount of neutral oil like vegetable or canola oil for butter. The cake will be moist but lose some richness and flavor that butter provides. Also, the texture might be slightly different, more tender but less fluffy.
What if I don’t have an electric mixer?
You can definitely mix by hand using a sturdy whisk or wooden spoon, but it will take more time and effort, especially for creaming the butter and sugar. Make sure your butter is very soft to make this easier. Mixing thoroughly is key to a light cake.
How do I know when the cake is done?
Insert a toothpick or thin skewer into the center of the cake. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the cake is done. Avoid waiting until it’s completely dry, as that can indicate overbaking and dryness.
Can I make this cake dairy-free?
Yes, substitute the butter with a dairy-free margarine or coconut oil and use a plant-based milk like almond or oat milk. The texture may vary slightly, but it will still be delicious.
Why does my cake sometimes sink in the middle?
Common causes include opening the oven door too early, overmixing the batter, or using expired baking powder. Make sure to follow mixing and baking times carefully and check your leavening agents.
Can I double this recipe?
Absolutely! Just double all ingredients and bake in a larger pan or two pans. Baking time may vary, so start checking doneness a bit earlier and adjust as needed.
What frosting pairs well with this cake?
Classic buttercream, cream cheese frosting, or a simple vanilla glaze all work beautifully. For a lighter option, whipped cream with fresh berries is lovely too.
Is it okay to use cake flour instead of all-purpose?
Yes, cake flour will produce an even lighter, softer crumb because it has less protein. Use slightly more cake flour (about 2 tablespoons extra per cup) or sift well to compensate for its lower density.
