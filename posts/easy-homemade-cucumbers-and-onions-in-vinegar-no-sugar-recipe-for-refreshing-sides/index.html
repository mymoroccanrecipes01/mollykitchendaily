Simple Cucumber and Onion Salad in Tangy Vinegar Dressing

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This crisp cucumber and onion salad is a refreshing side that adds a bright, tangy kick to any meal. The vinegar soak softens the onions just right, while keeping the cucumbers crunchy. Perfect for summer dinners or to lighten up hearty dishes!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this cucumber and onion salad years ago during a family barbecue, and it instantly became a staple in my summer cooking. What I love most about this version is its simplicity—the tangy vinegar soak really softens the onions without overpowering the crisp cucumbers. I used to rush the soaking step, and the onions were too sharp and biting, but now I let them mellow in the vinegar-water mix for at least 15 minutes. This little trick balances the flavors perfectly. The salad is light, refreshing, and super easy to whip up anytime you want to add a little brightness to your meal. It pairs beautifully with grilled meats or even rich comfort foods, cutting through heaviness with a crisp bite. Plus, it’s naturally vegan and sugar-free unless you choose to add a pinch of sugar for balance. The finished salad has this lovely mix of textures—crisp cucumbers with tender, tangy onions—and a fresh herb garnish can take it to the next level if you’re feeling fancy. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes I keep coming back to because it’s so reliable and pleasing to everyone at the table.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, 4 servings

  • 2 medium cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup white vinegar
  • 1/2 cup cold water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon sugar (optional)
  • Fresh dill or parsley for garnish (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing the cucumbers and red onion. Use a sharp knife or a mandoline for even slices—this helps the salad absorb the dressing evenly and gives a nice texture contrast. Thin slices of onion get tender quickly and infuse flavor without being too harsh. If your cucumbers have thick skin, peeling them partially can reduce bitterness and improve the salad’s overall taste.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Slices are even and thin, about 1/8 inch thick, with onions slightly translucent
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the white vinegar, cold water, salt, pepper, and sugar if using. This mixture creates the perfect tangy base that softens the onions and seasons the cucumbers gently. The sugar helps balance the acidity but is totally optional if you prefer a sharper bite. Stir until the salt and sugar dissolve completely for even flavor distribution.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The liquid is clear and well mixed with no grainy salt or sugar
Step 3
Add the sliced cucumbers and onions to the vinegar mixture, making sure they’re fully submerged. If they float, press them down gently with a spoon. Soaking them is key—it mellows the onion’s pungency and allows the cucumbers to soak up the bright, tangy flavor. Let this sit at least 15 minutes, or up to an hour in the fridge for a more intense flavor.
⏱ Time: 15-60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions turn slightly translucent and cucumbers look glossy but still firm
Step 4
Before serving, give the salad a gentle stir to redistribute the dressing. Taste and adjust seasoning if needed—sometimes a pinch more salt or pepper enhances the flavors. Drain off excess liquid if you prefer a less wet salad. Garnish with fresh herbs like dill or parsley for a pop of color and fresh aroma.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad is evenly coated, fresh herbs brightening the presentation
Step 5
Serve chilled or at room temperature alongside your favorite grilled meats, sandwiches, or comfort food dishes. This salad shines as a refreshing contrast to heavier meals, cutting through richness and adding a crisp bite. It’s also great for picnics or potlucks since it holds up well when chilled.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Salad is crisp, tangy, and fresh tasting
Step 6
If you want to prep in advance, cover the salad tightly and store it in the fridge. The flavors deepen over time, but cucumbers may soften a bit, so consume within 24 hours for best texture. Stir gently before serving to redistribute the dressing.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Salad remains fresh and vibrant but softer after refrigeration
Step 7
For variation, try adding thin slices of radish or a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes for a subtle heat. These additions bring extra crunch and a little kick, making the salad even more exciting without complicating the base recipe.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Extra ingredients evenly mixed and visually appealing
Step 8
If cucumbers are watery, sprinkle them lightly with salt and let them sit for 10 minutes before mixing with onions. Then rinse and pat dry to avoid a soggy salad. This step helps maintain crispness and prevents the dressing from becoming diluted.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes prep
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers feel firm and not overly wet
Step 9
Use a non-reactive bowl like glass or ceramic for mixing to avoid any metallic taste from the vinegar reacting with metal. It also preserves the fresh flavors better. Avoid aluminum or cast iron bowls for this step.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: No off-flavors detected, salad tastes fresh

Variations

Spicy Cucumber and Onion Salad

Add a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes or thinly sliced fresh chili to the vinegar mixture for a subtle heat that livens up the salad. This variation pairs wonderfully with grilled meats and adds a warming contrast to the cool cucumbers.

Note: Adjust soaking time to 10-15 minutes to allow chili flavor to infuse without overpowering.

Creamy Dill Cucumber Salad

Mix in a few tablespoons of sour cream or Greek yogurt with fresh dill after soaking the cucumbers and onions. This creates a creamy, tangy dressing that softens the sharpness of the vinegar and adds richness.

Note: Serve immediately after mixing to maintain cucumber crunch and avoid sogginess.

Sweet and Tangy Cucumber Salad

Increase sugar to 2 teaspoons and add a splash of honey for a sweeter profile that balances the vinegar’s acidity. This sweeter version is great for kids or those who prefer milder tang.

Note: Reduce soaking time slightly to prevent overly soft onions.

Asian-Inspired Cucumber Onion Salad

Replace white vinegar with rice vinegar and add a teaspoon of sesame oil and a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds. This twist brings a nutty aroma and subtle sweetness for an exotic flavor profile.

Note: Use less salt to compensate for the saltiness of sesame oil.

Radish and Cucumber Salad

Add thinly sliced radishes for extra crunch and a peppery bite. This variation adds color and a new flavor dimension, making the salad visually appealing and taste-wise more complex.

Note: Radishes soak well but maintain crunch; soak all vegetables together for even flavor.

Pro Tips

Use a mandoline slicer for uniform thin slices.
Uniform slices ensure even soaking of vinegar and consistent texture throughout the salad. Uneven slices can lead to some pieces being too soft or too crunchy, disrupting the harmony of flavors and mouthfeel.
Soak onions longer for milder flavor.
Allowing onions to soak for at least 15 minutes in the vinegar solution reduces their sharpness by breaking down sulfur compounds. Rushing this step results in harsh, overpowering bites that can overwhelm the salad.
Drain excess liquid before serving if you prefer a drier salad.
Too much liquid can dilute flavors and make the salad soggy. Draining helps maintain crispness and concentrates the vinegar seasoning on the vegetables.
Add fresh herbs like dill or parsley for extra freshness.
Herbs introduce aromatic oils and fresh flavors that brighten the salad and add complexity without changing the base recipe’s simplicity.
Salt cucumbers before mixing to reduce water content.
Salting draws out moisture from cucumbers, preventing a watery salad and helping them stay crisp longer. Rinsing excess salt afterward avoids over-seasoning.
Use a glass or ceramic bowl for mixing.
Vinegar can react with metal bowls, imparting off-flavors and discoloring the salad. Non-reactive bowls preserve the salad’s fresh taste and appearance.
Serve the salad chilled for maximum refreshment.
Cold temperatures slow enzymatic breakdown and maintain cucumber crispness, making the salad more refreshing especially on hot days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using thick cucumber slices that don’t soak well.

Why it happens People often slice too thick to save time or due to lack of proper tools, not realizing thick slices won’t absorb the dressing evenly.

Fix Invest in a mandoline or slice as thin as possible with a sharp knife. Thin slices soak quickly, balancing flavor and texture perfectly.

Skipping the vinegar soak step or soaking too briefly.

Why it happens Impatience or misunderstanding the recipe leads to under-soaked onions that remain pungent and overpower the salad.

Fix Allow at least 15 minutes soaking time. This mellows onion bite and blends flavors harmoniously.

Using a reactive metal bowl to mix the salad.

Why it happens Many cooks use whatever bowl is available without considering vinegar’s acidity reacting with metals.

Fix Always use glass, ceramic, or food-grade plastic bowls to avoid metallic off-flavors and discoloration.

Not adjusting salt and seasoning after soaking.

Why it happens People assume initial seasoning is enough, but flavors mellow after soaking, requiring adjustment before serving.

Fix Taste salad after soaking and add salt or pepper as needed to balance flavors.

Storing salad too long causing cucumbers to become mushy.

Why it happens Extended refrigeration breaks down cucumber cell walls, especially if stored with excess liquid.

Fix Consume within 24 hours and store in airtight containers. Drain excess liquid before storing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad can safely stay at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered. Beyond that, refrigeration is recommended to prevent spoilage and maintain freshness.

Refrigerator

Store the salad in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 24 hours. The cucumbers may soften slightly over time, so it’s best eaten within a day. Stir gently before serving to redistribute dressing.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers become mushy and lose their crisp texture upon thawing. For best quality, prepare fresh or store refrigerated.

Reheating

This salad is served cold or at room temperature and should not be reheated. If you prefer a warmer side, consider a different recipe.

Make Ahead

Slice cucumbers and onions separately and store in airtight containers. Prepare the vinegar dressing in advance. Combine and soak the vegetables in the dressing 15-30 minutes before serving for freshest flavor and texture.

FAQ

Can I use other types of vinegar for this salad?
Absolutely! While white vinegar is classic for its clean acidity, you can substitute with apple cider or rice vinegar for a slightly different flavor. Just keep in mind that darker vinegars may alter the salad’s color and taste. Adjust the amount to balance acidity to your preference.
How long can I store this salad in the fridge?
For best texture and flavor, consume within 24 hours. The cucumbers tend to release water and become soft over time. If you need to store longer, keep the vegetables and dressing separate and combine just before serving.
Is it necessary to soak the onions in vinegar?
Soaking the onions mellows their sharpness and makes the salad more balanced and enjoyable. Skipping this step can leave the onions too pungent and overpower the cucumbers.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Yes! Thinly sliced radishes, bell peppers, or even cherry tomatoes can add color and flavor. Just slice thinly so they soak evenly and maintain a pleasant texture.
What if I don’t have a mandoline slicer?
A sharp knife works fine—just take your time to slice as thinly and evenly as possible. Uniform slices soak better and create a more harmonious salad.
Can I make this salad vegan and sugar-free?
Definitely! This recipe is naturally vegan. The sugar is optional and only used to balance acidity. You can omit it or substitute with a natural sweetener like maple syrup if desired.
Why does the salad sometimes get watery?
Cucumbers have high water content that can release moisture over time. Salting cucumbers before mixing or draining excess liquid after soaking helps prevent a watery salad.
Can I prepare this salad a day ahead?
You can prep the slices and dressing separately a day ahead, but combine and soak just before serving to keep cucumbers crisp and onions perfectly softened.
