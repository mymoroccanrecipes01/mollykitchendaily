Soft and Chewy 5-Ingredient Gluten-Free Chocolate Chip Cookies

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
12 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These gluten-free chocolate chip cookies are incredibly simple and quick to whip up with just five ingredients. They come out soft and chewy every time, making them a perfect snack or last-minute dessert when you need something sweet fast.

Introduction

I stumbled on this recipe one afternoon when I had a sudden craving for chocolate chip cookies but wanted to avoid gluten. I’ve tried a bunch of gluten-free recipes before, but many ended up dry or crumbly. This one, however, surprised me with its soft and chewy texture despite using just five main ingredients. What really makes these cookies stand out is the almond flour; it keeps them moist and tender without any weird aftertaste. Plus, using coconut sugar adds a subtle caramel note that pairs beautifully with the chocolate chips. I’ve made these several times now, and each batch turns out perfectly. The trick I learned is to not overbake them—pull them out when the edges are just golden, and they’ll finish cooking on the tray, staying soft inside. These cookies are great for a quick snack or a last-minute dessert, and they’re so simple you can whip them up even on a busy day. I love keeping a batch in the freezer for whenever the sweet tooth hits!

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 cookies

  • 1 cup almond flour, finely ground
  • 1/4 cup coconut sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup dairy-free chocolate chips
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment paper. This step ensures your cookies bake evenly and don’t stick. Using parchment also helps with easy clean-up. Make sure your oven is fully preheated before baking so the cookies cook at the right temperature from the start, which prevents spreading too much or drying out.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven reaches steady 350°F and parchment paper is ready
Step 2
In a mixing bowl, combine 1 cup almond flour, 1/4 cup coconut sugar, 1/4 teaspoon baking soda, and 1/4 teaspoon salt. Mix these dry ingredients well to ensure even distribution of the leavening agent and salt, which helps the cookies rise slightly and enhances flavor balance.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are evenly mixed with no clumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together 1 large egg and 1 teaspoon vanilla extract until smooth. The egg binds the dough and adds moisture, while vanilla adds that classic cookie aroma. Whisking ensures the egg is fully blended for consistent texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Egg and vanilla form a smooth, pale mixture
Step 4
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and stir gently until just combined. Overmixing can lead to dense cookies, so mix until you no longer see dry flour. The dough should be slightly sticky but easy to handle.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough is uniform with no streaks of flour
Step 5
Fold in 1/4 cup dairy-free chocolate chips evenly into the dough. Folding rather than stirring aggressively keeps the chips intact and distributes them nicely throughout the dough for a perfect bite every time.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly scattered through dough
Step 6
Use a tablespoon or cookie scoop to drop dough balls onto the prepared baking sheet, spacing them about 2 inches apart. This spacing allows the cookies room to spread without merging. Slightly flatten each ball with your fingers for an even shape and quicker bake.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough balls evenly spaced and lightly flattened
Step 7
Bake in the preheated oven for 10-12 minutes. Watch for the edges to turn golden but keep the centers soft. Baking just until edges set ensures the cookies remain chewy rather than crisp or dry. Avoid opening the oven too early to prevent collapse.
⏱ Time: 12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges are lightly golden; centers appear set but soft
Step 8
Remove the cookies from the oven and let them cool on the baking sheet for 10 minutes. This resting time allows the cookies to firm up as residual heat finishes the baking process, preventing them from breaking apart when moved too soon.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cookies hold shape and are slightly firm to the touch
Step 9
Transfer cookies to a wire rack to cool completely before storing or serving. Cooling fully helps develop their chewy texture and prevents sogginess. Store in an airtight container once cooled to maintain freshness.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cookies are completely cool and chewy

Variations

Add Chopped Nuts

Mix in 1/4 cup chopped walnuts or pecans for extra crunch and flavor. The nuts add texture and a toasty note that complements the chocolate chips beautifully.

Note: Fold nuts in gently after mixing the dough to avoid overworking and maintain cookie tenderness.

Use Dark Chocolate Chunks

Swap chocolate chips for chopped dark chocolate bars for a richer, more intense chocolate flavor. The chunks melt unevenly, creating gooey pockets in the cookies.

Note: Chop chocolate into small pieces to ensure even melting and distribution.

Add a Pinch of Cinnamon

Stir in 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon for a warm, cozy twist. Cinnamon pairs wonderfully with almond flour and chocolate for an inviting flavor profile.

Note: Add cinnamon with the dry ingredients to distribute evenly.

Make Vegan with Flax Egg

Replace the egg with a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water). This keeps the recipe plant-based but may yield slightly less chewy cookies.

Note: Let the flax egg sit for 5 minutes to gel before mixing into dry ingredients.

Add Sea Salt Topping

Sprinkle a pinch of flaky sea salt on top of each cookie dough ball before baking. The salt enhances sweetness and adds a gourmet touch.

Note: Add salt just before baking so it remains visible and crunchy.

Pro Tips

Don’t overmix your dough once you add the wet ingredients.
Overmixing develops the almond flour’s oils too much and can make the dough dense instead of tender. Mixing just until combined ensures the cookies stay soft and chewy with a delicate crumb.
Use room temperature eggs for better mixing.
Room temperature eggs blend more evenly with dry ingredients, preventing clumps and ensuring a uniform dough texture. Cold eggs can cause the fat in almond flour to seize, creating uneven cookies.
Let cookies cool on the baking sheet before transferring.
Cookies continue to set as they cool due to residual heat. Moving them too early can cause them to break apart. Cooling on the sheet helps maintain shape and chewiness.
Measure almond flour carefully by spooning into your measuring cup.
Spoon and level almond flour rather than scooping directly from the bag to avoid compacting it. Too much flour results in dry, crumbly cookies; accurate measurement keeps texture perfect.
Use parchment paper or a silicone mat for baking.
This prevents sticking and promotes even baking. It also helps cookies brown evenly on the bottom without burning or sticking to the pan.
Add a pinch of salt even if your chocolate chips are sweet.
Salt balances sweetness and enhances flavor complexity. It also accentuates the nutty notes of almond flour and the caramel tones of coconut sugar.
Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature.
Proper storage keeps cookies soft and chewy for several days. Exposure to air dries them out quickly, so sealing them preserves moisture and flavor.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overbaking the cookies

Why it happens Many assume cookies should be browned all over before removing from the oven, but gluten-free cookies can dry out easily if baked too long.

Fix Remove cookies when edges are lightly golden but centers still look soft. Let them cool on the baking sheet to finish cooking gently and retain moisture.

Using the wrong type of almond flour

Why it happens Some use almond meal or coarser flour, which results in gritty texture rather than tender cookies.

Fix Use finely ground blanched almond flour for smooth dough and tender crumb. Avoid almond meal with skins included, as it produces rough texture.

Skipping the baking soda

Why it happens People sometimes think it’s optional, but without it, cookies can be dense and flat.

Fix Include the specified baking soda amount to help cookies rise slightly and brown properly, improving texture and flavor.

Not spacing cookies properly on the baking sheet

Why it happens Crowding causes cookies to merge and bake unevenly.

Fix Space dough balls about 2 inches apart to allow spreading and even heat exposure.

Mixing dough too vigorously

Why it happens Trying to get a perfectly smooth dough can lead to overmixing, toughening the cookies.

Fix Stir gently until ingredients are just combined, preserving tenderness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Cookies keep well at room temperature for up to 2 days if stored in an airtight container. Avoid leaving them exposed to air as they’ll dry out and lose their chewy texture.

Refrigerator

Store cookies in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. They may firm up slightly but can be softened by warming briefly before serving.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a sealed container or freezer bag for up to 3 months. To prevent freezer burn, separate layers with parchment paper. Thaw at room temperature before eating.

Reheating

Warm cookies in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5 minutes to restore softness. Avoid overheating as it can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the dough a day ahead and refrigerate it wrapped tightly. This allows flavors to meld and makes baking fresh cookies quick and easy the next day.

FAQ

Can I substitute almond flour with another gluten-free flour?
Almond flour gives these cookies their moist, tender texture, so swapping it for other gluten-free flours like rice or oat flour will change the result. These flours tend to be drier and might produce crumbly cookies. If substituting, consider adding a bit more fat or moisture to compensate.
What if I don’t have coconut sugar? Can I use regular sugar?
Yes, you can use brown sugar or granulated sugar instead. Brown sugar will keep some moisture and add richness similar to coconut sugar, while white sugar may make cookies slightly crisper. Just be mindful the flavor and color might vary a bit.
Why are my cookies spreading too much?
Overly warm dough or too much fat can cause spreading. Make sure your dough is chilled slightly before baking, and measure ingredients carefully. Also, spacing cookies properly prevents them from merging in the oven.
Can I make these cookies vegan?
Yes! Replace the egg with a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tablespoons water, set for 5 minutes). This works well but may yield a slightly less chewy texture compared to using a real egg.
How do I keep these cookies soft longer?
Store them in an airtight container at room temperature with a slice of bread or an apple wedge to maintain moisture. Avoid refrigeration which can dry them out faster.
Can I add other mix-ins like dried fruit or nuts?
Absolutely! Chopped nuts, dried cranberries, or shredded coconut add great texture and flavor. Just fold them in gently with the chocolate chips to distribute evenly.
What if my cookies are dry or crumbly?
This usually means too much flour or overbaking. Measure flour carefully by spooning it into the cup, and pull cookies from the oven as soon as edges turn golden but centers are still soft.
Can I double this recipe?
Yes, doubling works well. Just be sure to mix ingredients thoroughly and use multiple baking sheets so cookies bake evenly without crowding.
