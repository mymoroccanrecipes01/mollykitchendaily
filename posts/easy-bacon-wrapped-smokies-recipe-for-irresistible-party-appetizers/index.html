Sweet and Savory Bacon Wrapped Smokies That Everyone Will Love

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 14, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These bacon wrapped smokies are the perfect finger food for any party. The brown sugar glaze brings a sweet kick that balances the smoky bacon and juicy sausages. They’re quick to make and always a hit!

Introduction

I first made these bacon wrapped smokies years ago for a casual get-together, and they instantly became a staple in my party repertoire. What sets this version apart is the simple brown sugar glaze I add before baking — it caramelizes beautifully, giving a perfect sweet-savory balance that keeps everyone coming back for more. I’ve tried just wrapping the sausages in bacon alone, but the glaze really elevates the flavor and adds a nice glossy finish. The trick I learned is to secure the bacon tightly with toothpicks and bake them on a wire rack so the bacon crisps up evenly without sitting in grease. When they come out golden and sticky, the sausages inside stay juicy and tender. It’s such an easy recipe but feels special, and works great whether you’re hosting a game day party or just want a tasty snack. I love that you can prep these ahead and pop them in the oven right before guests arrive. Plus, they’re always a crowd-pleaser — finger food that’s fun and flavorful!

What You'll Need

Approximately 24 pieces, serves 4 as appetizer

  • 24 mini smoked sausages (cocktail smokies), thawed if frozen
  • 12 slices bacon, cut in half
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • Toothpicks for securing

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Line a baking sheet with aluminum foil for easy cleanup and place a wire rack on top. The wire rack is key here because it allows the bacon fat to drip away, helping the bacon crisp up evenly. If you skip the rack and bake directly on foil, the bacon can get soggy from sitting in its own grease. This setup also helps the smokies cook uniformly. Make sure the rack fits snugly and is stable to avoid any spills.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated and baking setup ready with foil and wire rack
Step 2
Wrap each mini smoked sausage with half a slice of bacon, starting at one end and spiraling to cover the sausage completely. Secure the bacon with a toothpick through the center to hold it tightly in place. Wrapping the bacon firmly is important so it doesn't unravel during baking. If the bacon is too thick, you can partially freeze it to make slicing easier. Avoid overlapping too much bacon as it can prevent even cooking.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: All sausages are tightly wrapped and secured with toothpicks
Step 3
In a small bowl, mix the brown sugar, smoked paprika, and black pepper. This blend adds a smoky sweetness with a subtle kick. The paprika enhances the smoky flavor of the bacon and smokies, while the pepper adds mild heat. Stir until evenly combined, then brush each bacon-wrapped smokie generously with the mixture. The sugar will caramelize in the oven, creating a beautiful glaze that contrasts nicely with the smoky bacon.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each smokie has a shiny, even coating of the brown sugar glaze
Step 4
Arrange the wrapped smokies on the wire rack in a single layer, spaced slightly apart to allow air circulation. This spacing is crucial for even cooking and crisping. Overcrowding the pan can cause uneven heat distribution and soggy bacon. If needed, bake in batches to maintain space.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smokies are evenly spaced on the rack without touching
Step 5
Place the baking sheet in the preheated oven and bake for 25-30 minutes, turning the smokies halfway through. Turning ensures all sides get crisp and the glaze caramelizes evenly. Keep an eye on them during the last 5 minutes to avoid burning the sugar glaze. The bacon should be crispy and browned, and the glaze sticky but not burnt.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bacon is crispy, glaze is caramelized, and sausages are heated through
Step 6
Once cooked, remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the smokies rest for 5 minutes on the rack. This resting time lets the glaze set slightly so it’s less sticky to the touch. It also helps the bacon retain its crispness rather than steaming from residual heat.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Glaze has firmed up slightly and smokies are cool enough to handle
Step 7
Transfer the bacon wrapped smokies to a serving platter, removing toothpicks carefully. Arrange them attractively for easy grabbing. If you want, add a sprinkle of chopped fresh parsley or a little extra black pepper for garnish. Serving warm is best to enjoy the crispy texture and juicy inside.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smokies are plated and ready to serve, toothpicks removed
Step 8
For an extra touch, you can prepare a quick dipping sauce like honey mustard or spicy ketchup to accompany the smokies. This adds a complementary tang or heat that balances the sweet glaze. Simply mix equal parts mustard and honey or use your favorite store-bought sauce.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dipping sauce prepared and placed alongside the smokies
Step 9
Enjoy your bacon wrapped smokies freshly baked! They’re perfect for parties, game days, or whenever you want a tasty, shareable snack. Leftovers can be reheated gently in the oven to crisp up the bacon again. Remember to store any extras properly to keep them fresh.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smokies served warm and enjoyed

Variations

Spicy Sriracha Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Add a spicy kick by mixing sriracha into the brown sugar glaze before brushing. This variation adds heat and tanginess that contrasts nicely with the smoky bacon and sweet glaze. Great if you like bold flavors!

Note: Adjust the amount of sriracha to your heat tolerance; too much can burn during baking so use a light hand.

Maple Glazed Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Swap brown sugar for pure maple syrup in the glaze for a rich, natural sweetness. The maple adds depth and a slight floral note that pairs beautifully with bacon’s saltiness.

Note: Brush maple syrup near the end of baking to prevent burning due to its thinner consistency.

Cheese-Stuffed Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Before wrapping, carefully slit each smokie and stuff with a small cube of cheddar or pepper jack cheese. This adds gooey, melty richness inside that pairs perfectly with the crispy bacon outside.

Note: Freeze stuffed smokies briefly before wrapping to prevent cheese from leaking during baking.

Herb-Infused Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Mix finely chopped fresh herbs like rosemary and thyme into the brown sugar glaze. The herbs add an aromatic, earthy flavor that elevates this appetizer for more sophisticated palates.

Note: Use fresh herbs finely minced to avoid overpowering the glaze texture.

BBQ Sauce Glazed Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Replace the brown sugar glaze with your favorite BBQ sauce for a tangy, smoky twist. This variation gives a saucier bite and is perfect for BBQ lovers.

Note: Apply BBQ sauce in the last 10 minutes of baking to avoid burning the sugars in the sauce.

Pro Tips

Use a wire rack on the baking sheet to crisp the bacon evenly.
The wire rack elevates the smokies, allowing fat to drip away so the bacon crisps instead of stewing in grease. Without this, bacon can become soggy and unevenly cooked, affecting texture and flavor.
Partially freeze bacon slices before cutting and wrapping.
Chilled bacon slices are firmer and easier to cut evenly, which helps when wrapping the smokies tightly. This prevents the bacon from tearing or slipping off during baking, ensuring a neat, uniform appearance.
Turn the smokies halfway through baking for even caramelization.
Rotating the smokies exposes all sides to heat evenly, preventing one side from burning while the other remains undercooked. This also helps the sugar glaze caramelize uniformly for a beautiful finish.
Brush the brown sugar glaze generously but evenly.
An even glaze ensures consistent caramelization without clumps that can burn. Too little glaze results in a lackluster sweetness, while too much can cause sticky spots or burnt sugar.
Let the cooked smokies rest a few minutes before serving.
Resting allows the glaze to set and the bacon to retain crispness. Serving immediately can make the glaze too sticky and the bacon steam, losing its crunch.
Use toothpicks to secure bacon tightly around smokies.
Toothpicks hold the bacon in place as it shrinks and crisps during cooking. Without securing, the bacon might unravel, resulting in uneven cooking and a messy presentation.
Serve the smokies warm for best flavor and texture.
Warm smokies have crisp bacon and juicy centers. If served cold, the bacon can become chewy and the glaze hardens, diminishing the overall eating experience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Wrapping bacon loosely around smokies

Why it happens Many assume bacon will stay put on its own, but as it cooks and shrinks, loose wrapping causes it to unravel.

Fix Secure the bacon firmly with toothpicks before baking. This keeps the bacon wrapped tightly and ensures even cooking without falling apart.

Skipping the wire rack and baking directly on foil

Why it happens Some think it’s easier to skip racks, but bacon cooks best elevated to drain fat.

Fix Always use a wire rack so fat drips away, allowing bacon to crisp rather than steam in its grease.

Applying too much brown sugar glaze

Why it happens Overenthusiastic glazing can lead to burnt sugar spots.

Fix Apply a thin, even layer of glaze. Excess sugar can burn quickly, so moderation is key.

Not turning smokies during baking

Why it happens Forgetting to rotate results in uneven browning and caramelization.

Fix Turn smokies halfway through baking to ensure all sides crisp and glaze caramelizes evenly.

Serving immediately without resting

Why it happens Impatience leads to sticky glaze and less crisp bacon.

Fix Let smokies rest 5 minutes post-baking so glaze sets and bacon stays crunchy.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Bacon wrapped smokies can be left out at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel. Beyond that, bacterial growth risk increases, so refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The bacon may soften slightly but reheating in the oven will help crisp it back up. They can be eaten cold but lose some of their charm.

Freezer

Freeze cooled smokies in a single layer on a baking sheet first, then transfer to freezer bags for up to 2 months. To prevent freezer burn, remove excess air and wrap tightly. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat in a 350°F oven for 10-15 minutes until warmed through and bacon crisps again. Avoid microwaving as it makes bacon chewy and soggy. A splash of water in the oven can prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

Prepare and wrap the smokies a day ahead, keep them covered in the fridge. Brush on the brown sugar glaze just before baking to keep flavors fresh and bacon crispy.

FAQ

Can I use regular-sized sausages instead of mini smokies?
You can, but cooking times will increase and the bacon may need to be adjusted in length or thickness. Larger sausages might not crisp as evenly, so consider cutting them into smaller pieces to maintain consistent cooking.
What if I don’t have smoked paprika? Can I substitute it?
If you don’t have smoked paprika, regular paprika can be used but the smoky depth will be missing. You might add a small dash of liquid smoke or chipotle powder to mimic smokiness, but use sparingly to avoid overpowering flavors.
How can I make these smokies less sweet?
Simply reduce the brown sugar or omit it altogether and brush the bacon with a light coat of olive oil and spices instead. You can also swap sugar for savory spices like garlic powder or cayenne for more heat.
Is it necessary to use a wire rack when baking?
While not strictly necessary, the wire rack helps bacon cook evenly and become crisp by letting fat drip away. Without it, bacon tends to sit in grease and can become soggy or unevenly cooked.
Can I prepare these smokies ahead of time and bake later?
Yes! Wrap the smokies and secure with toothpicks, then store them covered in the refrigerator for up to 24 hours. Add the glaze just before baking to keep the sugar from melting prematurely.
What’s the best way to reheat leftover bacon wrapped smokies?
Reheat in a 350°F oven for 10-15 minutes to restore crispiness without drying them out. Avoid microwaving as it makes bacon rubbery and less appealing.
Can I make these smokies gluten-free?
Yes, this recipe is naturally gluten-free if you use gluten-free sausages and ensure your brown sugar and spices don’t contain additives with gluten. Always check labels to be sure.
How do I prevent the bacon from burning before the sausages are cooked?
Baking at a moderate temperature (375°F) helps cook bacon evenly without burning. Wrapping tightly and using a wire rack also prevents hot spots. Turning halfway through encourages uniform cooking.
