Classic Chicken and Dumplings: Cozy Homemade Comfort in Every Bite

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 45 min cook 6 servings

This chicken and dumplings recipe is my go-to for chilly nights. Tender chicken stewed in a savory broth with fluffy, homemade dumplings makes it pure comfort food. It’s warm, hearty, and just like grandma used to make.

Why This Works

This recipe succeeds because it balances tender chicken, a rich broth, and perfectly cooked dumplings that are light and fluffy. The slow simmering of the chicken in broth extracts deep flavor without drying out the meat. Maintaining a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil during cooking prevents the chicken from becoming tough and the broth from clouding, resulting in a clear, savory base.

The dumplings rely on the right balance of flour, baking powder, and liquid mixed just until combined. Overmixing activates gluten, which leads to dense, chewy dumplings, so gentle mixing and resting the dough are key. Steaming the dumplings under a lid helps them rise and cook evenly by trapping moisture and heat, creating that tender, pillowy texture that is the hallmark of good dumplings.

Adding vegetables like carrots, celery, and onion not only enhances the flavor and texture of the stew but also contributes natural sweetness and depth. The fresh parsley at the end brightens the dish, balancing the richness of the broth and chicken. Altogether, these techniques and ingredient choices create a harmonious dish that feels both hearty and comforting, perfect for cold days or anytime you crave classic home cooking.

  • Choosing a whole chicken with bones and skin is essential because it adds rich flavor and body to the broth. The bones release gelatin during simmering, which gives the broth a silky mouthfeel and depth. Skin adds fat and richness, but you can remove it after cooking if preferred. Avoid boneless chicken breasts here, as they don’t impart the same flavor or texture to the broth.
  • All-purpose flour provides the right gluten content to create tender yet structured dumplings. Using self-rising flour can be a shortcut but might alter texture slightly. Sifting the flour before mixing helps avoid lumps and ensures even distribution of baking powder, which is crucial for lightness.
  • Baking powder acts as a leavening agent, releasing carbon dioxide when heated to make the dumplings rise and become fluffy. Using fresh baking powder is important; old or expired powder won’t produce the same lift, resulting in dense dumplings.
  • A good-quality chicken broth or stock is the backbone of this recipe. Homemade broth is ideal for its rich flavor, but store-bought works fine if it’s low sodium and flavorful. The broth should be clear and well-seasoned before adding dumplings to ensure the stew is tasty throughout.
  • Whole milk adds moisture and richness to the dumpling dough without making it too heavy. It helps tenderize the gluten in the flour, leading to softer dumplings. Using skim or plant-based milk will change texture and flavor, so stick to whole milk for best results.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken (about 3-4 lbs), cut into pieces
  • 8 cups chicken broth
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
  • 2 medium carrots, peeled and sliced
  • 2 celery stalks, sliced
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt and pepper to taste

I first stumbled upon this chicken and dumplings recipe on a cold winter evening when I was craving something warm and nostalgic. It reminded me of the kind of meals my grandma used to make — hearty, simple, and incredibly comforting. Over time, I tweaked the recipe to get the dumplings just right: light and fluffy, not dense or doughy. The trick I learned was to not overmix the dough and to let the dumplings steam gently on top of the simmering broth. This method keeps them tender and pillowy while the chicken stays juicy and flavorful. What I love most is how this dish fills the house with the smell of home and makes everyone gather around the table. The broth is rich but not heavy, and the vegetables add just the right balance. If you’re looking for a classic comfort food that’s perfect for chilly nights or when you need a hug in a bowl, this recipe is it. It might take a bit of time, but it’s so worth it — trust me, you’ll want to make it again and again.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preparing the chicken: rinse and pat dry the pieces, then season them lightly with salt and pepper. Place the chicken in a large pot and add the chicken broth. Bring it to a gentle boil over medium heat, then reduce to a simmer. This slow simmer extracts maximum flavor without toughening the meat. Avoid boiling vigorously, which can make the chicken dry. Simmer uncovered for about 30 minutes or until the chicken is cooked through and tender. You'll know it's ready when the meat easily pulls away from the bone and the broth smells deeply savory.

    ⏱ 30 minutes✓ Chicken meat pulls easily from the bone; broth is fragrant and clear
  2. Step 2

    While the chicken simmers, prepare your dumpling dough. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, salt, and pepper. Add the milk and melted butter, stirring gently until just combined. It's important not to overmix here—overworking the dough develops gluten and leads to tough dumplings. The dough should be slightly sticky but manageable. Cover the bowl and set aside until the chicken is ready. This resting helps hydrate the flour and ensures tender dumplings.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Dough is smooth, slightly sticky, and cohesive without lumps
  3. Step 3

    Once the chicken is cooked, remove the pieces from the pot and let them cool slightly. Meanwhile, add the chopped carrots, celery, onion, and garlic to the simmering broth. These veggies boost flavor and add texture to the stew. Simmer the vegetables for about 10 minutes until tender but not mushy. Stir occasionally and keep the heat gentle to prevent the broth from clouding. This step builds the base for your dumplings to cook in.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Vegetables are tender and broth remains clear and flavorful
  4. Step 4

    While the vegetables cook, shred the chicken meat from the bones into bite-sized pieces. Discard skin and bones. Shredding the chicken finely helps it mix evenly throughout the stew. Return the shredded chicken to the pot once the vegetables are tender. Stir gently to combine, then keep the broth at a low simmer to prepare for dumpling cooking.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Chicken is shredded with no large chunks; mixture is evenly combined
  5. Step 5

    Using a spoon or your hands, drop spoonfuls of the dumpling dough onto the simmering stew. Make sure the broth is at a gentle simmer—not boiling vigorously—to allow the dumplings to cook through without falling apart. Cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid to trap steam, which helps the dumplings puff up and cook evenly. Cook for about 15 minutes without lifting the lid to avoid releasing steam prematurely.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Dumplings are puffed, firm to the touch, and no longer doughy inside
  6. Step 6

    After 15 minutes, check a dumpling by cutting it in half to ensure it's cooked through. It should be fluffy and fully set, with no raw dough inside. If needed, cook for a few more minutes, but avoid overcooking as dumplings can become tough. Once done, sprinkle fresh parsley over the stew for a pop of color and fresh flavor. Stir gently to combine.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dumplings are fully cooked and fluffy; parsley looks vibrant
  7. Step 7

    Taste the stew and adjust seasoning with salt and pepper as needed. This final step ensures your dish is perfectly balanced. Because the broth reduces during cooking, flavors intensify, so be cautious not to over-salt. If the stew feels too thick, add a splash of broth or water to loosen it. Stir well and let it sit covered off heat for a few minutes before serving to let flavors meld.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Balanced seasoning and desired stew consistency
  8. Step 8

    Serve the chicken and dumplings hot in deep bowls. Pair with a simple green salad or steamed veggies for a complete meal. The dumplings should be soft and pillowy, soaking up the rich broth, while the chicken remains tender and juicy. This dish is best enjoyed fresh but also reheats wonderfully.

    ⏱ 0 minutes✓ Dish is hot and comforting, ready to enjoy
  9. Step 9

    To store leftovers, let the stew cool completely before transferring to airtight containers. When reheating, do so gently on the stovetop with a splash of broth or water to revive the dumplings’ softness. Avoid microwave reheating for long periods as dumplings can toughen. Enjoy within 3 days for best flavor and texture.

    ⏱ 0 minutes✓ Leftovers retain flavor and texture after proper reheating

Variations

  • Herbed Dumplings with Thyme and Sage: Add finely chopped fresh thyme and sage to the dumpling dough for an herby twist. This adds a lovely earthy aroma that pairs beautifully with the chicken broth. The dumplings become more flavorful and aromatic.
    Incorporate herbs gently to avoid overmixing dough; herbs can slightly affect dough moisture, so adjust milk if needed.
  • Creamy Chicken and Dumplings: Stir in 1/2 cup heavy cream or sour cream to the broth after cooking to create a richer, creamier stew. This variation is indulgent and extra comforting, perfect for those who love creamy textures.
    Add cream off heat or on very low heat to prevent curdling; adjust seasoning after adding cream.
  • Gluten-Free Dumplings: Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend and xanthan gum to make dumplings suitable for gluten intolerance. The texture will be slightly different but still tender and tasty.
    Gluten-free dough may need more liquid and careful mixing; cook dumplings gently to prevent breaking apart.
  • Vegetarian Mushroom Dumplings: Skip the chicken and use vegetable broth with sautéed mushrooms for a vegetarian-friendly version. Add mushrooms to the broth for umami depth, and make dumplings as usual.
    Simmer mushrooms with broth to concentrate flavor; dumpling technique remains unchanged.
  • Spicy Chicken and Dumplings: Add a pinch of cayenne pepper or some diced jalapeños to the broth for a spicy kick. This variation adds warmth and a little heat, perfect if you like your comfort food with a twist.
    Adjust spice level to taste; add spicy ingredients early to infuse broth adequately.

Pro Tips

  • Don’t overmix the dumpling dough. — Overmixing develops too much gluten, which makes dumplings tough and chewy instead of light and fluffy. Mixing just until ingredients come together ensures a tender texture.
  • Simmer the chicken gently, don’t boil. — A gentle simmer extracts flavor while keeping the meat tender. Boiling causes the chicken to toughen and the broth to become cloudy, ruining the dish’s clarity and texture.
  • Cover the pot while cooking dumplings. — Trapping steam under the lid helps the dumplings puff up and cook evenly. Lifting the lid releases steam and heat, which can cause uneven cooking or soggy dumplings.
  • Use bone-in chicken for broth richness. — Bones release collagen and gelatin during cooking, giving the broth body and richness that boneless chicken can’t provide.
  • Add vegetables after chicken is cooked. — Adding veggies later prevents them from overcooking and turning mushy, keeping texture and flavor balanced.
  • Rest the dumpling dough before cooking. — Resting hydrates the flour, which helps soften the dough and produces lighter dumplings.
  • Season the broth gradually. — Broth flavors concentrate during simmering, so seasoning little by little avoids over-salting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the chicken until dry.
    Why: People often boil chicken too fast or too long, thinking it cooks faster that way.
    Fix: Maintain a gentle simmer and check chicken tenderness early to avoid drying out the meat. Use a thermometer or test by pulling meat from the bone.
  • Overmixing dumpling dough.
    Why: It’s tempting to mix thoroughly for uniform dough, but this activates gluten too much.
    Fix: Mix ingredients just until combined and no dry flour remains. Handle the dough gently to keep it tender.
  • Cooking dumplings at too high heat.
    Why: High heat can cause dumplings to cook unevenly or fall apart.
    Fix: Keep broth at a low simmer and cover the pot to cook dumplings gently with steam.
  • Skipping resting time for dumpling dough.
    Why: Rushing the recipe leads to denser dumplings because flour isn’t hydrated.
    Fix: Let dough rest for 10 minutes to hydrate flour and relax gluten before cooking.
  • Adding vegetables too early.
    Why: Some add veggies at the start to save time, resulting in mushy, flavorless vegetables.
    Fix: Add vegetables after chicken is cooked to retain texture and sweet flavor.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Chicken and dumplings can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. After that, bacteria can grow rapidly, so refrigerate promptly to keep it safe. Always cover the dish with a lid or plastic wrap to prevent drying out.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The dumplings may firm up when cold; reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of broth or water to restore softness. Avoid microwaving too long or at high power to prevent toughening.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze chicken and dumplings in a sturdy container or freezer bag for up to 3 months. To prevent freezer burn, remove as much air as possible and wrap tightly. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating. Texture may change slightly but will remain tasty.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat on low heat in a saucepan with a little broth or water to loosen the stew and soften dumplings. Stir occasionally until warmed through. Avoid high heat or microwaving too long, which can make dumplings rubbery or dry.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can cook the chicken and broth base 1-2 days ahead and refrigerate. Prepare dumpling dough just before serving for best texture. Alternatively, make dumplings ahead and store separately, adding them to hot broth to cook fresh.

FAQ

Can I use chicken breasts instead of a whole chicken?
You can, but a whole chicken with bones and skin yields a richer, more flavorful broth thanks to the collagen and fat released during cooking. If you use breasts, consider adding chicken bones or stock cubes to boost flavor. Also, breasts cook faster and can dry out if overcooked.
How do I know when dumplings are fully cooked?
Dumplings are done when they puff up and feel firm but tender to the touch. Cutting one in half should reveal no raw dough inside. Cooking them over a gentle simmer for about 15 minutes usually suffices. Avoid lifting the lid too early to keep steam trapped for even cooking.
Can I freeze chicken and dumplings?
Yes, you can freeze this dish for up to 3 months. Make sure to cool it completely before freezing and store in airtight containers to prevent freezer burn. Thaw in the fridge overnight and reheat gently on the stove with a splash of broth to maintain dumpling softness.
What if my dumplings turn out dense or hard?
This usually happens from overmixing the dough or cooking at too high a temperature. Mix the dough gently and just until combined, then cook dumplings over a low simmer with the pot covered to trap steam. Avoid lifting the lid while cooking.
Can I add other vegetables to the stew?
Absolutely! Feel free to add peas, green beans, or potatoes for extra heartiness. Add harder vegetables like potatoes early to ensure they cook through, and delicate veggies like peas towards the end to keep their texture.
Is there a way to make this recipe dairy-free?
Yes, substitute the milk in the dumpling dough with unsweetened almond or oat milk. Use dairy-free butter or oil instead of regular butter. The texture might differ slightly but the dumplings will still be tasty and tender.
How long can I keep leftovers in the fridge?
Leftovers can be stored safely in the refrigerator for up to 3 days in an airtight container. After that, the risk of spoilage increases, and the dumplings may begin to lose their texture.
Why does my broth sometimes turn cloudy?
Boiling the chicken too vigorously causes proteins and fats to emulsify into the broth, making it cloudy. Simmer gently instead to keep the broth clear and flavorful.
