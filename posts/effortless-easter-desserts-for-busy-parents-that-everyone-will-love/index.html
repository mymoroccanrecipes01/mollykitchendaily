Easy Easter Desserts That Bring Festive Joy Without Fuss

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 30 min cook 6 servings

These Easter desserts are my go-to when time’s tight but I still want something festive and tasty. Simple ingredients, no stress, and perfect for any family gathering or last-minute celebration.

Why This Works

This recipe works so well because of the balance between simple ingredients and thoughtful technique. Creaming the butter and sugar incorporates air that lightens the texture, creating a tender crumb rather than a dense cake. The alternating addition of dry ingredients and milk prevents overmixing, which can develop gluten and make the dessert tough. Instead, the batter remains delicate and moist.

Using baking powder as the leavening agent is key to achieving a gentle rise that results in a soft, fluffy texture. The sifting of dry ingredients ensures even distribution and prevents lumps, which can cause uneven baking. The glaze made from powdered sugar and lemon juice adds acidity that balances the sweetness and keeps the dessert from feeling too heavy.

Decorating with pastel sprinkles not only brings visual appeal but also adds a slight crunch contrast to the soft dessert. The timing of glaze application is critical—applying it while the dessert is cool but before the glaze sets ensures the sprinkles stick well and the glaze forms a smooth, shiny finish. All these elements combined make this an easy yet impressive Easter treat.

Overall, the recipe’s success lies in the harmony of texture, flavor, and appearance, achieved through simple steps that anyone can follow. It’s a perfect example of how small details and proper technique elevate basic ingredients into something special.

  • Choose a good-quality all-purpose flour with moderate protein content (around 10-11%). This provides enough gluten structure for a tender crumb without making the dessert tough. Avoid bread flour, which has too much protein and can produce a chewy texture. Sifting the flour before use aerates it and removes lumps, which helps with even mixing and lightness.
  • Using unsalted butter lets you control the salt level precisely, which is important for balancing flavors. Butter should be softened to room temperature for smooth creaming; cold butter won’t incorporate air well, and melted butter changes the batter’s texture. High-fat butter adds richness and tenderness to the dessert.
  • Baking powder acts as a chemical leavener, releasing carbon dioxide bubbles when heated to make the dessert rise. Make sure your baking powder is fresh — expired leavening agents won’t produce the necessary lift, resulting in a dense final product. Measuring accurately is important; too much can cause a bitter taste.
  • Fresh lemon juice adds a bright acidity that balances the sweetness of the glaze and dessert. It also slightly reacts with the powdered sugar to create a smooth, glossy finish. Avoid bottled lemon juice with preservatives as it can affect flavor. Adjust quantity to get the right glaze consistency and tang.
  • Powdered sugar dissolves easily to form a smooth glaze without graininess. It contains a bit of cornstarch to prevent clumping, which is perfect for icing. When mixing with lemon juice, start with less liquid and add gradually to control thickness. Using pure powdered sugar ensures a clean sweet taste without added flavors.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • Assorted pastel sprinkles or edible decorations

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x13-inch baking pan lightly with butter or nonstick spray, ensuring every corner is covered to prevent sticking. This step is crucial because it helps your dessert come out cleanly without breaking apart. Using a well-prepared pan also means less cleanup later, which is always a win. Avoid skipping this or using too little fat, as it can cause the dessert to stick and crumble when serving.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Pan is evenly greased with no bare spots visible
  2. Step 2

    In a large mixing bowl, cream the softened butter with granulated sugar until light and fluffy. This usually takes about 3-5 minutes using an electric mixer on medium speed. Creaming incorporates air into the butter, which helps create a tender and light texture in the finished dessert. If you rush this step or skip it, your dessert could turn out dense and heavy, so take your time to get that fluffy consistency.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Mixture is pale in color and fluffy to the touch
  3. Step 3

    Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. Then stir in the vanilla extract. Adding eggs slowly ensures they are fully incorporated and helps maintain the batter's smooth texture. If you add them all at once, you risk curdling the mixture, which affects the final crumb. Mixing thoroughly but gently keeps the batter airy.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Batter is smooth and glossy without streaks
  4. Step 4

    In a separate bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, and salt. Sifting helps aerate the flour and removes lumps, which is key for a light, even texture. Baking powder ensures the dessert rises properly. Mixing these dry ingredients well prevents uneven pockets of baking powder, which could cause odd bumps or holes in your dessert.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Dry ingredients are evenly combined with no clumps
  5. Step 5

    Alternately add the dry ingredients and milk to the butter mixture, beginning and ending with the dry ingredients. Mix gently after each addition just until combined. This alternating method helps keep the batter smooth and prevents overmixing, which can develop gluten and make your dessert tough. Watch the texture closely — it should be thick but pourable.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Batter is uniform in color and texture without streaks
  6. Step 6

    Pour the batter into the prepared pan and spread it evenly with a spatula. Making sure the batter is level helps it bake uniformly, so no sections are undercooked or overdone. Avoid pressing too hard, which can create dense spots. The batter should fill the pan smoothly without air bubbles on the surface.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Batter surface is even and smooth
  7. Step 7

    Bake the dessert in the preheated oven for 25-30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Baking too long dries out the dessert, while underbaking leaves it gooey and unstable. Keep the oven door closed as much as possible to maintain temperature consistency.

    ⏱ 25-30 minutes✓ Toothpick comes out clean or with moist crumbs
  8. Step 8

    While the dessert cools, mix powdered sugar with lemon juice to create a smooth glaze. This tangy glaze adds brightness and cuts through the sweetness, making the dessert feel fresh and light. Adjust the lemon juice to get a pourable but thick consistency. Too thin, and it will run off; too thick, and it won't spread nicely.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Glaze is smooth, shiny, and pourable
  9. Step 9

    Drizzle the glaze evenly over the cooled dessert and sprinkle with pastel decorations or edible sprinkles immediately before the glaze sets. This step adds a festive touch and makes the dessert visually appealing for Easter. If you wait too long, the glaze hardens and sprinkles won’t stick well. Let it set for about 15 minutes before slicing and serving.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Glaze is set but still slightly soft to the touch

Variations

  • Chocolate Easter Glaze: Swap the lemon glaze for a simple chocolate glaze made with melted dark chocolate and a bit of cream. This adds richness and a decadent twist while keeping the dessert easy. Decorate with mini chocolate eggs or cocoa nibs for an Easter-friendly look.
    Chocolate glaze sets firmer and faster; apply while warm for smooth coverage.
  • Gluten-Free Version: Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend instead of all-purpose flour. Make sure it contains xanthan gum for structure. This adapts the recipe for gluten sensitivities without sacrificing texture or flavor.
    Gluten-free batters may be slightly thicker; bake at the same temperature but check doneness a few minutes earlier.
  • Add Fresh Berries: Fold in fresh blueberries or raspberries into the batter before baking for bursts of fruity flavor and moisture. This adds natural sweetness and makes the dessert feel more spring-like and fresh.
    Gently fold berries to avoid breaking them and discoloring the batter.
  • Coconut and Almond Twist: Add 1/2 cup shredded coconut and 1/4 cup sliced almonds into the batter for texture and a tropical flavor. Top with a coconut glaze instead of lemon for a different but equally festive taste.
    Toasting almonds before adding enhances their flavor.
  • Vegan Adaptation: Replace butter with vegan margarine, eggs with flaxseed 'eggs' (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water per egg), and use plant-based milk. This version keeps the dessert accessible for vegan diets while maintaining moistness and flavor.
    Flax eggs may slightly change texture; allow batter to rest 5 minutes before baking.

Pro Tips

  • Always sift your flour and baking powder together. — Sifting combines the dry ingredients evenly and aerates the flour, which leads to a lighter texture. Without sifting, you risk clumps of baking powder that can cause uneven rising and bitter pockets in the dessert.
  • Don’t overmix the batter after adding flour. — Overmixing develops gluten, which makes the dessert tough and dense. Mix just until ingredients are combined to keep the crumb tender and soft.
  • Use room temperature butter for creaming. — Softened butter traps air during creaming, which creates a fluffier texture. Cold butter won’t incorporate air well, and melted butter changes the batter’s structure.
  • Check your baking powder’s freshness before baking. — Expired baking powder loses its effectiveness, resulting in a flat, dense dessert. Test by mixing a small amount with hot water—if it bubbles vigorously, it’s still good.
  • Apply the glaze while the dessert is cool but not cold. — Applying glaze too hot causes it to run off, and too cold means it won’t spread evenly. A cool surface helps the glaze set smoothly and hold sprinkles.
  • Use a light hand when spreading batter in the pan. — Pressing too hard can compact the batter, leading to uneven baking and a dense texture. Spreading gently ensures even rise and crumb.
  • Let the dessert cool completely before slicing. — Cutting warm dessert can cause it to crumble or lose shape. Cooling allows it to set properly for clean slices.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the creaming step or rushing it
    Why: People often want to hurry and mix everything at once, thinking it saves time.
    Fix: Take your time to cream butter and sugar until light and fluffy. This step traps air for a tender crumb. Use an electric mixer and beat for 3-5 minutes until pale. Rushing this can lead to dense, heavy desserts.
  • Overmixing after adding flour
    Why: Eager to get the batter ready, many mix too much, not realizing gluten develops.
    Fix: Mix just until dry ingredients are barely combined. Stop as soon as you don’t see streaks of flour. Overmixing toughens the dessert’s texture.
  • Using expired baking powder
    Why: Baking powder is often forgotten in the back of the pantry and loses potency.
    Fix: Test baking powder by mixing a teaspoon with hot water; it should bubble immediately. Replace if no reaction occurs to ensure proper rise.
  • Applying glaze too soon or too late
    Why: Some apply glaze when dessert is hot or completely cold, affecting adhesion.
    Fix: Wait until the dessert is cool to the touch but not chilled. This helps glaze spread evenly and hold decorations.
  • Not greasing the pan properly
    Why: Skipping or doing a quick spray can cause sticking.
    Fix: Use butter or nonstick spray liberally and cover every corner. This prevents sticking and keeps the dessert intact.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Store the dessert covered loosely at room temperature for up to 2 days. Use a cake dome or airtight container to keep it moist and prevent it from drying out. Avoid leaving it uncovered as it will harden.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The dessert may become firmer and the glaze slightly crusty when cold. Bring to room temperature or warm gently before serving for best texture.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap tightly with plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or 2 hours at room temperature. Reheat gently in a low oven if desired to refresh texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm slices in a microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes. Add a splash of milk if it feels dry. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out or melting the glaze.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Prepare the dessert base and bake up to 2 days ahead. Store covered in the fridge without glaze. Add glaze and decorations the day of serving to keep them fresh and vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use salted butter instead of unsalted?
Yes, you can use salted butter, but reduce or omit any added salt in the recipe to avoid over-salting. Unsalted butter gives you more control over the final flavor, but salted butter won’t ruin the dessert.
What if I don’t have baking powder?
Baking powder is crucial for rise, but if you have baking soda and an acidic ingredient, you can make a substitute. Use 1/4 teaspoon baking soda plus 1/2 teaspoon cream of tartar for every teaspoon of baking powder. Without leavening, your dessert will be dense.
How do I know when the dessert is done baking?
Insert a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. If the toothpick has wet batter, it needs more time. Don’t overbake to keep it moist and tender.
Can I make the glaze ahead of time?
Yes, you can prepare the glaze a few hours ahead and store it covered in the fridge. Stir well before using, and adjust thickness with a bit of lemon juice or powdered sugar if needed.
What’s the best way to store leftover dessert?
Store leftovers in an airtight container either at room temperature (up to 2 days) or refrigerated (up to 5 days). This keeps the dessert moist and prevents the glaze from drying out too quickly.
Can I double this recipe?
Absolutely! Just use a larger pan or two pans to keep the thickness consistent. Baking time might increase slightly, so check with a toothpick for doneness starting at the original bake time.
What if my batter looks too thick or too thin?
The batter should be thick but pourable. If too thick, add a little more milk, one tablespoon at a time. If too thin, add a small amount of flour. Adjust gently to maintain texture.
How can I make these desserts more colorful for Easter?
Use pastel sprinkles, edible flowers, or colored glazes. Adding fresh berries or food coloring to the glaze can also brighten the look. Just be mindful not to overpower the delicate flavors.
