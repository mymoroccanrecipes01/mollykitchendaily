Fluffy British Cheddar Biscuits That Melt in Your Mouth

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 20 min cook 8 servings

These British cheddar biscuits are soft, buttery, and packed with sharp cheese flavor. The secret? Folding the dough gently to keep them fluffy. Great for cozy snacks or paired with your favorite soup!

Why This Works

The secret to fluffy, tender cheddar biscuits lies in the balance between cold butter and minimal handling. When cold butter is cut into the flour, it creates small pockets that steam during baking, which causes the dough to puff up and develop flaky layers. Overmixing or warming the butter too soon melts it prematurely, resulting in dense biscuits.

Using baking powder as a leavening agent introduces carbon dioxide bubbles when combined with moisture and heat. This reaction is what gives the biscuits their rise. Sifting the flour and evenly mixing the baking powder ensures a consistent texture without dense spots.

The folding technique helps develop layers similar to puff pastry but without the complexity. Folding the dough gently creates thin layers of dough separated by butter, which expand when baking to produce a light, airy crumb. The cold milk helps keep the butter solid until it hits the hot oven, preserving these layers.

Sharp cheddar cheese adds flavor and moisture. Its melting quality complements the flaky biscuit texture perfectly. Choosing a good-quality sharp cheddar ensures that the cheese melts smoothly and doesn’t become greasy or separate, which can happen with processed cheeses.

  • Choose a good-quality all-purpose flour with moderate protein content (around 10-12%). This balance helps create a biscuit that's tender but still has enough structure to rise well. Avoid bread flour, which has too much gluten and will make the biscuits tough. Sifting the flour before use aerates it and helps prevent clumps, resulting in a lighter texture.
  • Use cold, unsalted butter for the best flakiness and flavor control. Cold butter stays solid longer during mixing, creating steam pockets that puff the biscuits up. Unsalted butter lets you regulate salt precisely. Avoid using margarine or butter substitutes, as they lack the water content needed for proper steam and texture.
  • Opt for a sharp, aged cheddar with a firm texture and strong flavor. This cheese melts well without becoming oily and adds a nice tang that balances the richness of the butter. Avoid pre-shredded cheeses with anti-caking agents, as they don’t melt as smoothly. Grate the cheese fresh for best results.
  • Use fresh, double-acting baking powder to ensure proper rise. Baking powder reacts once when wet and again in the oven heat, giving biscuits their lift. Old or expired baking powder can lead to flat biscuits. Measure carefully and mix evenly into the flour to avoid uneven rising or bitter flavors.
  • Cold whole milk adds moisture and richness without making the dough heavy. The fat content helps tenderize the biscuits and contributes to a soft crumb. Using milk that’s too warm can melt the butter prematurely, reducing flakiness. You can substitute buttermilk for a tangier flavor and slightly different texture.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder (optional)
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional)
  • 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, cold and cubed
  • 1 1/4 cups sharp cheddar cheese, grated
  • 3/4 cup whole milk, cold

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this cheddar biscuit recipe during a cozy weekend in the English countryside. It was chilly outside, and these biscuits, warm from the oven, were the perfect comfort food. What makes them stand apart from your average biscuit is the sharp British cheddar folded gently into the dough, creating pockets of melty, cheesy goodness in every bite. I’ve tried many biscuit recipes, but this one nails the balance between fluffy and buttery without being heavy or dense. The trick I learned is to keep the butter cold and to fold the dough just enough to combine—too much mixing, and you lose the fluffiness. When baked, they puff up beautifully with a golden crust and a soft crumb inside that’s just begging to be eaten with a pat of butter or alongside a bowl of soup. These biscuits have become my go-to for weekend breakfasts or a quick snack when I want something satisfying but simple. Honestly, once you make these, you’ll wonder why you ever settled for plain biscuits before!

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 425°F (220°C). This high temperature is key to getting a nice rise and a golden crust on your biscuits. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper and set aside. Preheating the oven fully ensures the biscuits start baking immediately with steam inside, which helps them puff up.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Oven fully heated to 425°F, baking sheet ready
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, salt, garlic powder, and cayenne pepper if using. These dry ingredients set the foundation for the biscuit’s texture and flavor. Sifting helps aerate the flour for lighter biscuits and evenly distributes the baking powder, so you don’t get uneven rises.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dry ingredients well combined and lump-free
  3. Step 3

    Add the cold, cubed butter to the dry ingredients. Using a pastry cutter or your fingertips, cut the butter into the flour until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces of butter still visible. These bits of butter melt during baking, creating steam pockets that make the biscuits flaky and tender. Avoid warming the butter too much or overmixing here to keep the texture light.

    ⏱ 7 minutes✓ Mixture looks crumbly with pea-sized butter chunks
  4. Step 4

    Fold in the grated sharp cheddar cheese evenly, taking care not to overwork the dough. The cheese should be well distributed but not melted or clumped. This gives bursts of cheesy flavor throughout each biscuit. Using a sharp cheddar adds a nice tang and depth that mild cheeses can’t match.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Cheese evenly folded in without clumps
  5. Step 5

    Pour the cold milk into the flour mixture. Using a spatula, gently fold the milk into the dry ingredients until just combined. The dough will be sticky but cohesive. The cold milk helps keep the butter from melting prematurely and contributes to the biscuits’ tender crumb. Overmixing here can develop gluten, making biscuits tough, so fold until you see no dry flour.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Sticky dough formed with no dry flour patches
  6. Step 6

    Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface. Gently pat it into a rectangle about 3/4 inch thick. Fold the dough over itself 2-3 times, then gently pat it back down. This folding technique creates layers that puff up when baked, giving you those flaky, tender biscuits. Be gentle to avoid overworking the dough.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dough feels soft, layered, and about 3/4 inch thick
  7. Step 7

    Using a floured biscuit cutter or a glass, cut out biscuits by pressing straight down—avoid twisting, which can seal the edges and prevent rising. Place biscuits close together on the prepared baking sheet to encourage rising upwards instead of spreading out. Gather scraps, gently reshape, and cut more biscuits until all dough is used.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Biscuits cut cleanly with sharp edges, arranged on sheet
  8. Step 8

    Bake the biscuits in the preheated oven for 15-20 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through for even browning. You’re aiming for golden brown tops and a flaky texture inside. The high heat helps the butter steam quickly, creating lift and a tender crumb. Avoid opening the oven too often, or the heat will drop and affect rising.

    ⏱ 15-20 minutes✓ Golden brown tops and firm but tender texture when touched
  9. Step 9

    Remove the biscuits from the oven and let them cool slightly on a wire rack. They’re best served warm with butter or alongside soups and stews. Cooling lets the steam inside settle, so the texture isn’t gummy. These biscuits also reheat well, but fresh from the oven is unbeatable.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Biscuits warm, slightly cooled, and ready to enjoy

Variations

  • Herb and Garlic Cheddar Biscuits: Add 1 tablespoon of finely chopped fresh herbs like rosemary or thyme and 1 teaspoon of garlic powder to the dry ingredients. This gives the biscuits a fragrant, savory twist that pairs wonderfully with soups and stews.
    Be careful not to add too much moisture from fresh herbs; chop finely and pat dry if needed to keep dough consistency.
  • Buttermilk Cheddar Biscuits: Swap the whole milk for the same amount of cold buttermilk. This adds a tangy flavor and tender crumb thanks to the acidity reacting with baking powder, giving a slightly fluffier texture.
    Buttermilk may cause a slightly darker crust due to higher acidity, so watch baking time carefully.
  • Spicy Cheddar Biscuits: Add 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper and a pinch of smoked paprika to the dry ingredients for a smoky, spicy kick. Great for pairing with chili or barbecue dishes.
    Adjust spice levels to taste; too much cayenne can overpower the cheddar flavor.
  • Cheddar and Bacon Biscuits: Fold in 1/2 cup cooked, crumbled bacon with the cheese for a smoky, meaty addition. These are perfect for breakfast or brunch.
    Drain bacon well to avoid adding excess grease, which can affect dough texture.
  • Vegan Cheddar-Style Biscuits: Use vegan butter and a plant-based milk like oat milk. Substitute cheddar with a sharp vegan cheese shreds that melt well. These biscuits keep the buttery, cheesy feel without dairy.
    Vegan cheeses vary in melt quality; test your brand first to ensure good melting.

Pro Tips

  • Keep your butter and milk cold until just before mixing. — Cold butter creates steam pockets during baking that puff up the biscuits. If the butter melts before baking, you lose those pockets, resulting in dense biscuits. Similarly, cold milk helps keep the butter solid while mixing, preserving the flaky texture.
  • Don’t overmix your dough—fold gently until just combined. — Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make biscuits tough and chewy instead of tender. Gentle folding preserves the delicate layers and lets the biscuits rise properly.
  • Use a sharp biscuit cutter and press straight down without twisting. — Twisting the cutter seals the dough edges and prevents the biscuits from rising evenly. Pressing straight down cuts clean edges, allowing the biscuits to rise tall and fluffy.
  • Bake biscuits close together on the baking sheet. — Placing biscuits close allows them to support each other’s rise, keeping them tall and soft. If spaced too far apart, they spread out and become flatter.
  • Rotate the baking sheet halfway through baking. — Ovens often have hot spots causing uneven browning. Rotating the pan ensures all biscuits bake evenly to a golden brown without burning on one side.
  • Let biscuits cool slightly before serving. — Cooling lets steam inside settle so the texture firms up without becoming gummy. Biscuits eaten straight from the oven are delicious but can be fragile and crumbly.
  • Use fresh baking powder for best rise. — Old baking powder loses potency, leading to flat biscuits. Fresh baking powder ensures a strong, consistent rise for fluffy results.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using warm butter instead of cold
    Why: Many assume softened butter is best for mixing, but warm butter melts into the flour instead of creating steam pockets.
    Fix: Chill the butter thoroughly before cutting into the flour. If it softens too much, pop it back in the fridge for a bit to firm up again before continuing.
  • Overmixing the dough
    Why: In a rush to combine ingredients, it’s easy to mix too much, developing gluten that toughens biscuits.
    Fix: Mix until just combined and no dry flour remains. Use gentle folding with a spatula instead of vigorous stirring.
  • Twisting the biscuit cutter
    Why: Twisting is a natural instinct to cut through dough, but it seals edges and prevents proper rise.
    Fix: Press the cutter straight down and lift straight up without twisting to keep edges clean.
  • Not preheating the oven fully
    Why: Impatience or distraction can cause baking in a not-hot-enough oven, leading to poor rise and pale biscuits.
    Fix: Always preheat the oven completely to 425°F before baking. Use an oven thermometer if unsure.
  • Using pre-shredded cheese
    Why: Convenience leads many to use pre-shredded cheese, but it contains additives that affect melting.
    Fix: Grate your own sharp cheddar fresh for smoother melting and better flavor.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Biscuits can safely sit at room temperature for up to 4 hours if covered loosely with a clean towel to prevent drying out. For best texture, consume within this time if not refrigerated.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store cooled biscuits in an airtight container for up to 3 days. They may become slightly denser when cold but reheat well. Warm them briefly in the oven or microwave before serving to restore softness.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze biscuits individually wrapped in plastic wrap and stored in a freezer bag for up to 2 months. Thaw at room temperature or warm directly from frozen in a 350°F oven for 10-12 minutes to refresh texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat biscuits in a preheated 350°F oven for 8-10 minutes, wrapped in foil to prevent drying. Avoid microwaving too long as it can make them rubbery. Adding a small pat of butter before warming enhances flavor and moisture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the biscuit dough 1 day ahead, shaped and cut on the baking sheet, covered tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerated. Bake fresh the next day, adding a couple minutes to baking time if cold from fridge.

FAQ

Can I use self-rising flour instead of all-purpose flour?
Using self-rising flour is possible but you should omit the baking powder and salt from the recipe since self-rising flour already contains leavening and salt. The texture might be slightly different, often a bit softer and less flaky.
How do I make these biscuits dairy-free?
Substitute the butter with a vegan butter alternative that’s solid and cold, and swap the milk with plant-based milk like oat or almond milk. Use a dairy-free cheese that melts well if you want to keep the cheesy flavor.
Why are my biscuits dense and not fluffy?
Common causes include overmixing the dough, using warm butter, or not preheating the oven properly. Make sure to keep butter cold, mix gently, and bake at a high temperature for best rise.
Can I freeze the dough before baking?
Yes! You can freeze the shaped biscuits on the baking sheet until solid, then transfer to a freezer bag. Bake from frozen, adding a few extra minutes to the baking time.
What’s the best way to grate cheese for these biscuits?
Use a box grater or microplane to grate the cheese freshly. Avoid pre-shredded cheese since it often contains anti-caking agents that prevent smooth melting.
Can I add herbs or spices to the biscuits?
Absolutely! Fresh or dried herbs like thyme, rosemary, or chives work well. Garlic powder or smoked paprika add great flavor. Just fold them into the dry ingredients.
How do I store leftover baked biscuits?
Cool completely, then store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for 3 days. Reheat gently to restore softness.
Can I use sharp cheddar from different countries?
Yes, but the flavor and melting quality can vary. British or Irish cheddars tend to be sharper and creamier, while some American cheddars can be milder or waxier.
