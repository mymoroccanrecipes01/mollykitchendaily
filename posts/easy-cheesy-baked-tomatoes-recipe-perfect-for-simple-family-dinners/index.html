Cheesy Baked Tomatoes with a Golden Melted Topping for Cozy Dinners

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 2, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These baked tomatoes are a simple side with a rich, melty cheese topping that brings comfort to any meal. I love how quick they come together and their lovely balance of tangy tomato and creamy cheese. Perfect for weeknight dinners or holiday sides!

Introduction

I first made these cheesy baked tomatoes on a chilly fall evening when I wanted something comforting but light. What I love about this recipe is how few ingredients it takes to create something so flavorful — just fresh tomatoes, a blend of cheeses, and some simple seasonings. The trick I learned was to use ripe, juicy tomatoes so the natural sweetness shines through, and topping them with a mix of mozzarella and Parmesan gives a gooey yet slightly crispy texture. I have to admit, my first attempt was a bit soggy because I skipped the olive oil drizzle, but once I added that step, the tomatoes baked to perfection with a lovely caramelized edge. The finished dish is warm, cheesy, and bright with fresh herbs, making it a perfect side for weeknight dinners or a cozy weekend meal. I usually serve it alongside grilled chicken or pasta, and it’s always a hit. Plus, it’s a great way to enjoy tomatoes when they’re in season and at their best.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 large ripe tomatoes, halved
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • Fresh basil leaves, for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Start by washing your tomatoes and slicing them in half horizontally. This exposes the juicy interior, which will roast beautifully and soak up the flavors of the cheese and seasoning. Make sure to pat them dry with a paper towel to remove excess moisture, which helps prevent sogginess during baking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomato halves are dry on the surface and evenly sliced
Step 2
Place the tomato halves cut side up on a baking dish. Drizzle each half with olive oil and sprinkle evenly with minced garlic and dried oregano. This oil not only adds flavor but helps the cheese melt into a golden crust instead of just sitting on top. Season lightly with salt and pepper to enhance the tomato’s natural sweetness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomatoes glisten evenly with oil and garlic
Step 3
Next, sprinkle the shredded mozzarella generously over each tomato half. Mozzarella melts smoothly and creates that gooey texture we all love. Follow with a dusting of Parmesan cheese; its sharp, nutty flavor adds a perfect contrast and helps form a crispy topping when baked.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese covers the tomato surface evenly
Step 4
Place the baking dish in the preheated oven and bake for about 25-30 minutes. Keep an eye on the cheese — you want it melted and starting to turn golden brown but not burnt. The tomatoes should soften but still hold their shape, releasing a bit of their natural juice to mingle with the cheese.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese is bubbly and golden; tomatoes are tender but intact
Step 5
Once baked, remove the tomatoes from the oven and let them cool for a few minutes. This resting time allows the cheese to set slightly, making them easier to serve. Garnish with fresh basil leaves for a pop of color and an herbal brightness that cuts through the richness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese is set but still melty; basil is fresh and vibrant
Step 6
Serve these cheesy baked tomatoes warm as a side with your favorite main dishes. They pair wonderfully with grilled meats, pasta, or even a fresh salad. The balance of sweet tomato and savory cheese makes them a versatile addition to any meal.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dish is plated attractively and ready to enjoy
Step 7
If you want to add a little extra flavor, try sprinkling a pinch of red pepper flakes before baking for a subtle heat. Or mix some fresh herbs like thyme or rosemary into the olive oil drizzle for a more aromatic touch. These small tweaks can really elevate the dish.
⏱ Time: Optional
✓ Doneness: Herbs and spices evenly distributed
Step 8
For a richer version, you can add dollops of ricotta or cream cheese on top before baking. This adds a creamy texture that contrasts nicely with the melted mozzarella and Parmesan. Just be mindful not to overload the tomatoes to keep the bake balanced.
⏱ Time: Optional
✓ Doneness: Cheese mixture is creamy and slightly golden after baking
Step 9
To keep leftovers fresh, cool the tomatoes completely before storing them in an airtight container in the fridge. Reheat gently in the oven or microwave, adding a splash of olive oil if the cheese seems dry. This way, you can enjoy the rich flavors even the next day.
⏱ Time: Storage step
✓ Doneness: Leftovers reheat evenly without drying out

Variations

Spicy Cheesy Baked Tomatoes

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or a dash of hot sauce to the olive oil drizzle before baking. This gives the dish a gentle heat that pairs wonderfully with the creamy cheese and sweet tomatoes. Great for those who like a little kick!

Note: Be careful not to add too much spice as it can overpower the tomato’s natural sweetness. Adjust heat to taste.

Herbed Ricotta Topped Tomatoes

Swap some mozzarella for dollops of ricotta cheese mixed with fresh herbs like thyme and parsley. This adds a creamy, fresh flavor and a lighter texture to the dish, making it feel more delicate and elegant.

Note: Ricotta adds moisture, so bake slightly longer to avoid sogginess and ensure cheese sets.

Vegan Cheesy Baked Tomatoes

Use vegan mozzarella-style shreds and nutritional yeast instead of Parmesan. Drizzle with olive oil and sprinkle garlic and herbs as usual. This keeps the dish dairy-free but still rich and flavorful.

Note: Choose a high-quality vegan cheese that melts well for the best texture.

Stuffed Baked Tomatoes

Scoop out the tomato centers and fill with a mixture of cooked quinoa, herbs, and cheese before baking. This turns the side dish into a hearty, nutritious main or a more filling accompaniment.

Note: Scoop gently to keep tomato shells intact and adjust baking time to ensure filling is heated through.

Pesto and Mozzarella Baked Tomatoes

Spread a thin layer of basil pesto on cut tomato halves before adding cheese. The pesto adds an herby, nutty flavor that complements the tomatoes and cheese beautifully, creating a vibrant twist on the classic.

Note: Use a light hand with pesto to avoid overpowering the tomato flavor.

Pro Tips

Pat tomatoes dry before baking.
Excess moisture from tomatoes can make the cheese topping soggy and prevent it from crisping. By drying them, you ensure the cheese melts properly and the tomatoes roast to a perfect texture.
Use a blend of mozzarella and Parmesan cheese.
Mozzarella provides gooey meltiness while Parmesan adds sharpness and helps form a golden crust. This combo balances creaminess and texture for a more interesting bite.
Drizzle olive oil evenly on tomatoes.
Olive oil promotes caramelization and prevents the cheese from drying out. It also adds a fruity richness that complements the tomatoes’ acidity.
Bake tomatoes cut side up.
This positioning allows the cheese to melt on top without sliding off and lets the tomato juices concentrate beneath, enhancing flavor without making the dish watery.
Don’t overcrowd the baking dish.
Giving each tomato half space lets heat circulate evenly, ensuring uniform cooking and preventing steaming, which can cause sogginess.
Let dish rest briefly after baking.
Resting allows the cheese to set slightly, making the tomatoes easier to serve and the flavors to meld, improving overall texture and taste.
Use fresh basil as garnish.
The fresh herb adds a bright, aromatic counterpoint to the rich cheese and sweet tomatoes, balancing the dish beautifully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the olive oil drizzle

Why it happens Sometimes cooks overlook this step thinking it’s optional, but it’s easy to forget since it’s just a drizzle before baking.

Fix Always drizzle olive oil evenly on the tomato halves before adding cheese. This prevents drying out and helps develop caramelized edges for richer flavor and better texture.

Using underripe tomatoes

Why it happens Choosing tomatoes that are too firm or green because they look fresher or last longer.

Fix Pick ripe tomatoes with a slight softness and deep color to ensure sweetness and juiciness. This greatly improves the taste and texture after baking.

Overbaking the tomatoes

Why it happens Leaving them in the oven too long can happen if you get distracted or misjudge timing.

Fix Set a timer and watch for bubbly, golden cheese and softened but intact tomatoes. Remove promptly to avoid dryness or burnt cheese.

Not drying tomatoes before baking

Why it happens Rushing prep or not realizing moisture affects texture.

Fix Pat each tomato half dry with paper towels to reduce moisture, which improves cheese crispiness and prevents sogginess.

Crowding the baking dish

Why it happens Trying to cook too many tomatoes at once without enough space.

Fix Use a baking dish that fits tomatoes in a single layer with space between each half to promote even roasting and prevent steaming.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These baked tomatoes can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel. Beyond that, they should be refrigerated to prevent spoilage.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The cheese may firm up and tomatoes soften further, so reheat gently to restore texture. They can also be enjoyed cold but are best warmed.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the texture of the tomatoes and cheese changes significantly. If you must freeze, wrap tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe container for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat carefully.

Reheating

Reheat in a preheated oven at 350°F for 10-15 minutes until warmed through and cheese is melty again. You can add a light drizzle of olive oil to refresh the texture. Avoid microwaving for long as it can make the tomatoes mushy.

Make Ahead

Prepare the tomato halves with oil and seasoning up to 1 day ahead and keep refrigerated. Add cheese and bake just before serving for the freshest results. This saves prep time on busy days.

FAQ

Can I use cherry tomatoes instead of large tomatoes?
Absolutely! Cherry tomatoes work well but will bake faster due to their smaller size. Reduce baking time to about 15-20 minutes and watch closely to avoid overcooking. They also make cute bite-sized sides or appetizers.
Can I make this recipe vegan?
Yes, swap out the cheeses for vegan alternatives like cashew-based mozzarella shreds and nutritional yeast for a cheesy flavor. Use olive oil and fresh herbs as usual. The texture and flavor will be slightly different but still tasty.
How ripe should the tomatoes be?
Choose tomatoes that are ripe but still firm to the touch. Overripe tomatoes can become mushy when baked and release too much juice, making the dish soggy. A little softness is good for sweetness and roasting.
Can I prepare these in advance?
Yes! You can prep the tomatoes with oil, garlic, and seasonings a day ahead and store them covered in the fridge. Add cheese and bake just before serving to keep the cheese fresh and melty.
What’s the best way to reheat leftovers?
Reheat in a 350°F oven for 10-15 minutes to melt the cheese again and warm the tomatoes without drying them out. Avoid microwaving for long periods as it can make the tomatoes mushy.
Can I add other herbs or spices?
Definitely! Fresh thyme, rosemary, or a sprinkle of red pepper flakes add great flavor. Just be careful not to overpower the delicate tomato and cheese balance.
Why did my cheese not brown?
If your cheese is melted but not browning, your oven might be too cool or the cheese layer too thick. Try broiling for the last 1-2 minutes, watching carefully to avoid burning.
Can I use shredded cheese instead of fresh mozzarella?
Low-moisture shredded mozzarella works great and tends to brown better than fresh mozzarella, which has more moisture. Both are delicious; just adjust baking time to avoid sogginess with fresh mozzarella.
