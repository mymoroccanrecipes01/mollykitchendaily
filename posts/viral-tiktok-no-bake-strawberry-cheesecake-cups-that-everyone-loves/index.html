No Bake Strawberry Cheesecake Cups That Are Super Easy and Delicious

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 0 min cook 6 servings

These no bake strawberry cheesecake cups are creamy and fresh, perfect for a quick summer dessert. The secret is whipping cream cheese with fresh strawberries for a light, fruity flavor. Great for parties or a sweet snack anytime!

Why This Works

The magic behind these no bake strawberry cheesecake cups lies in the balance of texture and flavor, achieved without the need for baking. The key is whipping the cream cheese and heavy cream separately and then folding them together gently. This technique traps air, resulting in a light, fluffy texture that mimics the feel of a baked cheesecake but with a refreshing twist. The cold heavy cream, when whipped to soft peaks, incorporates tiny air bubbles that lighten the dense cream cheese, preventing the dessert from feeling heavy or overly rich.

Another crucial factor is the use of powdered sugar instead of granulated sugar. Powdered sugar dissolves quickly and evenly into the cream cheese, ensuring a silky smooth filling without any grittiness. The vanilla extract enhances the overall flavor, rounding out the tanginess of the cream cheese and the sweetness of the strawberries. Fresh strawberries add natural acidity and juiciness, which cuts through the richness and prevents the dessert from being cloying.

The graham cracker crust layer adds a delightful textural contrast. Crushing the crackers finely but not into dust ensures a buttery crunch that complements the creamy filling. Pressing the crumbs lightly creates a stable base that holds the cheesecake mixture without becoming soggy. Finally, chilling the cups allows the filling to set but remain creamy, ensuring the perfect bite every time. Skipping the chill would result in a runny texture that lacks structure.

Each step in this recipe is designed to maintain the integrity of the delicate mousse-like filling while maximizing flavor and texture contrasts. The result is a dessert that feels indulgent but fresh, perfect for hot days when baking is not an option.

  • Look for full-fat cream cheese for the richest, creamiest texture. Avoid low-fat versions as they tend to be watery and won't whip up properly. Softening it to room temperature is essential to avoid lumps and ensure a smooth, fluffy filling. If you forget to soften, you can microwave in short bursts but watch carefully to prevent melting.
  • Use cold, heavy whipping cream with at least 36% fat content. The fat is crucial for whipping to soft peaks and creating volume. Lower fat creams won’t hold air well and will result in a watery filling. Chill your bowl and whisk beforehand to speed whipping and improve volume.
  • Powdered sugar dissolves easily and sweetens without graininess. It also contains a small amount of cornstarch which helps stabilize the whipped cream mixture. Avoid substituting with granulated sugar as it won’t dissolve properly and can create a gritty texture.
  • Choose ripe but firm strawberries to add natural sweetness and bright flavor without becoming mushy. Avoid overripe berries as they release excess juice which can make the filling runny. Wash and dry thoroughly to prevent excess moisture in the mixture.
  • Use plain, unsweetened or lightly sweetened graham crackers for the base. Crushing them finely but not into powder provides a nice crunchy texture that contrasts well with the creamy filling. Press gently to form a stable base but avoid compacting too hard which can make it dense.

Ingredients

  • 8 ounces cream cheese, softened
  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream, cold
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup fresh strawberries, chopped
  • 6 graham cracker sheets, crushed
  • Extra strawberries for garnish

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by softening your cream cheese to room temperature. This is key because it allows the cream cheese to whip smoothly without lumps. If it’s too cold, it won’t blend well and you’ll end up with a grainy texture. Use a hand mixer or stand mixer with the paddle attachment on medium speed to beat the cream cheese until it’s fluffy and light, about 2-3 minutes. This aeration is what gives your cheesecake cups that creamy, melt-in-your-mouth texture.

    ⏱ 5✓ Cream cheese should look light, fluffy, and free of lumps.
  2. Step 2

    Next, add the powdered sugar and vanilla extract to the whipped cream cheese. The powdered sugar sweetens gently without any grittiness, and the vanilla adds depth of flavor. Beat again until fully incorporated, scraping down the sides to make sure everything is mixed evenly. Don’t rush this step; if you mix too fast or for too short, the sugar won’t dissolve properly and you’ll notice graininess in the final texture.

    ⏱ 3✓ Mixture should be smooth and uniformly pale.
  3. Step 3

    In a separate chilled bowl, whip the heavy cream to soft peaks. Cold cream whips better because the fat solidifies, trapping air and creating volume. Whipping to soft peaks means the cream holds its shape but still folds easily, which is important for folding it into the cream cheese mixture without deflating it. Overwhipping leads to butter formation and a grainy texture, so watch closely.

    ⏱ 5✓ Cream holds soft peaks that gently bend over when you lift the whisk.
  4. Step 4

    Gently fold the whipped cream into the cream cheese mixture using a spatula. Use a light hand and fold from the bottom up to keep as much air as possible in the mixture, which keeps it light and fluffy. Overmixing here will deflate the mixture, making the cheesecake cups dense and less creamy. Folding properly ensures a smooth, airy texture.

    ⏱ 3✓ Mixture is smooth, fluffy, and no streaks of cream remain.
  5. Step 5

    Chop the fresh strawberries into small pieces. Fresh strawberries add a bright, natural sweetness and a bit of texture contrast to the creamy filling. I recommend using ripe but firm strawberries so they hold up well in the cups and don’t turn mushy. Stir half the chopped strawberries gently into the cheesecake mixture to distribute the fruit evenly.

    ⏱ 5✓ Strawberries are evenly mixed but not crushed.
  6. Step 6

    Prepare your serving cups by layering a spoonful of crushed graham crackers at the bottom. The graham crackers add a buttery crunch that complements the creamy filling. Be sure to crush them finely so they layer well but still provide texture. Press lightly to create a base that will hold the filling nicely.

    ⏱ 5✓ Cracker layer is firm but not compacted too hard.
  7. Step 7

    Spoon a generous amount of the strawberry cheesecake mixture over the graham cracker base in each cup. Smooth the tops with the back of a spoon or spatula. This layering creates a pretty, inviting dessert with a nice balance of textures and flavors in every bite. Don’t overfill the cups so you can add some garnish on top.

    ⏱ 5✓ Filling sits evenly on cracker base with smooth surface.
  8. Step 8

    Top each cup with the remaining chopped strawberries and a small sprinkle of crushed graham crackers for an attractive finish. This last touch adds fresh color and texture contrast. You can also add a small mint leaf or drizzle of strawberry sauce if you want to dress them up further for guests.

    ⏱ 3✓ Cups look colorful and inviting with fresh strawberry garnish.
  9. Step 9

    Chill the cheesecake cups in the refrigerator for at least 2 hours before serving. Chilling allows the flavors to meld and the filling to firm up slightly for the perfect creamy texture. Don’t skip this step or the filling will be too soft and runny. When ready, serve cold and enjoy immediately for best texture and flavor.

    ⏱ 120✓ Filling is set but still creamy and holds shape when scooped.

Variations

  • Lemon Zest Strawberry Cheesecake Cups: Add 1 teaspoon of fresh lemon zest to the cream cheese mixture for a bright citrus twist that complements the strawberries beautifully. The lemon adds a refreshing zing that balances the sweetness and adds complexity.
    Incorporate the zest when mixing the cream cheese and sugar to evenly distribute the flavor. No change in chilling time needed.
  • Chocolate Graham Cracker Crust: Mix 2 tablespoons of cocoa powder into the crushed graham crackers for a chocolatey base. This adds a rich, bittersweet contrast to the sweet strawberry filling, perfect for chocolate lovers.
    Make sure to mix cocoa powder evenly with crumbs before pressing into cups. The texture remains the same; no additional moisture needed.
  • Blueberry and Strawberry Mixed Cheesecake Cups: Swap half the strawberries for fresh blueberries for a colorful berry medley. This adds a slightly different tartness and visual appeal, making the cups even more festive.
    Chop blueberries if large and fold gently into the filling as with strawberries. No change in preparation time.
  • Vegan No Bake Strawberry Cheesecake Cups: Use vegan cream cheese and coconut cream instead of dairy for a plant-based version. The coconut cream whips well and pairs nicely with strawberries for a creamy, dairy-free dessert.
    Chill coconut cream overnight and whip similarly to heavy cream. Vegan cream cheese should be softened like regular cream cheese.
  • Strawberry Cheesecake Parfait with Granola: Layer the cheesecake mixture and strawberries with crunchy granola instead of graham crackers for a parfait style dessert with extra texture and nutty flavor.
    Add granola just before serving to keep it crunchy. No chilling changes required.

Pro Tips

  • Always whip your heavy cream until soft peaks form, not stiff peaks. — Soft peaks ensure the cream is aerated enough to lighten the cream cheese but still folds smoothly. Overwhipping can cause the cream to turn grainy or separate into butter, ruining the texture of your cheesecake cups.
  • Fold the whipped cream into the cream cheese mixture gently using a spatula. — Folding preserves the air bubbles whipped into both mixtures, keeping the filling light and fluffy. Stirring or beating aggressively will deflate the mixture, resulting in a dense, heavy texture.
  • Let the cream cheese come to room temperature before starting. — Cold cream cheese is stiff and tough to blend, leading to lumps in your filling. Softened cream cheese whips easily, creating a smooth and creamy base for the dessert.
  • Chill the dessert for at least 2 hours before serving. — Chilling allows the filling to firm up slightly and for flavors to meld. Serving too soon will result in a runny texture that lacks the signature creamy consistency of cheesecake.
  • Use fresh strawberries that are ripe but firm. — Ripe strawberries provide natural sweetness and acidity, but overly ripe berries can make the filling watery. Firm berries hold their shape better, adding pleasant texture and color.
  • Crush graham crackers finely but not into powder. — This provides a crunchy base that contrasts nicely with the creamy filling. Too fine a crust can become soggy and lose texture, while too large pieces won’t layer well.
  • Avoid substituting powdered sugar with granulated sugar. — Granulated sugar doesn’t dissolve well in cold mixtures and can create a gritty texture. Powdered sugar ensures smooth sweetness and helps stabilize the whipped cream.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using cold cream cheese without softening
    Why: Many assume cream cheese can be mixed cold, but it’s too stiff and lumps easily.
    Fix: Always set cream cheese out for at least 30 minutes before whipping. If short on time, microwave in 10-second bursts and stir until soft but not melted.
  • Overwhipping heavy cream
    Why: It’s easy to keep mixing past soft peaks, especially with a mixer.
    Fix: Stop whipping as soon as the cream holds soft peaks. Watch carefully and test by lifting the whisk—soft peaks bend over slightly.
  • Stirring whipped cream aggressively into cream cheese
    Why: Some think mixing thoroughly means beating the mixture.
    Fix: Use a gentle folding motion with a spatula to keep air in. Avoid beating or stirring vigorously.
  • Skipping the chilling step
    Why: Impatience or lack of planning leads to serving immediately.
    Fix: Plan ahead and chill the cups for at least 2 hours to set the filling and develop flavor.
  • Using overripe strawberries
    Why: Overripe berries seem sweeter but release too much juice.
    Fix: Choose firm, ripe berries and pat dry. Avoid any that are mushy or overly soft.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: These cheesecake cups should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to the dairy content. Cover with plastic wrap or a lid to prevent contamination if you must keep them out briefly.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The texture may firm up more, but the cups remain creamy and delicious cold. Best served chilled.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freezing is not recommended as the texture may become grainy and watery upon thawing due to the whipped cream. If you must freeze, wrap tightly and thaw overnight in the fridge.
  • ♨️ Reheating: No reheating needed. Serve chilled straight from the refrigerator for the best creamy texture and fresh flavor.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the filling and chop strawberries 1-2 days ahead. Assemble the cups and chill them on the day you plan to serve to keep the graham cracker base crisp and the filling fresh.

FAQ

Can I use frozen strawberries for this recipe?
Frozen strawberries release a lot of water when thawed, which can make the filling watery and affect the texture. If you must use frozen, thaw completely, drain excess liquid, and pat dry thoroughly before adding.
How do I make sure the filling is smooth and lump-free?
Make sure your cream cheese is fully softened before mixing. Beat it well to remove lumps. Also, folding the whipped cream gently helps maintain a smooth, airy texture without lumps.
Can I make these cups ahead of time?
Yes! You can prepare the filling and strawberries a day or two ahead. Assemble the cups and refrigerate up to 3 days. Just add the fresh strawberry garnish right before serving for best appearance.
What can I substitute for graham crackers?
Digestive biscuits or vanilla wafers work well as a substitute. Just crush them finely and use the same amount. Avoid overly sweet or flavored cookies that might overpower the filling.
Why is my filling runny after chilling?
Runny filling usually means the cream wasn’t whipped enough or the cream cheese was too cold and not mixed properly. Make sure to whip cream to soft peaks and fold gently, and soften cream cheese fully before mixing.
Can I use low-fat cream cheese or cream?
Low-fat versions don’t whip or set as well, resulting in a less creamy and more watery texture. For best results, use full-fat cream cheese and heavy cream.
How long can I keep the cheesecake cups in the fridge?
If you want to make them a day in advance, that works perfectly and flavors actually meld nicely overnight.
Can I add gelatin to stabilize the filling?
You can add a small amount of gelatin if you want a firmer set, especially for warmer climates. However, this recipe is designed to be light and creamy without gelatin, so it’s optional.
