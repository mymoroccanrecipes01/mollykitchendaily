Moist and Flavorful Sugar-Free Banana Apple Cake You’ll Love

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 40 min cook 8 servings

This sugar-free banana apple cake is a moist, naturally sweet treat that’s perfect for guilt-free snacking. The ripe bananas and applesauce add natural sweetness and keep it tender. Great for afternoon cravings or a healthy dessert!

Why This Works

This sugar-free banana apple cake achieves its moist texture and natural sweetness primarily through the use of ripe bananas and unsweetened applesauce. Both ingredients contain natural sugars and a high moisture content, which keeps the crumb tender without the need for added refined sugar. The bananas also contribute a dense, creamy texture that gives the cake body and richness.

The inclusion of whole wheat flour adds a subtle nuttiness and more fiber, which balances the sweetness and creates a satisfying mouthfeel. However, because whole wheat flour can sometimes dry baked goods, the recipe carefully balances it with enough fat from coconut oil and moisture from the fruit to maintain softness.

Leavening agents like baking soda and baking powder work together to provide lift and a light crumb. Baking soda reacts with the acidity in the bananas and applesauce, producing carbon dioxide bubbles that help the cake rise. Meanwhile, folding the dry ingredients gently prevents gluten overdevelopment, which would otherwise toughen the texture.

Finally, the warm spice of cinnamon enhances the natural fruit flavors and adds depth without overpowering the cake. The optional walnuts give a pleasant crunchy contrast, making the eating experience more interesting. Overall, the recipe is a careful balance of moisture, sweetness, and texture that delivers a delicious sugar-free treat.

  • Look for bananas that are very ripe with brown spots for maximum sweetness and moisture. Overripe bananas have higher sugar content and softer texture, which helps naturally sweeten and tenderize the cake. If your bananas are not ripe enough, the cake might turn out less sweet and drier. You can freeze overripe bananas and thaw them before use to always have ripe ones on hand.
  • Choose unsweetened applesauce to avoid added sugars and control sweetness naturally. Applesauce adds moisture and a subtle fruity flavor that complements bananas well. It also helps bind ingredients together and keeps the cake tender. Store-bought or homemade applesauce works fine, but avoid sweetened varieties or ones with added flavors that might alter the cake's taste.
  • Whole wheat flour adds a hearty, nutty flavor and boosts fiber content, making the cake more nutritious. However, it can make baked goods dense if overmixed or if too much is used. This recipe balances whole wheat flour with moisture-rich ingredients to keep the cake soft. If you prefer a lighter texture, you can substitute half with all-purpose flour, but the flavor will be less robust.
  • These leavening agents are essential for the cake’s rise. Baking soda reacts with acidic components like banana and applesauce to produce carbon dioxide bubbles, which create lift and a fluffy texture. Baking powder adds additional rise and lightness. Using both ensures the cake isn’t too dense or flat. Make sure your leavening agents are fresh; old baking soda or powder can cause poor rising.
  • Coconut oil adds moisture and a subtle richness to the cake, helping maintain tenderness. It also contributes to a moist crumb that doesn’t dry out quickly. Using melted coconut oil distributes fat evenly through the batter. You can substitute with other neutral oils, but coconut oil’s slight flavor complements the banana and apple well. Avoid using butter here as it changes the texture and moisture balance.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup ripe mashed bananas (about 2-3 bananas)
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened applesauce
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/4 cup melted coconut oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups whole wheat flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup chopped walnuts (optional)

I first stumbled upon this sugar-free banana apple cake when I wanted a healthier treat that didn’t skimp on moisture or flavor. In the past, I tried sugar-free cakes that just ended up dry and crumbly, but this recipe changed that for me. The trick is using both ripe bananas and unsweetened applesauce — they add natural sweetness and keep the cake incredibly moist without any added sugar. Plus, the cinnamon adds a warm depth that’s just cozy and comforting. What I really love about this cake is how versatile it is. It’s perfect for an afternoon snack or a guilt-free dessert after dinner. I often make it on weekends and enjoy it with a cup of tea, and my family always asks for seconds. One secret I learned is to not overmix the batter; it helps keep the texture tender and light. Also, using whole wheat flour adds a subtle nuttiness and more fiber, making this cake feel a little more wholesome. When it’s done, you’ll see a beautiful golden crust with a soft, fragrant crumb inside. Honestly, it’s one of my go-to recipes when I want something sweet but still healthy and satisfying.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9-inch cake pan or line it with parchment paper. This step is important because a well-prepared pan prevents the cake from sticking, allowing for easy removal and clean edges. If you skip this, your cake might break apart or lose its shape when you try to take it out. Also, preheating the oven ensures that the cake bakes evenly from the start, which helps with proper rising and texture.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Oven fully preheated and cake pan greased or lined
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, mash the ripe bananas thoroughly until smooth with just a few small chunks remaining; then mix in the applesauce, eggs, melted coconut oil, and vanilla extract until well combined. The bananas and applesauce provide natural sweetness and moisture, so mixing them well ensures even distribution throughout the batter. Avoid overmixing here because it can make the cake dense and tough. The coconut oil adds richness and keeps the crumb tender.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Smooth mixture without lumps of egg or oil separation
  3. Step 3

    In a separate bowl, whisk together the whole wheat flour, baking soda, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt. This step is crucial to evenly distribute the leavening agents and spices, which allows the cake to rise properly and have consistent flavor. If you don’t mix these dry ingredients first, you might end up with uneven pockets of baking soda or cinnamon, which can cause off flavors or uneven texture.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Dry ingredients are uniformly combined and free of clumps
  4. Step 4

    Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, folding gently with a spatula until just combined. It’s important not to overmix here because overworking the flour can develop gluten, making the cake tough rather than tender. Folding gently helps keep the batter light and airy. Stop mixing as soon as you don’t see dry flour streaks. The batter should be thick but smooth.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ No visible dry flour, batter thick and soft
  5. Step 5

    If using, fold in the chopped walnuts gently at this point. Adding nuts adds a nice crunchy texture contrast and a toasty flavor that pairs beautifully with the banana and apple. Make sure to fold them in evenly so every slice has a bit of crunch. If you prefer nut-free, you can skip this or substitute with raisins or chocolate chips.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Walnuts evenly distributed without overmixing the batter
  6. Step 6

    Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan and spread it evenly using a spatula. This ensures the cake bakes uniformly and rises with a smooth, flat top. Uneven spreading can lead to lopsided baking or uneven texture. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles trapped in the batter.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Batter evenly spread and smooth on top
  7. Step 7

    Bake the cake in the preheated oven for 35-40 minutes, checking doneness at 35 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, it’s done. Baking too long can dry out the cake, while underbaking leaves a gummy texture. The cake should have a golden-brown crust and slightly pull away from the pan edges when ready.

    ⏱ 35-40 minutes✓ Golden crust, toothpick comes out clean or with moist crumbs
  8. Step 8

    Remove the cake from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring it to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling in the pan allows the cake to firm up slightly, which prevents it from breaking apart when you remove it. Transferring to a wire rack prevents condensation and sogginess by allowing air circulation around the cake.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Cake is firm enough to handle and cools evenly on rack
  9. Step 9

    Once cooled, slice and serve as is or with a dollop of Greek yogurt or nut butter for extra richness. This cake tastes great warm or at room temperature. Storing it properly in an airtight container keeps it moist for several days. Enjoy it as a healthy snack or a light dessert any time!

    ⏱ Immediate serving✓ Cake slices cleanly and tastes moist and flavorful

Variations

  • Add Chocolate Chips: For a sweeter twist, fold in 1/2 cup of dark or semi-sweet chocolate chips before baking. The chocolate melts slightly, adding gooey pockets of richness that pair wonderfully with the banana and apple flavors.
    Chocolate chips may cause the cake to brown a bit faster; watch the baking time closely and cover loosely with foil if browning too quickly.
  • Use Almond Flour Instead of Whole Wheat: Swap whole wheat flour for almond flour for a gluten-free, nutty alternative. The cake will be denser and moister, with a slightly different texture but equally delicious.
    Almond flour batter is wetter; reduce applesauce by 1-2 tablespoons to balance moisture and ensure proper rise.
  • Add Spices Like Nutmeg and Ginger: Enhance the warm flavor profile by adding 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg and 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger along with cinnamon. This makes the cake taste like a cozy fall treat.
    Spices intensify flavor, so adjust quantities to taste, especially if sensitive to strong spice notes.
  • Make Muffins Instead of Cake: Pour the batter into a muffin tin lined with paper cups for individual servings. Bake at 350°F for 18-22 minutes until a toothpick comes out clean. Perfect for grab-and-go snacks.
    Muffins bake faster due to smaller size; start checking doneness early to avoid drying out.
  • Add Shredded Carrots: Mix in 1/2 cup finely shredded carrots for extra moisture and a subtle sweetness. This adds a veggie boost and a bit of texture without overpowering the cake.
    Press excess moisture from carrots if very wet to prevent batter thinning and longer baking times.

Pro Tips

  • Use very ripe bananas for natural sweetness and moisture. — Ripe bananas contain more sugars and softer flesh, which not only sweeten the cake naturally but also add moisture that keeps the crumb tender. Using underripe bananas results in a drier, less flavorful cake.
  • Don’t overmix the batter once dry ingredients are added. — Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make the cake tough and dense. Folding gently until just combined ensures a light and tender crumb.
  • Preheat the oven fully before baking. — Starting the cake in a hot oven ensures proper rise and even baking. If the oven is not hot enough, the cake may bake unevenly or be dense.
  • Use whole wheat flour for added nutrition and flavor. — Whole wheat flour adds fiber and a nutty taste, enhancing the cake’s wholesome character. It also affects texture, so balancing it with moisture is key.
  • Let the cake cool in the pan before removing. — Cooling slightly allows the cake to set and reduces the chance of it breaking apart when transferred to a rack.
  • Tap the pan on the counter before baking to release air bubbles. — This prevents large air pockets that can cause holes or uneven texture in the finished cake.
  • Add optional walnuts for texture contrast. — Nuts add crunch and flavor complexity, making each bite more interesting and satisfying.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using underripe bananas
    Why: People sometimes use bananas that aren’t ripe enough because they want to use them before they spoil or don’t realize the difference ripe bananas make.
    Fix: Always choose bananas with plenty of brown spots and softness for this cake. If you only have underripe bananas, consider freezing them for a few days to speed ripening or wait until they darken for best results.
  • Overmixing the batter after adding flour
    Why: It’s natural to want to mix thoroughly, but overmixing is a common mistake that leads to tough, dense cake.
    Fix: Mix just until you don’t see dry flour anymore. Use a folding motion with a spatula instead of stirring vigorously to keep the crumb light.
  • Skipping greasing or lining the pan
    Why: Sometimes people forget this step or underestimate its importance, leading to sticking and broken cakes.
    Fix: Always grease the pan well or line it with parchment paper. This simple step ensures the cake releases cleanly and maintains its shape.
  • Baking at the wrong temperature
    Why: Ovens vary and sometimes people don’t preheat or set the correct temperature, which can affect baking time and texture.
    Fix: Preheat the oven to 350°F and use an oven thermometer if possible to ensure accuracy. Adjust baking time slightly if your oven runs hot or cold.
  • Not testing cake doneness properly
    Why: Relying on time alone can lead to underbaked or overbaked cake since ovens and pan sizes differ.
    Fix: Use a toothpick inserted in the center to check. It should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter. This is the best indicator of doneness.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: You can safely keep the cake covered at room temperature for up to 2 days. Use a cake dome or airtight container to prevent it from drying out or absorbing kitchen odors.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The cake may firm up and taste even better chilled. Reheat gently in the microwave or oven to restore softness.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil, or place in an airtight freezer-safe container. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and warm slightly before serving for best texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat slices in a microwave for 20-30 seconds or in a preheated 300°F oven for 10-15 minutes. Adding a small splash of water or covering loosely with foil helps retain moisture. Avoid overheating, which dries the cake.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can mix the batter 1 day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bring it to room temperature before baking. Alternatively, bake the cake in advance and store refrigerated, reheating slices as needed.

FAQ

Can I substitute all-purpose flour for whole wheat flour?
Yes, you can substitute all-purpose flour 1:1 for a lighter texture, but the flavor and fiber content will be less robust. The cake may also be a bit less dense. If using all-purpose, watch baking time as it may cook slightly faster.
What can I use instead of coconut oil?
Neutral oils like vegetable, canola, or light olive oil work well as substitutes. Butter can be used but will change texture and moisture levels, so reduce slightly. Avoid using solid fats without melting to ensure even mixing.
How do I know when the cake is done?
Insert a toothpick into the center; if it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the cake is done. The top should be golden brown and the edges slightly pulling from the pan. Avoid opening the oven too early to maintain rise.
Can I make this cake vegan?
To make it vegan, replace eggs with flax or chia eggs (1 tablespoon ground seed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a plant-based oil instead of coconut oil if needed. The texture may be slightly different but still delicious.
Why is my cake dense and heavy?
Dense cake often results from overmixing the batter, using underripe bananas, or insufficient leavening. Make sure to fold gently and use ripe bananas. Also, check that your baking soda and powder are fresh and measure accurately.
Can I add other fruits or nuts?
Absolutely! You can add raisins, dried cranberries, or other chopped nuts like pecans. Just fold them in gently at the end. Keep the total add-ins to about 1/2 cup to avoid weighing down the batter.
How should I store leftover cake?
Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months. Warm slices slightly before serving.
Can I make this recipe into muffins?
Yes! Divide the batter into a lined muffin tin and bake at 350°F for 18-22 minutes. Muffins will be moist and perfect for snacks. Keep an eye on baking time to prevent drying out.
