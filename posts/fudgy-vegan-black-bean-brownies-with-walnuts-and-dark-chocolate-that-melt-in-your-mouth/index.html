Fudgy Black Bean Brownies with Crunchy Walnuts and Dark Chocolate Chunks

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
9 Servings
Medium Difficulty

These brownies are rich and fudgy thanks to black beans replacing flour. Walnuts add a nice crunch, and dark chocolate chunks make every bite decadent. Perfect for a guilt-free dessert or anytime snack.

Introduction

I stumbled upon this recipe a few years ago when I wanted a chocolate fix but also needed something a bit healthier. Using black beans instead of flour sounded odd at first, but the result blew me away — these brownies are incredibly fudgy and moist, with a rich chocolaty flavor that no one would guess came from beans! What makes this version stand out is how the walnuts add a subtle crunch and the pockets of dark chocolate chunks melt into gooey bites. I’ve tried other flourless brownies before, but this one strikes the perfect balance between indulgence and nourishment. The trick I learned is to blend the beans really well so the batter is smooth, which keeps the texture dense but not grainy. When they come out of the oven, the brownies have a shiny, crackly top that promises the perfect fudgy center. I love serving these when friends drop by or as a treat after a long day. Honestly, they’ve become my go-to when I want dessert that feels like a cheat but isn’t. You’ll want to keep these in your regular rotation for sure!

What You'll Need

Approximately 9 brownies

  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup melted coconut oil
  • 1/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts
  • 3/4 cup dark chocolate chunks

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Line an 8x8 inch baking pan with parchment paper or lightly grease it to prevent sticking. This step is crucial because it ensures even baking and makes it easy to lift the brownies out once they're done. Don’t skip lining the pan, or you might struggle to remove the brownies without breaking them.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Oven reaches full temperature and pan is properly prepared with parchment or grease
Step 2
In a food processor or high-speed blender, combine the drained black beans, eggs, melted coconut oil, cocoa powder, brown sugar, vanilla extract, baking powder, and salt. Blend until the mixture is completely smooth with no bean chunks visible. This smoothness is key to the fudgy texture — any lumps can create a grainy feel in the brownies. Resist the urge to rush blending; it’s worth the few extra seconds.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter is homogenous, thick but pourable, and free of bean pieces
Step 3
Transfer the batter to a large mixing bowl. Fold in the chopped walnuts and dark chocolate chunks gently using a spatula. Folding rather than stirring vigorously helps keep the chocolate chunks intact and evenly distributed, so you get melty bites throughout without breaking them down.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Walnuts and chocolate chunks are evenly distributed without breaking up the chocolate
Step 4
Pour the batter into your prepared pan, spreading it evenly with a spatula. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any air bubbles. This helps the brownies bake evenly and prevents holes or cracks on the surface, giving you that classic crackly top.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and smooth in the pan, with no large air pockets
Step 5
Bake the brownies in the preheated oven for 25-30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center. It should come out with a few moist crumbs but not wet batter. Overbaking will dry them out, so watch carefully. The edges will be set and slightly pulling away from the pan when done.
⏱ Time: 25
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out with moist crumbs, edges are firm and pulling away slightly
Step 6
Remove the brownies from the oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling is important because the brownies continue to set as they cool, making them easier to cut and enhancing their fudgy texture. Cutting them while warm can cause crumbling.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Brownies feel firm to the touch and are completely cooled before slicing
Step 7
Once cooled, lift the brownies out using the parchment paper and place on a cutting board. Use a sharp knife to cut into squares. For clean cuts, wipe the knife between slices to prevent sticking. This keeps the edges neat and makes serving easier.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Squares have clean edges without crumbling or sticking
Step 8
Serve the brownies as is or with a scoop of vanilla ice cream for an extra special treat. They’re equally delicious warm or at room temperature. If you want a little extra crunch, sprinkle some chopped walnuts on top right before serving.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Brownies plated attractively, optionally garnished with extra walnuts or ice cream
Step 9
Store any leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days or in the fridge for up to a week. For longer storage, freeze the brownies wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and foil. Thaw at room temperature before serving to maintain their fudgy texture.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Brownies properly stored to maintain freshness and texture

Variations

Add a hint of espresso

Mix 1 teaspoon of instant espresso powder into the batter to deepen the chocolate flavor and add a subtle coffee note. This works wonderfully if you love mocha flavors and want a grown-up twist.

Note: Espresso powder dissolves easily and won’t affect texture; just blend it with the wet ingredients.

Swap walnuts for pecans

Use chopped pecans instead of walnuts for a slightly sweeter, buttery crunch. Pecans give a different nutty character but keep the texture contrast intact.

Note: Toast pecans lightly before folding in to enhance flavor similarly to walnuts.

Make it vegan

Replace eggs with a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg). This changes the texture slightly, making it a bit more cake-like but still delicious and fudgy.

Note: Let flax eggs sit for 5 minutes to gel before mixing into batter.

Add a swirl of peanut butter

Drop dollops of peanut butter on top of the batter and swirl gently before baking for a salty-sweet twist. It adds richness and a lovely marbled look.

Note: Use creamy peanut butter for easier swirling and even distribution.

Use semi-sweet chocolate chips instead of chunks

If you prefer smaller pockets of melted chocolate, use chips instead of chunks. This creates a more uniform chocolate presence throughout the brownies.

Note: Chips melt faster and blend more into the batter, so fold in gently to retain some texture.

Pro Tips

Blend black beans until completely smooth for the best texture.
Pureeing the beans thoroughly removes any grainy bits that can ruin the fudgy consistency. A smooth batter ensures the brownies are dense and moist without bean chunks disrupting the texture.
Use parchment paper to line your baking pan for easy removal.
Parchment prevents sticking and allows you to lift the entire brownie slab out for clean, even cutting. Skipping this can cause breakage and frustration.
Don’t overbake; check with a toothpick at 25 minutes.
Overbaking dries the brownies out and makes them crumbly. The toothpick test ensures they’re set but still moist inside, preserving that fudgy texture.
Fold in walnuts and chocolate chunks gently to avoid breaking.
Rough stirring can break chocolate chunks and crush walnuts, losing the textural contrast that makes these brownies special.
Let brownies cool completely before cutting.
Cooling lets the brownies set fully, making clean cuts easier and preventing crumbling or sticking.
Toast walnuts lightly before adding for extra flavor.
Toasting brings out the nuts’ oils and deepens their flavor, adding a pleasant aroma and crunch to the finished brownies.
Store brownies in an airtight container to keep moisture.
Proper storage prevents the brownies from drying out and maintains their fudgy texture for several days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not blending black beans fully smooth

Why it happens Sometimes people rush blending or use low-powered blenders, assuming a few lumps won’t matter.

Fix Use a high-speed blender or food processor and blend for at least 1-2 minutes until completely smooth. Scrape down the sides and blend again if needed.

Overbaking the brownies

Why it happens It’s tempting to bake longer to ‘make sure’ they’re done, especially with dense batters.

Fix Start checking at 25 minutes with a toothpick. Remove when it comes out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

Skipping parchment paper or greasing the pan insufficiently

Why it happens Sometimes overlooked because it seems like an extra step.

Fix Always line with parchment or grease well to prevent sticking and enable easy removal.

Mixing walnuts and chocolate chunks too vigorously

Why it happens Over-mixing can break chunks and crush nuts.

Fix Fold in gently with a spatula just until evenly distributed.

Cutting brownies while still warm

Why it happens Impatience to try them or serve immediately.

Fix Wait at least 30 minutes for them to cool and set fully for clean slices.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat to avoid drying out. Covering well prevents them from becoming stale.

Refrigerator

Keep brownies in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 7 days. The texture firms up when chilled, so you might want to let them sit at room temperature for 15 minutes before serving for a softer bite.

Freezer

Wrap brownies tightly in plastic wrap and then foil before freezing. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature for a few hours. Avoid microwaving frozen brownies directly to prevent uneven heating.

Reheating

Warm brownies gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes to soften. Add a small splash of milk or water if they feel dry. Avoid overheating, which can make them crumbly.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh the next day for best texture. Alternatively, bake and cool brownies, then store them wrapped tightly until ready to serve.

FAQ

Can I use dried black beans instead of canned?
Yes, but you’ll need to soak and cook the dried beans fully before using. Cooked beans should be soft and tender. Using canned beans saves time and ensures consistent moisture levels, which is key to the fudgy texture.
What if I don’t have coconut oil? Can I use butter?
Butter can be used instead of coconut oil and will add a richer, creamier flavor. Melt the butter before mixing. Keep in mind butter has a lower melting point, so brownies might be slightly less firm when cooled.
How do I store leftover brownies to keep them fresh?
Store brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days or in the fridge for up to a week. Wrapping them tightly prevents drying out and keeps them soft and fudgy.
Can I make these brownies gluten-free?
Absolutely! This recipe is naturally gluten-free because it uses black beans instead of flour. Just double-check that your baking powder and other ingredients are gluten-free certified.
Why do my brownies turn out crumbly sometimes?
This usually happens if the beans aren’t blended smooth enough or if you overbake them. Make sure to blend well and check for doneness early to keep them moist and fudgy.
Can I add other nuts or mix-ins?
Yes! You can swap walnuts for pecans, almonds, or even add dried fruit. Just fold them in gently to keep the texture balanced and avoid breaking up the chocolate chunks.
Will these brownies taste like beans?
No, when blended well, black beans have a neutral flavor that’s masked by cocoa and sugar. You’ll get rich chocolate flavor without any bean taste.
Can I double the recipe?
Yes, doubling works fine. Use a larger pan or two pans to ensure even baking. Keep an eye on baking time as it might vary slightly with thicker batter.
