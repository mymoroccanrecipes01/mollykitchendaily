How to Make Silky Clotted Cream at Home Using Your Air Fryer

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 28, 2026
15 min Prep Time
8 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This easy air fryer clotted cream recipe delivers that rich, velvety texture you love on scones. No special tools needed, just a simple slow heat method. Ideal for lazy weekend brunches or teatime treats!

Introduction

I first fell in love with clotted cream during a trip to the English countryside, where it’s a staple on every scone served with afternoon tea. But honestly, finding good clotted cream back home is pretty tough, and store-bought versions just don’t have that authentic, rich texture. So, I started experimenting in my kitchen. I was skeptical if an air fryer could do the trick, but it turns out it’s perfect for this slow, gentle heating process. The trick I learned is to use heavy cream that’s not ultra-pasteurized and let it cook low and slow in the air fryer. This method gently thickens the cream and develops that signature silky, golden crust on top. The result is luscious, spreadable clotted cream that’s perfect for scones, berries, or even drizzled on pancakes. It’s an easy little luxury that makes weekend breakfasts feel extra special. Plus, it’s super simple and requires just two ingredients. I think you’ll love how effortless it is to make this at home!

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 cup clotted cream, 4 servings

  • 2 cups heavy cream (not ultra-pasteurized)
  • A pinch of salt (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your air fryer to 180°F (82°C). Pour the heavy cream into a small, oven-safe dish that fits comfortably in your air fryer basket. The cream should be about 1-2 inches deep to allow even heating. This low temperature is crucial because it slowly evaporates water from the cream and encourages the formation of the rich, thick layer on top without curdling. Avoid higher temperatures or rushing this step as it can cause the cream to separate or burn.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The cream should be warm and gently steaming but not boiling.
Step 2
Place the dish with cream into the preheated air fryer basket and cook at 180°F (82°C) for 6 to 8 hours. This slow, prolonged heat allows the milk fats to rise and form a thick, golden crust on the surface. Resist the urge to stir or open the air fryer too often, as steady heat and minimal disturbance are key to developing the perfect texture. If your air fryer does not maintain a steady low temperature, check periodically to avoid overheating.
⏱ Time: 6-8 hours
✓ Doneness: A thick, slightly browned crust forms on top, and the cream underneath is silky and thickened.
Step 3
Once the cooking time is up, carefully remove the dish from the air fryer and let it cool to room temperature. Then cover it tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 8 hours or overnight. The chilling helps the clotted cream set fully, enhancing its spreadable texture and flavor. Avoid skipping this cooling step, or the cream will be too runny and won’t have that signature richness.
⏱ Time: 8 hours (refrigeration)
✓ Doneness: The cream is thick, cold, and spreadable with a rich texture.
Step 4
After chilling, use a spoon to gently scoop the thickened cream from the top, leaving any liquid whey behind. This top layer is your clotted cream, rich and silky. If you like, stir in a pinch of salt for balance, but it’s delicious plain too. The leftover liquid can be used in baking or smoothies, so don’t waste it!
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Thick, creamy texture that holds shape but spreads easily.
Step 5
Serve your homemade clotted cream fresh with warm scones, jam, or fresh fruit. It’s best enjoyed within 3-4 days for maximum flavor and texture. If you notice any sour smell or separation, discard it. The cream should smell fresh and have a delicate, buttery aroma.
⏱ Time: Immediate serving or storage
✓ Doneness: Fresh aroma, no off smells, and smooth texture.
Step 6
For best results, choose a heavy cream with at least 36% fat and avoid ultra-pasteurized versions, as they don’t clot properly. The fat content and minimal processing allow the cream to thicken and form that signature crust. If you can find raw cream or farm-fresh cream, even better!
⏱ Time: Ingredient selection
✓ Doneness: Cream with high fat content and fresh smell.
Step 7
If your air fryer doesn’t hold low temperatures well, consider using an oven set at 180°F (82°C) instead. The key is consistent, gentle heat over several hours. A slow cooker on the lowest setting can also work, but the air fryer is great for energy efficiency and easy cleanup.
⏱ Time: Alternative cooking method
✓ Doneness: Consistent slow heat without boiling.
Step 8
Avoid stirring or shaking the cream during the cooking process. The formation of the clotted cream layer depends on the cream sitting undisturbed so the fats can rise and thicken naturally. Any disturbance can break the delicate layer or mix it back into the liquid.
⏱ Time: Throughout cooking
✓ Doneness: Undisturbed cream surface with golden crust.
Step 9
When serving, bring the clotted cream out of the fridge about 15 minutes before to soften slightly. This will make it spread easier on scones or toast without melting away. The texture should be thick but spreadable, like a rich buttercream.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes before serving
✓ Doneness: Softened, easily spreadable cream.

Variations

Honey-Infused Clotted Cream

Add a tablespoon of your favorite honey to the cream before cooking for a subtly sweet, floral note. This variation pairs beautifully with fresh berries or warm scones, adding a new dimension of flavor without extra effort.

Note: Honey adds sugars that may slightly speed caramelization on top, so watch the crust closely to prevent burning.

Vanilla Bean Clotted Cream

Split a vanilla bean and scrape the seeds into the cream before cooking. This infuses the cream with a natural vanilla aroma, making it perfect for desserts or breakfast treats that need a little extra warmth and complexity.

Note: Vanilla seeds don’t affect texture but add flavor; you can substitute with vanilla extract added after cooking if preferred.

Cinnamon-Spiced Clotted Cream

Sprinkle a teaspoon of ground cinnamon into the cream before cooking for a cozy, spiced version. It’s lovely in fall or winter and goes great with apple preserves or pumpkin scones.

Note: Ground spices may slightly darken the cream’s top layer; ensure the air fryer temperature stays consistent to prevent burning.

Lemon Zest Clotted Cream

Add finely grated lemon zest to the cream before cooking for a fresh, citrusy twist. This brightens the rich cream and is fantastic with berry jams or on pancakes.

Note: Use only the yellow part of the zest to avoid bitterness; the citrus oils infuse gently during slow cooking.

Dairy-Free Clotted Cream Alternative

Use full-fat coconut cream instead of dairy cream and cook similarly in the air fryer. The result is a rich, silky vegan spread that’s great for those avoiding dairy but wanting that clotted cream feel.

Note: Texture will be slightly different and less thick; refrigerate well and consider adding a pinch of salt to balance sweetness.

Pro Tips

Use cream that is not ultra-pasteurized for best results.
Ultra-pasteurized cream undergoes higher heat treatment which can denature proteins needed for clotted cream formation, resulting in a thinner, less stable texture. Using fresh, minimally processed cream ensures the fats and proteins behave properly during slow cooking.
Don’t open the air fryer during cooking to keep the temperature steady.
Frequent opening lets heat escape and disrupts the slow coagulation process, causing uneven thickening or breaking the delicate crust. Maintaining a stable environment allows the cream to clot uniformly and develop that signature golden top.
Let the clotted cream cool completely and refrigerate overnight.
Cooling allows the cream to set fully, solidifying the texture and enhancing flavor. Serving it too warm results in runny cream that won’t spread properly, losing the luxurious mouthfeel that makes clotted cream so special.
Use a shallow dish to encourage even heat penetration.
A shallow layer of cream heats uniformly, preventing hot spots that can cause curdling or uneven clotting. This ensures the entire surface develops the characteristic thick crust and silky texture.
Avoid stirring or shaking the cream during cooking.
Disturbing the cream interrupts the fat rising and coagulation process, breaking the crust and mixing liquid back in, which ruins the texture. Patience is key for that perfect clotted cream layer.
Bring the cream to room temperature before serving.
Cold cream is stiff and doesn’t spread well. Letting it soften slightly makes it easy to spread while keeping the rich, thick texture intact, enhancing the eating experience.
Save the leftover whey for baking or smoothies.
The liquid left behind is full of nutrients and flavor and is a great way to reduce waste. It can add moisture and subtle tang to recipes, making your cooking more sustainable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using ultra-pasteurized cream

Why it happens Many people grab whatever heavy cream is on hand without checking the pasteurization type, assuming all cream behaves the same.

Fix Always check the label and select heavy cream that is not ultra-pasteurized. This ensures proper protein structure to form the thick clotted cream layer. If only ultra-pasteurized is available, the cream may not thicken well.

Cooking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Impatience or misunderstanding of the slow cooking process leads to cranking the heat, hoping to speed things up.

Fix Stick to a low temperature around 180°F (82°C). Higher heat causes the cream to curdle or separate, ruining texture. Slow and steady heat is essential for the silky crust to form.

Opening the air fryer too often

Why it happens Curiosity or concern about cooking progress causes frequent lid lifts, disrupting temperature.

Fix Resist checking too often. Set a timer and let the cream cook undisturbed. This stable environment is necessary for the fat to rise and clot correctly.

Stirring the cream during cooking

Why it happens Some think stirring will mix the cream evenly and speed up thickening.

Fix Do not stir. The clotted cream forms by the fat rising and coagulating undisturbed. Stirring breaks the layer and results in a runny texture.

Skipping the refrigeration step

Why it happens Eagerness to serve leads to skipping the chilling process.

Fix Refrigerate the cream for at least 8 hours after cooking. This is critical to set the texture and deepen flavor. Without chilling, the cream will be too loose and won’t spread properly.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Clotted cream should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours to avoid spoilage. Keep it covered loosely if serving out for a short period.

Refrigerator

Store clotted cream in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The texture may firm up but will soften when brought to room temperature before serving. Avoid freezing as it can alter texture.

Freezer

Freezing clotted cream is not recommended as it changes the texture and can cause separation upon thawing. If you must freeze, use airtight containers and consume within 1 month. Thaw gently in the fridge.

Reheating

Clotted cream is best served cold or at room temperature. Avoid reheating as heat breaks down the texture. Simply let sit at room temperature for 15-20 minutes before serving to soften.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the clotted cream up to 2 days in advance, cooking and refrigerating it ahead of time. This makes it perfect for planning weekend brunches or teatime. Just remember to bring it out early for best spreadability.

FAQ

Can I use ultra-pasteurized cream for this recipe?
Ultra-pasteurized cream usually doesn’t clot properly because the high heat treatment breaks down proteins needed for thickening. It may result in a runny or grainy texture. For best results, use heavy cream labeled as pasteurized but not ultra-pasteurized.
What if my air fryer doesn’t go as low as 180°F?
If your air fryer’s lowest temperature is higher, you can try using the oven at 180°F instead. Slow, steady heat is key, so avoid higher temperatures that can curdle the cream. Alternatively, use a slow cooker on low if available.
How long can I keep homemade clotted cream in the fridge?
Store clotted cream in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The flavor and texture are best fresh, but it keeps well refrigerated. Always check for any off smells or separation before use.
Why is my clotted cream watery after cooking?
This usually happens if the cream was disturbed during cooking, cooked at too high a temperature, or ultra-pasteurized cream was used. Also, skipping the refrigeration step prevents it from setting properly. Follow the slow, undisturbed cooking and chilling instructions.
Can I use whipping cream instead of heavy cream?
Whipping cream generally has a lower fat content (around 30-35%) and may not clot as thickly as heavy cream with 36% or more fat. You might get a thinner texture, so heavy cream is preferred for authentic clotted cream.
Can I add flavors like vanilla or honey?
Yes! Adding vanilla bean, honey, or spices before cooking can infuse lovely flavors. Just add them to the cream before placing it in the air fryer. Avoid adding extracts after cooking as they may alter texture.
Why is there liquid under the clotted cream?
The liquid is whey, a natural byproduct of the clotted cream process. It’s normal and can be drained off or saved for other recipes like smoothies or baking. Don’t mix it back into the clotted cream.
Can I use this clotted cream for recipes other than scones?
Absolutely! Clotted cream is great on pancakes, waffles, fresh fruit, or even as a luxurious topping for desserts like pies and crumbles. Its rich texture adds a wonderful indulgence to many dishes.
