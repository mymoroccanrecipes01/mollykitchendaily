Bite-Sized Caprese Skewers with Sweet Balsamic Glaze for Parties

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 0 min cook 6 servings

These fresh Caprese skewers are a fun, easy twist on the classic salad. The sweet balsamic glaze really brings all the flavors together. Perfect for quick snacks or serving at your next party!

Why This Works

What makes these Caprese skewers stand out is the balance of freshness and concentrated flavors. The balsamic glaze is the star here—it’s made by slowly reducing balsamic vinegar with honey, which thickens the liquid and intensifies its sweet and tangy notes. This glaze coats the skewers with a shiny, flavorful layer that ties the fresh ingredients together perfectly.

The choice of fresh mozzarella balls, or bocconcini, is crucial. Their creamy texture contrasts beautifully with the juicy cherry tomatoes and aromatic basil leaves, creating that classic Caprese trio in a convenient bite-sized form. Drying the mozzarella before assembling prevents excess moisture from making the skewers soggy, which keeps the texture light and satisfying.

Threading the ingredients in a specific order—tomato, basil, cheese—ensures each bite is balanced. The basil leaf acts as a flavor bridge between the rich cheese and sweet tomato. Adding salt and pepper just before serving enhances the natural flavors without overpowering them. The optional olive oil drizzle adds a silky mouthfeel and subtle richness, rounding out the dish.

Finally, assembling the skewers ahead but adding the balsamic glaze last-minute preserves freshness and texture. This recipe works because it respects the delicate nature of fresh ingredients while adding a touch of culinary technique with the glaze—making it both approachable and impressive.

  • Look for high-quality balsamic vinegar with a good balance of sweetness and acidity. Traditional aged balsamic is thicker and sweeter, but you can also use regular balsamic vinegar for the glaze. The vinegar’s acidity brightens the dish and its sugars caramelize during reduction, creating the glaze’s signature richness.
  • Choose fresh, soft mozzarella balls that are moist but not overly wet. Bocconcini hold their shape well on skewers and provide a creamy, mild flavor that complements the other ingredients. Drain and pat dry to avoid sogginess in the final dish.
  • Select firm, ripe cherry tomatoes that are vibrant in color and free from blemishes. Their natural sweetness and juiciness are key to balancing the creamy cheese and tangy glaze. Avoid overly soft tomatoes which can fall apart on the skewers.
  • Use fresh, bright green basil leaves with no signs of wilting or browning. Basil adds a fragrant, herbal note essential to Caprese. Handle gently to avoid bruising, which can cause bitterness. Large leaves can be folded to fit skewers neatly.
  • Honey adds a natural sweetness that balances the acidity of the balsamic during reduction. Use a mild-flavored honey to avoid overpowering the glaze. It also helps the glaze thicken smoothly and gives it a glossy finish.

Ingredients

  • 24 cherry tomatoes, washed
  • 24 small fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini), drained
  • 24 fresh basil leaves
  • 1/2 cup balsamic vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 24 small wooden skewers or toothpicks
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 2 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil (optional)

Introduction

I first made these bite-sized Caprese skewers a few summers ago for a backyard barbecue, and they instantly became a crowd favorite. What I love about this version is how the balsamic glaze adds a sweet, tangy punch that elevates the simple ingredients without overpowering them. Unlike traditional Caprese salads that can be a bit messy to serve, these skewers are perfectly portioned for snacking and make entertaining a breeze. A little trick I use is gently drying the mozzarella balls before threading everything on the skewers to prevent sogginess and keep the flavors fresh and vibrant. The finished skewers look colorful and inviting, with juicy tomatoes, creamy cheese, and fragrant basil leaves all tied together by a glossy balsamic drizzle. They’re light, refreshing, and perfect for any party or even a quick bite when you’re craving something fresh and satisfying. Honestly, I’ve tried a few different balsamic glaze recipes, but adding honey creates just the right balance of sweetness and acidity that keeps everyone coming back for more.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preparing the balsamic glaze. Pour the balsamic vinegar into a small saucepan and add the honey. Heat it over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until it reduces by about half and thickens to a syrupy consistency. This slow reduction concentrates the vinegar’s flavors and adds a lovely sweetness from the honey. Be careful not to let it burn, which can happen quickly once it thickens. Remove from heat and let it cool completely before using.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ The glaze should coat the back of a spoon and have a syrupy, glossy texture.
  2. Step 2

    While the glaze cools, rinse and dry the cherry tomatoes. Pat the fresh mozzarella balls dry with paper towels to remove excess moisture—this helps prevent sogginess on the skewers. Lay out the basil leaves and gently wash if needed, then dry them carefully to preserve their vibrant color and flavor.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Ingredients should be dry to the touch, with no visible water droplets.
  3. Step 3

    To assemble each skewer, start by threading a cherry tomato onto a wooden skewer or toothpick. Follow with a fresh basil leaf, folding it gently if it’s large, then add a mozzarella ball. Repeat this sequence if your skewers are longer, or keep one set of each for bite-sized pieces. This layering ensures every bite has a perfect balance of flavors and textures.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Skewers should look colorful and evenly balanced with tomato, basil, and cheese.
  4. Step 4

    Once all the skewers are assembled, arrange them on a serving platter. Lightly season with salt and freshly ground black pepper to enhance the flavors. Optionally, drizzle a small amount of extra virgin olive oil over the skewers for added richness and a silky finish.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Seasoning is evenly distributed and the platter looks fresh and inviting.
  5. Step 5

    Finally, drizzle the cooled balsamic glaze generously over the skewers right before serving. The glaze adds a beautiful shine and a sweet tang that complements the creamy mozzarella and fresh basil. If you drizzle too early, the glaze can make the skewers soggy, so timing is key for the best texture.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Skewers glisten with the glossy balsamic glaze without pooling at the bottom.
  6. Step 6

    Serve these Caprese skewers immediately or keep them refrigerated until ready to serve. They’re best enjoyed fresh to preserve the basil’s bright flavor and the cheese’s creamy texture. If making ahead, wait to add the balsamic glaze until right before serving to avoid sogginess.

    ⏱ No cooking, just refrigeration if needed✓ Skewers remain firm and fresh without wilted basil or watery cheese.
  7. Step 7

    If you want to add a little extra flair, sprinkle some finely chopped fresh herbs like oregano or a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes over the skewers for a subtle kick. This adds another flavor dimension without overpowering the classic Caprese taste.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Herbs are evenly sprinkled and enhance the overall presentation.
  8. Step 8

    For a fun twist, you can swap cherry tomatoes for grape tomatoes or even small heirloom varieties to vary the color and sweetness. Just make sure they’re firm enough to hold on the skewer without falling apart.

    ⏱ Optional prep time varies✓ Tomatoes remain intact on skewers and have vibrant color.
  9. Step 9

    Clean up is easy with these skewers since there’s no messy dressing to pour. Simply toss any leftover basil or tomatoes, and wipe the platter. If you have leftovers, cover tightly and refrigerate, but consume within a day for best taste.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Serving area is clean and leftovers are stored properly.

Variations

  • Add Prosciutto for a Savory Twist: Wrap a small piece of thinly sliced prosciutto around the mozzarella before skewering. This adds a salty, savory element that pairs wonderfully with the sweet balsamic glaze and fresh veggies.
    Since prosciutto is delicate, fold it gently and thread carefully to avoid tearing. Serve immediately to enjoy the best texture.
  • Use Pesto Instead of Basil Leaves: Swap fresh basil leaves for a small dollop of pesto on each mozzarella ball. This adds a richer, herbaceous flavor and a creamy texture that complements the balsamic glaze beautifully.
    Apply pesto sparingly to avoid overpowering the tomatoes and cheese. Best served chilled.
  • Make Vegan Caprese Skewers: Replace mozzarella balls with firm tofu cubes or vegan cheese alternatives. Keep the tomatoes, basil, and balsamic glaze the same for a plant-based version that’s just as fresh and tasty.
    Use extra firm tofu and marinate briefly in olive oil and herbs for better flavor and texture.
  • Add Fresh Strawberries for a Sweet Surprise: Alternate strawberries with cherry tomatoes on the skewers to add a juicy, sweet dimension. The strawberries pair nicely with balsamic glaze and basil for a unique flavor profile.
    Use firm, ripe strawberries and slice large ones to fit the skewers evenly.
  • Drizzle with Spicy Honey Balsamic Glaze: Add a pinch of chili flakes to the balsamic glaze reduction for a subtle heat that contrasts beautifully with the sweet and fresh ingredients. Perfect for those who like a little kick.
    Add chili flakes towards the end of the reduction and strain before cooling to avoid gritty texture.

Pro Tips

  • Pat mozzarella balls dry before assembling skewers. — Removing excess moisture from the mozzarella prevents the skewers from becoming soggy and watery. This keeps the texture light and ensures the balsamic glaze adheres nicely without sliding off.
  • Reduce balsamic vinegar slowly over medium heat. — Slow reduction allows the sugars in the vinegar and honey to caramelize gently, creating a rich, thick glaze without burning. Rushing this step can cause bitterness and uneven consistency.
  • Add balsamic glaze just before serving. — Applying the glaze too early can cause the fresh basil and cheese to wilt or become soggy. Adding it last preserves the vibrant appearance and crisp textures.
  • Use small wooden skewers or toothpicks for easy serving. — These make the skewers easy to handle and bite-sized, perfect for parties and snacking. Avoid metal skewers, which can be sharp and less convenient.
  • Season with salt and pepper right before serving. — Salt draws out moisture if added too early, which can water down flavors. Adding it last ensures the seasoning enhances the natural ingredients without affecting texture.
  • Fold large basil leaves to fit on skewers neatly. — Folding prevents tearing and keeps the presentation tidy. It also helps distribute the basil flavor evenly in each bite.
  • Serve skewers chilled or at room temperature. — Serving slightly chilled keeps the mozzarella firm and the tomatoes fresh, while room temperature enhances the flavors. Avoid serving cold right from the fridge as it can mute the taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding balsamic glaze too early.
    Why: People often prepare everything in advance and dress the skewers too soon, thinking it will save time.
    Fix: Wait to drizzle the balsamic glaze just before serving. This keeps the basil leaves vibrant and prevents the cheese and tomatoes from getting soggy or losing texture.
  • Using watery mozzarella without drying.
    Why: Some mozzarella varieties come packed in water, and it’s tempting to skip drying to save time.
    Fix: Always drain and pat mozzarella balls dry with paper towels before assembling. This step is essential to prevent excess moisture which dilutes flavors and can make the skewers soggy.
  • Choosing underripe or overly soft tomatoes.
    Why: Sometimes tomatoes look good visually but are either not ripe or too mushy.
    Fix: Pick firm, ripe cherry tomatoes with vibrant color and avoid those that feel too soft or have blemishes. Firm tomatoes hold up better on skewers and provide the right balance of sweetness and texture.
  • Overcrowding the skewer with too many ingredients.
    Why: Trying to fit too many pieces on one skewer can make it difficult to eat and unbalanced in flavor.
    Fix: Keep each skewer simple—usually one tomato, one basil leaf, and one mozzarella ball. This ensures each bite is balanced and easy to handle.
  • Not seasoning the skewers.
    Why: Some assume the glaze provides enough flavor.
    Fix: Lightly season with salt and freshly ground pepper right before serving to enhance the natural flavors of the ingredients without overpowering them.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: These skewers can be safely kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered to protect from insects and dust. Beyond that, refrigeration is recommended to maintain freshness and safety.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store assembled skewers without balsamic glaze in an airtight container for up to 24 hours. Add the glaze just before serving to keep them fresh and prevent sogginess. The basil may wilt slightly if stored too long.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freezing is not recommended for these fresh skewers as the texture of mozzarella and tomatoes will degrade significantly. It’s best to prepare fresh for optimal flavor and texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: These skewers are best served cold or at room temperature and are not suitable for reheating. If you want a warm appetizer, consider a different recipe.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the skewers up to a day ahead without the balsamic glaze. Keep them covered and refrigerated. Prepare the balsamic glaze in advance and store it in a sealed container at room temperature. Drizzle glaze just before serving for the best presentation and texture.

FAQ

Can I use other types of tomatoes for these skewers?
Yes, you can use grape tomatoes or small heirloom tomatoes as alternatives. Just make sure they are firm and ripe to hold up well on the skewers and provide a good balance of sweetness and acidity.
How do I make the balsamic glaze thicker if it’s too runny?
Simply return the glaze to the saucepan and simmer it gently for a few more minutes until it reduces further. Keep an eye on it to prevent burning. The glaze should coat the back of a spoon and be syrupy.
Can I assemble the skewers a day in advance?
You can assemble the skewers ahead but wait to add the balsamic glaze until right before serving. This prevents the basil from wilting and the cheese from becoming soggy, keeping the skewers fresh and vibrant.
What’s the best way to store leftover skewers?
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator without the balsamic glaze. Consume within 24 hours for best taste and texture. Add glaze fresh when ready to serve again.
Can I substitute the fresh mozzarella with shredded mozzarella?
Shredded mozzarella won’t work well for skewers because it lacks shape and creaminess. Fresh mozzarella balls provide the right texture and visual appeal for these bite-sized snacks.
Is it okay to use bottled balsamic glaze instead of making my own?
Bottled glaze can be a convenient substitute but often contains added sugars and thickeners. Making your own allows you to control sweetness and flavor, resulting in a fresher, more balanced taste.
How can I keep basil leaves from browning after assembly?
Keep the skewers refrigerated and add the balsamic glaze just before serving. Also, avoid washing basil leaves too far in advance and dry them gently to preserve freshness.
Can I make these skewers vegan?
Absolutely! Replace mozzarella with firm tofu cubes or a vegan cheese alternative. Marinate tofu briefly for extra flavor. The other ingredients and balsamic glaze remain the same for a delicious plant-based version.
