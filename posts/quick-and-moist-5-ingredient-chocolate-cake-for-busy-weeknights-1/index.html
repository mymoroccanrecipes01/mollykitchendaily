Quick and Moist 5-Ingredient Chocolate Cake for Cozy Nights

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
10 min prep 25 min cook 8 servings

This chocolate cake is a lifesaver when you want something sweet but don’t have much time or ingredients. Just five simple things make it super moist and rich. Perfect for a cozy weeknight treat!

Why This Works

This chocolate cake recipe succeeds because of the balance and simplicity of its ingredients and technique. The flour provides the structure, but sifting it with the cocoa powder and baking powder helps incorporate air and prevents lumps, which leads to a lighter crumb. Using baking powder instead of baking soda ensures a more controlled rise and a tender texture without any metallic aftertaste.

Milk plays a crucial role here, not only hydrating the dry ingredients but also adding fat and protein that contribute to moisture and richness. Using room temperature milk helps the batter mix evenly without shocking the leavening agents, resulting in a consistent rise. The sugar not only sweetens but also retains moisture during baking, which keeps the cake soft.

The technique of mixing just until combined is essential. Overmixing develops gluten, which toughens the crumb and makes the cake dense. By mixing gently, you preserve tenderness. The baking temperature and timing are carefully calibrated to allow the cake to rise fully and set without drying out, while the resting time post-baking lets the crumb stabilize, making it easier to slice and serve.

Finally, the minimal ingredient list and straightforward steps reduce the chance of errors and make this recipe accessible to bakers of all levels. It’s a perfect example of how simplicity can yield delicious, comforting results.

  • When choosing flour, opt for regular all-purpose flour rather than cake flour for a balance of structure and tenderness. Sifting is important to aerate and remove lumps, which affects the rise and texture. Avoid using self-rising flour here since the recipe already includes baking powder.
  • Plain white sugar is best for consistent sweetness and moisture retention. It dissolves well in the batter, helping create a tender crumb. Brown sugar could be substituted for a deeper flavor but might alter moisture levels slightly.
  • Choose a good-quality natural cocoa powder for a rich chocolate flavor without bitterness. Dutch-processed cocoa can be used but may require adjustments to baking powder because of its pH. Fresh cocoa powder ensures vibrant taste and color.
  • This is the leavening agent that makes the cake rise. Check that your baking powder is fresh for best results—old baking powder can lose potency, resulting in a dense cake. It reacts with moisture and heat to produce carbon dioxide bubbles that aerate the batter.
  • Whole milk adds fat and moisture that contribute to the cake’s richness and tender crumb. Using cold milk can slow down the leavening reaction, so room temperature milk is preferred. You can substitute with buttermilk or plant-based milk, but expect slight texture and flavor changes.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 cup whole milk, room temperature

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and greasing an 8-inch round cake pan with butter or nonstick spray. This ensures the cake won’t stick and will come out cleanly. Preheating is crucial because it allows the cake to bake evenly from the start, helping it rise properly and develop that perfect texture. Don’t skip greasing, or you might end up with a crumbly mess when trying to remove the cake.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Oven fully preheated, pan evenly coated without excess grease
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, sift together the flour, sugar, cocoa powder, and baking powder. Sifting breaks up lumps and aerates the dry ingredients, which helps the cake rise better and ensures there are no pockets of dry flour or cocoa. Mixing the dry ingredients first also distributes the baking powder evenly, so the cake gets a uniform lift.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dry ingredients appear light, fluffy, and lump-free
  3. Step 3

    Pour the room temperature milk into the dry ingredients and gently stir with a spatula or wooden spoon until just combined. It’s important not to overmix here; a few small lumps are okay. Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make the cake tough instead of tender. The milk hydrates the dry ingredients and activates the baking powder to start the rising process.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Batter has no large dry patches but is still slightly lumpy
  4. Step 4

    Transfer the batter to your prepared pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the cake bake evenly and gives a nice flat surface for frosting or dusting later. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any air bubbles trapped inside the batter, which can cause holes or uneven texture in the finished cake.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Batter evenly spread and surface smooth with no large air pockets
  5. Step 5

    Place the cake in the center of the preheated oven and bake for about 25 minutes. Avoid opening the oven door during the first 20 minutes to prevent the cake from collapsing. The baking powder will cause the cake to rise, and the heat will set the structure. Baking too long can dry out the cake, so start checking at 20 minutes with a toothpick inserted in the center—it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter.

    ⏱ 25 minutes✓ Toothpick inserted in center comes out mostly clean with a few crumbs
  6. Step 6

    Once baked, remove the cake from the oven and let it cool in the pan for about 10 minutes. This resting time allows the cake to firm up slightly, making it easier to remove from the pan without breaking. If you try to unmold it while it’s too hot, it might crumble or stick. After 10 minutes, run a knife around the edges and invert onto a wire rack to cool completely.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Cake pulls away from pan edges and feels slightly firm to touch
  7. Step 7

    If desired, dust the cooled cake with powdered sugar or cocoa powder, or spread a simple glaze or frosting. Because the cake is moist and flavorful on its own, you don’t need to overdo it, but a light topping adds a nice touch for serving. Use a sieve to dust evenly and avoid clumps.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Even, light coating of topping without clumps
  8. Step 8

    Slice the cake with a serrated knife for clean edges and serve at room temperature. This cake tastes best fresh but can be stored for later. The texture is tender and moist, with a rich cocoa flavor that’s not too bitter or too sweet. Pair it with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a cup of coffee for the ultimate cozy treat.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Clean slices with no crumbling or dense patches
  9. Step 9

    Clean up your workspace and enjoy the leftovers! If storing, wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container to keep moisture in. This prevents the cake from drying out and keeps the flavor fresh for a couple of days.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Cake tightly wrapped or sealed, no exposed edges

Variations

  • Add Espresso Powder for a Mocha Twist: Sprinkle a teaspoon of instant espresso powder into the dry ingredients to deepen the chocolate flavor with a subtle coffee note. This enhances richness without overpowering the cake.
    Espresso powder dissolves quickly and blends evenly, so no change to baking times or temperatures needed.
  • Swap Milk for Buttermilk for Extra Tenderness: Replace the whole milk with buttermilk to add acidity, which tenderizes the crumb and adds a slight tang. This version is even softer and more moist.
    Because buttermilk is acidic, you might reduce baking powder slightly or add a pinch of baking soda to balance leavening.
  • Mix in Chocolate Chips for Texture: Fold in 1/2 cup of semi-sweet chocolate chips into the batter for bursts of melty chocolate. It adds a fun texture contrast and extra indulgence.
    Add chips last and fold gently to avoid overmixing. No change to bake time required.
  • Top with Fresh Berries and Whipped Cream: Serve the cake with fresh strawberries, raspberries, or blueberries and a dollop of whipped cream for a fresh, lighter dessert option.
    This is a serving suggestion; no recipe adjustments needed.
  • Make It Vegan by Using Plant-Based Milk and Oil: Swap the milk for almond or oat milk and replace butter with vegetable oil or coconut oil. This keeps the cake moist and rich without dairy.
    Use a neutral-flavored oil and ensure the plant milk is unsweetened to maintain flavor balance.

Pro Tips

  • Always sift your dry ingredients together to avoid lumps and ensure even mixing. — Sifting aerates the flour and cocoa powder, which helps produce a lighter cake texture. It also breaks up clumps that can cause uneven baking or dense spots. Skipping this step often leads to a cake with inconsistent crumb and pockets of dry ingredients.
  • Use room temperature milk to help the batter mix smoothly and activate the baking powder properly. — Cold milk can cause the baking powder to react unevenly or slow down, resulting in poor rise and denser texture. Room temperature milk blends better with the dry ingredients and supports the chemical reactions needed for a fluffy cake.
  • Don’t overmix the batter; stir until just combined with no big dry patches. — Overmixing develops gluten strands in the flour, which toughens the cake and gives it a chewy texture rather than a tender crumb. Gentle mixing keeps the cake soft and moist, preserving the delicate structure.
  • Tap the cake pan gently on the countertop before baking to release air bubbles. — Air bubbles trapped in the batter can cause holes or uneven texture in the baked cake. Tapping the pan helps these bubbles rise to the surface and escape, resulting in a smoother, more even crumb.
  • Avoid opening the oven door during the first 20 minutes of baking. — Opening the door lets heat escape, which can cause the cake to collapse before it sets. The initial heat is critical for the baking powder to create lift and the batter to firm up properly.
  • Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes before removing it. — This resting period allows the cake to firm up slightly, preventing it from breaking apart when unmolded. Trying to remove it while hot often leads to crumbling or sticking.
  • Use a serrated knife to slice the cake for cleaner cuts. — A serrated knife cuts through the tender crumb without compressing or tearing it, preserving the cake’s texture and appearance for serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using cold milk straight from the fridge.
    Why: Many people grab whatever’s on hand without thinking about temperature, assuming it won’t affect the recipe.
    Fix: Let the milk come to room temperature before mixing. This ensures better mixing and proper activation of baking powder, resulting in a lighter, fluffier cake.
  • Overmixing the batter until smooth.
    Why: It’s natural to want a perfectly smooth batter, but overmixing damages the cake’s texture.
    Fix: Mix just until the dry ingredients are moistened. A few lumps are fine. This keeps the gluten development minimal and the cake tender.
  • Not preheating the oven fully before baking.
    Why: Sometimes bakers put the cake in too early to save time or forget to wait for the oven to reach the right temperature.
    Fix: Always preheat your oven completely to 350°F (175°C). This ensures even baking and proper rise.
  • Skipping greasing the pan.
    Why: Assuming nonstick pans don’t need greasing or forgetting this step.
    Fix: Grease and lightly flour the pan or use parchment paper to prevent sticking and preserve the cake’s surface.
  • Opening the oven door repeatedly during baking.
    Why: Curiosity or impatience leads to checking too often.
    Fix: Resist the urge to open the door until at least 20 minutes have passed to avoid sudden temperature drops that can make the cake collapse.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: This cake can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 24 hours. Use a cake dome or airtight container to keep it moist and protect it from drying out or absorbing odors.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store the cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The texture may firm up slightly when cold, so bring to room temperature before serving or warm gently to restore softness.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to freeze for up to 2 months. To thaw, place it in the fridge overnight or at room temperature for a few hours. Avoid microwaving frozen cake directly to prevent sogginess.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm slices gently in a microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a warm oven at 300°F (150°C) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small dab of butter or covering with foil helps keep the cake moist while reheating.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can bake the cake a day ahead and store it wrapped at room temperature or refrigerated. Add any frostings, glazes, or toppings just before serving to keep them fresh and vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use cake flour instead of all-purpose flour?
You can, but cake flour has less protein, which leads to a softer, more delicate crumb. You might notice the cake is lighter but less structured. If you swap, use about 1 cup plus 2 tablespoons of cake flour for every cup of all-purpose flour.
What if I don’t have baking powder?
Baking powder is essential here for the rise. If you only have baking soda, you’d need to add an acid like vinegar or lemon juice to activate it, but it’s tricky to get the balance right. I recommend sticking to baking powder for this recipe.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes, but you’ll want to use a gluten-free flour blend designed for baking that includes xanthan gum or similar binders. The texture might be slightly different, but it should still come out moist and tasty.
Why is my cake dense and not rising?
Common reasons include expired baking powder, overmixing the batter, or oven temperature too low. Check your baking powder’s freshness, mix gently, and ensure your oven is fully preheated to 350°F before baking.
Can I double the recipe for a larger cake?
Absolutely! Just use a larger pan and adjust the baking time accordingly—usually adding 10-15 minutes. Keep an eye on it and test with a toothpick for doneness.
Is it okay to use low-fat or skim milk?
You can, but the cake might be slightly less rich and moist because of the lower fat content. Whole milk provides the best tenderness and flavor balance.
How do I know when the cake is done without a toothpick?
Look for a firm top that springs back when gently pressed, and edges that start to pull away from the pan. The cake should also have a slight crackly surface.
Can I add frosting or glaze to this cake?
Definitely! This cake pairs well with chocolate ganache, buttercream, or a simple powdered sugar glaze. Just let the cake cool completely before applying any frosting to avoid melting.
