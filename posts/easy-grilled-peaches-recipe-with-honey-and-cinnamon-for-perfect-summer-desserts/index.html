Sweet and Juicy Grilled Peaches with Honey and Cinnamon

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 16, 2026
10 min Prep Time
10 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These grilled peaches are the perfect summer treat—simple, sweet, and full of caramelized honey and cinnamon goodness. They come together in minutes and are amazing served warm with ice cream for a quick dessert fix.

Introduction

I first tried grilled peaches a few summers ago at a friend's backyard BBQ, and I was instantly hooked. There's something magical about how the grill caramelizes the natural sugars in the fruit, making each bite juicy and sweet with a slight smoky depth. Since then, I've made this recipe countless times, tweaking it just a bit to add honey and cinnamon which really take it to the next level. What I love most is how quick and fuss-free it is—perfect for those warm evenings when you want something special but don't want to spend hours in the kitchen. The trick I learned early on is to brush the peaches with a little oil or butter before grilling to prevent sticking and help that beautiful caramelization. When done right, the peaches are tender but still hold their shape, with honey and cinnamon bubbling on top. Serve them warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream and you have a dessert that feels fancy but takes minutes to whip up. It's a total crowd-pleaser and honestly, one of my favorite ways to enjoy fresh peaches in summer.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 ripe peaches, halved and pitted
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil or melted butter
  • Vanilla ice cream, for serving (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your grill to medium-high heat. This ensures the peaches will caramelize nicely without burning. If using a charcoal grill, wait until the coals are glowing and covered with white ash. For gas grills, let it heat up for about 10 minutes. A properly heated grill creates that perfect sear and helps lock in the peach’s natural juices, giving you those lovely grill marks. Avoid placing peaches on a cold grill as they will cook unevenly and won’t develop the right texture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Grill is hot enough when you can hold your hand about 5 inches above the grate for only 2-3 seconds before it feels too hot
Step 2
Brush each peach half with olive oil or melted butter. This step is crucial because it prevents sticking and promotes even caramelization. Don’t skip this or you might end up with fruit stuck to the grill, which is frustrating and messy. The fat also helps the honey and cinnamon glaze stick better. Use a pastry brush or your fingers to coat the cut sides evenly, and don’t forget to lightly oil the grill grate if you want extra insurance against sticking.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peach surfaces look glossy and evenly coated without pooling oil
Step 3
Place the peaches cut side down on the grill. Let them cook undisturbed for about 4-5 minutes. This stillness allows the fruit to develop beautiful grill marks and caramelize naturally. Resist the urge to move them around too much or poke with a fork, as this can cause the peaches to fall apart. The heat will soften the flesh and intensify the sweet flavors. You’ll notice the edges start to darken slightly and the peaches will release a sweet aroma.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden grill marks appear and peaches soften slightly but hold shape
Step 4
Flip the peaches over carefully using tongs. Now, drizzle each half with honey and sprinkle with ground cinnamon. The warmth of the peaches will help the honey melt and mix with the cinnamon, creating a sticky, fragrant glaze. This step is where the magic happens—honey caramelizes quickly, so keep an eye on them to prevent burning. The cinnamon adds a cozy spice that pairs wonderfully with the sweetness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Honey glistens and starts bubbling gently on the peach surface
Step 5
Let the peaches cook on the honey and cinnamon side for another 3-4 minutes. This short time is enough for the glaze to thicken and caramelize without turning bitter. Keep the grill covered if possible to keep the heat consistent. If you notice any flare-ups from the honey dripping, briefly move the peaches away from direct flames to avoid burnt spots.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Honey glaze is thickened and slightly golden with cinnamon aroma released
Step 6
Remove the peaches from the grill and let them rest for a minute or two. Resting allows the juices to redistribute and the glaze to set slightly, making them easier to handle and more flavorful. Serve warm, ideally with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream for a refreshing contrast. The peaches should be tender but not mushy, with a rich, caramelized flavor and a hint of spice.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches feel tender to touch but still firm enough to hold their shape
Step 7
For an extra touch, sprinkle chopped fresh mint or toasted nuts over the peaches before serving. This adds a fresh or crunchy contrast to the soft, sweet fruit. It’s optional but highly recommended if you want to impress guests or elevate the dish for a special occasion. The mint brightens the flavor, while nuts add textural interest.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Garnishes are evenly distributed and fresh-looking
Step 8
If you want to make this dessert vegan, swap the honey for maple syrup or agave nectar. These alternatives also caramelize nicely but bring a different flavor profile. Adjust the amount to taste since they can be sweeter or less viscous than honey. This swap keeps the recipe inclusive and just as delicious.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes (ingredient swap)
✓ Doneness: Syrup coats peaches evenly and caramelizes similarly to honey
Step 9
Clean your grill grates after cooking peaches to prevent sticky residue buildup. Use a grill brush while the grates are still warm but not hot enough to burn you. This maintenance step keeps your grill in good shape and ready for the next cookout. Sticky residue can attract pests and cause flare-ups later.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Grates appear clean and free of caramelized honey or fruit bits

Variations

Spiced Grilled Peaches with Nutmeg and Cloves

Add a pinch of ground nutmeg and cloves along with cinnamon for a warm, spiced flavor that’s perfect for cooler summer evenings. This variation deepens the aroma and adds complexity. Pair with vanilla or caramel ice cream for extra indulgence.

Note: Use sparingly since these spices are potent; add with the cinnamon after flipping the peaches to avoid burning.

Grilled Peaches with Balsamic Glaze

Drizzle a balsamic reduction over grilled peaches instead of honey for a tangy-sweet twist. The acidity of balsamic vinegar balances the peach’s sweetness beautifully, creating a sophisticated dessert that’s still simple to make.

Note: Apply balsamic glaze after grilling to prevent burning and preserve its rich flavor.

Grilled Peaches Stuffed with Mascarpone and Honey

Before grilling, fill the peach cavities with a dollop of mascarpone cheese mixed with honey. The cheese melts slightly on the grill, creating a creamy, luscious filling that pairs wonderfully with caramelized fruit.

Note: Place stuffed peaches cut side up on the grill to prevent filling from spilling out.

Vegan Grilled Peaches with Maple Syrup

Replace honey with pure maple syrup and use coconut oil instead of butter for a vegan-friendly version. Maple syrup caramelizes nicely and adds a subtle earthy sweetness, while coconut oil gives a tropical hint.

Note: Monitor closely as maple syrup can burn faster than honey; add it after flipping peaches.

Grilled Peaches with Toasted Almonds and Mint

After grilling, sprinkle chopped toasted almonds and fresh mint over the peaches for added crunch and freshness. This variation adds texture contrast and bright herbal notes that balance the sweetness.

Note: Toast almonds in a dry pan until golden before sprinkling; add mint just before serving to keep it fresh.

Pro Tips

Always oil your grill grates before heating to prevent sticking.
Oiling the grates creates a non-stick surface that protects delicate fruit like peaches from tearing when flipped. Without this, caramelized sugars can bond to the metal, making cleanup harder and ruining presentation.
Use ripe but firm peaches for grilling.
Firm peaches hold their shape on the grill and develop a nice crust without falling apart. Overripe peaches turn mushy and can stick or burn easily, resulting in a less appealing texture.
Add honey and cinnamon only after flipping peaches on the grill.
Honey caramelizes quickly and can burn if exposed to direct heat too long. Adding it after flipping ensures it melts and thickens into a glaze without turning bitter.
Let peaches rest briefly after grilling before serving.
Resting allows juices to redistribute evenly, preventing them from running out immediately and keeping each bite juicy and flavorful.
Don’t overcrowd the grill; give peaches space.
Crowding traps steam and prevents proper caramelization and grill marks. Leaving space ensures dry heat circulates and enhances flavor development.
Use tongs gently to flip peaches to avoid breaking the fruit.
Peaches soften quickly on the grill, and aggressive flipping can tear the flesh. Being gentle preserves presentation and texture.
Serve grilled peaches warm with cold ice cream for contrast.
The hot-cold contrast enhances the sensory experience, balancing sweetness with creaminess and temperature, making the dessert more enjoyable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Grilling peaches on a cold or low heat grill

Why it happens Many assume a low flame is gentler and safer for fruit, but it actually prevents proper caramelization.

Fix Always preheat your grill to medium-high before adding peaches. High heat triggers the Maillard reaction, creating those beautiful grill marks and deep flavors. Cooking on low heat results in soggy, unappealing fruit.

Skipping the oil or butter coating on peaches

Why it happens Some think fruit doesn’t need fat when grilling, but this causes sticking and tearing.

Fix Brush on a thin layer of oil or melted butter before grilling. This creates a barrier that prevents sticking and helps develop a golden crust, improving texture and flavor.

Adding honey too early during grilling

Why it happens In a rush, cooks drizzle honey at the start, unaware it burns quickly.

Fix Wait until after flipping peaches before adding honey and cinnamon. This prevents burning and ensures a sticky, caramelized glaze instead of bitter char.

Flipping peaches too often

Why it happens Impatience leads to moving fruit around, breaking up the flesh.

Fix Place peaches on the grill and leave them undisturbed for 4-5 minutes per side. This allows proper caramelization and prevents the fruit from falling apart.

Using underripe or overripe peaches

Why it happens Choosing fruit based on appearance alone can lead to poor texture results.

Fix Pick peaches that are ripe but still firm to the touch. This balance ensures they soften on the grill without turning mushy or falling apart.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Grilled peaches are best served fresh but can sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours covered loosely. Beyond that, they may lose juiciness and texture due to exposure to air.

Refrigerator

Store leftover grilled peaches in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. They will firm up and lose some juiciness but can be reheated gently or served cold as a topping for yogurt or oatmeal.

Freezer

Peaches don’t freeze well once grilled due to texture changes. If needed, freeze raw peach halves instead and grill fresh when ready to serve. Freezing grilled fruit results in mushy texture upon thawing.

Reheating

Reheat grilled peaches gently in a skillet over low heat for 2-3 minutes or in the microwave for 20-30 seconds. Add a splash of water or honey if they seem dry. Avoid high heat to prevent burning the caramelized glaze.

Make Ahead

You can pit and halve the peaches a day ahead and store covered in the fridge. Add honey and cinnamon just before grilling. Pre-make any garnishes like toasted nuts or mint to save time on serving day.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches for grilling?
Frozen peaches are not ideal for grilling because they release excess water as they thaw, making them mushy and less likely to caramelize properly. If using frozen, thaw and pat dry thoroughly, but fresh ripe peaches will always yield better texture and flavor.
What if I don’t have a grill? Can I cook peaches another way?
Absolutely! You can use a grill pan on the stovetop or even broil peaches in the oven. The key is high direct heat to caramelize the sugars. Just watch closely to prevent burning and turn once to get grill marks or browning.
Why do my peaches stick to the grill?
Peaches stick when the grill or fruit isn’t properly oiled or when the grill isn’t hot enough. Make sure to preheat the grill well and brush both the grates and peach halves with oil or melted butter to create a non-stick surface.
Can I prepare the peaches ahead of time?
Yes! You can pit and halve peaches a day ahead and store them covered in the fridge. Just brush with oil and add honey and cinnamon right before grilling to keep the fruit fresh and prevent browning.
How do I prevent honey from burning on the grill?
Add honey only after flipping the peaches and grill on medium heat. Honey caramelizes quickly and can burn if exposed to high direct heat for too long. Keep a close eye and remove peaches promptly once glaze bubbles and thickens.
Can I use other spices instead of cinnamon?
Definitely! Nutmeg, cloves, or cardamom are lovely alternatives that add warm, aromatic notes. Use sparingly since these spices are strong. Add them with the honey after flipping to avoid burning.
What’s the best way to serve grilled peaches?
Serve warm with vanilla ice cream or whipped cream for a classic combo. Fresh mint, toasted nuts, or a drizzle of balsamic glaze can add extra flavor and texture. Enjoy immediately for the best taste and texture.
Are grilled peaches healthy?
Yes! Peaches are naturally low in calories and high in fiber and vitamins. Grilling adds no fat unless you add oil or butter, and honey provides natural sweetness. It’s a lighter dessert option compared to many baked goods.
