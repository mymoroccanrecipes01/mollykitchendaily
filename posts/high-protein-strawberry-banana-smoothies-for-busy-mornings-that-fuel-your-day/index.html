Creamy Strawberry Banana Smoothie with a Protein Boost for Busy Mornings

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 7, 2026
10 min Prep Time
2 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This strawberry banana smoothie is my go-to when I need a quick, creamy breakfast that keeps me full. Using Greek yogurt makes it extra smooth and packs in protein. Great for busy mornings or an afternoon pick-me-up!

Introduction

I’ve been making this strawberry banana smoothie for years, especially on mornings when I’m racing out the door. The first time I tried adding Greek yogurt instead of just juice or milk, I was hooked. It makes the smoothie so creamy and adds a satisfying protein boost that keeps me full until lunchtime. Honestly, I used to skip breakfast a lot until I found this combo — it’s quick, delicious, and feels like a treat rather than a chore. One little trick I learned is to use ripe bananas for natural sweetness and a perfectly smooth texture. Plus, adding vanilla extract gives it a subtle warmth that makes it feel extra special. The finished smoothie is thick, luscious, and just the right balance of sweet and tangy. It’s also versatile enough to tweak based on what I have on hand. Whether you’re a smoothie newbie or a regular blender fan, this recipe is foolproof and perfect for busy mornings or a quick snack anytime.

What You'll Need

Approximately 2 cups, 2 servings

  • 1 cup fresh strawberries, hulled
  • 1 medium ripe banana, peeled
  • 1 cup Greek yogurt, plain and full-fat
  • 1/2 cup milk (dairy or plant-based)
  • 1 tablespoon honey or maple syrup (optional)
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract (optional)
  • Ice cubes, as desired for thickness

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by prepping your fruit: wash and hull the strawberries, and peel the banana. Using ripe bananas is key because they naturally sweeten the smoothie and help create a creamy texture without needing extra sugar. If your strawberries are frozen, you can skip the ice cubes later. This prep step sets the foundation for a smooth blend, so don’t rush it.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Fruits are clean, hulled, and ready for blending without any stems or peel.
Step 2
Add the strawberries, banana, Greek yogurt, and milk into your blender. Greek yogurt is the secret to that creamy texture and protein boost. Pouring in milk thins the smoothie to the perfect consistency. If you like it sweeter, add honey or maple syrup here. The vanilla extract adds a lovely depth of flavor but is optional. Blend on high for about 30 seconds until smooth and creamy.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Smoothie is uniformly blended with no visible chunks, creamy and thick but pourable.
Step 3
Check the consistency and thickness. If you want it colder or thicker, add a handful of ice cubes and blend again for 15-20 seconds. Ice not only chills the smoothie but also thickens it, making it more refreshing. Be careful not to over-blend once ice is added to avoid a watery texture.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Smoothie thickened and chilled to your liking, with a frosty appearance.
Step 4
Taste your smoothie and adjust sweetness or thickness as needed. If it’s too thick, add a splash more milk and blend briefly. If it’s not sweet enough, add a little more honey or maple syrup. This step is important because everyone's fruit sweetness varies, so tailor it to your preference.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Balanced flavor with desired sweetness and smooth pourable texture.
Step 5
Pour the smoothie into glasses and serve immediately. Freshness is key here to enjoy the bright flavors and creamy texture. If you let it sit too long, it might separate or lose some of its vibrant taste. Drinking it right away ensures the best experience.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Smoothie served chilled and fresh, with no separation.
Step 6
Optional: garnish with a few sliced strawberries or a sprinkle of chia seeds for added texture and nutrients. This makes the smoothie look more inviting and adds a little extra health boost. It’s also a nice way to make it feel special if you’re serving guests.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Garnish looks fresh and colorful, enhancing the presentation.
Step 7
Clean your blender right after use to avoid smoothie residue drying and sticking. Rinse it immediately with warm water and a drop of dish soap, then blend with water for a few seconds. This keeps your blender in good shape and ready for next time.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Blender is clean and free of any smoothie residue.
Step 8
If you want to prep ahead, measure your fruit portions and freeze them in zip-top bags. This way, you can just grab and blend with yogurt and milk in the morning. It saves time and keeps your smoothie fresh and flavorful.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Fruit portions frozen and ready for quick blending later.
Step 9
Experiment with adding other ingredients like spinach, protein powder, or flax seeds for extra nutrition. Blend these in at the same time as your fruit and yogurt. Just be mindful of how these additions affect flavor and texture so you can adjust liquids accordingly.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Smoothie remains smooth and palatable with added ingredients.

Variations

Green Strawberry Banana Smoothie

Add a handful of fresh spinach or kale to your strawberry banana smoothie for a nutrient-packed green drink. The mild flavor of the greens blends well and boosts vitamins without overpowering the fruit. It’s a great way to sneak in extra veggies.

Note: Blend greens first with liquids for best texture, then add fruit and yogurt. You may need to add a little more liquid to maintain smoothness.

Protein Powder Boosted Smoothie

Mix in a scoop of vanilla or unflavored protein powder to increase the protein content, perfect for post-workout recovery or longer-lasting energy. This variation keeps the creamy texture and adds muscle fuel.

Note: Add protein powder along with yogurt and milk to ensure even blending. Adjust sweetness as protein powders can sometimes add bitterness.

Dairy-Free Strawberry Banana Smoothie

Swap Greek yogurt for coconut or almond yogurt and use your favorite plant milk to make this smoothie vegan and dairy-free. The coconut yogurt adds a tropical twist while keeping it creamy.

Note: Choose unsweetened dairy-free yogurts to control sugar levels. Texture may be slightly thinner depending on yogurt brand.

Peanut Butter Strawberry Banana Smoothie

Add 1-2 tablespoons of peanut butter for a rich, nutty flavor and extra protein and healthy fats. This variation makes the smoothie more filling and indulgent, great for breakfast or snack.

Note: Add peanut butter with the yogurt to help it mix smoothly. May require a bit more milk for blending.

Chia Seed Strawberry Banana Smoothie

Stir in a tablespoon of chia seeds after blending for added fiber, omega-3s, and texture. Let the smoothie sit a few minutes to let seeds swell for a pudding-like consistency.

Note: Chia seeds thicken smoothies over time, so drink within 10-15 minutes or enjoy as a snack with thicker texture.

Pro Tips

Use frozen fruit instead of ice for a thicker smoothie.
Frozen fruit chills the smoothie and thickens it without diluting flavor like ice cubes can. Ice melts quickly and can water down your drink, whereas frozen fruit maintains richness and texture.
Add a splash of milk gradually to control thickness.
Pouring milk in increments lets you get the perfect smoothie texture. Too much milk makes it thin, while too little makes it hard to blend. Adjusting slowly helps you avoid wasting ingredients and ensures a smooth consistency.
Blend the yogurt and milk first before adding fruit.
Starting with liquids and yogurt creates a vortex that pulls fruit down, resulting in smoother blending. Adding fruit all at once can cause chunks and uneven texture.
Use a high-speed blender for the creamiest results.
High-speed blenders break down fibrous fruit and yogurt proteins more thoroughly, producing a silky, lump-free smoothie. Lower-powered blenders might leave bits or require longer blending, which can heat the mixture.
Add vanilla extract for a subtle flavor boost.
Vanilla adds warmth and depth that balances the tanginess of yogurt and brightness of fruit. It’s a simple way to elevate the flavor without overpowering the smoothie.
Don’t over-blend once ice is added.
Over-blending ice can cause the smoothie to become watery as ice melts too much. Blend just enough to break ice into small pieces and chill the drink.
Use ripe bananas for natural sweetness and creaminess.
Ripe bananas have higher sugar content and softer texture, which sweeten and thicken the smoothie naturally. Unripe bananas can taste bitter and make the texture gritty.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using unripe bananas

Why it happens People often grab any banana on hand without considering ripeness, thinking the smoothie will taste fine either way.

Fix Always use ripe bananas with brown spots for sweetness and smooth texture. If you only have unripe bananas, try adding a bit more honey or maple syrup to compensate.

Adding too much ice

Why it happens Trying to make the smoothie colder or thicker, cooks might overload on ice without realizing it dilutes flavor.

Fix Use frozen fruit instead of ice for thickness. If using ice, add it gradually and blend briefly to avoid watery smoothies.

Skipping the yogurt

Why it happens Some assume smoothies are just fruit and liquid, neglecting the protein and creaminess yogurt adds.

Fix Don’t skip Greek yogurt; it’s essential for texture and satiety. Use plain yogurt to keep flavors balanced.

Over-blending

Why it happens Trying to get smoothness, cooks might blend too long, especially after ice is added.

Fix Blend just until smooth and ice is crushed. Over-blending heats the smoothie and can make it watery.

Not adjusting sweetness

Why it happens Assuming the fruit is always sweet enough, cooks skip tasting and adjusting sugar.

Fix Taste before serving and add honey or syrup if needed. This ensures the smoothie matches your preference.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This smoothie is best enjoyed immediately and should not be left at room temperature for more than 1 hour to avoid spoilage. Cover with a lid or plastic wrap if you need to wait briefly.

Refrigerator

Store leftover smoothie in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 24 hours. The smoothie may thicken or separate; stir or re-blend before drinking. Cold storage preserves freshness but flavor may mellow.

Freezer

You can freeze smoothie portions in ice cube trays or freezer-safe containers for up to 1 month. Thaw in the fridge overnight or blend frozen cubes with a splash of milk for a quick treat. Freezing can slightly change texture.

Reheating

Smoothies are not meant to be reheated. Instead, stir or re-blend chilled smoothie with a little milk if too thick after refrigeration.

Make Ahead

Prep and freeze fruit portions 1-2 days ahead for quick blending in the morning. Keep yogurt and milk refrigerated until ready to blend. This saves time while keeping ingredients fresh and flavorful.

FAQ

Can I use frozen strawberries instead of fresh?
Absolutely! Frozen strawberries work great and help chill the smoothie without needing ice. Just reduce or skip ice cubes if using frozen fruit to avoid watery texture. Frozen fruit also helps thicken the smoothie naturally.
What if I don’t have Greek yogurt? Can I use regular yogurt?
You can substitute regular yogurt, but it will be thinner and less creamy since Greek yogurt is strained to remove whey. You might want to reduce milk slightly to maintain thickness. Protein content will also be lower.
How do I make this smoothie vegan?
Swap Greek yogurt for a plant-based yogurt like coconut or almond yogurt and use plant milk instead of dairy. Choose unsweetened versions to control sugar. The texture may be slightly different but still delicious.
Can I add protein powder to this smoothie?
Yes! Adding a scoop of your favorite protein powder is an easy way to boost protein content. Add it with the yogurt and milk to ensure smooth blending. Adjust sweetness if your protein powder is unflavored or bitter.
How thick should my smoothie be?
It depends on personal preference. This recipe aims for a creamy, drinkable consistency. Use ice or frozen fruit to thicken, and add milk gradually to thin. If too thick, add a splash more milk; if too thin, add more frozen fruit or yogurt.
Can I prepare this smoothie the night before?
You can blend it the night before and refrigerate, but expect some separation and a less fresh taste. Stir or re-blend before drinking. For best flavor and texture, blend fresh in the morning.
What’s the best blender to use?
A high-speed blender like a Vitamix or Blendtec works best to create a smooth, creamy texture without chunks. Lower-powered blenders may require longer blending or result in a grainier texture.
Can I add greens like spinach?
Yes, adding a handful of spinach or kale boosts nutrition without affecting flavor much. Blend greens with liquids first to avoid chunks. You might need to add a bit more liquid for smooth blending.
