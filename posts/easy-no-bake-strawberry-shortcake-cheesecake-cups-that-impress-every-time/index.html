No-Bake Strawberry Shortcake Cheesecake Cups with a Crunchy Graham Cracker Crust

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 12, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These no-bake strawberry shortcake cheesecake cups are creamy, sweet, and super easy to make. The graham cracker crust adds a perfect crunch, and fresh berries on top make them bright and refreshing. Great for a quick summer dessert!

Introduction

I first made these strawberry shortcake cheesecake cups on a sweltering summer afternoon when I wanted something sweet but didn’t want to turn on the oven. The idea of layering creamy cheesecake filling over a crunchy graham cracker crust with fresh strawberries just felt like the perfect light treat. What sets this recipe apart for me is how easy it is—no baking required, which means it comes together quickly, and the texture is just right: silky cheesecake with a bit of crunch. The trick I learned early on was to whip the heavy cream until stiff peaks form before folding it gently into the cream cheese mixture. This keeps the filling light and airy, mimicking that classic shortcake fluffiness. Plus, using fresh strawberries on top adds a bright, juicy finish that balances the richness beautifully. Honestly, my first attempt was a little too dense because I didn’t fold the cream carefully, so take your time with that step! The final cups look so inviting with the red berries peeking through the creamy layers, and they taste even better chilled. They’re perfect for a quick dessert when friends drop by or just a sweet summer treat to beat the heat.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups graham cracker crumbs
  • 5 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
  • 2 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 8 ounces cream cheese, softened
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup fresh strawberries, sliced
  • Additional strawberries for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by making the crust. In a medium bowl, combine graham cracker crumbs, melted butter, and granulated sugar. Stir until the mixture resembles wet sand, which ensures the crust will hold together without being soggy. Press this mixture firmly and evenly into the bottoms of your serving cups or jars. Firm pressing is key here so the crust doesn’t crumble when you scoop the cheesecake. Avoid over-pressing as that can make it too compact and hard to eat. Set aside in the fridge to chill while you prepare the filling.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: The crust should be evenly packed and hold its shape when gently pressed.
Step 2
Next, whip the heavy cream in a chilled bowl using an electric mixer on medium-high speed. Whip until stiff peaks form, meaning when you lift the beaters, the peaks stand straight without folding over. This step is crucial for a light, airy filling. If you over-whip, the cream can become grainy and separate, so watch closely. Set the whipped cream aside and clean your mixing bowl for the next step.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Stiff peaks that hold their shape firmly without drooping.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, beat the softened cream cheese with powdered sugar and vanilla extract until smooth and creamy. Make sure the cream cheese is fully softened to avoid lumps, which can ruin the silky texture of the filling. Scrape down the sides of the bowl as you mix to fully incorporate all ingredients. The mixture should be smooth and slightly thick.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, lump-free cream cheese mixture with a slight sheen.
Step 4
Gently fold about a third of the whipped cream into the cream cheese mixture to lighten it. Use a spatula and fold carefully to keep as much air in the mixture as possible. Then fold in the remaining whipped cream until fully combined. Folding instead of stirring vigorously is important here to maintain the fluffy texture that makes these cups so delightful.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Light, airy filling with no streaks of cream or cream cheese.
Step 5
Slice the strawberries and fold half of them gently into the cheesecake filling. This adds bursts of fresh strawberry flavor throughout the cups. Be gentle so you don’t crush the berries and make the filling too watery. The remaining strawberries will be used as a fresh topping for extra color and texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling evenly dotted with strawberry pieces but still thick and creamy.
Step 6
Spoon the cheesecake mixture evenly over the chilled graham cracker crust in each cup. Use a small spatula or the back of a spoon to smooth the tops. This layering is what makes the shortcake cups visually appealing and gives you that perfect bite with crust, filling, and fruit.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, even layers in each cup with no gaps or air pockets.
Step 7
Top each cheesecake cup with the remaining fresh strawberry slices. This final touch makes the dessert look fresh and inviting. You can also add a small mint leaf or a dusting of powdered sugar if you want to get fancy. Pop the cups into the refrigerator to chill for at least 1 hour so the filling sets and flavors meld.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bright, fresh-looking strawberry garnish on top of each cup.
Step 8
When ready to serve, take the cups out of the fridge about 10 minutes beforehand to soften slightly for the best texture. These cheesecake cups should be creamy but firm enough to hold their shape. Serve with a small spoon and enjoy the perfect balance of crunchy, creamy, and fruity.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes rest
✓ Doneness: Filling slightly softened but still holding shape when scooped.
Step 9
If you want to make these ahead of time, prepare everything up to the chilling step and cover the cups tightly with plastic wrap. They keep well in the fridge for up to 2 days. Just add fresh strawberries on top before serving to keep that bright color and fresh flavor.
⏱ Time: Preparation for storage
✓ Doneness: Cups fully set and covered to maintain freshness.

Variations

Lemon Blueberry Cheesecake Cups

Swap strawberries for fresh blueberries and add a teaspoon of lemon zest to the cream cheese mixture for a zesty twist. The lemon brightens the flavor and pairs beautifully with blueberries, creating a refreshing variation perfect for spring or summer.

Note: Lemon zest adds acidity that can slightly firm the filling, so fold it in gently and taste as you go to avoid overpowering.

Chocolate Graham Cracker Crust Cheesecake Cups

Mix cocoa powder into the graham cracker crumbs for a chocolatey crust base. Add mini chocolate chips into the filling for extra indulgence. This version is perfect for chocolate lovers craving a rich, no-bake dessert.

Note: Cocoa powder can dry out the crust mixture slightly; add a bit more melted butter if needed to keep it bindable.

Vegan Strawberry Cheesecake Cups

Use vegan cream cheese and coconut cream whipped to stiff peaks instead of dairy cream. Substitute butter with coconut oil for the crust. This creates a dairy-free, creamy dessert that’s just as delicious and perfect for plant-based diets.

Note: Coconut cream whips differently than heavy cream; chill it overnight and scoop only the solid part for best results.

Peach and Raspberry No-Bake Cheesecake Cups

Replace strawberries with diced peaches and raspberries for a summery, juicy combo. The sweetness of peaches and tartness of raspberries balance the creamy filling nicely, giving a fresh flavor twist.

Note: Peaches have more moisture than strawberries; drain excess juice if necessary to prevent watery filling.

Spiced Pumpkin Cheesecake Cups

For a fall-inspired treat, stir in pumpkin puree and pumpkin pie spices into the cream cheese mixture. Top with chopped pecans for crunch. This variation is creamy, spiced, and perfect for autumn gatherings.

Note: Pumpkin puree adds moisture; reduce whipped cream slightly to maintain filling firmness.

Pro Tips

Chill your mixing bowls and beaters before whipping cream.
Cold equipment helps the cream whip faster and achieve stiff peaks more easily. Warm bowls cause the fat in the cream to soften, preventing it from aerating properly and resulting in a runny or unstable whipped cream.
Use a rubber spatula to fold whipped cream into the cream cheese gently.
Folding preserves the air bubbles whipped into the cream, keeping the filling light and fluffy. Stirring or beating vigorously deflates the mixture, making it dense and heavy, which changes the texture drastically.
Press the graham cracker crust firmly into the cups but don’t over-compact.
Firm pressing ensures the crust holds together when scooping, but over-packing can create a hard, rock-like base that’s unpleasant to eat. Aim for a balanced firmness that’s easy to bite through.
Let the assembled cups chill at least one hour before serving.
Chilling allows the filling to set and the flavors to blend. Serving too soon results in a loose, runny texture that won’t hold shape and will be messier to eat.
Use powdered sugar instead of granulated sugar in the filling.
Powdered sugar dissolves quickly and helps stabilize the cream cheese mixture for a smoother texture. Granulated sugar can leave a gritty mouthfeel if not fully dissolved.
Slice strawberries just before folding into the filling to avoid excess juice.
Strawberries release water once cut, which can thin out the filling and make it watery. Adding them last and folding gently minimizes this effect and keeps the filling thick.
Serve these cups slightly chilled but not ice cold.
Serving straight from the fridge can make the filling too firm and mute flavors. Letting them sit out for 10 minutes softens the texture and enhances the creamy, fresh taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not softening the cream cheese enough before mixing.

Why it happens People often try to rush the process by mixing cold cream cheese, assuming it will blend fine. The cold temperature makes the cream cheese lumpy and hard to incorporate smoothly.

Fix Leave the cream cheese out at room temperature for at least 30 minutes before starting. If you’re short on time, soften it gently in the microwave in 10-second bursts, stirring in between, until smooth but not melted.

Over-whipping the heavy cream.

Why it happens It’s easy to get carried away and whip cream past stiff peaks, turning it grainy and eventually into butter.

Fix Watch the cream closely as it whips and stop as soon as stiff peaks form. If you accidentally over-whip slightly, gently fold in some fresh unwhipped cream to soften it back.

Mixing the whipped cream aggressively into the cream cheese mixture.

Why it happens Some assume faster mixing is better, but vigorous stirring deflates the cream and ruins the texture.

Fix Use a gentle folding motion with a spatula, turning the bowl slowly and lifting the mixture from the bottom to the top. This keeps the filling airy and light.

Pressing the crust too firmly or unevenly.

Why it happens In a rush, some press the crumbs too hard or unevenly, causing a crust that’s too dense or crumbly in spots.

Fix Press the crumbs evenly with a flat-bottomed glass or spoon, applying firm but gentle pressure to create a uniform base that’s easy to bite into.

Adding cut strawberries too early and stirring too much.

Why it happens Cut berries release juice quickly and over-stirring can make the filling watery and loose.

Fix Fold strawberries in gently at the end and add the rest on top just before chilling. This keeps the filling thick and the berries fresh.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These cheesecake cups can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours when covered to prevent contamination. Beyond that, refrigeration is recommended to keep the filling fresh and safe.

Refrigerator

Store the cups in airtight containers or covered with plastic wrap in the fridge for up to 2 days. The texture will firm up more when cold; let them sit out for 10 minutes before serving to soften slightly and improve flavor.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended for best texture, as the cream cheese and whipped cream filling may separate and become grainy after thawing. If freezing, wrap tightly and consume within 1 month, thawing slowly in the fridge overnight.

Reheating

No reheating needed. Serve chilled or slightly softened at room temperature. Avoid microwaving as it will ruin the creamy texture and may cause separation.

Make Ahead

Prepare the graham cracker crust and cheesecake filling up to 1-2 days ahead, store separately or assembled covered in the fridge. Add fresh strawberries just before serving to keep the topping bright and fresh.

FAQ

Can I make these cheesecake cups without heavy cream?
Heavy cream is essential for whipping to stiff peaks, which gives the filling its light, airy texture. If you skip it, the filling will be dense and heavy. For a dairy-free option, coconut cream can be whipped similarly, but regular milk or half-and-half won’t work.
How long do these cheesecake cups keep in the fridge?
They keep well for up to 2 days refrigerated in an airtight container. After that, the crust may start to soften and the filling can lose its freshness. Always add fresh berries before serving for best flavor and appearance.
Can I use frozen strawberries instead of fresh?
Frozen strawberries tend to release excess moisture when thawed, which can make the filling watery. If you use frozen, thaw and drain them thoroughly before adding, and fold in gently to avoid breaking the berries.
Why is my filling grainy or lumpy?
This usually happens if the cream cheese isn’t softened enough or the whipped cream is overmixed or deflated. Make sure the cream cheese is room temperature and fold whipped cream gently into the mixture to keep it smooth.
Can I make this recipe vegan?
Yes! Use vegan cream cheese alternatives and substitute heavy cream with chilled coconut cream whipped to stiff peaks. Replace butter with coconut oil for the crust. The texture and flavor will be slightly different but still delicious.
How do I prevent the graham cracker crust from becoming soggy?
Press the crust firmly to create a solid base and chill it before adding the filling. Also, folding fruit into the filling last and adding fresh berries on top right before serving helps keep the crust crisp longer.
Can I double this recipe for a larger batch?
Absolutely! Just double all ingredients and use larger serving containers or more cups. Mixing and folding techniques are the same, just be careful not to overmix the filling to maintain lightness.
Is it possible to add other fruits besides strawberries?
Yes, fresh blueberries, raspberries, or diced peaches work wonderfully. Just be mindful of moisture content—some fruits release more juice, which can thin the filling, so drain excess liquid when needed.
