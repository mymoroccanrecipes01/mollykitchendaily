Nostalgic Orange Slice with Tangy Citrus Jelly and Sweet Coconut Base

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 12, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Medium Difficulty

This orange slice is a delightful nod to childhood treats, combining a zesty citrus jelly layer with a creamy coconut base. The fresh orange zest really lifts the flavor, making it perfect for afternoon tea or a light dessert.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this orange slice recipe while rummaging through my grandma’s old cookbook. It instantly took me back to those lazy afternoons spent nibbling on tangy, sweet treats with family. What makes this version special is the fresh orange zest folded right into the coconut base — it gives the whole slice a brightness that really sets it apart from the usual store-bought versions. I’ve tried making it without fresh zest before, and it just wasn’t the same. The jelly layer is delightfully tangy but not overpowering, balancing perfectly with the creamy, sweet coconut underneath. I also like that it’s not too heavy; it feels like a treat but doesn’t leave you stuffed. The secret to the jelly’s perfect set is blooming the gelatin properly and gently folding it into the warm orange juice mixture. This recipe is straightforward but requires a bit of patience to let everything chill and set just right. The finished slice looks beautiful with its vibrant orange top and snowy white base, making it perfect for a casual afternoon tea or a simple dessert when you want something nostalgic yet fresh. I think you’ll love how the flavors come together — it’s like sunshine on a plate!

What You'll Need

Approximately 8 slices, 8 servings

  • 1 cup desiccated coconut, unsweetened
  • 1/2 cup sweetened condensed milk
  • 2 tablespoons butter, softened
  • Zest of 2 oranges, finely grated
  • 1/2 cup orange juice, freshly squeezed
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 2 teaspoons gelatin powder
  • 2 tablespoons cold water
  • Extra orange zest, for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preparing the coconut base. In a mixing bowl, combine the desiccated coconut, softened butter, sweetened condensed milk, and the finely grated zest of two oranges. Mix everything thoroughly until it forms a moist, sticky mixture. This base is the foundation of your orange slice, so make sure the ingredients are well incorporated. The fresh orange zest adds that bright, citrus aroma which is key to balancing the sweetness. Avoid overmixing, which can make the base too dense.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Mixture should be sticky but spreadable, with visible flecks of orange zest evenly distributed.
Step 2
Press the coconut mixture evenly into a lined 8x8 inch baking dish, creating a firm and level base. Use the back of a spoon or your fingers to smooth it out. This step matters because an even base ensures the jelly layer sets uniformly and the slices cut cleanly. If the base is uneven or too thin in spots, the final slices might crumble or have an unbalanced texture. Chill the base in the fridge for at least 15 minutes while you prepare the jelly layer.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Base feels firm and cool to the touch, holding its shape when pressed gently.
Step 3
Sprinkle the gelatin powder over cold water in a small bowl and let it bloom for 5 minutes. This is crucial because blooming hydrates the gelatin, allowing it to dissolve smoothly without lumps. Skipping this step can result in grainy jelly that won’t set properly. After blooming, gently warm the gelatin mixture over a double boiler or in short bursts in the microwave until fully dissolved, but don’t let it boil.
⏱ Time: 8
✓ Doneness: Gelatin should be clear and liquid with no granules visible.
Step 4
In a small saucepan, combine freshly squeezed orange juice and sugar. Warm over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves completely. Remove from heat and slowly stir in the dissolved gelatin. The heat helps the gelatin incorporate evenly into the juice mixture, creating a smooth jelly layer. If the juice is too hot when adding gelatin, it can degrade the gelatin’s setting ability, so let it cool slightly before mixing.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Mixture is warm, smooth, and free of sugar crystals.
Step 5
Pour the orange jelly mixture gently over the chilled coconut base, ensuring an even layer. Pour slowly to avoid disturbing the base, which could cause the layers to mix. This layer will set into the tangy, glossy top of your orange slice. Once poured, return the dish to the fridge and chill for at least 2 hours, or until fully set. Patience here is key — rushing the set can result in a runny jelly layer.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Jelly layer is firm and no longer jiggles when gently shaken.
Step 6
Once the jelly has fully set, run a knife gently along the edges of the dish to loosen the slice. Carefully lift it out using the parchment paper. This prevents sticking and helps keep the slice intact. Cutting immediately without loosening may cause the jelly to crack or the base to crumble. Place the slice on a cutting board for slicing.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Edges lift cleanly from the dish without tearing.
Step 7
Using a sharp knife, cut the orange slice into squares or rectangles. To get clean cuts, wipe the knife blade with a damp cloth between slices to prevent the jelly from sticking. If your knife is dull or sticky, the jelly layer may tear or pull away unevenly. Take your time cutting to maintain neat edges.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Slices have clean, straight edges with no jelly tearing.
Step 8
Garnish each slice with a little extra fresh orange zest on top for a pop of color and extra zestiness. This final touch elevates the presentation and enhances the citrus aroma. You can also add a light dusting of desiccated coconut if you want to amp up the coconut flavor.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Garnish looks fresh and evenly distributed on each slice.
Step 9
Serve the orange slices chilled, ideally within 24 hours for the best texture and flavor. They pair beautifully with a cup of tea or coffee for a nostalgic afternoon treat. If storing leftover slices, keep them in an airtight container in the fridge to maintain freshness and prevent the jelly from drying out.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Slices are firm, flavorful, and chilled well.

Variations

Lemon Slice Twist

Swap orange juice and zest for lemon to create a tangy lemon slice. The lemon’s sharper acidity gives the jelly a refreshing zing, while the coconut base remains sweet and creamy. It’s a perfect variation if you want a brighter, more tart dessert.

Note: Lemon juice is more acidic, so taste the jelly mixture before setting and adjust sugar if needed. The gelatin setting remains the same.

Tropical Mango Orange Slice

Add 1/4 cup pureed ripe mango to the orange jelly mixture for a tropical twist. The mango adds sweetness and a smooth texture that blends beautifully with the citrus notes, giving your slice a sunny, exotic flavor.

Note: Puree mango until smooth and gently fold into the warm orange juice mixture before adding gelatin.

Vegan Orange Slice with Agar Agar

Use agar agar powder instead of gelatin to make this slice vegan-friendly. Agar sets firmer and faster, so adjust chilling time accordingly. The texture will be a bit different—less jiggly but still deliciously fresh and bright.

Note: Dissolve agar agar in orange juice and bring to a boil for 1-2 minutes before pouring over the base.

Chocolate Coconut Orange Slice

Mix 2 tablespoons melted dark chocolate into the coconut base for a rich chocolate twist. The bittersweet chocolate pairs wonderfully with the zesty orange jelly, creating a sophisticated flavor contrast that’s sure to impress.

Note: Allow melted chocolate to cool slightly before mixing into the coconut base to avoid melting the butter.

Crunchy Nut Orange Slice

Stir 1/4 cup chopped toasted almonds or walnuts into the coconut base for added crunch and nuttiness. The nuts add texture and depth, making the slice more satisfying and complex in flavor.

Note: Toast nuts lightly in a dry pan before chopping to enhance their flavor.

Pro Tips

Bloom gelatin properly before adding to warm liquids.
Blooming gelatin hydrates the granules so they dissolve evenly without clumping. Skipping this step or adding gelatin directly to hot liquids can cause lumps or prevent the jelly from setting properly, ruining the texture of your orange slice.
Use fresh orange zest instead of dried or pre-packaged.
Fresh zest contains essential oils that provide intense citrus aroma and flavor. Dried zest lacks this vibrancy and can taste dull or bitter, which diminishes the bright, fresh character of the orange slice.
Press the coconut base firmly and evenly into the pan.
A compact, level base supports the jelly layer and ensures clean slices. If the base is loose or uneven, the slice may crumble or the jelly layer may crack when cutting or serving.
Chill the base before pouring the jelly layer.
Chilling the base firms it up so the jelly layer won’t mix or sink into it. This preserves the distinct layers and prevents a messy, blended texture.
Wipe your knife between cuts for clean slices.
A sticky knife can tear the jelly layer and cause uneven edges. Cleaning the blade between cuts helps maintain smooth, neat slices, improving both presentation and texture.
Use unsweetened desiccated coconut for better texture control.
Sweetened coconut can make the base overly sugary and affect moisture balance. Unsweetened coconut lets you control sweetness via condensed milk and keeps the texture chewy but not soggy.
Don’t rush the chilling time for the jelly layer.
Proper chilling ensures the jelly sets firmly and holds shape. Cutting too soon results in a runny top layer that won’t slice cleanly and can spill over the base.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping gelatin blooming

Why it happens People often try to save time by adding gelatin powder directly to warm juice, not realizing it needs to be hydrated first.

Fix Always sprinkle gelatin over cold water and let it bloom for 5 minutes before dissolving. This prevents lumps and ensures the jelly sets properly.

Using bottled orange juice

Why it happens Convenience leads many to use store-bought juice, but it lacks fresh flavor and acidity.

Fix Use freshly squeezed orange juice for best taste and proper jelly setting. The natural acidity helps gelatin firm up and adds brightness.

Pouring jelly over warm base

Why it happens Sometimes the base isn’t chilled fully before adding jelly, causing layers to blend.

Fix Chill the coconut base thoroughly until firm before pouring the jelly layer to keep distinct layers.

Cutting before jelly sets

Why it happens Impatience can lead to slicing the jelly while still soft, causing mess and uneven texture.

Fix Allow at least 2 hours chilling or until jelly is firm and doesn’t jiggle before cutting.

Not pressing base firmly

Why it happens Rushing or neglecting this step results in a crumbly base that falls apart.

Fix Press the base mixture firmly and evenly with the back of a spoon or your fingers to create a solid foundation.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

It’s best to keep the orange slice chilled and avoid leaving it at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Cover loosely with plastic wrap if you need to leave it out briefly to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store the orange slices in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. The jelly layer may firm up more when cold, which is ideal for serving. They taste best chilled but can be brought to room temperature before serving if preferred.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the jelly layer can become watery and the texture may degrade. If you must freeze, wrap slices tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe container for up to 1 month. Thaw in the refrigerator overnight and expect some texture loss.

Reheating

This dessert is best served cold and does not require reheating. Avoid microwaving as heat will melt the jelly and ruin the texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare the coconut base and jelly layer a day ahead and store in the refrigerator overnight. This resting time helps the flavors meld and ensures the jelly sets perfectly. Cut and garnish just before serving for the freshest look.

FAQ

Can I use powdered gelatin sheets instead of powder?
Yes, you can substitute powdered gelatin with sheets. Typically, 1 tablespoon of gelatin powder equals about 4 sheets. Soak the sheets in cold water until soft, squeeze out excess water, then dissolve in warm orange juice. This method works well but be sure to adjust quantities accordingly.
What if I don’t have fresh oranges for zest?
Fresh zest is best for flavor, but if you don’t have fresh oranges, you can use high-quality dried orange peel or a small amount of orange extract. Keep in mind that dried zest has less aroma and can taste slightly bitter, so use sparingly.
How do I prevent the jelly layer from cracking?
Cracking often happens if the jelly sets too quickly or the temperature changes abruptly. Chill the jelly slowly in the fridge and avoid moving the dish during setting. Also, ensure the gelatin is fully dissolved and the jelly layer isn’t poured too thickly.
Can I make this recipe gluten-free?
Absolutely! This recipe is naturally gluten-free since it uses coconut and gelatin. Just double-check that your condensed milk and other ingredients don’t contain any hidden gluten additives.
How long does the jelly layer need to set?
The jelly layer usually needs at least 2 hours in the refrigerator to set fully. For best results, chill overnight. If you try to cut it too soon, the jelly may be too soft and won’t hold its shape.
Is there a vegan alternative to gelatin?
Yes, agar agar is a popular vegan substitute for gelatin. Use about 1 teaspoon of agar powder per cup of liquid, dissolve in orange juice, and bring to a boil to activate. Agar sets firmer and faster than gelatin, so watch the setting time closely.
Can I use canned orange juice?
Canned juice often contains preservatives and added sugars that can affect the jelly’s flavor and setting. Freshly squeezed juice is highly recommended for the best taste and texture, but if using canned juice, choose 100% pure with no additives.
What’s the best way to cut the slices evenly?
Use a sharp knife and wipe the blade clean between each cut to prevent sticking. For perfectly even slices, score the jelly gently before cutting all the way through. Cutting when the jelly is fully chilled also helps maintain clean edges.
