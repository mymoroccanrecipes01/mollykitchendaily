Fudgy Gluten-Free Zucchini Brownies That Actually Taste Like Chocolate

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 29, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These gluten-free zucchini brownies are moist and fudgy, thanks to zucchini's natural moisture and rich chocolate. The veggies hide perfectly without messing with the classic brownie taste. Ideal for a quick dessert or snack!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this gluten-free zucchini brownie recipe a few years ago when I was desperate to find a dessert that could satisfy my chocolate cravings without the guilt. Honestly, I was skeptical about sneaking veggies into brownies, but this one blew me away. The zucchini adds moisture and a subtle texture that keeps these brownies incredibly fudgy, but you’d never guess there’s a veggie hiding inside. What I love most is how this recipe balances rich chocolate flavor with a healthy twist — it’s not just about being gluten-free or sneaky with veggies; it’s genuinely delicious. I've tweaked it over time to get the right amount of sweetness and to make sure the zucchini doesn’t overpower the chocolate. One trick I learned is to squeeze out as much water as possible from the grated zucchini to keep the batter from getting too watery, which is key for that perfect fudgy texture. The result is a batch of dense, moist brownies that are perfect for an afternoon snack, a quick dessert, or even a lunchbox treat. Plus, they’re super easy to whip up on a busy day. I hope you enjoy making (and eating!) these as much as I do.

What You'll Need

8 brownies

  • 1 cup finely grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 3/4 cup gluten-free all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/4 cup melted coconut oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup dark chocolate chips (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchini finely and then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth. Squeeze out as much liquid as you can—this step is crucial to prevent the batter from becoming too watery, which would affect the fudgy texture. If you skip this, your brownies might turn out cakey or soggy, so take your time here.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels mostly dry to the touch and no longer dripping with water.
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the gluten-free flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt. Mixing these dry ingredients evenly ensures your brownies will have a consistent texture and flavor. If you rush this, you might end up with clumps of cocoa or uneven rising.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no lumps or streaks visible.
Step 3
In a separate large bowl, beat the brown sugar with melted coconut oil until smooth. The oil gives the brownies a lovely richness and helps keep them moist. Using melted coconut oil instead of butter makes this recipe dairy-free too, which is a nice bonus.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks glossy and well combined, no graininess from sugar.
Step 4
Add eggs one at a time to the sugar and oil mixture, beating well after each addition. This helps incorporate air and creates a smooth batter. Adding eggs slowly also prevents curdling, which can happen if eggs are added too quickly.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and slightly thickened with no visible egg streaks.
Step 5
Stir in the vanilla extract and then fold in the grated zucchini until evenly distributed. Folding gently is key here to keep the batter light while still mixing everything well. Overmixing can make brownies tough, so just combine until no large streaks remain.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is evenly mixed, and batter looks uniform with flecks of green.
Step 6
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet, folding carefully to combine. This slow addition prevents overmixing and helps keep the brownies tender. If you dump all the flour at once and stir vigorously, the brownies could turn out dense and dry.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, fudgy, and smooth with no pockets of flour.
Step 7
If using, fold in the dark chocolate chips now for extra melty pockets of chocolate. This step is optional but highly recommended if you want that extra chocolate punch. Use good quality chips so they melt nicely without overpowering the batter.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly scattered through the batter.
Step 8
Pour the batter into a greased or parchment-lined 8x8 inch baking pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. Even spreading ensures the brownies bake uniformly. Uneven batter can cause some parts to dry out while others stay gooey.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth and level in the pan.
Step 9
Bake in a preheated 350°F (175°C) oven for 25-30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center—look for a few moist crumbs but not wet batter. Overbaking dries them out, so watch closely!
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges look set and toothpick comes out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

Variations

Nutty Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans to the batter for a crunchy contrast that pairs beautifully with the fudgy texture. Nuts add a satisfying crunch and subtle earthiness to every bite.

Note: Fold nuts in gently with the chocolate chips to avoid breaking them up and distribute evenly.

Spicy Mocha Brownies

Incorporate 1 teaspoon instant espresso powder and 1/4 teaspoon cinnamon into the dry ingredients to give the brownies a warm, spicy mocha twist. This enhances the chocolate flavor with a subtle kick.

Note: Make sure espresso powder dissolves evenly by mixing well with dry ingredients before combining with wet.

Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use maple syrup instead of brown sugar. This makes the brownies entirely plant-based without losing moisture or taste.

Note: Let flax eggs sit for 5 minutes to thicken before adding to wet ingredients for best results.

Peanut Butter Swirl Brownies

Drop spoonfuls of peanut butter on top of the batter before baking and swirl with a knife for a creamy, nutty ribbon through the brownies. It adds richness and a delicious flavor contrast.

Note: Swirl gently to avoid deflating the batter and maintain a marbled look.

Orange Zest Brownies

Add 1 tablespoon of fresh orange zest to the batter for a bright, citrusy note that complements the chocolate and zucchini. It adds freshness and a lovely aroma.

Note: Use a fine grater to avoid bitter pith and mix zest into wet ingredients for even flavor distribution.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess water from the grated zucchini.
Water weighs down the batter and can make brownies soggy or cakey instead of fudgy. Removing moisture concentrates the zucchini’s flavor and helps maintain the right batter consistency for dense brownies.
Use room temperature eggs for better mixing.
Cold eggs can cause the melted coconut oil to solidify or curdle when mixed, leading to uneven texture. Room temperature eggs blend smoothly, helping create a uniform batter that bakes evenly.
Don’t overmix once you add the dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops any gluten present or breaks down the batter’s air bubbles, resulting in tough brownies. Fold gently just until combined to keep the texture tender and fudgy.
Line your baking pan with parchment paper.
This makes removing the brownies easier and prevents sticking. It also helps protect the edges from overbaking, ensuring a moist interior with slightly crisp edges.
Test for doneness using a toothpick with moist crumbs.
Brownies continue to cook after removal from the oven, so pulling them out when the toothpick shows moist crumbs prevents overbaking and dryness.
Add chocolate chips for extra melty pockets.
Chocolate chips melt during baking, giving little bursts of gooey chocolate that enhance the richness and texture without overpowering the zucchini flavor.
Cool brownies completely before cutting.
Cutting warm brownies can cause them to crumble or fall apart. Cooling allows the structure to set, making clean, neat slices easier.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough water from the zucchini.

Why it happens People often underestimate how much moisture zucchini contains or rush this step to save time.

Fix Take time to grate finely and then squeeze the zucchini tightly in a clean towel or cheesecloth. Repeat squeezing if needed until no more liquid drips out. This ensures the batter stays thick and fudgy.

Overbaking the brownies.

Why it happens Some assume brownies should be fully set and dry before removing from the oven.

Fix Start checking at 25 minutes and pull the brownies out when a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with moist crumbs, not wet batter. This keeps them soft and fudgy.

Using a single gluten-free flour without binder.

Why it happens Not realizing that gluten-free baking requires binders like xanthan gum for structure.

Fix Use a gluten-free all-purpose blend that includes a binder or add xanthan gum separately. This prevents crumbly or dry brownies.

Mixing dry ingredients directly into wet without sifting or whisking.

Why it happens Skipping this step can leave clumps of cocoa or baking powder in the batter.

Fix Whisk dry ingredients together in a separate bowl before adding to wet mixture for even distribution and smooth batter.

Cutting brownies too soon after baking.

Why it happens Impatience or desire to serve warm leads to messy slices.

Fix Allow brownies to cool completely in the pan before slicing to let them set properly.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the brownies covered at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep them in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap to prevent drying out. They’re best eaten fresh but will stay moist for a couple of days.

Refrigerator

Brownies can be stored in the fridge in an airtight container for up to 5 days. Refrigeration firms them up and can slightly intensify the chocolate flavor. Bring to room temperature or warm gently before serving for the best texture.

Freezer

Freeze brownies wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then in a freezer-safe bag or container for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a few hours or warm gently in the microwave. Freezing may slightly change texture but they remain tasty.

Reheating

Warm brownies in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk or water before warming can help restore moisture. Avoid overheating which can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter 1 day ahead and refrigerate it in a covered bowl. Bring to room temperature and stir gently before baking. Alternatively, bake the brownies and store them as described; reheating just before serving keeps them fresh.

FAQ

Can I use regular flour instead of gluten-free flour?
Absolutely! You can substitute regular all-purpose flour 1:1 if gluten isn’t a concern. The texture might be slightly different—usually a bit more tender—but the brownies will still be delicious.
What if I don’t have coconut oil? Can I use butter?
Yes, melted butter works well as a substitute for coconut oil. It adds a richer flavor but is not dairy-free. Use the same amount and melt it before mixing to maintain the right texture.
How do I know when the brownies are done?
The best way is the toothpick test: insert a toothpick in the center and look for moist crumbs clinging to it. If it comes out wet with batter, bake a few minutes longer. Avoid waiting until it’s completely dry to keep them fudgy.
Can I make these brownies vegan?
Yes! Replace each egg with a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tablespoons water) and use maple syrup or coconut sugar instead of brown sugar if desired. The texture will be slightly different but still tasty.
Will the zucchini flavor be noticeable?
Nope, the zucchini adds moisture and a bit of texture but doesn’t overpower the chocolate flavor. It’s a great way to sneak in veggies without anyone noticing!
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Definitely! Chopped walnuts, pecans, or chocolate chips are great additions. Just fold them in at the end before baking to distribute evenly.
How should I store leftover brownies?
Keep leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. You can also freeze them for longer storage.
Can I double the recipe?
Yes, you can double the ingredients and use a larger pan (like 9x13 inches). Just keep an eye on baking time, which may increase by 5-10 minutes—use the toothpick test to check doneness.
