Cozy Spiced Pear Dessert with Cinnamon and Cloves for Fall Nights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 19, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This warm spiced pear dessert is my go-to for chilly evenings. The pears simmer slowly in cinnamon and cloves, soaking up all those cozy flavors. It’s a simple treat that feels fancy enough for guests or just a quiet night in.

Introduction

I first made this spiced pear dessert on a crisp autumn evening when I wanted something sweet but not too heavy. The idea was to let the natural pear flavors shine while adding that warm, cozy spice vibe. What makes this version special is the gentle simmering process — it’s slow enough to let the pears soak up the cinnamon and cloves but not so long that they turn mushy. I also add a splash of vanilla at the end to deepen the flavor without overpowering the fruit. I’ve tried other recipes that cook the pears too fast or use too much sugar, which ends up masking the delicate pear taste. This one strikes a perfect balance between fragrant and fresh. When finished, the pears are tender but still hold their shape, swimming in a fragrant syrup that’s perfect spooned over ice cream or enjoyed on its own with a cup of tea. Honestly, it’s become my favorite fall dessert to whip up when I want something simple but still feels special enough to serve guests. Plus, it only takes about 45 minutes total, making it a great weeknight treat too.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 ripe pears, peeled, cored, and halved
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 whole cloves
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by peeling, coring, and halving your pears. Choosing ripe but firm pears is key so they hold their shape during cooking. Place them in a large saucepan to prepare for simmering. This step ensures even cooking and lets the pears absorb the flavors better throughout the process. Avoid overly soft pears here, or they’ll break down too much.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Pears are halved evenly with no bruises or soft spots
Step 2
Add the brown sugar, cinnamon stick, cloves, water, lemon juice, and a pinch of salt to the saucepan with the pears. The lemon juice brightens the flavors and helps prevent the pears from browning. The sugar and spices will slowly dissolve and infuse the pears as they cook. Stir gently to combine without breaking the fruit.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Sugar starts dissolving and liquid begins to simmer gently
Step 3
Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer over medium heat. Lower the heat to maintain a soft simmer – too high and the pears will become mushy and lose shape. Simmer uncovered for about 25 minutes, turning the pears occasionally to cook evenly and soak up the syrup. This slow cooking releases the spices’ aroma while softening the fruit just right.
⏱ Time: 25
✓ Doneness: Pears are tender but still hold their shape; syrup is fragrant and slightly thickened
Step 4
Once the pears are tender, remove the pan from heat and stir in the vanilla extract. This last step adds a warm, sweet depth to the syrup that complements the spices beautifully. Avoid boiling after adding vanilla, or you’ll lose that delicate aroma.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Vanilla aroma is noticeable and syrup has a glossy finish
Step 5
Use a slotted spoon to transfer pears to serving bowls, then spoon the spiced syrup over them. The syrup is packed with flavor and a perfect balance of sweet and spice. Serve warm on its own or with a scoop of vanilla ice cream for an extra indulgent treat.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Pears glisten with syrup and bowls smell invitingly spiced
Step 6
If you want to thicken the syrup further, return it to low heat and simmer for a few more minutes until it reduces by about a third. Keep stirring gently to avoid burning. A thicker syrup clings better to the pears and makes a richer dessert experience.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Syrup coats the back of a spoon and has a syrupy consistency
Step 7
For a decorative touch, sprinkle some freshly chopped toasted nuts or a pinch of ground cinnamon over the pears before serving. This adds texture and an extra layer of flavor that pairs wonderfully with the soft fruit and warm spices.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Nuts are lightly toasted and aroma is nutty and fresh
Step 8
Allow any leftovers to cool completely before storing. The flavors actually deepen overnight, so if you have time, refrigerate and serve cold or gently reheat before serving. This makes it a great make-ahead dessert for busy days.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Dessert is cooled and syrup slightly thickened in fridge
Step 9
When reheating, warm gently on the stovetop or microwave in short bursts to avoid overcooking the pears. Add a splash of water if syrup has thickened too much. This keeps the dessert fresh and delicious without drying out the fruit.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Pears warmed through without becoming mushy, syrup fluid and fragrant

Variations

Spiced Pears with Orange Zest

Add 1 teaspoon of fresh orange zest to the simmering syrup for a bright citrus twist that pairs beautifully with cinnamon and cloves. This variation adds a fresh aromatic layer and a slightly tangy note.

Note: Add orange zest with the spices at the start of simmering to infuse the syrup evenly.

Honey-Sweetened Spiced Pears

Replace brown sugar with 1/3 cup honey for a more floral sweetness and a slightly thinner syrup. Honey brings a natural complexity that complements the spices and fruit.

Note: Simmer gently as honey caramelizes faster; watch syrup consistency closely to avoid burning.

Spiced Pears with Star Anise

Add one whole star anise pod with the cinnamon and cloves for a subtle licorice flavor that elevates the dessert. It’s great if you want a more complex spice profile.

Note: Remove the star anise before serving to avoid overpowering the pears.

Vegan Spiced Pears with Maple Syrup

Use maple syrup instead of brown sugar for a vegan-friendly version with a deeper, woodsy sweetness. The maple adds richness and enhances the fall feel.

Note: Simmer gently and adjust sweetness to taste, as maple syrup varies in intensity.

Spiced Pear Compote with Ginger

Add 1 teaspoon of freshly grated ginger to the syrup for a zesty kick that balances the sweetness and adds warmth. This variation adds a fresh, spicy note that livens up the dish.

Note: Add ginger early in simmering to allow its flavor to mellow and blend with spices.

Pro Tips

Use firm, slightly underripe pears for the best texture.
Firmer pears hold up better to simmering, preventing the dessert from becoming mushy. If pears are too ripe, they’ll break down quickly and lose shape, making the dessert less visually appealing.
Simmer gently and avoid boiling.
High heat breaks down the pears too fast and causes the syrup to evaporate unevenly. A gentle simmer allows flavors to infuse slowly and pears to soften evenly without losing texture.
Add vanilla extract off the heat.
Vanilla is delicate and evaporates quickly at high temperatures. Adding it after cooking preserves its aroma and enhances the syrup with a warm, sweet note.
Turn the pears occasionally during cooking.
This ensures even exposure to the syrup and spices, allowing all sides to absorb flavor and cook uniformly without sticking to the pan.
Use a wide pan for simmering.
A wider pan increases surface area, helping the syrup reduce evenly and coat the pears better, enhancing flavor and texture.
Don’t skip the lemon juice.
Lemon juice balances sweetness and prevents oxidation, keeping the pears looking fresh and tasting bright.
Serve warm or chilled based on preference.
Serving warm highlights the spices and syrup, while chilling lets flavors meld and offers a refreshing alternative. Both ways work wonderfully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using overly ripe pears

Why it happens People often pick the sweetest pears without considering texture, assuming ripe means better flavor.

Fix Choose pears that are ripe but still firm to the touch. If you only have very ripe pears, reduce simmering time and watch closely to prevent them from turning mushy.

Boiling the syrup too hard

Why it happens Impatience or high heat settings can cause rapid boiling, thinking it speeds up cooking.

Fix Maintain a gentle simmer instead of a boil. This slow cooking lets spices infuse properly and prevents pears from breaking down excessively.

Skipping the lemon juice

Why it happens Some may think it’s unnecessary or worry about adding acidity.

Fix Always add fresh lemon juice to keep pears from browning and to balance sweetness. It makes a big difference in flavor and appearance.

Adding vanilla too early

Why it happens Forgetting to add vanilla off heat or mixing it in with other ingredients.

Fix Add vanilla extract only after removing from heat to preserve its delicate aroma and flavor.

Not turning pears during simmering

Why it happens Assuming pears will cook evenly without attention.

Fix Gently turn pears every 7-10 minutes to ensure all sides absorb syrup and cook evenly.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This dessert can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Keep it covered to avoid contamination and drying out of the pears.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The pears will become softer and syrup thicker when chilled. Reheat gently before serving or enjoy cold for a refreshing treat.

Freezer

Freeze in a sealed container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently to restore syrup consistency and soften pears.

Reheating

Warm gently on the stovetop over low heat or microwave in short bursts, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water if syrup has thickened too much to keep it saucy and fresh.

Make Ahead

Prepare the pears and syrup up to 2 days ahead and refrigerate. Reheat gently before serving. This allows flavors to deepen and saves time on busy days.

FAQ

Can I use canned pears instead of fresh?
While canned pears can be used in a pinch, fresh pears provide better texture and flavor. Canned pears are often softer and may break down too much when simmered, resulting in a less appealing texture.
How do I make this dessert vegan?
This recipe is naturally vegan as it uses no animal products. Just make sure your brown sugar is vegan-friendly (some are processed with bone char). Alternatively, substitute honey for maple syrup if you want to keep it fully vegan.
Can I add other spices to this dish?
Absolutely! Star anise, cardamom, or fresh ginger work well. Just add them at the start of simmering so their flavors have time to infuse without overpowering the pears.
What’s the best way to serve this dessert?
Serve warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream. You can also enjoy it chilled as a light, refreshing treat. Either way, the spiced syrup makes it special.
How do I prevent pears from turning mushy?
Use firm pears and simmer gently at low heat. Avoid boiling or overcooking. Turning them occasionally helps them cook evenly without falling apart.
Can I prepare this dessert ahead of time?
Yes, it actually tastes better after resting overnight in the fridge as the flavors meld. Just reheat gently before serving to refresh the syrup and soften the pears.
What if I don’t have whole spices?
Ground spices can be used but add them later in cooking to avoid bitterness. Whole spices release flavor more slowly and produce a cleaner, fresher taste.
Is this dessert gluten-free?
Yes, all ingredients used are naturally gluten-free, making it a safe choice for gluten-sensitive individuals.
