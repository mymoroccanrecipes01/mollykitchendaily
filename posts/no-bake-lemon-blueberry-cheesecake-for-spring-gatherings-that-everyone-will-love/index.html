No-Bake Lemon Blueberry Cheesecake That’s Pure Spring Bliss

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 6, 2026
15 min Prep Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This no-bake cheesecake is creamy and tangy with fresh lemon and juicy blueberries. It’s super easy to whip up and perfect for a spring or summer dessert when you want something refreshing without turning on the oven.

Pro Tips

Use a chilled bowl and beaters when whipping the cream.
Cold utensils help the cream whip faster and hold its structure better. Warm cream whips slowly and can become loose or watery, which affects the cheesecake's light texture.
Don’t overmix the filling after adding whipped cream.
Overmixing deflates the air bubbles incorporated in the whipped cream, leading to a dense, heavy cheesecake rather than a fluffy one. Fold gently to keep the airy texture.
Press the crust firmly but not overly hard.
Firm crust holds the base together but pressing too hard makes it compact and hard to cut. A light but even press ensures a tender yet stable crust.
Chill the cheesecake overnight if possible.
Long chilling helps the filling set fully, improving sliceability and flavor melding. Cutting too soon results in a runny texture and messy slices.
Use a hot knife for slicing.
Heating the knife with hot water and drying it before slicing creates smooth cuts without dragging or tearing the soft filling, making for prettier slices.
Avoid zesting the white pith of lemons.
The white pith is bitter and can spoil the bright lemon flavor. Only zest the colorful outer peel to get fragrant citrus oils.
Gently fold in blueberries at the end.
Folding carefully prevents the berries from breaking and turning the filling purple, keeping the cheesecake visually appealing with bursts of intact fruit.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using cold cream cheese directly from the fridge.

Why it happens Many people rush the process and don’t allow the cream cheese to soften, leading to lumps in the filling.

Fix Let the cream cheese sit at room temperature for at least 30 minutes before beating. This ensures a smooth, lump-free filling.

Overwhipping the heavy cream.

Why it happens It’s easy to go too far when whipping cream, turning it grainy or even into butter.

Fix Stop whipping as soon as stiff peaks form. The cream should look fluffy and hold shape but still be smooth.

Pressing the crust too hard.

Why it happens Trying to make the crust super compact can make it dense and difficult to cut.

Fix Press the crust firmly but gently, using a flat-bottomed cup to evenly distribute pressure without overcompressing.

Skipping the chilling time.

Why it happens Impatience or time constraints lead some to cut the cheesecake too soon, resulting in a runny filling.

Fix Chill for at least 4 hours or overnight to allow the filling to set properly for clean slices.

Mixing blueberries too early.

Why it happens Adding berries before folding in whipped cream causes them to break and color the filling.

Fix Fold in blueberries last, gently, to keep their shape and color intact.

Variations

Strawberry Lemon No-Bake Cheesecake

Swap blueberries for fresh strawberries sliced thinly. The strawberries add a sweeter, slightly different fruity note that pairs beautifully with lemon. You can also swirl in a strawberry puree for more color and flavor.

Note: Slicing strawberries thinner helps distribute them evenly and prevents large chunks from sinking to the bottom.

Lemon Blueberry Cheesecake with a Nutty Crust

Add finely chopped toasted almonds or pecans to the crust mix for extra crunch and flavor. This nutty twist complements the citrus and adds texture contrast to the creamy filling.

Note: Toast nuts lightly before chopping to enhance their aroma and crunch.

Vegan No-Bake Lemon Blueberry Cheesecake

Use vegan cream cheese and coconut cream instead of dairy cream. Sweeten with maple syrup and use a vegan graham cracker crust. This version is deliciously creamy and perfect for dairy-free diets.

Note: Chill the coconut cream overnight and scoop the solid part to whip for best results.

Lemon Blueberry Cheesecake with White Chocolate

Melt white chocolate and fold it into the cream cheese mixture for a richer, sweeter flavor. The white chocolate pairs wonderfully with lemon and blueberries for a more decadent dessert.

Note: Cool the melted white chocolate slightly before folding in to avoid curdling the cream cheese.

Mini No-Bake Lemon Blueberry Cheesecakes

Divide the filling and crust into individual ramekins or jars for easy portioning and adorable presentation. Perfect for parties or picnics where everyone can grab their own dessert.

Note: Adjust chilling time slightly as smaller portions set faster, usually 2-3 hours.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Keep the cheesecake covered and at room temperature for no more than 2 hours to prevent spoilage. It’s best served chilled, so avoid leaving it out too long.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container or cover tightly with plastic wrap. It keeps well for up to 4 days. The texture remains creamy, but the crust may soften slightly. Serve chilled or at room temperature.

Freezer

Wrap the cheesecake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator to preserve texture. The crust may lose some crunch but the filling stays smooth.

Reheating

No reheating needed. Serve chilled. If slightly firm after refrigeration, let it sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes before serving for the best texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare the crust and filling a day ahead, assemble, and refrigerate overnight. This saves time on the day you plan to serve and allows flavors to meld beautifully.

FAQ

Can I use frozen blueberries in this cheesecake?
Yes, but thaw and drain them well before folding into the filling to avoid excess moisture, which can make the cheesecake watery. Fresh blueberries are preferred for best texture and appearance.
What can I use instead of graham cracker crumbs for the crust?
You can substitute digestive biscuits, vanilla wafers, or even crushed pretzels for a salty twist. Just make sure to finely crush and mix with melted butter to bind the crust properly.
How do I prevent the cheesecake from being too runny?
Make sure to fully chill the cheesecake for at least 4 hours or overnight. Also, don’t skip whipping the cream properly and folding it gently to maintain structure.
Can I make this cheesecake vegan or dairy-free?
Absolutely! Use vegan cream cheese and coconut cream instead of dairy. Sweeten with maple syrup or agave. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious and creamy.
Why is my whipped cream turning grainy?
That happens if you overwhip the cream and it starts turning into butter. Stop whipping as soon as stiff peaks form to keep a smooth, fluffy texture.
Can I add gelatin to make it firmer?
You can, but it’s not necessary if you use full-fat cream cheese and whip the cream properly. Gelatin will firm it up more but changes the texture slightly.
How long can I store leftover cheesecake?
Keep leftovers refrigerated in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The filling stays creamy but the crust may soften over time.
Can I make this cheesecake without a springform pan?
Yes, you can use a regular pie dish or any round dish with a removable bottom if you have one. Just be gentle when removing the cheesecake to keep it intact.

What You'll Need

8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups graham cracker crumbs, finely crushed
  • 1/3 cup unsalted butter, melted
  • 16 ounces cream cheese, softened
  • 1 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon lemon zest
  • 1 1/2 cups heavy cream, cold
  • 1 cup fresh blueberries, plus more for topping

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by making the crust: mix together the graham cracker crumbs and melted butter until the mixture resembles wet sand. This step is crucial because the butter binds the crumbs together, creating the perfect base that holds its shape without crumbling. Press the mixture firmly into the bottom of a 9-inch springform pan or pie dish. Use the bottom of a flat glass or measuring cup to pack it tightly and evenly. This ensures a sturdy crust that won't fall apart when you slice the cheesecake. Avoid pressing too hard, which can make the crust too dense and hard to cut through.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Crust feels compact and evenly pressed, with no loose crumbs on the surface.
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, beat the softened cream cheese until smooth and creamy, about 2-3 minutes on medium speed. This step is important to avoid lumps and achieve a silky texture in the filling. Next, add the powdered sugar, vanilla extract, lemon juice, and lemon zest. Beat again until fully combined and smooth. The acidity from the lemon juice brightens the flavor and also helps slightly firm the filling as it chills. Don’t rush this step; fully incorporating the ingredients ensures an even, tangy taste throughout the cheesecake.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Filling is smooth, creamy, and free of lumps with a fresh lemon aroma.
Step 3
In a separate chilled bowl, whip the cold heavy cream until stiff peaks form. This means when you lift the whisk, the peaks stand straight up without collapsing. Whipping the cream to the right consistency is key because it lightens the dense cream cheese mixture, giving the cheesecake its airy texture. Be careful not to overwhip, or you’ll end up with grainy butter. Using a cold bowl and cream helps speed up the whipping process and results in a better texture.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Whipped cream holds stiff peaks and looks fluffy and smooth.
Step 4
Gently fold one-third of the whipped cream into the cream cheese mixture to lighten it. Use a spatula and fold carefully to avoid deflating the air you just whipped in. Then fold in the remaining whipped cream until just combined. This folding technique keeps the filling light and airy, preventing it from becoming dense. Overmixing here can ruin the texture, so be patient and gentle.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Filling looks smooth and fluffy, with no streaks of cream or cream cheese.
Step 5
Fold in the fresh blueberries gently to avoid breaking them and turning the filling purple. The blueberries add bursts of juicy sweetness and a beautiful contrast to the creamy lemon filling. If you want, reserve some blueberries for topping after chilling. Incorporating fruit at this stage ensures every slice has a fruity surprise.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Blueberries are evenly distributed without bursting.
Step 6
Pour the filling over the prepared crust and smooth the top with an offset spatula or the back of a spoon. This step is important for a neat presentation and even setting. Tap the pan gently on the countertop to release any trapped air bubbles, which helps prevent cracks on the surface once chilled.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Top is smooth and even with no visible air pockets.
Step 7
Cover the cheesecake loosely with plastic wrap or foil and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight. Chilling allows the filling to set firmly and the flavors to meld. Don’t rush this step—if you slice it too soon, the cheesecake will be too soft and may collapse. Overnight chilling is best for a clean slice and perfect texture.
⏱ Time: 240
✓ Doneness: Cheesecake feels firm to the touch and slices cleanly without wobbling.
Step 8
Before serving, top the cheesecake with reserved fresh blueberries and a little extra lemon zest for a bright, fresh finish. This adds a pop of color and enhances the lemony aroma right before you dig in. Presentation matters, and these little touches make the dessert look as good as it tastes.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Topping looks fresh, colorful, and evenly scattered.
Step 9
Use a sharp knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry to slice the cheesecake. This technique helps make clean cuts without dragging or tearing the filling. Wipe the knife clean between each slice for neat, photogenic pieces. Serve chilled and enjoy the creamy, tangy, and fruity flavors in every bite.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Slices are clean with smooth edges and no crumbling.

Final Thoughts

Give this no-bake lemon blueberry cheesecake a try and enjoy a bright, creamy dessert that’s as easy as it is delicious. I’d love to hear how yours turns out!
