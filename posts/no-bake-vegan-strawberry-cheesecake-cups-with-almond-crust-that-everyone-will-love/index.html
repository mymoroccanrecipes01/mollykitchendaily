No Bake Vegan Strawberry Cheesecake Cups with Crunchy Almond Crust

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 0 min cook 4 servings

These no bake vegan cheesecake cups are light and creamy with a fresh strawberry twist and a crunchy almond crust. The secret? Soaked cashews blended until silky smooth for that perfect texture. Great for a quick, refreshing dessert on warm days!

Why This Works

This no-bake vegan cheesecake relies on the magic of soaked cashews to create a rich, creamy texture that mimics traditional dairy-based cheesecakes. Soaking softens the cashews, allowing them to blend into a smooth consistency without graininess, which is crucial for a silky mouthfeel. The coconut cream adds an extra layer of richness and fat, making the filling luscious and indulgent without the need for dairy.

The almond crust is an excellent gluten-free, vegan alternative to traditional graham cracker crusts. Ground almonds combined with melted coconut oil bind well to form a crumbly yet cohesive base. The natural oils in almonds provide a buttery flavor and crunchy texture that contrasts beautifully with the creamy filling.

Maple syrup is used as a natural sweetener, balancing the tartness of the lemon juice and fresh strawberries while keeping the dessert refined sugar-free. The lemon juice acts like a natural acid to brighten the flavors and help the filling set, giving it a slight tang reminiscent of classic cheesecake.

The no-bake method preserves the freshness of the strawberries and the delicate flavors of the filling. Chilling rather than baking prevents the cashew mixture from drying out or curdling, ensuring a smooth, creamy dessert that’s light and refreshing, perfect for warm weather.

  • Choose raw, unsalted cashews for the best creamy texture in the filling. Soaking them softens their tough texture and removes any bitterness. Avoid roasted or salted cashews as they alter the flavor and don't blend as smoothly. Soak at least 4 hours or overnight for optimal creaminess.
  • Use thick coconut cream from the top of a chilled can for richness and fat content. It adds smoothness and mimics the dairy creaminess found in traditional cheesecakes. Avoid watered-down coconut milk, which won’t provide the same texture or mouthfeel.
  • Pure maple syrup is the best natural sweetener here, offering a gentle sweetness with subtle caramel notes. It dissolves well and complements the nutty and fruity flavors. Honey or agave syrup can be alternatives but may change the flavor profile slightly.
  • Use raw, whole almonds ground finely for the crust. The natural oils provide a buttery flavor and the coarse grind creates a pleasant crunch. Avoid pre-ground almond flour which might be too fine or contain additives that affect texture.
  • Fresh lemon juice adds brightness and acidity that balances the sweetness and helps the filling set. Bottled lemon juice lacks the fresh acidity and flavor punch needed here, so fresh is best.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup raw cashews, soaked in water for 4 hours
  • 1/4 cup coconut cream, chilled
  • 1/3 cup maple syrup
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup fresh strawberries, chopped
  • 1 cup almonds, finely ground
  • 2 tablespoons coconut oil, melted
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • Pinch of salt

Introduction

I first tried making vegan cheesecake a few years ago, and honestly, it was a bit of a disaster—the texture was grainy, and the flavor was off. But after some experimenting, I discovered that soaking cashews overnight and blending them really well makes all the difference. This recipe is my go-to when I want a fresh, creamy dessert that’s also easy and no bake. What makes it stand out is the crunchy almond crust that adds the perfect contrast to the silky filling. Plus, the fresh strawberries on top brighten it all up with a pop of color and flavor. It’s light, refreshing, and comes together in under 20 minutes, which is perfect for those hot summer days when you don’t want to turn on the oven. I love serving these in little cups for a casual dinner party or just as a quick treat after dinner. The best part? It’s completely plant-based, so everyone can enjoy it. If you’ve been hesitant about vegan desserts, this one might just change your mind!

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by soaking your raw cashews in water for at least 4 hours or overnight. This softens them enough so that when blended, they achieve a creamy, smooth texture without any graininess. Make sure to rinse them well after soaking to remove any bitterness. Skipping this step or under-soaking can result in a gritty filling, so patience here really pays off.

    ⏱ 4 hours soaking✓ Cashews should feel soft and tender when pressed between fingers
  2. Step 2

    While the cashews soak, prepare the almond crust by finely grinding the almonds in a food processor until they resemble coarse flour. Be careful not to over-process into almond butter. Add melted coconut oil and a pinch of salt, then pulse a few more times until the mixture holds together when pressed. This crust will provide a nutty crunch and a buttery texture without baking.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Mixture clumps together when pressed but is not oily or pasty
  3. Step 3

    Spoon the almond crust evenly into the base of your serving cups or jars. Press down firmly with the back of a spoon or your fingers to create a compact layer. This helps the crust hold its shape and provides a sturdy base for the cheesecake filling. Avoid pressing too hard, or it may become too dense and hard to eat.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Crust layer is even and tightly packed without cracking
  4. Step 4

    Drain the soaked cashews and add them to a high-speed blender along with coconut cream, maple syrup, vanilla extract, lemon juice, and a pinch of salt. Blend on high until completely smooth and silky. This might take a couple of minutes, and you may need to scrape down the sides occasionally. The lemon juice brightens the flavor and helps set the filling.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Mixture is creamy, thick, and free of lumps
  5. Step 5

    Gently fold in half of the chopped strawberries into the cashew mixture with a spatula, being careful not to overmix so you keep some fruit texture. This adds a fresh burst of flavor and a pretty pink tint to the filling. If you want a smoother look, puree the strawberries before blending with cashews.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Filling has visible strawberry pieces but remains creamy
  6. Step 6

    Spoon the cheesecake filling over the almond crust in each cup, filling them almost to the top. Smooth the surface with the back of a spoon for a clean look. This layering creates a lovely contrast between crunchy base and creamy topping. Avoid overfilling to prevent spills when chilling.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Filling layer is smooth and evenly distributed
  7. Step 7

    Top each cheesecake cup with the remaining fresh strawberries for garnish. You can slice them or leave them whole for a rustic look. The fresh fruit adds a juicy pop and vibrant color that makes the dessert look irresistible.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Cheesecake cups are visually appealing with fresh strawberry topping
  8. Step 8

    Place the cheesecake cups in the fridge for at least 2 hours to chill and firm up. This step allows the flavors to meld and the filling to set to a perfect sliceable consistency without baking. If you’re short on time, freezing for 1 hour works too but texture may be firmer.

    ⏱ 2 hours chilling✓ Filling is firm to the touch but still creamy
  9. Step 9

    Serve chilled straight from the fridge. These cheesecake cups are best enjoyed within 24 hours for optimal freshness. If you like, drizzle a little extra maple syrup or sprinkle toasted almonds on top before serving for extra texture and sweetness.

    ⏱ Immediate serving✓ Dessert is cool, creamy, and flavorful with crunchy crust

Variations

  • Blueberry Lemon Vegan Cheesecake Cups: Swap strawberries for fresh or frozen blueberries and add a teaspoon of lemon zest to the filling. This gives the cheesecake a bright, tangy twist with the same creamy texture and crunchy almond crust.
    If using frozen berries, thaw and drain excess liquid before folding into the filling to avoid watery texture.
  • Chocolate Vegan Cheesecake Cups: Add 1/4 cup of melted vegan dark chocolate or cocoa powder to the filling for a rich chocolate flavor. Keep the almond crust plain or add a little cocoa powder for a chocolate crust.
    Melt chocolate gently to avoid seizing and blend thoroughly for a smooth, even chocolate filling.
  • Tropical Mango Coconut Cheesecake Cups: Replace strawberries with fresh mango chunks and add shredded coconut to the crust for a tropical vibe. The mango's sweetness complements the creamy cashew filling beautifully.
    Use ripe mangoes for best flavor and blend half into the filling for a smooth texture while folding in the rest for chunks.
  • Nut-Free Vegan Cheesecake Cups: Use sunflower seeds instead of almonds for the crust to make this nut-free. The seeds provide a similar crunch and mild flavor, perfect for those with nut allergies.
    Grind sunflower seeds finely and mix with coconut oil as usual; watch for a slightly different texture but similar binding.
  • Spiced Vegan Cheesecake Cups: Add a teaspoon of ground cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg to the filling for a warm, cozy flavor. This version works great in cooler months or as a holiday treat.
    Mix spices into the filling before blending to ensure even distribution and balanced flavor.

Pro Tips

  • Soak cashews overnight for the creamiest filling. — Soaking cashews softens them thoroughly, allowing the blender to break them down into a silky smooth paste. If you skip or shorten this step, the filling can turn out grainy or chalky, ruining the texture of your cheesecake.
  • Use a high-speed blender for the best results. — A powerful blender ensures the cashews and coconut cream emulsify properly, creating a smooth, lump-free filling. Lower-powered blenders might leave bits of cashew, affecting texture and mouthfeel.
  • Press the almond crust firmly but gently. — Firm pressing helps the crust hold together when eating, but pressing too hard can make it too dense and tough. The right balance creates a crunchy yet tender base that contrasts perfectly with the creamy filling.
  • Chill the cheesecake cups for at least 2 hours. — Chilling firms up the filling so it holds its shape and enhances the flavors. Without adequate chilling, the filling might be too soft and runny, making the dessert hard to serve and enjoy.
  • Add lemon juice to brighten flavors and help set filling. — The acidity from lemon juice cuts through the richness of cashews and coconut cream, balancing sweetness and improving overall flavor. It also helps the filling firm slightly during chilling.
  • Use fresh strawberries for garnish and folding into filling. — Fresh strawberries provide natural sweetness and acidity, plus a lovely color contrast. Using frozen or cooked berries alters texture and flavor, making the dessert less vibrant and fresh.
  • Store leftovers in airtight containers in the fridge. — To maintain freshness and texture, keep cheesecake cups tightly sealed to prevent moisture loss and absorption of fridge odors. This preserves flavor and keeps the crust crunchy longer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the cashew soaking step
    Why: Some might rush the recipe or overlook soaking, thinking blending alone will suffice.
    Fix: Always soak cashews for at least 4 hours or overnight. If pressed for time, soak in very hot water for 1 hour to soften them enough for smooth blending.
  • Over-processing almonds into almond butter
    Why: Without careful pulsing, almonds can quickly turn oily and sticky.
    Fix: Pulse almonds in short bursts, checking texture frequently. Stop once coarse crumbs form to maintain the ideal crust texture.
  • Not pressing the crust firmly enough
    Why: Inexperience or impatience leads to a loose crust that falls apart.
    Fix: Use the back of a spoon or your fingers to press crust firmly and evenly. This compacts the base so it holds together when eating.
  • Blending ingredients too briefly
    Why: Some assume quick blending is enough and skip scraping down sides.
    Fix: Blend cashews and filling ingredients for at least 2-3 minutes, stopping to scrape down the blender to ensure smooth, lump-free filling.
  • Serving without chilling
    Why: Impatience or lack of planning leads to a runny filling.
    Fix: Plan ahead and chill cheesecake cups for a minimum of 2 hours to let the filling firm and flavors meld properly.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: These vegan cheesecake cups can be safely kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered to prevent drying out or contamination. Beyond that, refrigeration is necessary to maintain texture and freshness.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store in airtight containers in the fridge for up to 3 days. The crust may soften slightly over time, but the filling stays creamy. Serve chilled for the best texture and flavor.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cheesecake cups for up to 1 month. Wrap tightly with plastic wrap and place in freezer-safe containers to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before serving to restore texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: No reheating needed—best served chilled. If slightly too firm after refrigeration, let sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes before eating to soften.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the crust and filling separately 1-2 days in advance. Store crust in the fridge and filling in an airtight container. Assemble and chill the cups the day you plan to serve for maximum freshness.

FAQ

Can I use other nuts instead of cashews for the filling?
Cashews are ideal because they blend into a creamy, smooth texture without a strong flavor. Other nuts like almonds or walnuts don’t soften as well and can result in a grainy or bitter filling. If you have a nut allergy, try soaked sunflower seeds but expect a slightly different taste and texture.
Do I have to soak the cashews overnight?
While overnight soaking is best for softness and creaminess, if you’re short on time, soaking in very hot water for 1 hour works as a quicker alternative. Avoid skipping soaking entirely, as it will affect the texture and blending.
Can I make this cheesecake gluten-free?
Yes! This recipe is naturally gluten-free since the crust uses ground almonds instead of graham crackers or flour. Just make sure all your ingredients are certified gluten-free if you have sensitivities.
How long do these cheesecake cups keep in the fridge?
They stay fresh and delicious for up to 3 days refrigerated in airtight containers. The crust may soften a bit over time, but the filling maintains its creamy texture. Always keep covered to avoid absorbing fridge odors.
Can I freeze the cheesecake cups?
Absolutely! Freeze them tightly wrapped in plastic wrap and placed in freezer containers for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge before serving. The texture remains close to fresh, though the crust might be slightly softer.
What can I use instead of maple syrup?
You can substitute agave syrup or brown rice syrup for a similar sweetness and consistency. Honey works too if you’re not strictly vegan but may change the flavor slightly. Avoid granulated sugar as it won’t dissolve well in the filling.
Is it possible to bake this cheesecake?
This recipe is designed as a no-bake dessert to keep it fresh and creamy. Baking would change the texture and might cause the cashew filling to firm up too much or dry out. If you want a baked vegan cheesecake, look for recipes specifically designed for that method.
Can I use frozen strawberries in the filling?
You can, but thaw and drain them well to avoid excess moisture that can make the filling watery. Fresh strawberries are preferred for the best texture and flavor, especially for garnishing.
