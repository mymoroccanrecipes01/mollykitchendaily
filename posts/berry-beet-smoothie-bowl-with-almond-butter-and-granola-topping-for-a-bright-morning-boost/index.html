Bright Beet and Berry Smoothie Bowl with Almond Butter Swirl

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 0 min cook 2 servings

This smoothie bowl mixes earthy beets with juicy berries and creamy almond butter for a fresh, energizing breakfast. I love topping it with crunchy granola for a satisfying texture. Perfect for a colorful morning boost!

Why This Works

This smoothie bowl works because it balances flavors and textures through careful ingredient choices and preparation techniques. Beets provide an earthy sweetness and vibrant color, but raw beets can be tough and bitter. Cooking them softens their fibers and converts some starches to sugars, making them naturally sweeter and easier to blend into a creamy base. This step is crucial for a smooth texture and pleasant flavor.

The frozen berries add tartness and natural antioxidants, while the banana contributes creaminess and natural sugars that round out the flavor profile. Using frozen fruit also thickens the smoothie without needing ice, maintaining a rich texture.

Almond butter is the secret ingredient that adds healthy fats, protein, and a luscious creaminess that contrasts the fruit’s acidity. Swirling it on top not only enhances flavor but also creates an appealing visual effect that invites you to dig in. The crunchy granola topping provides textural contrast, preventing the bowl from feeling one-dimensional. This combination of creamy, crunchy, sweet, and earthy flavors keeps every bite interesting and satisfying.

Temperature and consistency are also important—keeping the smoothie thick enough to hold toppings but not too stiff ensures a perfect balance. Altogether, these elements create a nourishing, colorful breakfast that’s as delicious as it is good for you.

  • Choose small to medium fresh beets that are firm with smooth skin. Cooking (roasting or boiling) breaks down tough fibers and converts starches to sugars, making the beet sweeter and easier to blend. Peeled cooked beets blend smoothly, avoiding gritty texture. Raw beets can overpower the flavor and create an unpleasant texture, so cooking is essential here.
  • Frozen berries provide natural tartness and antioxidants. They also help thicken the smoothie without diluting flavor like ice would. Choose unsweetened frozen berries for the best balance. Fresh berries can be used but the smoothie may be thinner and less cold.
  • Ripe bananas add natural sweetness and creaminess, rounding out the tartness of the berries and earthiness of the beet. They also help thicken the smoothie. Use ripe bananas with brown spots for maximum sweetness and soft texture.
  • Almond butter adds healthy fats, protein, and a rich creamy texture that contrasts the fruit base. Choose natural almond butter without added sugars or oils for the best flavor and nutrition. Warm slightly if needed to drizzle smoothly. It also acts as a visual highlight when swirled on top.
  • Granola topping adds much-needed crunch and texture contrast. Opt for a variety with nuts or seeds for extra nutrition and crunch. Avoid granola with too much added sugar to keep the bowl balanced. Adding granola just before serving prevents sogginess.

Ingredients

  • 1 small cooked beet, peeled and chopped
  • 1 cup frozen mixed berries
  • 1 ripe banana, peeled
  • 2 tablespoons almond butter
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened almond milk
  • 1 tablespoon honey or maple syrup (optional)
  • 1/4 cup crunchy granola for topping

Introduction

I first stumbled upon beet smoothie bowls a few years ago when I wanted something vibrant and healthy to kickstart my mornings. The earthy sweetness of beets combined with berries intrigued me, but the real game-changer was swirling in almond butter for a creamy texture that feels indulgent without the guilt. This version is different from the usual fruit-only bowls because the beet adds a unique depth and beautiful color that makes every spoonful a treat. The trick I learned is to use cooked beets instead of raw—they blend smoother and taste sweeter, which takes the bowl from good to amazing. I usually top mine with crunchy granola for that satisfying bite and a contrast in texture. The final bowl looks gorgeous with the deep pink hue and creamy swirls, and it tastes fresh, slightly sweet, and nourishing all at once. It’s become my go-to for busy mornings or even an afternoon pick-me-up when I need a natural energy boost.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preparing your beet if you haven't already. Cooking the beet until tender (either by roasting or boiling) is key because raw beets can be tough and earthy. Once soft, peel off the skin and chop into small pieces. This step ensures the beet blends smoothly and sweetly into the smoothie without any gritty texture. Avoid skipping cooking as raw beets can overpower the flavor and make the smoothie less creamy.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Beet is tender when easily pierced with a fork and skin peels off smoothly.
  2. Step 2

    Place the cooked beet, frozen berries, banana, almond butter, and almond milk into a blender. Blend on high until completely smooth and creamy. The frozen berries keep the smoothie thick and cold, while the banana adds natural sweetness and creaminess. If you want it sweeter, add honey or maple syrup and blend again briefly. Rushing this step may leave chunks of beet or frozen fruit, so blend thoroughly for the best texture.

    ⏱ 3-5 minutes✓ Smoothie should be thick, vibrant in color, and free of lumps.
  3. Step 3

    Spoon the smoothie mixture into bowls. The consistency should be thick enough to hold toppings without sinking. If it's too runny, add a bit more frozen fruit or a handful of ice and blend again. A thick base is what makes it a true smoothie bowl rather than a drink. This allows you to add crunchy toppings that stay on the surface.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Smoothie holds shape in bowl and supports toppings.
  4. Step 4

    Drizzle almond butter on top of each bowl in a swirl or dollops. This adds richness and a nutty flavor that balances the earthiness of the beet and tartness of the berries. You can warm the almond butter slightly if it’s too thick to drizzle easily. The visual swirl also makes the bowl look more inviting and artisanal.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Even almond butter swirl visible on the surface.
  5. Step 5

    Add a generous sprinkle of crunchy granola over the top. The granola provides a satisfying crunch and contrast to the creamy smoothie. Choose your favorite granola—whether nutty, fruity, or oat-based—to customize the texture and flavor. Don’t skip this step, as it elevates the eating experience from just a smoothie to a fulfilling breakfast bowl.

    ⏱ 1 minute✓ Granola evenly distributed and crunchy.
  6. Step 6

    Optional: Add fresh berries or sliced banana on top for extra color and freshness. This also adds a juicy burst that complements the creamy base. It’s a simple way to make the bowl look more vibrant and appetizing.

    ⏱ 1 minute✓ Fresh fruit arranged attractively on top.
  7. Step 7

    Serve immediately for the best texture and flavor. The smoothie bowl is best enjoyed fresh because the granola will stay crunchy and the smoothie cold. If you wait too long, the granola can get soggy, and the smoothie may separate slightly.

    ⏱ Immediate✓ Smoothie bowl is cold, creamy, and granola crunchy.
  8. Step 8

    If preparing ahead, store the smoothie base in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 24 hours. Stir well before serving and add fresh toppings and granola at serving time to keep them crisp. Avoid prepping the entire bowl too far in advance to maintain texture contrast.

    ⏱ N/A✓ Smoothie base is well mixed and chilled.
  9. Step 9

    Experiment by adding a spoonful of chia seeds or flaxseeds into the blend for extra fiber and omega-3s. These add nutritional benefits and a slight thickness, but be sure to let the mixture sit a few minutes if you add them so they soften a bit. This is a great way to personalize your bowl.

    ⏱ Optional, 5 minutes soak✓ Smoothie slightly thickened if seeds added.

Variations

  • Tropical Beet Smoothie Bowl: Swap berries for frozen mango and pineapple for a tropical twist. Add a squeeze of lime juice for brightness. The texture will be similarly creamy but with a tangy, exotic flavor profile.
    Because tropical fruits are softer, use less almond milk to maintain thickness.
  • Green Beet Smoothie Bowl: Add a handful of fresh spinach or kale along with the beet and berries for extra greens. This variation boosts nutrients but may require a bit more banana or honey to balance the earthiness.
    Blend greens thoroughly to avoid leafy chunks and maintain smooth texture.
  • Chocolate Beet Smoothie Bowl: Add a tablespoon of cocoa powder and a splash of vanilla extract for a chocolatey version. The cocoa pairs surprisingly well with beet’s earthiness and almond butter’s nuttiness.
    Add cocoa powder gradually and taste to avoid bitterness.
  • Protein-Packed Beet Smoothie Bowl: Include a scoop of your favorite protein powder for a post-workout boost. This makes the bowl more filling and supports muscle recovery while keeping it delicious.
    Choose unflavored or vanilla protein to avoid overpowering the beet flavor.
  • Spiced Beet Smoothie Bowl: Add a pinch of cinnamon and nutmeg for warming spice notes. This is perfect for cooler months and adds a cozy flavor dimension to the bowl.
    Start with small amounts of spices to avoid overwhelming the fresh fruit flavors.

Pro Tips

  • Use cooked beets instead of raw for a sweeter, smoother texture. — Cooking breaks down the tough fibers in beets and converts starches into sugars, making them sweeter and easier to blend. Raw beets can be gritty and bitter, which affects the smoothie’s flavor and texture negatively.
  • Freeze your banana beforehand to keep the smoothie thick and cold. — Frozen banana adds creaminess and chill without needing ice, which can water down the flavor. This helps maintain a rich texture and vibrant taste.
  • Drizzle almond butter warm for an even swirl effect. — Warming almond butter slightly makes it more fluid and easier to swirl attractively on top, improving both taste and presentation.
  • Add granola just before serving to keep it crunchy. — If granola sits too long on the smoothie, it absorbs moisture and becomes soggy, losing its textural contrast. Add it last minute for the best crunch.
  • Blend frozen berries and banana first to create a thick base before adding beet. — This ensures a smooth, creamy texture by breaking down the frozen fruit before incorporating the beet, which can be denser.
  • Use unsweetened almond milk to control sweetness. — Using unsweetened milk keeps the natural flavors balanced and avoids an overly sweet smoothie, allowing the beet and berries to shine.
  • Experiment with toppings like chia seeds or sliced fresh fruit for variety. — Adding different textures and flavors keeps the bowl interesting and adds nutritional benefits like omega-3s and extra fiber.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using raw beets without cooking
    Why: Many assume raw beets can be blended directly since they’re vegetables, but they’re tough and earthy raw.
    Fix: Always cook beets fully before blending to ensure sweetness and smooth texture. Roasting or boiling until fork-tender works well.
  • Blending too briefly, leaving chunks
    Why: Impatience or low-powered blenders can cause under-blending, resulting in gritty or lumpy smoothie.
    Fix: Blend until completely smooth, scraping down sides if needed. Use a high-speed blender for best results.
  • Adding granola too early
    Why: Some add granola before serving, causing it to absorb moisture and become soggy.
    Fix: Add granola only right before eating to keep it crunchy and texturally pleasing.
  • Using overly sweetened almond butter
    Why: Pre-sweetened nut butters can make the smoothie too sweet and mask other flavors.
    Fix: Choose natural almond butter without added sugars to maintain balanced flavors.
  • Not adjusting thickness
    Why: People sometimes blend with too much liquid, resulting in a runny smoothie bowl.
    Fix: Use frozen fruit and add almond milk gradually to achieve a thick consistency that holds toppings well.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Keep the smoothie bowl at room temperature for no more than 1 hour. Cover with plastic wrap or a lid to prevent contamination and drying out, but it’s best enjoyed fresh for texture and flavor.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store the smoothie base in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 24 hours. Add toppings just before serving to keep them crisp. The smoothie may thicken when chilled; stir well before eating.
  • 🧊 Freezer: You can freeze the smoothie base in a sealed container for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge and stir well before serving. Avoid freezing granola or fresh toppings as they lose texture.
  • ♨️ Reheating: This smoothie bowl is meant to be eaten cold or at room temperature. If needed, stir gently to loosen the texture after refrigeration. Avoid heating as it changes flavor and texture.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Prepare the smoothie base 1 day ahead and store chilled. Portion out fresh granola and toppings separately to add at serving time for best texture contrast.

FAQ

Can I use raw beets instead of cooked in this smoothie bowl?
Raw beets are very firm and have a strong earthy flavor that can be overpowering. They also don’t blend as smoothly, resulting in a gritty texture. Cooking the beets softens them, brings out their natural sweetness, and ensures a creamy, enjoyable smoothie. If you want to use raw beets, peel and grate them finely, but expect a different texture and flavor.
What can I substitute for almond butter if I’m allergic to nuts?
Sunflower seed butter or tahini are great nut-free alternatives that provide creaminess and a rich flavor. They won’t taste exactly the same but will still add healthy fats and protein. Adjust the amount to taste, as some alternatives can be more intense in flavor.
How do I keep the granola crunchy if I want to pack this smoothie bowl for later?
Store the granola separately in an airtight container and add it just before eating. If mixed in too early, the granola absorbs moisture and becomes soggy, losing its crunch and texture contrast.
Can I make this smoothie bowl vegan?
Yes! Use maple syrup or agave instead of honey, and choose a plant-based almond milk to keep it fully vegan. All other ingredients are naturally vegan-friendly.
What blender works best for this recipe?
A high-speed blender like a Vitamix or Blendtec works best to achieve a smooth, creamy texture. If using a standard blender, blend longer and scrape down the sides to avoid chunks, especially from the beet.
Can I use fresh berries instead of frozen?
Fresh berries work but the smoothie will be less thick and not as cold. You may want to add ice cubes or freeze the banana beforehand to maintain a thick, chilled texture.
How long will leftovers keep?
The smoothie base keeps well in the fridge for about 24 hours. However, the granola and fresh toppings should be added fresh to avoid sogginess. Stir the base before serving if it separates.
Is this smoothie bowl suitable for kids?
Absolutely! The natural sweetness from banana and berries makes it kid-friendly. The bright color is fun and inviting. Just watch portion sizes and avoid added sweeteners if serving young children.
