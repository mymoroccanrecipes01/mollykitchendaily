Easy Moist Zucchini Bread with a Touch of Cinnamon and Nutmeg

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 15, 2026
15 min Prep Time
45 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This zucchini bread is my go-to for a quick, moist treat that feels homemade. Grating fresh zucchini adds natural sweetness and keeps it tender. Great to enjoy warm with morning coffee or as an afternoon snack!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this zucchini bread recipe years ago when I had a surplus of zucchini from my garden and wanted something quick and satisfying to bake. I’ve tried countless versions, but this one stands out because it’s incredibly moist without being overly sweet, thanks to the fresh zucchini and balanced spices. What really makes it different is squeezing out just enough moisture from the zucchini to keep the bread tender but not soggy. I also love that it uses pantry staples, so it’s easy to pull together on a whim. The warm notes of cinnamon and nutmeg add a cozy touch that feels just right for breakfast or an afternoon pick-me-up. Whenever I bake this, the smell fills the kitchen and my family always asks for seconds. I think the key is mixing gently after adding the dry ingredients to avoid overworking the batter, which keeps the crumb soft. Plus, letting the bread cool before slicing helps it set perfectly. It’s simple but feels special—like a little homemade gift you can whip up anytime.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, approximately 8 servings

  • 2 cups grated fresh zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating your fresh zucchini using the large holes of a box grater. Once grated, place the zucchini in a clean kitchen towel or paper towels and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step is crucial because too much liquid will make your bread soggy and dense instead of light and moist. A good squeeze will leave the zucchini damp but not dripping. If you skip this, the texture will suffer, so take your time here.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but no excess water drips when squeezed.
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg. This ensures the spices and leavening agents are evenly distributed so your bread rises uniformly and has consistent flavor. Rushing this step or mixing unevenly can lead to pockets of baking soda or uneven spice bursts.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no clumps or streaks.
Step 3
In another bowl, beat together the granulated sugar, brown sugar, eggs, vegetable oil, and vanilla extract until smooth and well combined. The sugars dissolve into the eggs and oil, creating a smooth base that will help your bread stay moist and tender. If you don’t mix these ingredients well, your bread may have a grainy texture or uneven sweetness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, slightly glossy, and uniform in color.
Step 4
Add the grated zucchini to the wet ingredients and stir gently to combine. The zucchini adds natural moisture and subtle sweetness, so folding it in carefully ensures it’s evenly incorporated without breaking down too much. Overmixing here can make the batter watery and affect the crumb.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is evenly distributed with no large clumps.
Step 5
Pour the dry ingredients into the wet zucchini mixture and fold together gently using a spatula. Mix just until you no longer see dry flour patches. Overmixing activates gluten, which can make your bread tough instead of tender. The goal is a slightly lumpy batter that will bake up moist and soft.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is mostly smooth but slightly lumpy and thick.
Step 6
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with butter or non-stick spray. Pour the batter into the pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. The even surface helps the bread bake uniformly and develop a nice crust. Don’t overfill or the bread may overflow while baking.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level in the pan with no spills.
Step 7
Bake the bread for about 45 minutes, checking at 40 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center. It should come out mostly clean with a few moist crumbs. Baking too long will dry out the bread, while underbaking leaves it gummy in the middle. Oven temperatures vary, so keep an eye after 40 minutes.
⏱ Time: 45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter.
Step 8
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. Cooling in the pan allows the bread to set without sticking, and moving it to a rack prevents sogginess from steam. Cutting too soon can cause it to crumble or feel gummy.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm and slightly pulling away from pan edges.
Step 9
Once completely cool, slice and serve. This bread is delicious on its own or with a pat of butter or cream cheese. Storing it properly keeps it moist for several days. Enjoying a warm slice fresh from the oven is my favorite part – the aroma and tender crumb are unbeatable!
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Slices hold shape and have a moist, tender crumb.

Variations

Walnut Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts to the batter for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor. The nuts pair beautifully with the warm spices and add a satisfying bite. Perfect if you like a bit of contrast in your bread.

Note: Fold in walnuts gently after mixing the batter to avoid overmixing. Baking time may increase by 5 minutes due to added moisture.

Chocolate Chip Zucchini Bread

Fold in 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips for a sweeter, kid-friendly version. The chocolate melts slightly during baking, adding pockets of gooey goodness that complement the moist zucchini base.

Note: Add chips last and fold gently. Watch for slightly faster browning; cover with foil if top darkens too quickly.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Bread

Use a 1-to-1 gluten-free flour blend instead of all-purpose flour. The texture will be a bit denser but still moist and flavorful. This version is great for gluten-sensitive eaters without sacrificing taste.

Note: Add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t include it to help with structure.

Lemon Zucchini Bread

Add zest of one lemon and replace vanilla with lemon extract for a fresh, citrusy twist. The bright flavor cuts through the sweetness and adds a refreshing note to the moist bread.

Note: Be careful not to add too much lemon extract to avoid bitterness; start with 1/2 teaspoon.

Spiced Zucchini Bread with Ginger

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger and a pinch of cloves to the spice mix for a more complex, warming flavor profile. This version feels a bit like autumn in every bite and pairs well with tea.

Note: Adjust spices carefully to avoid overpowering the zucchini flavor; a little goes a long way.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from the zucchini before mixing.
Removing extra water prevents the batter from becoming too wet, which would result in a dense, gummy loaf. The right moisture level keeps the bread tender but firm, allowing it to bake evenly and slice cleanly.
Mix dry ingredients separately before combining with wet ingredients.
Whisking the flour, leaveners, salt, and spices ensures even distribution. This prevents uneven rising and pockets of raw flour or clumpy spices, giving a consistent texture and flavor throughout the bread.
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture gently and just until combined.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, making the bread tough and chewy instead of tender. A light fold keeps the crumb soft and moist, which is key for zucchini bread.
Use both brown and granulated sugar for balanced sweetness and moisture.
Brown sugar adds moisture and a subtle molasses flavor, while granulated sugar helps with structure and sweetness without heaviness. This combo creates a moist, flavorful loaf.
Let the bread cool completely before slicing.
Cooling allows the bread to set and firm up, making it easier to cut without crumbling. Warm bread can be fragile and gummy, so patience here improves presentation and texture.
Use fresh spices for the best aroma and flavor.
Old spices lose potency, which dulls the warm notes in the bread. Fresh cinnamon and nutmeg brighten the flavor and make the bread feel cozy and inviting.
Grease your loaf pan well or line it with parchment paper.
Proper greasing prevents sticking and helps the bread release cleanly from the pan. This makes serving easier and keeps the loaf intact.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be added directly without draining, thinking moisture will bake off.

Fix Use a clean towel to press and squeeze the zucchini firmly until excess water no longer drips. This step is key to avoid soggy, dense bread and ensures the right crumb texture.

Overmixing the batter after adding dry ingredients

Why it happens Some think more mixing will create a smoother batter and better rise.

Fix Mix gently and just until the flour disappears. Overmixing develops gluten, which toughens the bread and ruins the tender crumb.

Skipping baking powder or baking soda

Why it happens Sometimes people omit one leavener thinking it’s redundant.

Fix Use both as the recipe states. Baking soda reacts with acidic ingredients, and baking powder provides extra lift. Omitting either causes dense, flat bread.

Cutting the bread too soon after baking

Why it happens Impatience leads to slicing warm bread that hasn’t set.

Fix Cool the bread completely on a wire rack before slicing to prevent crumbling and gummy texture.

Using old or stale spices

Why it happens Spices lose their aroma over time, and many don’t check freshness.

Fix Use fresh ground cinnamon and nutmeg for best flavor. Replace spices yearly for optimal taste.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the zucchini bread covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel at room temperature for up to 2 days. Avoid airtight containers at room temp to prevent sogginess. It’s best eaten fresh but keeps well for a couple of days.

Refrigerator

Keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The bread will firm up and may dry slightly, so warming it briefly before serving helps restore moisture. It can be served cold but tastes best warmed.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled loaf tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Reheat slices gently in the microwave or oven to refresh texture.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a toaster oven or microwave for 20-30 seconds until warm but not hot. Adding a small pat of butter or a splash of milk before microwaving can help restore moisture. Avoid overheating as it can dry out the bread.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze the zucchini a day ahead, storing it tightly wrapped in the fridge. Mix and bake the bread on the day you want to serve it for best freshness. Alternatively, bake the loaf a day early and keep refrigerated; warm slices before serving.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this bread?
Yes, but thaw and drain it very well before using to remove excess water. Frozen zucchini tends to be more watery, so squeezing out moisture is even more important to prevent soggy bread.
What if I don’t have both baking powder and baking soda?
You can use baking powder alone but the bread may not rise as much and could be denser. Baking soda alone won’t work properly without an acidic ingredient. It’s best to use both for optimal rise and texture.
How do I know when the bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick or skewer into the center. If it comes out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter, the bread is done. If it’s sticky, bake a few more minutes and test again.
Can I substitute oil with butter?
You can melt butter and use it instead of oil, which adds richness and flavor. However, oil keeps the bread moister for longer. Butter may yield a slightly denser texture.
Is it necessary to cool the bread before slicing?
Yes, cooling helps the bread set and prevents it from crumbling or becoming gummy when sliced. If you slice it too soon, the texture may be fragile and sticky.
Can I make muffins instead of a loaf?
Absolutely! Divide the batter into a greased or lined muffin tin and bake at 350°F for 20-25 minutes. Muffins will be slightly quicker to bake and perfect for single servings.
What’s the best way to store leftover bread?
Wrap it tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container in the fridge. This prevents drying out and keeps it fresh for up to 5 days. Freezing is great for longer storage.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Yes, folding in nuts or chocolate chips adds texture and flavor variety. Just add about 1/2 cup to the batter and fold in gently before baking.
