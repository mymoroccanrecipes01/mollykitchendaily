Moist and Creamy Leo Birthday Cake with Pastel and Gold Zodiac Charm

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 6, 2026
30 min Prep Time
40 min Time
12 Servings
Medium Difficulty

This Leo birthday cake is a dreamy mix of soft pastel colors with golden zodiac details. Super moist and creamy, it’s a sweet centerpiece made with love, perfect for celebrating a special girl’s big day.

Introduction

I first baked this Leo birthday cake for my niece’s 9th birthday, and it quickly became a family favorite. The idea was to make something that felt special and personalized, so I played around with pastel shades and added golden zodiac accents to celebrate her Leo pride. What makes this cake stand out is its super moist crumb, thanks to the sour cream and buttermilk combo, and the buttercream frosting that’s whipped just right to be creamy but stable enough for decorating. I’ve tried other zodiac cakes before, but this one has the perfect balance of flavor and festive charm without being over the top. The secret? Taking the time to sift the flour and carefully alternate adding wet and dry ingredients to keep the batter light and airy. When the layers bake up golden and soft, it’s such a satisfying moment. Decorating with edible gold paint adds a little magic that kids and adults both love. The finished cake looks dreamy with soft pastel swirls and a touch of gold that catches the light beautifully. It’s honestly one of those cakes that tastes as good as it looks – perfect for birthdays, zodiac parties, or any celebration that needs a personal touch.

What You'll Need

One 3-layer 9-inch cake, 12 servings

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 2 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 3/4 cups granulated sugar
  • 4 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 tbsp vanilla extract
  • 1 cup whole milk, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • Gold edible paint or gold leaf for decoration
  • Pastel food coloring (pink, lavender, mint)
  • Buttercream frosting ingredients: 1 cup unsalted butter, 4 cups powdered sugar, 1/4 cup heavy cream, 1 tsp vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease three 9-inch round cake pans. Line the bottoms with parchment paper to ensure the cakes release easily after baking. This prep is crucial because a well-greased pan prevents sticking, which can ruin the cake’s smooth edges. Don’t skip the parchment paper—it’s a small step that makes a big difference when you’re ready to decorate.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pans are evenly greased with no dry spots and parchment paper is snugly fitted
Step 2
In a medium bowl, sift together the flour, baking powder, and salt. Sifting aerates the flour and helps prevent lumps, which leads to a lighter, fluffier cake. Mixing dry ingredients separately also ensures even distribution of the leavening agent, so your cake rises uniformly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are light and lump-free
Step 3
In a large mixing bowl, beat the softened butter and sugar together on medium speed until light and fluffy, about 4-5 minutes. This creaming step is key to incorporating air into the batter, which helps the cake rise and gives it a tender crumb. If you rush this or skip it, the cake can turn out dense and heavy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is pale in color and fluffy in texture
Step 4
Add eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition to ensure they fully incorporate. Then mix in the vanilla extract. Adding eggs slowly helps the batter emulsify properly, preventing it from curdling and creating a smooth texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and glossy without streaks
Step 5
Alternately add the sifted dry ingredients and milk with sour cream to the butter mixture, beginning and ending with the dry ingredients. Mix on low speed just until combined after each addition. Overmixing at this stage can develop gluten and make the cake tough, so gentle folding is best.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and slightly thick, no flour pockets remain
Step 6
Divide the batter evenly among the three prepared pans. Tap the pans lightly on the counter to release any large air bubbles, which helps the cake bake evenly without large holes. This little trick prevents uneven textures and ensures a beautiful crumb.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is level and smooth
Step 7
Bake for 35-40 minutes, rotating pans halfway through for even baking. Check doneness by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Avoid opening the oven too often, or the cake may sink in the middle.
⏱ Time: 35-40 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown top and toothpick comes out clean
Step 8
Allow cakes to cool in pans for 10 minutes, then turn out onto wire racks to cool completely. Cooling on racks prevents soggy bottoms and helps the layers set firmly for stacking and decorating.
⏱ Time: 1 hour
✓ Doneness: Cakes are fully cooled and firm to the touch
Step 9
Prepare buttercream by beating softened butter until creamy, then gradually adding powdered sugar, heavy cream, and vanilla. Beat until light and fluffy. Use pastel food coloring to tint portions of the frosting as desired. This frosting whips up airy but holds shape well, perfect for smooth layering and pretty swirls.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Frosting is smooth, spreadable, and holds soft peaks
Step 10
Stack the cooled cake layers with a generous spread of buttercream between each. Cover the entire cake with a crumb coat and chill for 30 minutes. Then apply a final smooth layer of frosting. Use edible gold paint or gold leaf to add zodiac accents carefully with a small brush. This two-step frosting process seals crumbs and creates a polished finish.
⏱ Time: 45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake surface is smooth with clean edges and decorative gold accents

Variations

Chocolate Leo Birthday Cake

Swap 1 cup of flour for unsweetened cocoa powder and add 1/4 cup extra sugar for a rich chocolate twist. Use chocolate buttercream tinted with pastel colors for the same zodiac charm. Expect a denser, fudgier crumb with a deep chocolate flavor.

Note: Cocoa powder absorbs moisture differently, so reduce milk by 2 tablespoons to keep the batter balanced.

Lemon and Lavender Leo Cake

Add 2 tsp lemon zest and 1 tbsp lavender buds to the batter for a fragrant, floral flavor. Use lemon buttercream with a hint of lavender extract for a fresh, elegant cake that’s perfect for spring birthdays.

Note: Infuse lavender buds in warm milk before adding to batter to maximize flavor without texture changes.

Vegan Leo Birthday Cake

Replace eggs with flax eggs (3 tbsp ground flax + 9 tbsp water), use plant-based butter and milk alternatives. The cake remains moist and tender with a slightly nutty flavor, perfect for vegan celebrations.

Note: Flax eggs add moisture and binding but may yield a slightly denser crumb.

Gluten-Free Leo Cake

Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend and add 1/2 tsp xanthan gum if not included. The cake stays soft and moist, though the crumb may be a bit more delicate. Perfect for gluten-sensitive guests.

Note: Allow batter to rest 10 minutes before baking to hydrate gluten-free flour for better texture.

Mini Leo Cupcakes

Divide batter into cupcake liners and bake for 18-22 minutes. Frost with pastel buttercream and add small gold zodiac decals or edible glitter for individual servings that are easy to share.

Note: Adjust baking time and watch closely to avoid drying out cupcakes.

Pro Tips

Always bring eggs and dairy to room temperature before mixing.
Room temperature ingredients blend more smoothly and trap air better during mixing, resulting in a lighter, fluffier cake. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, leading to dense spots.
Don’t overmix your batter once you add the flour.
Overmixing activates gluten, which makes cakes tough and chewy. Mix just until the flour is incorporated to keep the texture tender and soft.
Use an oven thermometer to check your oven’s true temperature.
Ovens often run hotter or cooler than the dial indicates. Accurate temperature ensures cakes bake evenly and prevents under- or over-baking, which affects texture and moisture.
Chill the cake after crumb coating before applying the final frosting.
Chilling firms up the crumb coat, trapping crumbs and creating a smooth base. This step prevents crumbs from mixing into the final frosting layer, giving a clean, professional finish.
Tap the cake pans lightly after pouring batter to release air bubbles.
Trapped air bubbles can cause holes or uneven texture. Tapping helps them rise to the surface and pop, ensuring a uniform crumb.
Use a serrated knife or cake leveler to trim domed tops.
Level layers stack more neatly and reduce the risk of the cake tipping. This creates a stable base for decorating and a polished look.
Apply edible gold paint with a fine, soft brush for best control.
A delicate brush allows you to create precise zodiac details without smudging or wasting the paint. Let the paint dry fully to avoid transfer or smearing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using cold eggs or milk straight from the fridge.

Why it happens Many people forget to take ingredients out ahead of time or rush the process.

Fix Plan ahead and let your eggs and dairy sit at room temperature for at least 30 minutes before baking. This helps the batter mix evenly and traps air better, leading to a lighter cake.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour.

Why it happens In a hurry, it’s tempting to mix quickly to get it done.

Fix Mix just until the flour disappears. Use a spatula to gently fold if needed. This prevents tough gluten from forming and keeps the cake tender.

Skipping the parchment paper in pans.

Why it happens Sometimes overlooked or assumed unnecessary.

Fix Always line pans with parchment after greasing. It ensures the cake releases cleanly without breaking or sticking, preserving the cake’s shape.

Opening the oven door frequently during baking.

Why it happens Curiosity or nervousness about doneness.

Fix Resist opening the door until near the end of baking to maintain a steady oven temperature. Sudden temperature drops can cause the cake to sink.

Applying frosting on a warm cake.

Why it happens Impatience to decorate right away.

Fix Wait until cakes are completely cool before frosting. Warm cake melts the buttercream, causing it to slide off and look uneven.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The cake can safely stay at room temperature for up to 12 hours if covered loosely with a cake dome or plastic wrap to prevent drying out. Avoid leaving it uncovered as the frosting can crust and the cake may stale.

Refrigerator

Store the cake in an airtight container or cake carrier for up to 4 days. The frosting may firm up and the cake could dry slightly, so bring to room temperature 30 minutes before serving for best flavor and texture.

Freezer

Wrap cake layers tightly in plastic wrap and then foil; freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator, then bring to room temperature before frosting or serving to preserve moisture and texture.

Reheating

Avoid microwaving frosted cake as it can melt the frosting. For unfrosted layers, warm gently in a low oven (300°F) wrapped in foil for 10-15 minutes to refresh softness. Add a splash of milk if needed when reheating layers.

Make Ahead

Bake the cake layers 1-2 days ahead and keep wrapped tightly at room temperature or refrigerated. Prepare the buttercream a day ahead and store in an airtight container. Assemble and decorate the day of serving for the freshest look and taste.

FAQ

Can I make this cake dairy-free or vegan?
Yes! You can substitute the butter with vegan margarine or coconut oil, use plant-based milk like almond or oat milk, and replace eggs with flax or chia eggs. Keep in mind the texture might be slightly different—usually a bit denser—but still delicious and moist.
How do I prevent my cake from drying out?
Make sure not to overbake and keep an eye on your oven temperature with a thermometer. Also, store the cake properly covered at room temperature or refrigerated. Adding sour cream helps keep the crumb moist, so don’t skip it.
What’s the best way to get pastel frosting colors?
Use gel or paste food coloring instead of liquid. They provide vibrant color without thinning your buttercream. Add a tiny amount at a time to reach soft pastel shades without overpowering the flavor.
Can I prepare this cake in advance?
Absolutely! You can bake the layers and make the frosting a day or two ahead. Keep layers wrapped tightly and refrigerated or frozen if longer storage is needed. Assemble and decorate just before serving for the freshest look.
Why does my cake sometimes sink in the middle?
This usually happens if the oven temperature is too low, the cake is underbaked, or you open the oven door too early. Make sure to bake fully and avoid disturbing the oven until close to the end.
Can I use cake flour instead of all-purpose flour?
Yes, cake flour will make the cake even softer and more tender due to its lower protein content. You may need to slightly adjust the liquid amounts since cake flour absorbs less moisture.
How do I apply edible gold paint without smudging?
Use a fine, soft brush and apply the paint gently in thin layers. Let each layer dry completely before adding more. Avoid touching the painted areas until fully dry to prevent smearing.
What if I don’t have sour cream?
You can substitute sour cream with plain Greek yogurt or a mixture of milk and lemon juice as a buttermilk substitute. This will still add moisture and acidity to tenderize the crumb.
