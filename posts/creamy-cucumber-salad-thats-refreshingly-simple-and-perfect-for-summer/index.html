Refreshing Creamy Cucumber Salad with Tangy Sour Cream Dressing

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 25, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This creamy cucumber salad is my go-to for hot days. The tangy sour cream dressing adds a fresh zip that makes it way better than your usual salad. Perfect alongside grilled meats or just as a light snack!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this creamy cucumber salad recipe during a summer potluck a few years ago, and it instantly became a favorite in my family. What caught my attention was how simple the ingredients were, yet the flavor was so fresh and satisfying. Unlike heavy mayo-based salads, this one uses sour cream, which keeps it light and tangy, making it perfect for hot days when you want something cool but flavorful. I love adding fresh dill because it gives the salad a subtle herbaceous note that really elevates the dish. One trick I learned is to slice the cucumbers thinly and let them sit with salt for about 10 minutes before mixing with the dressing. This step draws out excess moisture, preventing the salad from becoming watery. The finished salad is crisp, creamy, and slightly tangy, with just a touch of sweetness from the sugar balancing everything out. It's a crowd-pleaser every time I bring it to a barbecue or a casual lunch. Trust me, once you try this, it’ll be your go-to summer side too!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, 4 servings

  • 3 medium cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup sour cream, full fat
  • 2 tablespoons white vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 cup fresh dill, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 small red onion, thinly sliced (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing the cucumbers thoroughly and then slice them as thinly as you can. Using a sharp knife or a mandoline slicer works best here to get uniform slices that will soak up the dressing nicely. Once sliced, sprinkle the cucumbers with salt and toss gently. Let them rest in a colander or bowl for about 10 minutes. This step is crucial because it helps draw out excess water from the cucumbers, which keeps your salad from becoming soggy later on. You’ll notice little droplets of water collecting at the bottom — that’s exactly what you want. Avoid skipping this step to maintain a crisp texture in the final salad.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers should look slightly wilted and release water but remain firm to the touch
Step 2
While the cucumbers are resting, prepare the dressing. In a mixing bowl, combine the sour cream, white vinegar, sugar, black pepper, and chopped fresh dill. Whisk these ingredients together until smooth and creamy. The vinegar adds a nice tang that balances the richness of the sour cream, and the sugar cuts through the acidity for a well-rounded flavor. Make sure the sugar dissolves fully by whisking well. If you prefer, you can add thinly sliced red onions to the dressing for an extra bite. Set this aside while the cucumbers finish draining.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dressing should be smooth and creamy with no grainy sugar texture
Step 3
After the cucumbers have drained, gently pat them dry with paper towels to remove any remaining moisture. This helps the dressing cling better and prevents the salad from becoming watery. Transfer the cucumbers into a large mixing bowl. Pour the prepared sour cream dressing over the cucumbers and toss gently to coat all the slices evenly. Be careful not to break the cucumber slices; use a folding motion rather than stirring vigorously. This ensures every bite gets that creamy, tangy flavor without becoming mushy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers should appear glossy and evenly coated with dressing
Step 4
Taste the salad and adjust the seasoning if needed. Sometimes a pinch more salt or a little extra vinegar can brighten the flavor even more. If the salad tastes too thick or heavy, add a splash of cold water or more vinegar to loosen the dressing slightly. This step allows you to customize the balance of tanginess and creaminess to your liking. Remember, the flavors will meld and mellow as the salad chills, so don’t over-season at this stage.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced taste with mild tang and creamy texture
Step 5
Cover the salad with plastic wrap or transfer it to an airtight container. Refrigerate for at least 30 minutes before serving. This chilling step is key because it lets the cucumbers absorb the flavors from the dressing and dill. The salad tastes best when it's cold and refreshing, especially on warm days. If you’re short on time, 20 minutes will do, but letting it sit longer only improves the flavor.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad is chilled and flavors have melded together
Step 6
Before serving, give the salad a gentle toss to redistribute any dressing that may have settled at the bottom. If the salad looks a bit thick, stir in a teaspoon of cold water to loosen it up. This keeps the texture light and fresh. Serve it in a pretty bowl garnished with a sprig of dill or a few thin cucumber slices for a nice presentation. This salad pairs beautifully with grilled meats, sandwiches, or as a standalone snack.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad looks fresh, glossy, and evenly coated
Step 7
If you want to add a little crunch, sprinkle some toasted sunflower seeds or chopped walnuts on top just before serving. This adds a nice texture contrast to the creamy cucumbers. Alternatively, for a spicy twist, add a pinch of cayenne or some freshly cracked black pepper. These small tweaks can elevate the salad and keep it interesting if you make it often.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Additional toppings evenly distributed and visually appealing
Step 8
For best flavor, consume within 24 hours. The cucumbers will release more water over time, which can dilute the dressing. If storing leftovers, drain any excess liquid before serving again to maintain the texture and flavor. This salad doesn’t hold well past a day or two, so it’s best enjoyed fresh.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Salad remains crisp and creamy without excess liquid
Step 9
Experiment with herbs! While dill is classic, fresh mint or chives work wonderfully too. Adding fresh herbs changes the flavor profile slightly and keeps the salad feeling new. Just chop finely and fold them in at the same time as the dill. This is a fun way to customize based on what you have on hand or your mood.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Herbs evenly mixed and fresh aroma present

Variations

Add Red Onion for a Sharp Bite

Thinly sliced red onions add a spicy crunch that contrasts nicely with the creamy dressing. Toss them in with the cucumbers before salting to mellow their sharpness slightly. This version feels a bit more robust and pairs well with grilled dishes.

Note: Slice onions thinly and soak in cold water briefly if you want to reduce their pungency before mixing.

Mint and Lime Twist

Swap dill for fresh mint and replace vinegar with fresh lime juice for a brighter, more refreshing salad. The mint adds a cool herbal note while lime juice gives a citrusy zing perfect for summer.

Note: Use fresh lime juice just before serving to keep the salad vibrant and fresh.

Greek Yogurt Dressing

For a tangier, protein-packed version, substitute sour cream with Greek yogurt. This makes the salad a bit lighter and adds a pleasant tartness. It’s great if you want a healthier twist without sacrificing creaminess.

Note: Greek yogurt can be thicker; thin it slightly with a splash of water or milk to mimic sour cream’s consistency.

Spicy Kick with Jalapeños

Add finely chopped jalapeños or a dash of cayenne pepper to the dressing for some heat. This spicy twist livens up the creamy salad and pairs well with milder main dishes.

Note: Adjust the amount of spice carefully to avoid overpowering the delicate cucumber flavor.

Add Toasted Nuts for Crunch

Sprinkle toasted walnuts, pecans, or sunflower seeds on top for added texture and nutty flavor. This variation adds richness and interest, making the salad more substantial.

Note: Toast nuts lightly in a dry pan until fragrant to enhance their flavor before adding to the salad.

Pro Tips

Salt the cucumbers and let them rest to draw out moisture.
This step uses osmosis to pull water from the cucumbers, preventing the salad from becoming watery and soggy. It also intensifies the cucumber’s flavor, resulting in a crisper texture and more concentrated taste.
Use a sharp knife or mandoline for thin, even cucumber slices.
Uniform slices soak up the dressing evenly and cook or chill uniformly. Thick or uneven slices can lead to inconsistent texture and flavor, making some bites watery and others too crunchy.
Whisk the dressing ingredients thoroughly before mixing with cucumbers.
Proper whisking dissolves sugar and blends flavors smoothly, ensuring the dressing coats the cucumbers evenly without graininess or separation.
Chill the salad for at least 30 minutes before serving.
Chilling allows the flavors to meld and the cucumbers to absorb the dressing, enhancing the overall taste and texture. Serving immediately results in less flavorful, less cohesive salad.
Add fresh dill last to preserve its aroma and color.
Dill loses its brightness and flavor when mixed too early. Adding it at the end keeps its fresh herbal notes vibrant and visually appealing.
Pat cucumbers dry after salting to remove excess water.
Drying cucumbers prevents the dressing from becoming diluted and helps it cling better, resulting in a creamier, more flavorful salad.
Adjust seasoning after mixing, not before.
Flavors develop as ingredients combine and chill. Tasting and adjusting at the end ensures the salad isn't over- or under-seasoned.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the salting and draining step

Why it happens Many assume cucumbers can be used straight away, but they contain a lot of water that can make the salad watery and bland.

Fix Always salt and let cucumbers sit for 10 minutes, then pat dry to remove excess moisture. This keeps the salad crisp and prevents a watery mess.

Using thick cucumber slices

Why it happens Some cooks slice cucumbers too thickly, thinking it adds crunch, but thick slices don’t absorb dressing well and can feel heavy.

Fix Use a sharp knife or mandoline to slice cucumbers thinly and evenly. This ensures better texture and flavor absorption.

Overdressing the salad

Why it happens Pouring too much dressing can overpower the delicate cucumber flavor and make the salad soggy.

Fix Add dressing gradually and toss gently. You can always add more if needed after tasting.

Not chilling the salad before serving

Why it happens Impatience often leads to serving immediately, resulting in less flavor melding and a less refreshing salad.

Fix Refrigerate for at least 30 minutes so flavors develop and the salad tastes its best.

Using dried dill instead of fresh

Why it happens Convenience or lack of fresh herbs leads some to substitute dried, but it lacks brightness.

Fix Use fresh dill chopped finely for the best aroma and flavor.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cucumber salad should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to the dairy in the dressing. Cover with plastic wrap if serving outdoors and refrigerate promptly after.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 2 days. The salad will soften and release water over time, so drain excess liquid before serving again. Best enjoyed chilled for freshness.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers become mushy and watery upon thawing, ruining the texture of the salad.

Reheating

This salad is served cold and should not be reheated. Simply give it a gentle toss and serve chilled.

Make Ahead

You can slice and salt the cucumbers a day ahead, storing them drained in the fridge. Prepare the dressing separately and combine just before serving to keep the salad fresh and crisp.

FAQ

Can I use Greek yogurt instead of sour cream?
Yes, Greek yogurt is a great substitute and makes the salad a bit lighter and tangier. Use full-fat Greek yogurt and whisk it well. You may want to thin it slightly with a splash of water or milk to match the sour cream’s creaminess.
Why do I need to salt the cucumbers first?
Salting draws out excess moisture from cucumbers via osmosis, preventing the salad from becoming watery and soggy. It also concentrates the flavor and ensures the dressing clings better.
Can I make this salad vegan?
To make it vegan, substitute sour cream with a plant-based yogurt or vegan sour cream alternative. Be aware that this may slightly change the texture and tanginess of the dressing.
How long can I store this salad?
Best consumed within 1 to 2 days when refrigerated. Over time, cucumbers release water which can dilute the dressing and soften the salad’s texture.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Absolutely! Thinly sliced radishes, red onions, or cherry tomatoes can add color and flavor. Just be mindful of water content to avoid sogginess.
What if my salad turns watery?
Drain excess liquid before serving by gently pressing the salad or using a slotted spoon. Next time, make sure to salt and drain cucumbers properly beforehand.
Is fresh dill necessary?
Fresh dill adds brightness and aroma that dried dill can’t match. If unavailable, you can skip it or substitute with fresh mint or chives, but the flavor will differ.
Can I prepare this salad ahead for a party?
Yes, slice and salt cucumbers a day ahead, store drained in the fridge, and prepare dressing separately. Combine just before serving to maintain freshness and texture.
