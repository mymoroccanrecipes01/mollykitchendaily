Chewy Five-Ingredient Chocolate Chip Cookies Ready in Under 30 Minutes

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
10 min prep 15 min cook 12 servings

These chewy chocolate chip cookies are a lifesaver when you want something sweet fast. Just five ingredients, including a bit of brown sugar for that perfect chew, and you're set. Great for an after-dinner treat or a quick snack!

Why This Works

Achieving the perfect chewy chocolate chip cookie is all about balancing moisture, fat, and sugar, while controlling the baking time and temperature. The brown sugar in this recipe plays a crucial role because it contains molasses, which not only adds flavor but also retains moisture, giving the cookies their signature chewiness. Unlike granulated sugar, which encourages crispness, brown sugar keeps the interior soft and tender.

Butter, when creamed with sugar, traps air that helps leaven the cookies slightly and contributes to a tender crumb. Using softened butter rather than melted ensures the dough holds its shape better and prevents excessive spreading. Overmixing the flour activates gluten, which can toughen cookies, so mixing just until combined keeps them soft.

Baking at 350°F strikes a perfect balance, allowing the edges to crisp up while the centers remain soft. Pulling the cookies from the oven when the centers look just set but still soft is key — they continue to cook slightly as they cool, resulting in that perfect chewy texture. Cooling on the baking sheet first helps the cookies set without breaking, and moving them to a wire rack prevents steaming and sogginess.

Finally, the chocolate chips melt and create gooey pockets of sweetness throughout the cookie, enhancing the overall experience. The simplicity of just five ingredients highlights these interactions beautifully, making this recipe both accessible and reliably delicious.

  • Look for packed brown sugar with a rich molasses content to maximize chewiness and flavor. Light or dark brown sugar can be used, but dark will give a deeper molasses taste. Keep it tightly sealed to prevent hardening, and if it does harden, soften it by placing a slice of bread in the container overnight.
  • Use unsalted butter so you can control the salt level precisely. Softened butter is best for creaming, as melted butter will cause the cookies to spread too much. High-quality butter with a good fat content enhances flavor and texture significantly.
  • Standard all-purpose flour provides the right protein level to create structure without toughness. Avoid bread flour, which has too much gluten, or cake flour, which may make the cookies too delicate. Sifting is optional but can help with even mixing.
  • Granulated sugar adds sweetness and helps with spreading during baking. It balances the moisture-retaining brown sugar to create a cookie that's chewy but with a slightly crisp edge. Measuring accurately prevents overly sweet or dry cookies.
  • Choose good-quality semi-sweet chocolate chips that melt well but hold their shape. Higher cocoa content chips offer a richer flavor but can be less sweet. You can substitute with chunks or a mix of chocolates for texture variation.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C). This ensures the oven is hot enough to bake your cookies evenly right from the start. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper or a silicone mat to prevent sticking and make cleanup easier. Having the oven fully preheated is key because if you put the dough in too early, the cookies might spread too much or bake unevenly.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Oven reaches a steady 350°F indicated by the preheat signal
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, cream together the softened butter, brown sugar, and granulated sugar until the mixture is light and fluffy. This usually takes about 3-4 minutes with an electric mixer on medium speed. Creaming incorporates air into the batter, which helps create a tender texture and slightly lifts the cookies. Make sure the butter is softened but not melted; otherwise, the dough can become greasy and spread too much during baking.

    ⏱ 4 minutes✓ Mixture looks pale and fluffy, with no visible lumps of butter
  3. Step 3

    Gradually add the all-purpose flour to the creamed butter and sugars, mixing on low speed to combine. Avoid overmixing here because too much gluten development will make the cookies tough instead of chewy. Mix just until you no longer see dry flour patches. The dough should be soft but hold together well enough to scoop. If you mix too vigorously, the cookies might turn out cakey, which isn’t what we want.

    ⏱ 2-3 minutes✓ Dough comes together smoothly without streaks of flour
  4. Step 4

    Fold in the chocolate chips gently with a spatula, distributing them evenly throughout the dough. This step is important to ensure every cookie has just the right amount of melty chocolate goodness. Be careful not to overwork the dough here as well; you want the chips intact and nicely spread out.

    ⏱ 1-2 minutes✓ Chocolate chips are evenly mixed, no clumps of dough around them
  5. Step 5

    Using a cookie scoop or tablespoon, drop rounded balls of dough onto the prepared baking sheet, spacing them about 2 inches apart. Proper spacing prevents the cookies from merging into one giant cookie while baking. If the dough feels too sticky, chill it for 10-15 minutes to make scooping easier and to help control spreading in the oven.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Uniformly sized mounds spaced evenly on the baking sheet
  6. Step 6

    Bake the cookies in the preheated oven for 12-15 minutes. Keep an eye on them starting around the 12-minute mark. The edges should be light golden brown, but the centers will still look slightly soft. This underbaking is what keeps the cookies chewy and moist inside once they cool. If you bake them until fully firm, they’ll be more crisp and less chewy.

    ⏱ 12-15 minutes✓ Edges are golden and set, centers look just set but still soft
  7. Step 7

    Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the cookies cool on it for about 5 minutes. This resting time allows the cookies to firm up so they don’t fall apart when you transfer them. Moving cookies too soon is a common mistake that can cause them to break or stick to the sheet.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Cookies hold their shape but are still warm to the touch
  8. Step 8

    Transfer the cookies to a wire rack to cool completely. This step helps air circulate around the cookies, preventing sogginess. If you skip this and leave cookies on the baking sheet, they can steam and lose that chewy texture you’re aiming for.

    ⏱ 15 minutes✓ Cookies are fully cool to the touch and have a tender but firm texture
  9. Step 9

    Enjoy your cookies with a cold glass of milk or your favorite hot beverage. Store any leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature to keep them fresh. The next day, if they’ve firmed up too much, a quick 10-second microwave zap softens them right up. Sharing these with friends and family always makes me smile!

    ⏱ Immediate serving or storage✓ Cookies remain chewy and flavorful after cooling

Variations

  • Add chopped nuts for crunch: Stir in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans along with chocolate chips for a delightful crunch and nutty flavor that complements the sweet chewiness.
    Nuts add texture and slightly slow spreading; no baking time adjustment needed.
  • Use dark chocolate chunks instead of chips: Swap semi-sweet chips for chopped dark chocolate bars for richer, more intense chocolate pockets with varied melting textures.
    Chunks melt unevenly, giving a rustic look; bake as usual.
  • Make oatmeal chocolate chip cookies: Replace 1/2 cup of flour with rolled oats for heartier cookies with chewy oats and classic chocolate chip flavor.
    Oats absorb moisture differently; dough may be drier, so watch baking time closely.
  • Add a pinch of cinnamon for warmth: Mix 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon into the dry ingredients to add a subtle warm spice that pairs beautifully with chocolate.
    Cinnamon won’t affect baking time; enhances flavor complexity.
  • Make them vegan with coconut oil and flax eggs: Replace butter with solid coconut oil and eggs with flaxseed meal mixed with water for a plant-based chewy cookie alternative.
    Texture might be slightly different; chill dough well before baking.

Pro Tips

  • Use room temperature butter for proper creaming. — Butter that’s too cold won’t cream well with sugar, resulting in dense cookies. If it’s melted, the dough will be too loose and spread too much. Properly softened butter traps air when creamed, creating a tender, light texture.
  • Don’t overmix the dough after adding flour. — Overmixing develops gluten, which makes cookies tough and cakey instead of chewy. Mix just until the flour disappears to keep the texture soft and tender.
  • Chill the dough if it feels too sticky before baking. — Chilling firms up the dough, making it easier to scoop and controlling cookie spread in the oven. This results in thicker, chewier cookies rather than thin, crispy ones.
  • Pull cookies out when centers still look slightly underbaked. — Cookies continue cooking on the hot baking sheet after removal. Taking them out early ensures a soft, chewy center rather than a dry, crunchy cookie.
  • Use parchment paper or silicone mats for even baking and easy cleanup. — These liners prevent sticking and promote even heat distribution, helping cookies bake uniformly without burning the bottoms.
  • Space cookies about 2 inches apart on the baking sheet. — Proper spacing prevents cookies from merging as they spread, maintaining individual shapes and ensuring even baking.
  • Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature. — Exposure to air causes cookies to harden and lose chewiness. Airtight storage preserves moisture and texture for days.
  • Add a pinch of salt if using salted butter. — Salt balances sweetness and enhances flavor. If your butter is salted, reduce or omit extra salt in the recipe to avoid over-salting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using melted butter instead of softened butter
    Why: Many assume melted butter speeds up mixing, but it changes dough consistency.
    Fix: Always soften butter to room temperature, not melt it. This helps trap air during creaming, producing cookies that rise properly and don’t spread too thin.
  • Overbaking the cookies
    Why: It’s tempting to bake until fully firm, but cookies continue cooking after removal.
    Fix: Remove cookies when edges are set and centers still look slightly soft. This preserves chewiness once cooled.
  • Overmixing after flour addition
    Why: Trying to fully incorporate flour quickly can lead to tough cookies.
    Fix: Mix gently and only until flour disappears to avoid gluten development that toughens cookies.
  • Skipping chilling the dough when sticky
    Why: Sticky dough is hard to handle and can spread too much in the oven.
    Fix: Chill dough for 10-15 minutes if sticky. This firms it up for easier scooping and better cookie shape.
  • Not spacing cookies properly on the baking sheet
    Why: Crowding leads to cookies merging into one big cookie.
    Fix: Leave about 2 inches between dough balls to allow room for spreading and even baking.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Cookies keep well at room temperature for up to 3 days when stored in an airtight container. Avoid leaving them uncovered as they will dry out and lose chewiness quickly.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store cookies in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 7 days. They may firm up and lose some softness, so warming them slightly before serving helps restore chewiness.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cookies in a sealed container or freezer bag for up to 3 months. Separate layers with parchment paper to prevent sticking. Thaw at room temperature for 30 minutes before serving.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Warm cookies in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5 minutes to revive softness. Avoid overheating as it can dry them out.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the dough 1-2 days ahead and store it tightly wrapped in the fridge. Scoop and bake fresh when ready for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I substitute brown sugar with white sugar?
You can use all white sugar, but the cookies will be less chewy and have a lighter flavor. Brown sugar adds moisture and a slight caramel note, which is key for chewiness. If substituting, expect a crisper cookie.
What if I don’t have semi-sweet chocolate chips?
You can use milk or dark chocolate chips or even chopped chocolate bars. Each will change the sweetness and richness slightly, but all work well. Just be sure to use good-quality chocolate for best results.
Why did my cookies spread too much?
This usually happens if the butter was too warm or melted when mixed, or if the dough wasn’t chilled when sticky. Make sure to use softened, not melted butter, and chill the dough if needed to control spreading.
How can I make these cookies gluten-free?
Substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free flour blend that includes xanthan gum. The texture might be slightly different, but you’ll still get chewy, tasty cookies.
Can I double the recipe?
Yes, doubling works great. Just mix ingredients thoroughly and bake in batches. Make sure not to overcrowd the baking sheet to maintain even baking.
Why are my cookies cakey instead of chewy?
Overmixing flour or baking too long can create cakey cookies. Mix just until combined and pull cookies from the oven when centers are still a bit soft to keep chewiness.
Is it okay to use salted butter?
Yes, but reduce or omit any added salt in the recipe. Salted butter can vary in salt content, so taste the dough if possible to avoid over-salting.
How should I store leftover cookies?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days to maintain chewiness. For longer storage, freeze as described in the storage section.
