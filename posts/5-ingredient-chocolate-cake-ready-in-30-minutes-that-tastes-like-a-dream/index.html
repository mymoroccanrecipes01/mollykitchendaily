Easy 5-Ingredient Chocolate Cake That’s Ready in Just 30 Minutes

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on April 11, 2026
10 min Prep Time
20 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This chocolate cake is a total lifesaver when you want something sweet but don’t want to fuss. Just five simple ingredients, a quick mix, and it’s ready in half an hour. Great for last-minute celebrations or a cozy treat!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this chocolate cake recipe on a hectic weeknight when I desperately wanted something sweet but didn’t want to spend ages baking. Honestly, it was a total game-changer. What makes this cake stand out is how few ingredients it uses — just five pantry staples — and how quickly it comes together. No creaming butter or separating eggs, just a simple mix and pop it in the oven. The trick I learned was to sift the cocoa powder and flour together; it really helps the cake stay light and fluffy despite the minimal ingredients. Also, using oil instead of butter keeps it super moist and forgiving if you overbake a bit. The finished cake is rich, chocolatey, and soft with a tender crumb — perfect for when you want a cozy dessert without fuss. I’ve made it for last-minute birthday celebrations and casual weekend treats, and it never disappoints. Plus, it pairs beautifully with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream when you want to dress it up. You’ll love how easy it is to whip up this chocolate fix anytime!

What You'll Need

One 8-inch cake, approximately 8 servings

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease an 8-inch round cake pan with a little oil or butter. This step ensures your cake won’t stick and will come out cleanly after baking. Make sure the oven is fully heated before baking to help the cake rise evenly and develop a tender crumb. If your oven runs hot or cold, consider using an oven thermometer to get the temperature just right.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven fully preheated to 350°F, greased pan ready
Step 2
In a large bowl, sift together the flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt. Sifting breaks up any lumps and aerates the dry ingredients, which helps the cake rise and keeps the texture light. It also ensures your cocoa powder is evenly distributed so every bite has consistent chocolate flavor. If you skip sifting, you might get clumps or dense spots in the cake.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are smooth, lump-free, and evenly mixed
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together the sugar, vegetable oil, water, and vanilla extract until well combined. The water and oil mixture keeps this cake moist without butter, and whisking helps dissolve the sugar slightly, improving texture. Don’t skip whisking here; it ensures the wet ingredients blend smoothly with the dry ones later on.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are fully combined, no visible sugar crystals
Step 4
Slowly pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients while stirring gently with a spatula or wooden spoon. Mix just until combined — overmixing can develop gluten in the flour, making the cake tough instead of tender. You want a smooth batter with no streaks of flour but don’t worry if a few small lumps remain.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no dry flour patches, slightly thick
Step 5
Pour the batter into your prepared cake pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the cake bake evenly and develop a nice flat surface for frosting or serving as-is. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles trapped inside.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread in pan with a smooth surface
Step 6
Bake in the preheated oven for 18-22 minutes. Start checking around 18 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center — it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Baking too long can dry out the cake, so keep an eye on it especially if your oven tends to run hot.
⏱ Time: 18-22 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick test passes; cake springs back lightly when pressed
Step 7
Remove the cake from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes. This resting period allows the cake to firm up slightly, making it easier to remove from the pan without breaking. Avoid cooling it completely in the pan, or it might stick.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake edges slightly pulling away from pan, warm but firm
Step 8
Run a knife around the edges of the cake pan to loosen it, then carefully invert the cake onto a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling on a rack prevents sogginess by allowing air to circulate underneath. If you leave it in the pan, steam can make the bottom mushy.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake releases cleanly and cools evenly on rack
Step 9
Once cooled, slice and serve as is or with your favorite topping — whipped cream, fresh berries, or ice cream. Because this cake is simple and moist, it pairs well with almost anything! If you want to store it, wrap tightly in plastic wrap to maintain moisture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake slices cleanly, moist crumb, rich chocolate aroma

Variations

Add Espresso for a Mocha Twist

Mix a teaspoon of instant espresso powder into the wet ingredients to deepen the chocolate flavor with a subtle coffee note. It brightens the chocolate and adds complexity without bitterness.

Note: Espresso powder dissolves quickly in the wet mix, so no adjustment in baking time is needed.

Make It Gluten-Free

Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend instead of regular flour. This keeps the cake accessible for gluten-sensitive eaters while maintaining a similar texture and flavor.

Note: Gluten-free flours vary, so add a teaspoon of xanthan gum if not included in your blend to improve texture and structure.

Swap Water for Milk

Replace the water with whole milk for a richer, creamier cake. This adds a subtle dairy flavor and extra tenderness but slightly increases calories.

Note: Milk adds fat and proteins that can brown faster, so watch baking time closely to avoid overbaking.

Top with Chocolate Ganache

After cooling, pour a simple ganache made from chocolate and cream over the cake for a glossy, decadent finish. It elevates the simple cake into a special occasion dessert.

Note: Let ganache cool slightly before pouring to avoid melting the cake surface.

Add Fresh Berries

Fold fresh raspberries or blueberries into the batter before baking for bursts of juicy fruit that complement the chocolate. It adds freshness and a pop of color.

Note: Coat berries lightly in flour before folding in to prevent sinking to the bottom.

Pro Tips

Always sift your dry ingredients together.
Sifting breaks up clumps and aerates the flour and cocoa powder, which helps the cake achieve a light texture. Clumps of cocoa powder can cause bitter spots and uneven baking, so sifting ensures a smooth, uniform batter.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which leads to a tougher, denser cake. Mixing just until the ingredients come together keeps the crumb tender and soft, which is key for this simple chocolate cake.
Use fresh baking powder for proper rise.
Leavening agents lose potency over time. Using fresh baking powder ensures your cake rises properly and has a light, airy texture. Old baking powder can cause a flat, dense cake.
Preheat your oven fully before baking.
Baking in a properly preheated oven allows the cake to rise evenly and sets the structure quickly. If the oven is not hot enough, the cake might bake unevenly or become dense.
Let the cake cool slightly before removing from the pan.
Cooling for 10 minutes helps the cake firm up and prevents it from breaking apart when you remove it. Removing it too soon can cause it to crumble or stick to the pan.
Tap the cake pan gently before baking.
Tapping releases large air bubbles trapped in the batter, preventing holes or uneven texture in the finished cake. It helps create a uniform crumb.
Use a neutral oil like canola or vegetable oil.
Neutral oils don’t overpower the chocolate flavor and keep the cake moist. Using flavored oils like olive oil can impart an unwanted taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the sifting of dry ingredients

Why it happens Many cooks assume sifting is optional or time-consuming, so they mix dry ingredients directly, leading to clumps.

Fix Always sift your flour and cocoa powder together to break up lumps and aerate the mixture. This prevents bitter pockets and helps the cake rise evenly, resulting in a smooth batter and tender crumb.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Eager to combine ingredients quickly, some mix vigorously or too long, thinking it ensures better blending.

Fix Mix just until the flour disappears and the batter is uniform. Overmixing develops gluten, causing a tough, dense cake. Gentle folding keeps the texture soft and delicate.

Using expired baking powder

Why it happens Baking powder can be forgotten in the back of the pantry and lose its potency without cooks realizing.

Fix Test your baking powder by mixing a teaspoon in warm water; it should bubble vigorously. Replace if no reaction occurs to ensure your cake rises properly.

Not preheating the oven

Why it happens Sometimes cooks start baking before the oven reaches temperature to save time.

Fix Always preheat the oven fully to 350°F (175°C). This ensures even baking and proper rise. An unheated oven can cause uneven texture and dense cake.

Removing cake from pan too soon

Why it happens Impatience or fear of overcooking leads some to take the cake out immediately.

Fix Let the cake cool in the pan for at least 10 minutes to firm up. This prevents it from breaking apart or sticking, making removal clean and easy.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cake can be safely kept at room temperature for up to 2 days if covered tightly with plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. This keeps it moist and prevents it from drying out or absorbing odors.

Refrigerator

Store the cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The cake may become slightly denser and firmer when cold but can be served chilled or warmed briefly before serving to restore softness.

Freezer

Wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap and then in foil or a freezer bag to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and bring to room temperature before serving for best texture.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk or cream before warming helps keep the cake moist. Avoid overheating which can dry it out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter 1 day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bring to room temperature before baking. Alternatively, bake the cake and store it wrapped; add toppings or frostings just before serving to keep it fresh.

FAQ

Can I substitute the vegetable oil with butter?
You can use melted butter instead of vegetable oil, but keep in mind that butter solidifies when cooled, which can make the cake less moist and alter the texture slightly. Also, melted butter adds a richer flavor but requires careful mixing to avoid a dense cake.
Why is my cake dense and not fluffy?
Dense cake usually results from overmixing the batter, expired baking powder, or incorrect oven temperature. Mix just until ingredients combine, check your leavening agent’s freshness, and ensure your oven is properly preheated to get a light, airy texture.
Can I make this cake vegan?
Yes! This recipe is already egg-free. Just make sure your sugar is vegan-friendly and use a plant-based oil. The cake will come out moist and chocolatey without eggs or dairy.
How do I know when the cake is done?
Insert a toothpick into the center around 18 minutes. It should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. The cake should also spring back lightly when pressed gently on top.
Can I double the recipe?
You can double the ingredients and bake in a larger pan or two pans. Just watch the baking time closely as larger cakes may take longer to bake through. Use the toothpick test to check doneness.
What if I don’t have an 8-inch pan?
You can use a 9-inch pan, but the cake will be thinner and bake faster—start checking for doneness a few minutes earlier. For smaller pans, increase baking time slightly but monitor closely.
How should I store leftovers?
Store leftover cake in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or in the fridge for up to 5 days. Wrap well to keep it moist and prevent it from drying out.
Can I add frosting or glaze?
Absolutely! This cake pairs wonderfully with chocolate ganache, whipped cream, or a simple powdered sugar glaze. Just make sure the cake is completely cooled before adding toppings.
