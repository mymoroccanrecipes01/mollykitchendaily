Creamy Spinach and Mushroom Pasta That’s Cozy and Quick for Weeknights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 27, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This creamy spinach and mushroom pasta is my go-to cozy dinner when I want something indulgent but easy. The garlicky cream sauce and fresh spinach make it feel special. Perfect for busy weeknights or when you need a comforting meal fast!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 12 ounces dried pasta (fettuccine or penne)
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 8 ounces cremini mushrooms, sliced
  • 5 cups fresh spinach, roughly chopped
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)

How To Do It

Step 1
Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add the pasta and cook according to package instructions until al dente, usually about 9-11 minutes. It’s important not to overcook the pasta because it will continue to cook slightly when you toss it in the sauce later. Once cooked, drain the pasta, reserving about a cup of the pasta water. This starchy water will help thin the sauce if needed and make it cling better to the noodles.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pasta should be tender but still have a slight bite—no mushiness.
Step 2
While the pasta cooks, heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the sliced mushrooms in a single layer, making sure not to overcrowd the pan. Sauté without stirring for about 3-4 minutes to let them brown, then stir and cook for another 3-4 minutes until golden and caramelized. Browning the mushrooms develops deep, savory flavors through the Maillard reaction, giving the dish its rich taste. Avoid stirring too much early on, or they’ll steam instead of brown.
⏱ Time: 8 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mushrooms should be golden brown and slightly crisp at the edges.
Step 3
Lower the heat to medium and add the minced garlic to the skillet with mushrooms. Sauté for about 1 minute until fragrant but not browned, as burnt garlic tastes bitter. This step infuses the oil with garlic flavor, which will carry through the sauce. Stir constantly here to avoid burning.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Garlic should smell aromatic and look lightly translucent.
Step 4
Add the fresh chopped spinach to the skillet. Cook, stirring frequently, until the spinach wilts down, about 2-3 minutes. The spinach releases moisture, so it’s important to stir so it cooks evenly and doesn’t clump. This step adds freshness and a vibrant green color that balances the richness of the cream.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Spinach should be fully wilted but still bright green.
Step 5
Pour in the heavy cream and stir to combine with the mushrooms and spinach. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer over medium heat. Simmer for about 5 minutes until the cream thickens slightly. This gentle simmer allows the sauce to reduce and intensify in flavor without curdling. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce should coat the back of a spoon and look creamy.
Step 6
Add the Parmesan cheese to the skillet and stir until melted and fully incorporated into the sauce. The cheese adds saltiness and a nutty depth that enriches the cream base. If the sauce feels too thick, add a splash of reserved pasta water to loosen it up. This step creates a smooth, glossy sauce that clings to the pasta beautifully.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce should be smooth, cheesy, and slightly thickened.
Step 7
Season the sauce with salt, freshly ground black pepper, and red pepper flakes if using. Taste and adjust the seasoning carefully—Parmesan is salty, so add salt sparingly. The pepper flakes add a subtle heat that balances the creaminess, but you can skip them if you prefer mild flavors.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Sauce is well-seasoned, balanced between salty, creamy, and slightly spicy.
Step 8
Add the drained pasta to the skillet with the sauce. Toss gently to combine, making sure every strand is coated in that luscious cream sauce. If the sauce seems thick, add a bit more reserved pasta water to reach your desired consistency. This step is key for even flavor distribution and a silky texture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pasta should be evenly coated, glossy, and creamy.
Step 9
Serve the pasta immediately, garnished with freshly chopped parsley and extra Parmesan if you like. Fresh herbs add a pop of color and brightness that lifts the dish. Enjoy this warm, cozy meal with a simple side salad or crusty bread for a complete dinner.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Dish looks vibrant with green parsley and melted cheese on top.

Variations

Add Grilled Chicken for Extra Protein

Toss in sliced grilled chicken breast to make this pasta a heartier meal. The smoky, savory chicken pairs beautifully with the creamy sauce and veggies, turning it into a balanced dinner that satisfies meat lovers.

Note: Add cooked chicken slices at the final step when tossing pasta and sauce to warm through without drying out the meat.

Use Half-and-Half for a Lighter Sauce

Substitute heavy cream with half-and-half to reduce calories and fat while keeping the sauce creamy. The texture will be slightly lighter but still rich enough to coat the pasta nicely.

Note: Simmer half-and-half gently and avoid boiling to prevent curdling, as it has lower fat content than heavy cream.

Swap Spinach for Kale for More Texture

Replace fresh spinach with chopped kale for a heartier green with a bit more chew. Kale stands up well to cooking and adds a slightly earthy flavor that complements mushrooms.

Note: Massage kale with a pinch of salt before cooking to soften it, and cook a bit longer than spinach to achieve tenderness.

Make It Vegan by Using Coconut Cream and Nutritional Yeast

Use coconut cream instead of heavy cream and sprinkle nutritional yeast for a cheesy flavor to make this dish vegan-friendly. It’s a delicious plant-based alternative that still delivers creamy richness.

Note: Simmer coconut cream gently and add nutritional yeast at the end to avoid grainy texture.

Add Sun-Dried Tomatoes for a Tangy Twist

Stir in chopped sun-dried tomatoes along with the mushrooms for a pop of tangy sweetness that brightens the sauce. It adds complexity and a Mediterranean flair to the dish.

Note: Add sun-dried tomatoes during the garlic sauté step to infuse their flavor into the sauce.

Pro Tips

Don’t overcrowd your pan when sautéing mushrooms.
Overcrowding traps moisture and causes mushrooms to steam rather than brown, resulting in a soggy texture and less flavor. Cooking in batches ensures even caramelization, which develops the savory depth essential for this dish.
Always reserve some pasta cooking water before draining.
The starchy pasta water helps loosen the sauce and allows it to cling better to the noodles through emulsification. Without it, the sauce might be too thick or separate, making the pasta less creamy and cohesive.
Add spinach just before the cream to preserve its bright color and fresh flavor.
Cooking spinach too long can make it lose vibrancy and turn mushy. Adding it right before the cream wilts it just enough to blend into the sauce while maintaining a fresh taste and appealing green color.
Use freshly grated Parmesan for the best melt and flavor.
Pre-grated cheese often contains additives that prevent smooth melting and can affect sauce texture. Freshly grated Parmesan melts evenly, enriching the sauce with a rich, nutty flavor and silky texture.
Simmer the cream gently to avoid curdling.
High heat can make cream separate and curdle, ruining the sauce’s texture. A gentle simmer allows the sauce to thicken and flavors to meld smoothly, resulting in that luscious, creamy mouthfeel.
Season the sauce at the end and taste carefully.
Since Parmesan adds saltiness, adding salt too early can make the dish overly salty. Seasoning at the end lets you adjust flavors precisely, balancing salt, pepper, and any heat from red pepper flakes.
Toss pasta and sauce together off the heat.
Mixing the pasta with the sauce off the heat prevents the sauce from thickening too much or drying out. It also helps the sauce coat the noodles evenly for a silky finish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overcooking the pasta until mushy.

Why it happens Many cooks assume softer pasta is better, but overcooking leads to a mushy texture that ruins the dish’s balance.

Fix Cook pasta just until al dente according to package instructions, then drain immediately. The pasta will finish cooking slightly when tossed with sauce, maintaining a pleasant bite.

Crowding mushrooms in the pan.

Why it happens Trying to cook all mushrooms at once saves time but causes steaming instead of browning.

Fix Sauté mushrooms in smaller batches over medium-high heat to get that golden caramelization and deepen the flavor.

Adding spinach too early or cooking it too long.

Why it happens Some think spinach should be cooked fully, but overcooking makes it lose color and become mushy.

Fix Add spinach at the end of the mushroom and garlic sauté and cook just until wilted to keep it fresh and vibrant.

Not reserving pasta water before draining.

Why it happens Beginners often forget this step, missing out on the starch needed to perfect the sauce consistency.

Fix Always save about a cup of pasta water before draining to adjust sauce texture and help it adhere to the pasta.

Heating cream too quickly or at too high temperature.

Why it happens Rushing this step causes cream to curdle or separate, ruining the sauce’s smooth texture.

Fix Simmer cream gently over medium heat, stirring often, to thicken without breaking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This pasta is best enjoyed fresh but can safely sit out covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate leftovers promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The sauce may thicken and the spinach darken slightly when chilled. Reheat gently on the stovetop or microwave, adding a splash of water or cream to loosen the sauce.

Freezer

You can freeze this pasta for up to 2 months. Portion into freezer-safe containers, covering tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating. Texture of spinach may soften after freezing but still tastes good.

Reheating

Reheat on medium-low heat in a skillet, stirring often, and add a splash of cream or water to restore sauce creaminess. Microwave reheating works too; cover loosely and heat in short intervals to avoid drying out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the mushroom and spinach sauce 1-2 days ahead and store it separately in the fridge. Cook pasta fresh or reheat cooked pasta briefly before tossing with the warmed sauce on serving day for best texture.

FAQ

Can I use frozen spinach instead of fresh?
Yes, you can substitute frozen spinach, but be sure to thaw and squeeze out excess moisture before adding it to the sauce. Frozen spinach has more water, which can thin the sauce if not drained properly.
What pasta shape works best for this recipe?
Fettuccine, penne, or linguine work well because their shapes hold the creamy sauce nicely. Avoid very thin pastas like angel hair, which can get overwhelmed by the sauce.
How can I make this recipe vegan?
Replace heavy cream with coconut cream or cashew cream and use nutritional yeast instead of Parmesan to mimic the cheesy flavor. Sauté mushrooms and spinach as usual, then add vegan cream and seasoning.
Why does my sauce sometimes separate?
Sauce separation usually happens if the cream is boiled too rapidly or if cheese is added to very high heat. Simmer gently and stir constantly when adding cheese to ensure a smooth sauce.
Can I prepare this dish ahead of time?
You can make the sauce a day or two ahead and refrigerate it. Cook pasta fresh or reheat pasta separately before combining. This keeps the pasta texture from becoming mushy.
Is it okay to use pre-grated Parmesan?
Pre-grated Parmesan often contains anti-caking agents that affect melting. For the creamiest sauce, freshly grated cheese is best, but pre-grated will still work in a pinch.
How do I prevent mushrooms from being soggy?
Cook mushrooms on medium-high heat without overcrowding the pan. Let them brown undisturbed for a few minutes before stirring. This caramelization is key to flavor and texture.
Can I add other vegetables to this pasta?
Absolutely! Peas, roasted red peppers, or sun-dried tomatoes all pair nicely. Add them according to their cooking times—frozen peas near the end, roasted peppers with the spinach.
