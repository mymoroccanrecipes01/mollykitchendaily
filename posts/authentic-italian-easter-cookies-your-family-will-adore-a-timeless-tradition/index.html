Soft Italian Easter Cookies Bursting with Almond and Anise Flavors

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
20 min prep 15 min cook 24 servings

These soft Italian Easter cookies are a family favorite with delicate almond and anise notes. The colored sugar adds a festive touch. Perfect for celebrating spring or any cozy gathering!

Why This Works

This recipe shines because of the delicate balance of almond flour and eggs, which create the cookie’s tender, moist texture. Almond flour is naturally higher in fat than regular flour, contributing to softness and a rich almond flavor. Unlike wheat flour, it doesn’t develop gluten, which keeps these cookies from becoming tough or chewy.

The inclusion of anise extract is a subtle but vital flavor enhancer. Anise contains compounds that complement the nutty almond taste and add a slightly sweet, licorice-like aroma that feels festive without overwhelming the palate. This flavor pairing is traditional in many Italian baked goods, especially around Easter.

Using granulated sugar instead of powdered sugar helps maintain a slight crunch on the cookie’s exterior, especially when topped with colored sanding sugar. The sanding sugar not only adds visual appeal but also texture contrast, giving each bite a pleasant crunch followed by a melt-in-your-mouth center.

Finally, the baking time and temperature are calibrated to gently cook the cookies so they stay soft. Overbaking would dry them out, while underbaking would leave them too fragile. The brief cooling on the baking sheet before moving to a rack prevents breakage and helps maintain their signature soft texture.

  • When buying almond flour, look for finely ground, blanched almond flour for the best texture and light color. Coarser almond meal can produce a grittier cookie. Fresh almond flour has a sweet, nutty aroma that enhances the cookie’s flavor. Store it in a cool, dry place or refrigerate to prevent rancidity.
  • Granulated sugar adds sweetness and a slight texture that powdered sugar can’t provide. It also helps create a lightly crisp exterior when baked, especially when combined with sanding sugar on top. Avoid using brown sugar, as it would add moisture and change the texture.
  • Anise extract is key for that subtle licorice flavor traditional in Italian Easter cookies. Use sparingly because it’s potent and can overpower the almond notes. If you don’t have anise extract, fennel extract can be a mild substitute, but the flavor will differ slightly.
  • Eggs bind the almond flour and sugar, giving structure and moisture to the dough. Using large eggs at room temperature helps the ingredients combine smoothly. Overbeating the eggs can make the dough too liquidy, so mix just until combined.
  • This decorative sugar adds festive color and a slight crunch to the cookie tops. It withstands baking better than regular granulated sugar, maintaining its sparkle and texture. If unavailable, coarse sugar crystals can be used but won’t have the same vibrant effect.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups almond flour, finely ground
  • 1 cup granulated sugar, plus extra for sprinkling
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon anise extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon almond extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Colored sanding sugar, for topping

What I love most about these cookies is how soft and tender they are, unlike the usual crisp almond cookies. The secret is the combination of almond flour and the gentle addition of anise extract, which gives them a subtle licorice-like aroma that’s not overpowering but unmistakably special. I remember one year I tried skipping the anise, and the cookies just didn’t have that magic that makes them feel like a celebration. Another trick I learned is to sprinkle colored sanding sugar on top before baking—it adds a festive look and a tiny bit of crunch. These treats are perfect for sharing during Easter brunch or just whenever you want to add a little Italian charm to your day. The finished cookies are lightly sweet, fragrant, and have a melt-in-your-mouth texture that keeps everyone coming back for more. Honestly, they’re one of those recipes I find myself making again and again because they’re just so comforting and joyful.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. This ensures even cooking and prevents sticking, which is crucial since these cookies are soft and delicate. Using parchment also helps with easy cleanup and keeps the bottoms from browning too much. Avoid using greased pans, as that can cause the cookies to spread too much and lose their shape.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Oven fully preheated and baking sheets ready with parchment paper
  2. Step 2

    In a large bowl, whisk together the almond flour, sugar, and salt until well combined. Mixing these dry ingredients first helps distribute the sugar evenly, which is key for consistent sweetness and texture throughout the cookies. Almond flour tends to clump, so make sure to break up any lumps to get a smooth dough later on.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dry ingredients look evenly mixed with no clumps
  3. Step 3

    In a separate bowl, beat the eggs lightly, then stir in the anise and almond extracts. This step infuses the dough with aromatic flavors that are signature to this cookie. Be careful not to add too much anise extract, as it can become overpowering and mask the almond flavor.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Egg mixture is smooth and fragrant
  4. Step 4

    Slowly pour the egg mixture into the dry ingredients and stir with a wooden spoon or spatula until a sticky dough forms. It's important not to overmix here; you want the dough to be just combined to keep the cookies tender. The dough will be slightly sticky but manageable.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dough is sticky and cohesive but not runny
  5. Step 5

    Using a spoon or small cookie scoop, drop rounded tablespoons of dough onto the prepared baking sheets, spacing them about 2 inches apart. This spacing allows the cookies room to expand slightly without merging. If the dough is too sticky to handle, lightly wet your hands to shape the cookies gently.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Evenly spaced dough mounds on baking sheets
  6. Step 6

    Sprinkle each dough mound generously with colored sanding sugar. This not only adds a festive, colorful finish but also a slight crunch that contrasts nicely with the soft cookie. Don’t skip this step if you want that classic Easter vibe!

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Cookies are topped with a bright, even layer of sugar
  7. Step 7

    Bake the cookies in the preheated oven for 12 to 15 minutes. Keep a close eye starting at 12 minutes because these cookies should be lightly golden at the edges but still soft in the center. Overbaking will dry them out, losing their signature tenderness.

    ⏱ 12-15 minutes✓ Edges are lightly golden, centers look set but soft
  8. Step 8

    Remove the cookies from the oven and let them cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes. This resting time lets them firm up slightly so they don’t fall apart when transferred. Then, gently transfer them to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling on a rack prevents sogginess from trapped steam.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Cookies are firm enough to move without breaking
  9. Step 9

    Once cooled, store the cookies in an airtight container at room temperature. They keep well for several days and the flavors even deepen after a day. Enjoy these with a cup of espresso or tea for the perfect springtime treat.

    ⏱ N/A✓ Cookies are fully cooled and stored properly

Variations

  • Lemon Zest Italian Easter Cookies: Add 1 tablespoon of finely grated lemon zest to the dough for a bright citrus twist. The lemon pairs beautifully with almond and anise, giving the cookies a fresh, springtime flavor. Expect a slightly zestier aroma and a subtle tang on the palate.
    Incorporate zest with dry ingredients and monitor baking time closely, as citrus oils can affect browning.
  • Chocolate-Dipped Italian Easter Cookies: Once cooled, dip half of each cookie in melted dark chocolate and let set. This adds a rich, bittersweet contrast to the soft almond cookie, making them extra special for celebrations.
    Allow cookies to cool completely before dipping to avoid melting or breaking.
  • Gluten-Free Almond and Anise Cookies: This original recipe is naturally gluten-free thanks to almond flour. For extra chewiness, add a tablespoon of tapioca starch. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious and suitable for gluten-sensitive guests.
    Adjust baking time by a minute or two if tapioca starch is added, watching for doneness.
  • Spiced Italian Easter Cookies: Add 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves to the dry mix for a warm, cozy spice profile. These spices complement almond and anise for a festive flavor perfect for cooler spring days.
    Mix spices with dry ingredients thoroughly to ensure even distribution.
  • Honey-Sweetened Italian Easter Cookies: Replace half the granulated sugar with 1/4 cup honey for a natural sweetness and moist crumb. Honey adds floral notes that elevate the almond flavor, though the cookies will be softer and slightly stickier.
    Reduce oven temperature by 10°F and watch closely to prevent overbrowning.

Pro Tips

  • Chill the dough briefly if it feels too sticky to handle. — Chilling firms the dough, making it easier to scoop and shape without sticking to your hands. This prevents the cookies from spreading too much during baking, preserving their soft, rounded shape.
  • Use parchment paper instead of greasing the baking sheet. — Parchment paper prevents sticking without adding extra fat, which could cause the cookies to flatten. It also promotes even baking and easy cleanup, ensuring the cookies maintain their delicate texture.
  • Don’t skip the colored sanding sugar topping. — Besides adding a festive look, sanding sugar adds a subtle crunch that contrasts nicely with the soft cookie, enhancing the overall texture and eating experience.
  • Measure almond flour carefully by spooning into your measuring cup. — Almond flour can compact easily, leading to too much flour and dense cookies. Lightly spooning it in and leveling off ensures the right ratio for tender cookies.
  • Let cookies cool on the baking sheet for a few minutes before transferring. — This resting time allows the cookies to set up and become firm enough to move without breaking, preserving their shape and texture.
  • Avoid overbaking; remove cookies when edges are just golden. — Overbaking dries out the cookies, losing their soft, melt-in-your-mouth quality. The centers will continue to cook slightly after removal from the oven.
  • Use fresh almond flour for best flavor and texture. — Older almond flour can become rancid or lose its nutty aroma, negatively affecting taste. Fresh flour ensures a bright, rich almond flavor in every bite.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the anise extract
    Why: Sometimes people omit anise because they’re unsure about the flavor or don’t have it on hand.
    Fix: While you can omit it, the cookies lose their signature licorice note that makes them special. If you don’t have anise extract, try a small amount of fennel extract or simply use almond extract alone, but know the flavor will be less traditional.
  • Overbaking the cookies
    Why: It’s easy to leave them in the oven too long, especially if you’re used to crisp cookies.
    Fix: Watch the edges carefully and remove the cookies when they’re just turning golden. They should feel set but still soft in the center to keep their tender texture.
  • Using coarse almond meal instead of almond flour
    Why: Some people use whatever ground almonds they have without checking the grind size.
    Fix: Use finely ground almond flour to ensure a smooth, tender cookie. Coarser meal leads to a gritty texture and less cohesive dough.
  • Not spacing cookies properly on the baking sheet
    Why: Sometimes cookies are placed too close together, causing them to merge.
    Fix: Space cookies at least 2 inches apart to allow slight spreading without touching, preserving their pretty shape.
  • Mixing the dough too vigorously
    Why: Overenthusiastic mixing can happen when trying to combine sticky dough.
    Fix: Stir gently just until ingredients are combined. Overmixing can make the dough too soft, affecting texture and shape.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Store the cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight and moisture to preserve their softness and prevent sugar from melting.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: You can refrigerate the cookies for up to one week in an airtight container. They may firm up slightly but will soften to room temperature quickly. Avoid refrigeration if you prefer them very soft.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cookies in a sealed container or freezer bag for up to 3 months. Separate layers with parchment paper to prevent sticking. Thaw at room temperature for several hours before serving.
  • ♨️ Reheating: If you want to refresh cookies after storage, warm them in a 300°F oven for 3-5 minutes to regain softness. Avoid microwaving as it can make them rubbery or dry out quickly.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the dough 1 day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Shape and bake the cookies the next day for freshest results. Alternatively, bake them and freeze fully baked cookies for easy thaw-and-serve treats.

FAQ

Can I substitute almond flour with regular flour?
Substituting almond flour with regular wheat flour isn’t recommended because the texture and flavor will change dramatically. Almond flour gives these cookies their moist, tender crumb and nutty taste. Using wheat flour would produce a drier, less flavorful cookie.
Why are my cookies too crumbly?
Crumbly cookies usually mean the dough is too dry or hasn’t been mixed enough to bind properly. Make sure to measure almond flour correctly and mix the dough until it just comes together. Adding a little extra egg can also help with binding if needed.
Can I make these cookies vegan?
These cookies rely on eggs for structure and moisture, so vegan substitutions would require significant adjustments. You might try flax eggs or commercial egg replacers, but the texture and flavor will differ. This recipe is best enjoyed as is or with minor tweaks.
How do I store the cookies to keep them soft?
Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature with a slice of bread or a damp paper towel to maintain moisture. Avoid refrigeration, which can dry them out. Proper storage keeps them soft for several days.
Can I freeze the dough?
Yes, you can freeze the dough shaped into balls on a tray, then transfer to a freezer bag. Bake from frozen, adding a couple of extra minutes to the baking time. This is great for making ahead and enjoying fresh cookies anytime.
Why do my cookies spread too much?
Spreading often happens if the dough is too warm or the baking sheet is greased. Use parchment paper and chill the dough briefly if it feels too soft. Also, avoid adding too much liquid or overmixing, which can cause spreading.
Can I add other flavors to the cookies?
Absolutely! Lemon zest, cinnamon, or even dipping in chocolate are great ways to customize. Just keep the core almond and anise flavors balanced to maintain the cookie’s character.
How long will these cookies keep their flavor?
Stored properly, these cookies keep their flavor and texture for up to a week at room temperature. Almond and anise flavors may intensify slightly over time, which many find delightful.
