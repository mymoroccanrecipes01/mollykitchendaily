Bright and Moist Lemon Zucchini Bread with a Tangy Glaze

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 4, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon zucchini bread is a fresh take on the classic loaf. Packed with juicy zucchini and zesty lemon, it’s moist and flavorful. The tangy lemon glaze finishes it perfectly—great for breakfast or a snack!

Introduction

I first stumbled on this lemon zucchini bread recipe during a late summer zucchini overload at my house. I’d made zucchini bread plenty of times before, but adding lemon zest and juice completely changed the game — it brightened the whole loaf and made it feel fresh, not heavy. Plus, the lemon glaze on top is the perfect tangy finish that keeps you sneaking slice after slice. I’ve tried a bunch of recipes over the years, but this one stands out because the zucchini is grated finely and squeezed really well, so the bread stays moist without getting soggy. I also mix the sugars to balance sweetness with a little depth from the brown sugar. The result is a loaf that’s tender, flavorful, and not too sweet — perfect for breakfast, a snack, or even a light dessert. I think you’ll love how the lemon zing pairs so well with the subtle earthiness of the zucchini. It’s a recipe that’s forgiving and simple, but still special enough to impress family and friends. Just a heads up: don’t skip squeezing the zucchini dry! I learned that the hard way when my first batch was too wet and dense. Now I always make sure to press out as much moisture as possible before mixing it in. Trust me, it makes all the difference.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • For the glaze:
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating your zucchini finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or several layers of paper towels. Squeeze firmly to remove as much moisture as possible. This step is crucial because zucchini holds a lot of water, which can make your bread soggy if not drained well. Taking the time to do this ensures your loaf stays moist but not wet, giving you that perfect crumb. Avoid rushing this step—if the zucchini is too wet, the bread won’t bake properly and could be dense.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels dry to the touch and no liquid pools when squeezed
Step 2
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon. Mixing these dry ingredients first helps distribute the leavening agents evenly so your bread rises nicely and bakes uniformly. If you skip this step or mix too roughly later, you might get uneven pockets or a flat loaf.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no clumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, whisk the granulated sugar, brown sugar, eggs, vegetable oil, and vanilla extract until smooth and slightly fluffy. This step incorporates air into the batter for a lighter texture. Use room temperature eggs to help the mixture emulsify properly. If the eggs are too cold, your batter might curdle or separate, which can affect the final texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks smooth and slightly thickened
Step 4
Add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture in two parts, folding gently with a spatula to combine. Overmixing can develop gluten in the flour, resulting in a tougher bread. Fold just until you don’t see dry flour anymore, then stop. This gentle mixing keeps the crumb tender and soft.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no visible flour streaks but not overly mixed
Step 5
Fold in the grated and squeezed zucchini along with the lemon zest and lemon juice. The lemon zest adds bright citrus oils that elevate the flavor, while the juice adds moisture and a subtle tang. Mixing these in last ensures the zest stays fresh and fragrant. Be careful not to overmix here to keep your bread tender.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and lemon evenly distributed without overworked batter
Step 6
Pour the batter into a greased or parchment-lined 9x5 inch loaf pan, smoothing the top gently with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and develop a nice crust. If your batter is uneven, the loaf might bake unevenly or crack awkwardly.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and smooth in the pan
Step 7
Bake in the preheated oven for about 45-50 minutes. Start checking around 45 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Baking too long can dry the bread out, while underbaking leaves it gummy. Keep an eye on the color too — it should be golden brown and fragrant.
⏱ Time: 45-50 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown top and clean toothpick test
Step 8
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling in the pan too long traps steam and can make the crust soggy. Cooling fully before glazing ensures the glaze doesn’t melt off.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm and slightly warm, not hot
Step 9
Make the lemon glaze by whisking powdered sugar with fresh lemon juice until smooth. Drizzle over the cooled loaf. The glaze adds a bright, tangy sweetness that complements the zucchini’s earthiness and moist crumb. If you glaze while the bread is warm, the glaze will melt and run off, so patience here pays off!
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, shiny glaze coating the loaf

Variations

Add Chopped Walnuts

Stir in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts for a delightful crunch and nutty flavor that pairs wonderfully with lemon and zucchini. This adds texture and a bit of richness without overpowering the bright flavors.

Note: Fold nuts in at the same time as zucchini and lemon to ensure even distribution; don’t chop too finely or they’ll get lost in the crumb.

Swap Lemon for Orange

Use orange zest and juice instead of lemon for a sweeter, less tart citrus note. This variation is great if you prefer a milder citrus flavor that still adds freshness.

Note: Orange juice is less acidic, so the rise may be slightly less vigorous; consider adding a pinch of cream of tartar if needed.

Make It Gluten-Free

Replace all-purpose flour with a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This allows those with gluten sensitivities to enjoy the bread with minimal texture change.

Note: Gluten-free batters are often more fragile; avoid overmixing and bake slightly longer while monitoring doneness.

Add Spices Like Nutmeg or Ginger

Enhance the flavor by adding 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg or ground ginger to the dry ingredients. These warm spices complement the lemon and zucchini for a cozy twist.

Note: Add spices with the flour mixture to ensure even flavor distribution.

Use Coconut Oil Instead of Vegetable Oil

Swap vegetable oil for melted coconut oil for a subtle tropical note and slightly firmer crumb. This works well if you enjoy a hint of coconut alongside lemon.

Note: Use refined coconut oil for a neutral flavor or unrefined for more pronounced coconut aroma.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the zucchini dry before adding it to the batter.
Removing excess moisture prevents the bread from becoming soggy or dense. Too much water in the batter dilutes the structure, leading to a gummy texture instead of a tender crumb.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice to maximize bright citrus flavor.
Lemon zest contains essential oils that give a fresh aroma and flavor, while fresh juice adds acidity that balances sweetness and interacts with the baking soda for rise. Bottled lemon juice lacks these qualities.
Mix dry ingredients thoroughly before combining with wet ingredients.
Evenly distributing baking powder and baking soda ensures consistent rise and texture. Skipping this can cause uneven bubbles or dense spots in the bread.
Fold the batter gently to avoid overworking the gluten.
Overmixing toughens the bread by developing gluten strands too much. Gentle folding keeps the crumb soft and tender.
Let the bread cool completely before glazing.
Applying glaze while warm causes it to melt and slide off, losing the glossy tangy finish. Cooling lets the glaze set beautifully on top.
Use room temperature eggs for better emulsification.
Cold eggs can cause the batter to curdle or separate, affecting texture and rise. Room temperature eggs blend more smoothly, creating a uniform batter.
Check for doneness with a toothpick starting at 45 minutes.
Ovens vary, so checking early prevents overbaking which dries out the bread. A clean toothpick with a few moist crumbs means perfect doneness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing zucchini dry enough

Why it happens It's easy to underestimate how much water zucchini holds and skip this step to save time.

Fix Take the time to grate finely and squeeze firmly using a towel or paper towels. This prevents excess moisture that causes soggy, dense bread.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Some think mixing longer makes a smoother batter, but it develops gluten.

Fix Fold ingredients gently until just combined. Stop as soon as no dry flour remains to keep the bread tender.

Using old or inactive leavening agents

Why it happens Leavening powders and soda lose potency over time and many forget to check freshness.

Fix Test baking soda with vinegar and baking powder with hot water before baking; replace if no fizz occurs to ensure good rise.

Skipping the lemon zest or juice

Why it happens Some omit it to simplify or due to lack of lemons.

Fix Don’t skip these! They provide the bright flavor and acidity that make this bread special. Substitute fresh if possible.

Glazing while the bread is still warm

Why it happens Impatience to finish leads to glazing too soon.

Fix Cool completely on a wire rack before drizzling glaze to prevent melting and sliding off.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This bread keeps well at room temperature for up to 2 days when wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. Keeping it sealed prevents it from drying out or absorbing other odors.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 5 days. The bread may become slightly firmer when chilled; bring to room temperature or warm briefly before serving for best texture and flavor.

Freezer

Freeze wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil or a freezer bag for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a few hours or warm gently in the oven to restore freshness.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a toaster oven or regular oven at 325°F for about 5-7 minutes until warmed through. Avoid microwaving as it can make the bread gummy. A light brush of butter or a quick zap with a lemon drizzle can refresh the flavor.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh the next day for best rise and texture. Alternatively, bake the bread fully, then freeze slices individually for quick breakfasts or snacks.

FAQ

Can I use yellow squash instead of zucchini?
Absolutely! Yellow squash has a similar moisture content and mild flavor, so it works well as a substitute. Just grate and squeeze it dry the same way to prevent excess moisture from affecting the bread texture.
What if I don’t have lemons for zest and juice?
You can omit lemon zest and juice, but the bread will lose its bright citrus flavor. A good alternative is using a teaspoon of vanilla extract and a splash of orange juice for a different but pleasant flavor profile.
Why is my bread dense and not rising properly?
Common causes include not squeezing zucchini dry, expired baking powder or soda, or overmixing the batter. Make sure leavening agents are fresh, zucchini is drained well, and fold batter gently to keep it light.
Can I make this bread dairy-free?
Yes! This recipe already uses vegetable oil instead of butter, so it’s naturally dairy-free. Just ensure your powdered sugar for glaze is dairy-free and you’re good to go.
How do I store leftover bread to keep it fresh?
Wrap it tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. For longer storage, refrigerate or freeze slices wrapped individually to maintain moisture and flavor.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips to this bread?
Yes, adding 1/2 cup chopped nuts or chocolate chips works well. Fold them in gently with the zucchini and lemon zest to avoid overmixing. This adds texture and extra flavor without compromising the crumb.
Is it necessary to use both baking powder and baking soda?
Using both ensures proper rise. Baking soda reacts with the acidic lemon juice for immediate lift, while baking powder provides additional leavening during baking. Omitting one can affect the bread’s texture and height.
Can I make this bread vegan?
To make it vegan, replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a plant-based oil. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious.
