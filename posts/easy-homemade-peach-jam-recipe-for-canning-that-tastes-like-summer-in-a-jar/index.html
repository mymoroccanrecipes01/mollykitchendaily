Easy Homemade Peach Jam to Capture Summer’s Sweetest Flavors

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 15, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This peach jam recipe is a total game changer for using up ripe peaches. It’s simple, with just a few ingredients, and locks in that juicy summer sweetness. Perfect for spreading on toast or gifting to friends.

Introduction

I first tried making peach jam a few summers ago when I had a basket overflowing with ripe peaches from a local farmer’s market. I’d never canned anything before, so I was a bit nervous, but this recipe turned out so easy and rewarding that I’ve made it every year since. What makes this version stand out is how it uses just a handful of simple ingredients to let the peach flavor shine without any fuss. I love that you can tweak it with a bit of cinnamon for warmth or keep it pure and fresh. The trick I learned was to peel the peaches carefully and cook the jam just long enough to thicken without losing that bright, fresh taste. The final result is a beautifully golden, sweet, and slightly tangy jam that’s perfect on toast, swirled into yogurt, or even as a topping for desserts. It’s like capturing a jar of summer sunshine that you can enjoy all year round. Plus, it makes a lovely homemade gift that feels really special.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 half-pint jars, 4 servings

  • 4 cups fresh peaches, peeled and chopped
  • 2 cups granulated sugar
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice, fresh
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon (optional)
  • 1 packet fruit pectin (optional for thicker jam)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by peeling your peaches. The easiest way is to blanch them in boiling water for about 30 seconds, then immediately transfer to ice water. This loosens the skin making it easy to slip off with your fingers. Peeling is important because peach skin can add a bit of bitterness and texture that you might not want in your jam. Once peeled, chop the peaches into small pieces, roughly uniform so they cook evenly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peach skins easily slide off and chopped peaches are uniform in size
Step 2
Combine the chopped peaches, sugar, and lemon juice in a large heavy-bottomed pot. The lemon juice helps balance sweetness and aids in setting the jam. Let the mixture sit for about 10 minutes to macerate, which helps draw out the juices and dissolve the sugar. This step is key for a smooth, well-blended jam.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sugar is mostly dissolved and mixture looks juicy and syrupy
Step 3
Place the pot over medium heat and bring the peach mixture to a gentle boil, stirring frequently to prevent sticking or burning. Once boiling, reduce the heat slightly to maintain a steady simmer. Cooking the jam at this temperature allows the fruit to break down and the sugars to concentrate, thickening the mixture naturally. Avoid rushing this process or cooking on too high heat as it can scorch the jam and alter the flavor.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture thickens and bubbles steadily without burning
Step 4
If you want a thicker jam, stir in the fruit pectin now, following the packet instructions carefully. Pectin helps the jam set firm and is especially useful if your peaches are less ripe or you prefer a spreadable texture. Stir continuously for a minute or two until fully incorporated. If you prefer a looser jam, you can skip this step and rely on natural thickening.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pectin is fully dissolved and jam starts to thicken noticeably
Step 5
Add cinnamon if using, and continue to simmer the jam gently, stirring often. This allows the flavors to meld and the jam to reach the perfect consistency. Keep an eye on the texture — it should coat the back of a spoon and not be runny. To test, place a small spoonful on a chilled plate; if it wrinkles when pushed with your finger, it’s ready.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Jam gels on a cold plate and wrinkles when nudged
Step 6
Remove the pot from heat and let the jam cool for a few minutes before ladling into sterilized jars. Filling the jars while the jam is still hot helps create a good seal during canning. Be sure to wipe the rims clean to prevent sealing issues.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Jam is hot but not boiling, jars are filled cleanly
Step 7
Seal the jars with sterilized lids and process them in a boiling water bath for 10 minutes. This step is crucial for preserving the jam safely and extending its shelf life. The water bath also helps create a vacuum seal that keeps the jam fresh for months.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Lids pop down indicating a proper seal
Step 8
Carefully remove the jars from the water bath and let them cool undisturbed on a towel or rack. Avoid moving them while hot as this can break the seal or cause cracks. After cooling, check the seals by pressing the center of each lid — it should not flex up or down.
⏱ Time: 1 hour
✓ Doneness: Lids are concave and do not move when pressed
Step 9
Label your jars with the date and store them in a cool, dark place. Properly sealed jars can last up to a year. Once opened, keep the jam refrigerated and use within a few weeks. Enjoy your homemade peach jam on toast, biscuits, or stirred into oatmeal for a little sunshine any time.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Jars clearly labeled and stored appropriately

Variations

Spiced Peach Jam with Ginger and Cardamom

Add a teaspoon of freshly grated ginger and a pinch of ground cardamom during cooking for a warming, exotic twist. This variation adds depth and a subtle spicy note that pairs beautifully with peach sweetness.

Note: Add spices early in the simmering stage to allow their flavors to infuse fully without overpowering the fruit.

Peach and Raspberry Jam Blend

Mix equal parts chopped peaches and fresh raspberries for a colorful, tangy jam. The raspberries add natural pectin and a bright tartness that balances the peach’s sweetness.

Note: Reduce sugar slightly due to raspberries’ natural tartness and pectin content to avoid an overly sweet jam.

Low-Sugar Peach Jam

Use half the sugar and add extra lemon juice to compensate. This results in a lighter, less sweet jam that highlights the peaches’ natural flavor but may have a looser set.

Note: Expect a softer set; consider adding commercial pectin designed for low-sugar jams.

Peach Jam with Vanilla Bean

Scrape the seeds from a vanilla bean pod into the jam for a luscious, aromatic flavor. This adds a creamy sweetness that complements the peaches perfectly.

Note: Add vanilla seeds near the end of cooking to preserve their delicate aroma.

Chunky Peach Jam

For a rustic texture, chop peaches into larger pieces and reduce cooking time slightly. This keeps some fruit chunks intact for a jam with more bite and texture.

Note: Monitor cooking closely to avoid undercooked jam; test set frequently.

Pro Tips

Always use ripe but firm peaches for best flavor and texture.
Ripe peaches have the best natural sweetness and pectin content, which helps your jam set without needing too much added pectin. Overripe peaches can make the jam too runny and affect flavor, while underripe peaches lack sweetness.
Peel peaches by blanching them in boiling water briefly.
Blanching loosens the skin, making it easy to peel without wasting fruit or adding bitterness from the skin. Skipping this can result in a jam with chewy bits and less smooth texture.
Don’t rush the cooking process; simmer gently.
Slow simmering allows the fruit to break down evenly and the sugar to dissolve fully, preventing burning or uneven texture. High heat can scorch the jam and dull its fresh flavor.
Test jam set by placing a spoonful on a cold plate.
This simple test shows if the jam gels properly — if it wrinkles when pushed, it’s done. Without this test, you risk undercooking (runny jam) or overcooking (too stiff).
Sterilize jars and lids before filling.
Sterilization prevents bacterial growth and spoilage, ensuring your jam stays safe and fresh for months. It also helps jars seal properly during the water bath.
Let jars cool undisturbed after water bath canning.
Moving jars while hot can break seals or cause cracks, compromising preservation. Cooling on a stable surface ensures a tight vacuum seal forms.
Label jars with date and store in a cool, dark place.
Proper storage preserves flavor and safety. Light and heat can degrade jam quality over time, so a pantry or cellar is best.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not peeling peaches before cooking

Why it happens People often skip peeling to save time or because they don’t realize the skin affects texture and flavor.

Fix Always blanch peaches briefly in boiling water to loosen skins, then peel carefully. This ensures a smooth jam without bitter or chewy bits.

Using too high heat and rushing cooking

Why it happens Impatience or multitasking can lead to cranking up the heat, hoping to speed things along.

Fix Simmer gently over medium heat, stirring often. This prevents burning and ensures even thickening and flavor development.

Skipping the water bath canning process

Why it happens Some might think jam is safe because of its sugar content or don’t have canning equipment.

Fix Always process jars in boiling water bath to sterilize and seal. This preserves the jam safely for long-term storage.

Not testing jam set before jar filling

Why it happens Inexperience or rushing can cause skipping the plate test.

Fix Use the cold plate test to check consistency. If it doesn’t wrinkle, cook a bit longer and test again.

Using underripe peaches

Why it happens Sometimes peaches look good but aren’t fully ripe, especially if bought early or stored improperly.

Fix Choose peaches that are fragrant and slightly soft but not mushy. This ensures natural sweetness and pectin for a better jam.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Once sealed and processed properly, peach jam can be kept at room temperature in a cool, dark pantry for up to one year. Store jars away from direct sunlight and heat sources to maintain flavor and color.

Refrigerator

After opening, store jam in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 weeks. The texture may thicken slightly when cold but will soften at room temperature. Stir before serving if needed.

Freezer

Jam can be frozen for up to 6 months in freezer-safe containers, leaving some headspace. Thaw in the refrigerator overnight and stir gently to restore consistency. Freezing may slightly alter the texture but not the flavor.

Reheating

Peach jam is typically served cold or at room temperature, but if you want to warm it, do so gently on the stovetop or microwave in short bursts. Avoid boiling as it can change the texture and flavor. Add a splash of water if too thick.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the peach mixture a day ahead and keep it refrigerated overnight before cooking. This allows flavors to meld and reduces active cooking time. Just be sure to bring the mixture back to room temperature before cooking.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches to make this jam?
Yes, frozen peaches work well if fresh ones aren’t available. Thaw them completely and drain excess liquid before cooking to avoid watery jam. The texture might be slightly softer, but the flavor will still be delicious.
How do I know when my jam has set properly?
Use the cold plate test: place a spoonful of hot jam on a chilled plate and let it cool for a minute. Push it gently with your finger — if it wrinkles and doesn’t flood back, your jam is set. If it’s runny, cook a bit longer and test again.
Do I have to use commercial pectin?
No, peaches have natural pectin, so you can make jam without added pectin, but the set may be softer. Adding pectin helps achieve a firmer texture and can reduce cooking time. It’s especially helpful if your peaches are very ripe or low in pectin.
How long can I store unopened jars of peach jam?
Properly sealed and processed jars can last up to one year when stored in a cool, dark place. After opening, refrigerate and use within 3 weeks for best quality and safety.
Can I reduce the sugar in this recipe?
You can reduce sugar, but it may affect the jam’s texture and shelf life. Less sugar means a softer set and shorter preservation time. If reducing sugar, consider adding pectin designed for low-sugar jams and refrigerate after opening.
What’s the best way to peel peaches quickly?
Blanch peaches in boiling water for 30 seconds, then transfer to ice water. The skins slip off easily after this. This method is faster and neater than peeling with a knife or peeler.
Can I add spices like cinnamon or ginger?
Absolutely! Adding spices like cinnamon, ginger, or vanilla enhances the flavor and adds warmth. Add spices during simmering so they infuse the jam without overpowering the peach flavor.
Why is my jam too runny?
Runny jam usually means it hasn’t cooked long enough to set or there wasn’t enough pectin or acid. Try cooking longer, adding commercial pectin, or increasing lemon juice slightly to help it gel.
