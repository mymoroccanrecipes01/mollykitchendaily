Moist Gluten Free Zucchini Bread Muffins Perfect for Breakfast or Snack

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These gluten free zucchini muffins are super moist and tender thanks to fresh zucchini. They make a perfect quick breakfast or snack, especially when you want something soft and wholesome without gluten. I love baking them on cozy weekend mornings!

Introduction

I first made these gluten free zucchini bread muffins when I was craving something soft and comforting but needed to keep things gluten free for a friend. The result totally surprised me — they turned out super moist and tender, thanks to the fresh zucchini that adds natural moisture without any gluten. I’ve tried a few gluten free muffin recipes before, but this one really nails the texture and flavor. One trick I learned was to squeeze the zucchini really well to avoid soggy batter but still keep that wonderful moisture. Plus, using a mix of brown and granulated sugar gives them a lovely depth of sweetness without being overpowering. When these muffins come out of the oven, they have a tender crumb with just a hint of cinnamon spice, perfect for breakfast or a cozy afternoon snack. They’re also easy enough to whip up on a busy morning or for a weekend bake day. Honestly, I love how they stay soft even after a couple of days, which makes them great for meal prep too. If you’re looking for a naturally gluten free treat that feels homemade and wholesome, I think you’ll really enjoy these muffins!

What You'll Need

About 12 muffins

  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 2 cups gluten free all-purpose flour blend
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a muffin tin with paper liners or grease it lightly. This step is important so your muffins don’t stick and come out cleanly. A well-preheated oven ensures the muffins rise properly and bake evenly, giving you that perfect tender crumb. Avoid skipping the liners or grease or you might have trouble removing the muffins later.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven is fully heated and muffin tin is prepared with liners or greased surface
Step 2
Grate the zucchini using a box grater or food processor, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much moisture as possible. This step is crucial because excess water can make your muffins soggy and dense instead of light and fluffy. If you skip squeezing, the batter will be too wet and won’t bake properly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is dry to the touch and no longer dripping liquid
Step 3
In a large bowl, whisk together the gluten free flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon until fully combined. Mixing the dry ingredients first ensures the leavening agents are evenly distributed, which helps the muffins rise evenly. If you don’t mix well, you might get uneven texture or pockets of baking soda taste.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are uniform with no clumps or streaks
Step 4
In another bowl, beat the eggs with both sugars, oil, and vanilla extract until smooth and slightly frothy. The eggs act as a binder, while the sugars add sweetness and moisture. Beating them well incorporates air that contributes to a lighter muffin texture. If you rush this or don’t mix enough, your muffins may be dense.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet mixture is smooth, pale, and slightly foamy
Step 5
Slowly add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients, stirring gently until just combined. Overmixing can develop the gluten-free flours too much and cause toughness, so mix just until no dry flour remains. The batter should be thick but not dry.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly combined with no streaks of flour, thick but smooth
Step 6
Fold in the squeezed zucchini carefully with a spatula, distributing it evenly without overworking the batter. The zucchini adds moisture and tenderness, so it’s important to keep it intact to preserve texture. Avoid mixing vigorously or breaking down the zucchini too much.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is evenly folded in, batter looks homogenous
Step 7
Spoon the batter evenly into the prepared muffin tin, filling each cup about 3/4 full. This helps ensure muffins bake evenly and rise nicely without spilling over. Filling too full can cause overflow, too little can yield dry muffins.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffin cups are evenly filled with batter, about 3/4 full
Step 8
Bake the muffins in the preheated oven for 25 to 30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center — it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Baking too long will dry them out, too short and they’ll be undercooked and gummy.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean or with moist crumbs
Step 9
Let the muffins cool in the tin for about 10 minutes before transferring them to a wire rack. Cooling in the pan too long can cause sogginess from trapped steam, while removing too soon might make them break apart. Proper cooling helps set the crumb and keeps them tender.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffins feel firm enough to handle without falling apart

Variations

Add Walnuts and Raisins

Mix in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts and 1/3 cup raisins for extra texture and flavor. The nuts add crunch while raisins bring a burst of natural sweetness. This variation turns the muffins into a heartier snack or breakfast option.

Note: Fold in walnuts and raisins gently after adding zucchini to avoid overmixing and breaking the mix-ins.

Cinnamon and Nutmeg Spice Mix

Enhance the warm spice flavor by adding 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg along with the cinnamon. This gives the muffins a cozy, autumnal vibe perfect for chilly mornings or holiday baking.

Note: Add the nutmeg with the dry spices to ensure even distribution.

Lemon Zest and Poppy Seeds

For a bright twist, add the zest of one lemon and 1 tablespoon poppy seeds. The citrus zest adds freshness, while poppy seeds provide a slight crunch and visual appeal.

Note: Mix zest and poppy seeds into the dry ingredients before combining with wet.

Vegan Version

Replace eggs with flax eggs (2 tablespoons ground flaxseed + 6 tablespoons water) and use coconut oil instead of vegetable oil. This makes the muffins vegan-friendly while keeping them moist and tender.

Note: Allow flax eggs to gel for 5 minutes before mixing with wet ingredients to mimic egg binding.

Chocolate Chip Muffins

Stir in 1/2 cup dairy-free or regular chocolate chips for a sweet treat. This variation is perfect for kids or anyone craving a bit of chocolate with their zucchini muffins.

Note: Fold chips in gently at the end to prevent them from melting into the batter.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze your grated zucchini well to avoid soggy batter.
Zucchini contains a lot of water, which can make your muffins wet and dense if not removed. Squeezing it out ensures the batter is the right consistency, so the muffins bake up tender but not gummy. Moisture balance is crucial in gluten free baking.
Use a good quality gluten free flour blend with xanthan gum included.
Xanthan gum mimics gluten’s elasticity and helps trap air bubbles, giving your muffins structure and lightness. Without it, muffins can crumble or fall apart. A pre-mixed blend saves time and improves consistency over mixing individual flours yourself.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add the dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops the starches too much, leading to tough muffins. Mixing just until combined keeps the crumb tender and light. You want to keep some air bubbles intact, which helps with rise and softness.
Use a combination of brown and granulated sugar for best flavor and moisture.
Brown sugar’s molasses adds moisture and a rich depth of flavor, while granulated sugar helps with sweetness and texture. This balance keeps muffins from being too dry or overly sweet.
Let muffins cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack.
Cooling slightly in the pan lets the muffins set so they don’t fall apart when moved. Removing too early can cause breakage, while leaving too long traps steam causing sogginess. Ten minutes is the sweet spot.
Preheat your oven fully before baking.
Starting in a hot oven ensures the leaveners activate immediately, creating lift and a tender crumb. A cold oven leads to dense muffins that don’t rise well.
Fill muffin cups about 3/4 full to avoid overflow.
Overfilling causes batter to spill over while baking, creating uneven shapes and mess. Underfilling can dry out the muffins. Three-quarters full is ideal for even, pretty muffins.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be used as is, forgetting how watery it is. This leads to overly wet batter and soggy muffins.

Fix Use a clean towel or cheesecloth to press out excess liquid thoroughly. The batter should be thick but moist, not runny. This step is key to avoid gummy texture.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix longer to get everything combined, but gluten free flours can become tough if overworked.

Fix Stir gently and only until no dry flour remains. Use a spatula and fold rather than beat the batter to keep muffins tender.

Skipping the oven preheat

Why it happens Impatience or distraction can cause bakers to put muffins in before the oven is hot.

Fix Always preheat the oven fully to 350°F. This ensures proper rise and texture from the start of baking.

Using the wrong flour or no xanthan gum

Why it happens Some try to use a single gluten free flour or omit xanthan gum, thinking it’s optional.

Fix Use a well-balanced gluten free blend with xanthan gum to mimic gluten’s structure. This prevents crumbly, dense muffins.

Filling muffin tins too full or too low

Why it happens Guessing how much batter to pour can cause overflow or dry muffins.

Fix Fill each muffin cup about 3/4 full for even rise and shape.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat to maintain softness. Covering well prevents them from drying out.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate muffins in an airtight container for up to 5 days. They may firm up slightly but can be warmed in the microwave for 15-20 seconds to restore softness. Cold muffins are still tasty but warming brings back that fresh-baked feel.

Freezer

Freeze muffins individually wrapped in plastic wrap and placed in a freezer-safe bag for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a couple hours or microwave briefly. Proper wrapping prevents freezer burn and moisture loss.

Reheating

Warm muffins in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of water on a paper towel when microwaving helps retain moisture. Avoid overheating which can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze zucchini 1-2 days ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix dry ingredients in advance as well. Combine everything and bake fresh on the day you want to serve for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use regular wheat flour instead of gluten free flour?
Yes, you can substitute with regular all-purpose flour if you don’t need gluten free. The texture will be slightly different—usually a bit more elastic and less crumbly—but the recipe will still work fine. Reduce baking powder by 1/4 teaspoon if desired.
My muffins turned out dry, what did I do wrong?
Dry muffins usually mean too much flour or overbaking. Make sure to measure flour properly—spoon and level instead of scooping. Also, don’t overbake; start checking for doneness at 25 minutes. Overmixing batter can also make muffins tough and dry.
Can I freeze these muffins?
Absolutely! These muffins freeze well for up to 3 months. Wrap each muffin tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer bag. Thaw at room temperature or microwave briefly before serving for best texture.
What if I don’t have brown sugar?
You can substitute with all granulated sugar but muffins may be less moist and flavorful. Adding a tablespoon of molasses per cup of sugar can mimic brown sugar’s moisture and taste if you have it.
Can I make this recipe vegan?
Yes, replace eggs with flax or chia eggs (1 tablespoon ground seed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a plant-based oil like coconut or vegetable oil. The texture may be slightly different but still delicious.
How do I know when the muffins are done baking?
Insert a toothpick in the center of a muffin—if it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, they’re done. If batter sticks, bake a few minutes longer but check often to avoid drying out.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Definitely! Fold in about 1/2 cup of chopped nuts or chocolate chips after mixing in zucchini. This adds texture and flavor without changing baking times.
Why is squeezing zucchini moisture so important?
Zucchini holds a lot of water which can make muffins soggy if not removed. Squeezing out excess moisture keeps batter consistency right and ensures muffins bake with a tender, not wet crumb.
