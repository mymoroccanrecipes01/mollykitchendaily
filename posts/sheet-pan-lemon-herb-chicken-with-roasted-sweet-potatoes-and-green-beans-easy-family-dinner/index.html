Easy Sheet Pan Lemon Herb Chicken with Sweet Potatoes and Green Beans

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 4, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This sheet pan chicken dinner is a total game changer when you want something tasty but fuss-free. The lemon and herbs give the chicken a fresh zing, while sweet potatoes and green beans roast up perfectly all in one pan. Perfect for busy weeknights!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this sheet pan chicken recipe when I was desperate for a quick, healthy dinner that didn’t require standing over the stove for ages. What I love most about this version is how everything roasts together, soaking up the lemony herb flavors without any extra pots or pans. It’s a total time-saver, and the clean-up is a breeze — which, let’s be honest, makes weeknight cooking way more enjoyable. The trick I learned is to toss the sweet potatoes and green beans separately with a bit of olive oil and seasoning before adding the chicken on top. This way, nothing gets soggy, and each component roasts perfectly. When it’s done, you get juicy chicken breasts bursting with fresh lemon and oregano, alongside tender sweet potatoes with a slight caramelized edge and crisp-tender green beans. It’s a simple, wholesome meal that feels a bit fancy but is so easy to pull off. I’ve made this a dozen times now, and it’s always a hit with my family. Plus, it’s great for meal prepping or doubling up for leftovers!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 boneless skinless chicken breasts
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 lemon, zested and juiced
  • 2 teaspoons dried oregano
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 large sweet potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 12 ounces fresh green beans, trimmed

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 425°F (220°C). This high temperature is key to getting the chicken skin nicely browned and the sweet potatoes caramelized without drying out the chicken. While the oven heats, line a large baking sheet with parchment paper or foil for easy cleanup. Make sure your chicken breasts are patted dry with paper towels — moisture on the surface can steam the chicken instead of roasting it, which affects texture and color.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven fully preheated and chicken surface dry and ready
Step 2
In a large bowl, toss the cubed sweet potatoes with 1 tablespoon olive oil, half the lemon zest, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and a pinch of black pepper. Spread them out on one side of the baking sheet in a single layer. This seasoning helps the sweet potatoes caramelize and develop a natural sweetness as they roast. Crowding them will cause steaming, so keep them spaced out.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sweet potatoes evenly coated and spread in a single layer
Step 3
In the same bowl, toss the green beans with the remaining olive oil, a pinch of salt, and the minced garlic. Arrange the green beans next to the sweet potatoes on the baking sheet, keeping them in a single layer. Garlic adds a lovely aroma and flavor during roasting but be careful not to overdo it because it can burn at high temperatures. Spreading the veggies out ensures even roasting and crisp edges.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Green beans glossy and evenly coated with oil and garlic
Step 4
In a small bowl, mix the lemon juice, remaining lemon zest, oregano, salt, and pepper. Brush this lemon herb mixture all over the chicken breasts. The acidity in the lemon juice tenderizes the chicken while infusing bright flavor. Don’t skip this step — it’s what really lifts the dish. Make sure to coat both sides evenly for balanced flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken breasts fully coated with lemon herb marinade
Step 5
Place the chicken breasts on the baking sheet, nestled between the sweet potatoes and green beans but not touching. Spacing is important so heat circulates evenly and the chicken roasts rather than steams. If the pan is overcrowded, the chicken won’t brown properly and veggies can get soggy.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken breasts spaced apart and surrounded by veggies
Step 6
Roast everything in the preheated oven for 25-30 minutes. Halfway through, use tongs to flip the chicken breasts over and toss the veggies gently to ensure even cooking and browning. This flipping step helps the chicken cook evenly and the veggies get crisp on all sides. If you skip this, you might end up with unevenly cooked chicken or burnt edges on the vegetables.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken reaches an internal temperature of 165°F; veggies are tender and lightly caramelized
Step 7
Remove the pan from the oven and let the chicken rest for 5 minutes before slicing. Resting allows the juices to redistribute throughout the meat, making every bite juicy and tender. If you cut into it immediately, the juices will run out and the chicken can be dry.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken feels firm but yields slightly when pressed
Step 8
Taste the vegetables and adjust seasoning if needed — sometimes a sprinkle of flaky sea salt or a dash of freshly ground black pepper brightens everything up right before serving. You can also squeeze a little extra lemon juice over the top for a fresh pop of acidity. These finishing touches make a big difference.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced seasoning and bright citrus aroma
Step 9
Serve the lemon herb chicken alongside the roasted sweet potatoes and green beans. This meal pairs wonderfully with a simple green salad or crusty bread if you want to round it out. It’s wholesome, colorful, and has that perfect mix of savory, sweet, and tangy flavors. Enjoy!
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Plate looks vibrant and inviting, ready to eat

Variations

Spicy Sheet Pan Chicken with Paprika and Chili Flakes

Add 1 teaspoon smoked paprika and 1/2 teaspoon chili flakes to the lemon herb marinade for a smoky, spicy kick. This variation pairs beautifully with the sweetness of the potatoes and adds warmth to the green beans.

Note: Spices may darken faster; keep an eye during roasting to prevent burning.

Mediterranean Style with Olives and Feta

After roasting, toss the green beans with halved Kalamata olives and crumble feta cheese over the chicken and veggies. This adds a briny, creamy contrast that brightens the meal.

Note: Add olives and feta only after cooking to maintain their texture and flavor.

Honey Mustard Glazed Chicken

Mix 2 tablespoons honey and 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard into the lemon herb marinade. This glaze adds a sweet tang that caramelizes beautifully on the chicken during roasting.

Note: Honey can brown quickly, so monitor the chicken closely towards the end of cooking.

Garlic Parmesan Roasted Veggies

Sprinkle freshly grated Parmesan over the green beans and sweet potatoes before the last 5 minutes of roasting. The cheese melts and crisps up, adding savory richness.

Note: Add cheese late to prevent burning and maintain a crispy texture.

Sheet Pan Chicken with Brussels Sprouts and Bacon

Swap green beans for halved Brussels sprouts and toss with chopped cooked bacon before roasting. The smoky bacon flavor complements the lemon herb chicken perfectly.

Note: Brussels sprouts may require a slightly longer roasting time for tenderness.

Pro Tips

Pat the chicken dry before seasoning.
Drying the chicken removes excess moisture that can cause steaming instead of roasting. This helps achieve a beautifully browned, flavorful crust. If the chicken is wet, it won’t brown properly and can end up rubbery.
Don’t overcrowd the baking sheet.
Giving ingredients space allows hot air to circulate, which is essential for even roasting and caramelization. Crowded pans cause steaming, resulting in soggy veggies and pale chicken.
Flip the chicken halfway through cooking.
Flipping ensures both sides brown evenly and helps prevent drying out. It also redistributes juices inside the meat, making it juicier and more tender.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice.
Fresh lemon zest contains aromatic oils that brighten the dish, while fresh juice tenderizes the chicken and balances flavors. Bottled lemon juice lacks these qualities and can taste harsh.
Trim green beans before roasting.
Removing tough ends helps the beans cook evenly and improves texture. Untrimmed beans can have fibrous bits that detract from the eating experience.
Let chicken rest before slicing.
Resting allows juices to redistribute inside the meat, resulting in moist, tender chicken. Cutting immediately causes juices to escape, leaving the chicken dry.
Use parchment paper or foil for easy cleanup.
Lining the baking sheet prevents sticking and makes cleanup a breeze, encouraging you to make this healthy meal more often without the dreaded mess.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Cooking chicken straight from the fridge.

Why it happens Many people rush to cook chicken immediately after taking it out of the fridge, assuming it saves time.

Fix Let the chicken sit at room temperature for 15-20 minutes before roasting. This helps it cook more evenly and prevents the outside from drying out while the inside remains undercooked.

Skipping the toss and flip halfway through roasting.

Why it happens Some skip this step to save time, not realizing it affects texture and cooking evenness.

Fix Take the extra few minutes to flip the chicken and stir the veggies. This ensures even browning and prevents burnt or undercooked spots.

Overcrowding the pan.

Why it happens Trying to fit everything on one pan can cause overcrowding, especially when doubling the recipe.

Fix Use two pans if needed. Spacing ingredients properly allows hot air circulation, which is crucial for roasting rather than steaming.

Using bottled lemon juice instead of fresh.

Why it happens Bottled juice is convenient but often used out of habit.

Fix Always use fresh lemon juice and zest for the brightest flavor and tenderizing benefits. It makes a noticeable difference.

Not drying vegetables before tossing in oil.

Why it happens People often rinse veggies but don’t dry them thoroughly.

Fix Pat veggies dry with a towel before oiling to help them roast rather than steam, resulting in better caramelization and texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This dish can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil. Beyond that, bacteria can grow, so it’s best to refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The chicken may firm up, and veggies can lose some crispness. Reheat gently to avoid drying out, or enjoy cold in salads.

Freezer

Freeze portions in airtight containers or heavy-duty freezer bags for up to 2 months. To prevent freezer burn, remove as much air as possible before sealing. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat gently in a 350°F oven for about 15 minutes covered with foil, or microwave covered on medium power to retain moisture. Add a splash of water or broth if it seems dry. Avoid high heat reheating that can toughen chicken.

Make Ahead

You can prep the marinade and chop veggies 1-2 days ahead, storing them separately in airtight containers. Assemble the sheet pan on the day of cooking for best texture and freshness.

FAQ

Can I use bone-in chicken thighs instead of breasts?
Absolutely! Bone-in thighs add more flavor and stay juicy, but they take longer to cook — about 35-40 minutes at the same temperature. Make sure to check the internal temperature reaches 165°F and adjust cooking time accordingly.
What if I don’t have sweet potatoes? Can I use regular potatoes?
Yes, regular russet or Yukon gold potatoes work well too. They might need a bit longer to roast to become tender, so cut them into smaller pieces for even cooking alongside the chicken.
How do I prevent the green beans from getting mushy?
Trim and dry the green beans well before roasting, and don’t overcrowd the pan. Roast them in a single layer and avoid overcooking by checking their texture at 20 minutes; they should be tender but still crisp.
Can I prepare this recipe in advance?
You can marinate the chicken and chop the veggies a day ahead, storing them separately in the fridge. Assemble and roast the dish on the day you want to serve for best flavor and texture.
What can I substitute for olive oil?
Avocado oil or light vegetable oil are good substitutes as they have high smoke points and neutral flavors. Avoid butter for roasting at this high heat as it can burn.
Is this recipe gluten-free?
Yes, this recipe is naturally gluten-free as long as you use gluten-free seasonings. Just double-check any spice blends or packaged ingredients you use to be sure.
How can I make this recipe spicier?
Add chili flakes or cayenne pepper to the lemon herb marinade for some heat. You can also toss the veggies with a pinch of smoked paprika or chili powder before roasting.
Can I use frozen green beans?
Frozen green beans tend to release more moisture and can become soggy when roasted. If you use frozen, thaw and pat them dry thoroughly before tossing with oil and seasoning.
