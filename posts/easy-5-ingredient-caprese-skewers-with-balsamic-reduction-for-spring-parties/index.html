Fresh and Easy Caprese Skewers with Sweet Balsamic Glaze

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 9, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These Caprese skewers are my quick fix for a fresh, light snack. Juicy tomatoes, creamy mozzarella, and basil come together with a sweet balsamic drizzle. Perfect for spring picnics or casual get-togethers!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon Caprese skewers at a friend's spring picnic a few years ago, and I've been hooked ever since. They're such a fresh, vibrant way to enjoy classic Caprese salad flavors but in a fun, bite-sized form that's perfect for sharing. What I love about this version is the homemade balsamic glaze—it's sweet and tangy, and it really elevates the simple ingredients. The trick I learned is to simmer the balsamic vinegar slowly with honey until it thickens into a syrupy glaze, which adds that perfect touch of richness without overpowering the freshness. Whenever I serve these, whether for casual backyard gatherings or just a light snack, they disappear fast. The combination of juicy tomatoes, creamy mozzarella, and fragrant basil is just so satisfying. Plus, they're super easy to assemble, making them a no-fuss option for busy days or last-minute guests. The colors are so inviting too, making the platter look gorgeous and appetizing. Honestly, these skewers are one of my go-to appetizers when I want something quick, fresh, and crowd-pleasing.

What You'll Need

Approximately 20 skewers, 4 servings

  • 20 cherry tomatoes, washed
  • 20 small fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini), drained
  • 20 fresh basil leaves
  • 1/2 cup balsamic vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 20 small wooden skewers

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preparing the balsamic glaze. Pour the balsamic vinegar into a small saucepan and add the honey. Bring it to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally. The goal is to reduce the vinegar by about half until it thickens into a syrupy consistency. This slow simmer intensifies the flavor and sweetness, balancing the acidity of the vinegar. Be careful not to let it boil rapidly or burn, as that will make the glaze bitter. Once thickened, remove from heat and stir in the olive oil to add a silky texture and mellow the sharpness.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: The glaze should coat the back of a spoon and have a syrupy thickness.
Step 2
While the glaze cools, prepare the skewers. Start by threading one cherry tomato onto a wooden skewer, then fold a fresh basil leaf in half and add it next. Follow with a mozzarella ball. Repeat this pattern for all skewers. The order helps keep the basil fresh and prevents it from bruising too much. Using fresh basil is key here because it adds a fragrant, peppery note that complements the creamy cheese and sweet tomatoes. Avoid overloading the skewers to keep them easy to eat and visually appealing.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers should look balanced with vibrant colors and no bruised leaves.
Step 3
Arrange the skewers on a serving platter. Lightly sprinkle salt and freshly ground black pepper over them. This seasoning step is important because it enhances the natural flavors of the tomatoes and mozzarella. Don't skip it—just a pinch will do. The salt draws out a bit of the tomato’s juice and brings out the creamy texture of the cheese, while the pepper adds a subtle bite.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly seasoned skewers with a hint of salt and pepper on the surface.
Step 4
Drizzle the cooled balsamic glaze generously over the skewers right before serving. This final step ties all the flavors together. The glaze’s sweetness contrasts beautifully with the savory cheese and fresh basil, creating a perfect balance. If you drizzle too early, the glaze might soak into the tomatoes and make them soggy, so timing is important.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers glossy with a thin, even layer of glaze.
Step 5
Serve the Caprese skewers immediately for the freshest taste and best texture. If you need to prepare them ahead, wait to add the balsamic glaze until just before serving to prevent sogginess. These skewers are great as finger food at parties or as a light appetizer before dinner.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fresh and vibrant skewers ready to enjoy.
Step 6
If you want to add a little extra flair, sprinkle some freshly chopped oregano or drizzle a bit of high-quality extra virgin olive oil over the skewers for an herbal boost. This enhances the Mediterranean vibe and adds depth without overpowering the main ingredients.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Herb garnish evenly distributed and olive oil sheen visible.
Step 7
For a slight twist, try swapping cherry tomatoes for grape tomatoes or heirloom mini tomatoes. Each variety offers a unique flavor and texture, so experiment to find your favorite. Just be sure to choose ripe, firm tomatoes that hold up well on skewers.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomatoes look plump and fresh on skewers.
Step 8
If mozzarella balls are large, slice them in half before skewering to keep bites small and manageable. This makes eating easier and ensures the perfect balance of tomato, basil, and cheese in every mouthful.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mozzarella pieces are bite-sized and evenly sized.
Step 9
Keep the skewers chilled until serving to maintain freshness. If you need to prepare them earlier, cover the platter with plastic wrap and refrigerate. Just remember to add the balsamic glaze last minute to avoid sogginess.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers stay fresh and vibrant after refrigeration.

Variations

Spicy Caprese Skewers

Add a small slice of fresh jalapeño or a sprinkle of crushed red pepper flakes to each skewer for a spicy kick. The heat pairs surprisingly well with the sweet balsamic glaze and creamy mozzarella, adding complexity without overpowering the classic flavors.

Note: Be careful not to add too much jalapeño to avoid overwhelming the delicate basil and cheese flavors.

Caprese Skewers with Pesto Drizzle

Swap the balsamic glaze for a drizzle of fresh basil pesto for an herbal, nutty twist. This variation adds richness and a vibrant green color, perfect for pesto lovers looking for a fresh appetizer.

Note: Use a thin, smooth pesto to keep drizzling easy and avoid overpowering the mozzarella.

Vegan Caprese Skewers

Replace mozzarella balls with marinated tofu cubes or vegan mozzarella alternatives. The balsamic glaze and fresh basil keep the flavor vibrant and delicious, making this a great option for plant-based diets.

Note: Choose a firm tofu and marinate it briefly in olive oil and herbs to mimic the creamy texture.

Caprese Skewers with Prosciutto

Add a small folded slice of prosciutto to each skewer for a salty, savory boost. This makes the appetizer heartier and ideal for meat lovers who want a bit more substance.

Note: Fold prosciutto thinly to keep skewers neat and balanced.

Heirloom Tomato Caprese Skewers

Use a mix of colorful heirloom cherry tomatoes for a beautiful presentation and slightly varied flavor profile. Their natural sweetness and acidity add extra interest to this classic bite.

Note: Choose firm heirlooms to maintain skewer integrity.

Pro Tips

Simmer the balsamic glaze slowly over low heat to avoid burning.
Balsamic vinegar contains sugars that can quickly burn if heated too fast or at too high a temperature, resulting in a bitter taste. Slow simmering allows the sugars to caramelize gently, producing a rich, smooth glaze that enhances the dish’s flavor without bitterness.
Add the balsamic glaze just before serving to keep tomatoes firm.
If the glaze is added too early, the acidity can break down the tomatoes and mozzarella, making them mushy and soggy. Waiting until the last minute preserves the fresh textures and vibrant colors, maintaining the dish’s appealing bite and appearance.
Use small wooden skewers or toothpicks for easy serving and eating.
Short skewers make these bite-sized appetizers manageable and mess-free, encouraging guests to grab and enjoy without utensils. Longer skewers can be unwieldy and less elegant, so size matters for presentation and practicality.
Drain mozzarella balls well to avoid watery skewers.
Excess moisture from the cheese can dilute the flavors and cause the skewers to become soggy. Proper draining ensures that the mozzarella retains its creamy texture and that the overall skewer stays fresh and appetizing.
Choose ripe but firm cherry tomatoes for the best texture.
Tomatoes that are too soft will fall apart on the skewer, while underripe ones lack sweetness and juiciness. Firm, ripe tomatoes provide a perfect juicy pop without compromising the skewer’s structure.
Season lightly with salt and pepper after assembling the skewers.
A light seasoning enhances the natural flavors without overpowering them. Salt draws out sweetness and balances acidity, while pepper adds a subtle kick. Seasoning before skewering can cause uneven distribution or bruising of the basil.
Keep skewers refrigerated until serving to maintain freshness.
Chilling helps the mozzarella stay firm and the basil leaves stay vibrant. Room temperature can cause the cheese to soften too much and the herbs to wilt, affecting both taste and presentation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Adding balsamic glaze too early

Why it happens Many think drizzling the glaze early will save time, but it causes the tomatoes and cheese to release moisture and become soggy.

Fix Always add the balsamic glaze right before serving to keep the ingredients fresh and maintain the ideal texture contrast.

Using low-quality mozzarella

Why it happens Sometimes pre-shredded or low-moisture mozzarella is used for convenience, but it lacks the creamy texture and flavor needed.

Fix Use fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini) and drain well to get that authentic, creamy bite essential for Caprese.

Skipping seasoning with salt and pepper

Why it happens People often rely on the glaze alone for flavor and forget basic seasoning, leading to a bland taste.

Fix Lightly season the skewers after assembling to enhance all the fresh flavors without overpowering.

Bruising the basil leaves

Why it happens Handling basil roughly or placing it on the outside causes bruising and browning, which can turn bitter.

Fix Fold basil leaves gently and place them between tomato and mozzarella to protect their color and flavor.

Using overripe or watery tomatoes

Why it happens Overripe tomatoes can burst and release too much juice, making skewers soggy and messy.

Fix Choose firm, ripe cherry tomatoes that hold their shape but still have juicy sweetness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Caprese skewers can safely sit out for up to 2 hours during serving. Cover loosely with a clean cloth or dome to protect from insects and dust. Beyond this, freshness and safety decline.

Refrigerator

Store assembled skewers without balsamic glaze in an airtight container for up to 1 day. Add glaze just before serving. The basil may wilt slightly and the cheese soften when chilled, so serve soon after removing from fridge.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as fresh tomatoes, basil, and mozzarella lose texture and flavor. For longer storage, keep components separate and assemble fresh.

Reheating

No reheating needed—serve chilled or at room temperature. If desired, allow skewers to sit out 10 minutes before serving to soften mozzarella slightly.

Make Ahead

Prep the balsamic glaze and keep it refrigerated up to 3 days. Assemble skewers without glaze up to 1 day ahead and cover tightly. Drizzle glaze just before serving for best freshness and texture.

FAQ

Can I use regular mozzarella instead of fresh mozzarella balls?
Regular mozzarella, especially low-moisture shredded types, won’t give the same creamy texture or fresh flavor. Fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini) are softer and have a delicate milkiness that’s essential for authentic Caprese skewers. If using larger fresh mozzarella blocks, slice into bite-sized pieces to mimic the texture.
How do I make the balsamic glaze thicker?
Simmer the balsamic vinegar and honey slowly over low heat, stirring occasionally until the mixture reduces by about half and coats the back of a spoon. Avoid boiling rapidly as it can burn. If the glaze isn’t thick enough, keep simmering gently until the desired syrupy consistency is reached.
Can I prepare the skewers in advance?
Yes, you can assemble the skewers without the balsamic glaze up to 1 day ahead. Keep them covered and refrigerated. Add the glaze just before serving to prevent sogginess and keep the tomatoes and basil fresh.
What can I substitute for fresh basil if I don’t have any?
Fresh basil is key for classic Caprese flavor, but if unavailable, fresh oregano or mint can be used as alternatives. They provide a fresh herbal note, though the flavor profile will shift slightly. Avoid dried herbs as they lack the vibrant aroma and texture.
Is there a way to make these skewers vegan?
Absolutely! Replace mozzarella with marinated tofu cubes or store-bought vegan mozzarella alternatives. The balsamic glaze and fresh basil still provide plenty of flavor, making it a delicious vegan option.
How long does the balsamic glaze keep?
Stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator, balsamic glaze can last up to 2 weeks. Reheat gently or bring to room temperature before drizzling to restore its syrupy consistency.
Can I use grape tomatoes instead of cherry tomatoes?
Yes, grape tomatoes work well as a substitute. They are slightly less round but similarly sweet and juicy. Just make sure they are ripe and firm to hold up on the skewers.
Why did my balsamic glaze turn bitter?
Balsamic vinegar can burn easily if cooked at too high heat or for too long. This caramelization turns bitter. To avoid this, simmer gently over low heat and stir frequently. Remove from heat as soon as it thickens.
