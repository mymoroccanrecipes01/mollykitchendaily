Cozy Pumpkin Spice Muffins for Perfect Fall Mornings

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 6, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These pumpkin spice muffins are my go-to for chilly mornings. The fresh pumpkin puree and warm spices make them taste like autumn in every bite. Great for breakfast or a comforting snack.

Introduction

I first made these pumpkin spice muffins a few years ago when I was craving something warm and cozy for a crisp fall morning. I’d tried a few recipes before, but they often came out dry or just a bit bland. What changed everything was using fresh pumpkin puree instead of canned, which gives the muffins a natural sweetness and moist texture that’s hard to beat. Plus, I love adding a blend of warming spices like cinnamon, nutmeg, and a hint of clove to really bring out that classic autumn vibe. One trick I learned is to mix the wet and dry ingredients just until combined — overmixing can make the muffins tough, and nobody wants that! The result is these soft, fluffy muffins that are bursting with fall flavor and perfect for breakfast or an afternoon snack. Whenever I bake a batch, the whole house smells amazing, and it’s like a little celebration of the season. I think you’re going to love how easy and rewarding this recipe is to make, especially when you want something comforting and delicious without fuss.

What You'll Need

12 muffins

  • 1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 cup fresh pumpkin puree
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup packed light brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a 12-cup muffin tin with paper liners or grease it lightly. Getting the oven fully heated ensures the muffins bake evenly and rise properly. If the oven isn’t hot enough, the muffins might come out dense or undercooked. Setting up your tin now also helps speed up the process once the batter is ready.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated to 350°F and muffin tin prepared
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, and ginger. Mixing the dry ingredients well distributes the leavening agents and spices evenly, which is crucial for consistent texture and flavor throughout the muffins. Avoid lumps by whisking thoroughly but don’t overwork the flour mixture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients fully combined with no clumps
Step 3
In another bowl, combine the pumpkin puree, granulated sugar, brown sugar, vegetable oil, eggs, and vanilla extract. Beat these together until smooth and well blended. The sugars dissolve into the pumpkin and oil, creating a moist base that helps tenderize the muffins. The vanilla adds depth to the flavor. Make sure the eggs are fully incorporated to avoid streaks in the batter.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients form a smooth, uniform mixture
Step 4
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and gently fold together using a spatula. Mix just until no dry flour remains. This step is key — overmixing develops gluten and makes muffins tough. The batter should be thick but still a bit lumpy. Stop folding once combined for tender, fluffy muffins.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is just combined, slightly lumpy, thick consistency
Step 5
Fold in the chopped nuts if using. Nuts add a lovely crunch and complement the spices, but this is optional. Stir gently to distribute evenly without breaking them up too much. If you prefer a nut-free version, you can skip this or substitute with chocolate chips for a sweeter twist.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Nuts evenly mixed in without over-handling the batter
Step 6
Spoon the batter evenly into the muffin cups, filling each about 2/3 full. This allows room for the muffins to rise without overflowing. Using an ice cream scoop or large spoon helps portion evenly for uniform baking. Avoid filling too full or too low to ensure proper muffin shape and texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each muffin cup filled evenly about two-thirds full
Step 7
Bake in the preheated oven for 20-25 minutes. Check at 20 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center of a muffin—if it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, they’re done. Baking too long dries them out, while underbaking leaves them gooey inside. The muffins should be golden brown on top and spring back slightly when touched.
⏱ Time: 20-25 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden tops and toothpick inserted comes out clean
Step 8
Remove the muffins from the oven and let them cool in the tin for about 5 minutes. This resting time helps them firm up a bit so they don’t crumble when you take them out. Then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely or enjoy warm. Cooling fully allows the flavors to settle and the texture to set perfectly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffins slightly cooled and firm enough to handle
Step 9
Serve warm or at room temperature, plain or with a little butter or cream cheese. These muffins are wonderful fresh but also store well for a few days. Reheat gently before serving to revive that fresh-baked taste. Perfect with coffee or tea on a crisp fall morning!
⏱ Time: Immediate serving or storage
✓ Doneness: Muffins taste moist, tender, and flavorful when eaten

Variations

Chocolate Chip Pumpkin Muffins

Add 1/2 cup of semi-sweet chocolate chips to the batter for a sweet twist. The melty chocolate pairs wonderfully with pumpkin and spices, giving a rich flavor contrast. Great for kids and chocolate lovers!

Note: Fold chocolate chips in gently after combining wet and dry ingredients to avoid breaking them up.

Vegan Pumpkin Muffins

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use coconut oil instead of vegetable oil. These muffins stay moist and flavorful without any animal products.

Note: Let flax eggs sit for 5 minutes to thicken before mixing into wet ingredients.

Gluten-Free Pumpkin Muffins

Swap all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend. Add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t contain it to help with structure. Expect a slightly denser texture but still delicious and moist.

Note: Mix batter gently and avoid overmixing to keep muffins tender.

Pumpkin Walnut Streusel Muffins

Top muffins with a simple streusel made from brown sugar, flour, butter, and chopped walnuts before baking. It adds a crunchy, sweet topping that contrasts beautifully with the soft pumpkin crumb.

Note: Press streusel lightly on top of batter before putting in the oven to ensure it sticks.

Spiced Pumpkin Banana Muffins

Add one ripe mashed banana to the wet ingredients for extra moisture and natural sweetness. The banana complements the pumpkin and spices, making these muffins extra tender and flavorful.

Note: Reduce sugar by 1/4 cup to balance the banana’s sweetness.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature ingredients for better mixing.
Ingredients like eggs and pumpkin puree blend more evenly when at room temperature, preventing lumps and ensuring uniform batter. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to seize or separate, leading to uneven texture in the muffins.
Don't overmix the batter after combining wet and dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which makes muffins tough and chewy instead of tender and soft. Fold gently until the flour is just incorporated, even if the batter looks a bit lumpy—that’s perfect.
Add a touch of oil instead of butter for moistness.
Oil remains liquid at room temperature, which helps keep the muffins moist longer than butter, which solidifies. It also contributes to a tender crumb without weighing down the texture.
Use fresh spices for maximum flavor.
Spices lose potency over time, so fresh ground cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves ensure your muffins have that fragrant, warm aroma that defines fall baking. Old spices can make baked goods taste dull.
Fill muffin cups about two-thirds full.
Filling too full causes muffins to overflow and bake unevenly, while filling too low results in flat muffins. Two-thirds full gives enough room for the batter to rise perfectly with a nice dome.
Test doneness with a toothpick near the end of baking.
Checking with a toothpick prevents overbaking, which dries the muffins, or underbaking, which leaves them gooey inside. A clean or slightly crumbly toothpick means perfect doneness.
Let muffins cool slightly before removing from the tin.
Cooling in the tin allows muffins to set and reduces crumbling. Removing them too soon risks breaking or sticking to the pan.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Many people think mixing longer makes batter smoother, but overmixing activates gluten in the flour, leading to dense, rubbery muffins.

Fix Mix just until the dry ingredients disappear and the batter is slightly lumpy. Use a gentle folding motion with a spatula, and stop as soon as combined.

Using canned pumpkin with added spices or sugars

Why it happens Some canned pumpkins come pre-seasoned, which can throw off the recipe’s balance and cause overpowering flavors.

Fix Always use plain 100% pumpkin puree without additives. If unsure, check the label carefully or substitute with fresh cooked pumpkin.

Not preheating the oven

Why it happens Starting muffins in a cold oven slows rising and can result in dense texture.

Fix Make sure the oven is fully preheated to 350°F before baking. This ensures proper rise and even cooking.

Filling muffin cups unevenly

Why it happens Uneven filling causes muffins to bake irregularly, some overcooked, some undercooked.

Fix Use a scoop or spoon to portion batter evenly into cups, filling about two-thirds full.

Skipping the cooling step

Why it happens Removing muffins too soon can cause them to break apart or stick to the pan.

Fix Let muffins cool in the tin for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to finish cooling.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Cover well to prevent drying out. They’re best enjoyed fresh but remain soft for a short time.

Refrigerator

Keep muffins in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. They may firm up slightly; reheat gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds to restore softness before eating.

Freezer

Wrap muffins individually in plastic wrap and place in a freezer bag. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature and warm briefly before serving to bring back moistness.

Reheating

Reheat muffins in the microwave for 15-30 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of water before warming helps keep them moist. Avoid overheating to prevent dryness.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and refrigerate it overnight. Give it a gentle stir before scooping into muffin cups and baking. Alternatively, bake muffins fully, then freeze for quick grab-and-go snacks.

FAQ

Can I use canned pumpkin instead of fresh puree?
Yes, 100% pure canned pumpkin works well if fresh isn’t available. Just avoid pumpkin pie filling, which has added sugar and spices that can throw off the recipe. Canned pumpkin might make muffins slightly denser but still delicious.
How do I store leftover muffins to keep them fresh?
Store muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for a couple of days. For longer storage, refrigerate or freeze them wrapped tightly. Reheat gently before serving to restore moisture and softness.
Can I make these muffins gluten-free?
Absolutely. Substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend and add xanthan gum if needed. The texture might be a bit denser, but the flavor stays wonderful.
Why did my muffins turn out dry?
Dry muffins often result from overbaking or overmixing the batter. Make sure to bake just until a toothpick comes out clean and mix the batter gently until combined.
Can I add mix-ins like nuts or chocolate chips?
Yes, adding nuts or chocolate chips is a great way to customize these muffins. Fold them in gently after combining the wet and dry ingredients to avoid breaking them up.
How do I prevent muffins from sticking to the pan?
Use paper liners or grease the muffin tin well with butter or non-stick spray. Let muffins cool slightly before removing them from the pan to reduce sticking.
Can I make the batter ahead of time?
Yes, you can prepare the batter and refrigerate it overnight. Give it a gentle stir before scooping into muffin cups. This can actually enhance the flavors as the spices meld.
Why are my muffins dense and not fluffy?
Dense muffins usually result from overmixing, using expired baking soda/powder, or incorrect flour measurements. Make sure to measure ingredients accurately and mix just until combined.
