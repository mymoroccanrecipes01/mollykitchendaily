Simple Zucchini Boats Stuffed with Seasoned Ground Chicken and Cheese

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 31, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These zucchini boats are a wholesome, easy dinner that feels a bit fancy. The secret is baking them until the cheese bubbles and turns golden. Perfect for a quick weeknight meal that still feels special.

Introduction

I first tried zucchini boats a few years ago when I wanted a light but satisfying dinner that didn’t feel like a salad. Since then, I’ve tweaked this recipe to make it my go-to for busy weeknights. Using ground chicken keeps it lean, and seasoning it well gives the filling great depth. The trick I learned is to scoop the zucchini just right—not too thin to hold the filling, but not too thick so it bakes tender. Baking until the cheese melts and browns gives it that irresistible golden finish that makes everyone come back for seconds. It’s a simple recipe, but the flavors and textures feel special—kind of like a cozy dinner that’s both healthy and comforting at once. I love serving it with a side salad or some crusty bread to soak up any juices. If you’re looking for something quick, wholesome, and a little different, this recipe’s a winner—trust me, my family asks for it often!

What You'll Need

4 stuffed zucchini boats, serves 4

  • 4 medium zucchinis, halved lengthwise
  • 1 lb ground chicken
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian seasoning
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (optional, for garnish)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). While it warms up, wash the zucchinis and slice them lengthwise. Using a spoon, carefully scoop out the center flesh, leaving about a 1/4-inch thick shell to hold the filling. Be gentle so the boats don’t break apart during baking. This step is key because it creates the perfect vessel that balances tender zucchini with a sturdy base for the filling. Avoid scooping too deep or the boats will collapse.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini halves are hollowed but still firm and intact
Step 2
Heat olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add chopped onion and sauté until soft and translucent, about 3-4 minutes. Then add minced garlic and cook for another 30 seconds until fragrant. This gentle cooking releases the flavors without burning, which would add bitterness. Avoid rushing this step; properly softened onions add sweetness and depth to the filling.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions are soft and translucent, garlic is fragrant but not browned
Step 3
Add the ground chicken to the skillet with onions and garlic. Break it up with a spatula and cook until no longer pink, around 7-8 minutes. Stir occasionally to ensure even cooking and to prevent clumps. Properly cooked chicken is essential for safety and texture—undercooked meat can be chewy or unsafe, while overcooked can dry out.
⏱ Time: 8 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken is fully cooked, no pink remains, and is crumbly
Step 4
Chop the scooped zucchini flesh finely and add it to the skillet along with dried Italian seasoning, salt, and pepper. Cook for another 3-4 minutes to soften the zucchini and meld flavors. This step adds moisture and a subtle zucchini flavor to the filling, ensuring nothing goes to waste. Skipping this could leave the filling dry or underflavored.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini flesh is tender and well combined with chicken
Step 5
Spoon the chicken mixture evenly into each zucchini boat, pressing lightly to fill them well. Don’t overfill or the filling might spill over during baking. Even distribution helps the boats cook uniformly and ensures every bite has a good meat-to-zucchini ratio.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: All boats are filled evenly with no overflow
Step 6
Sprinkle shredded mozzarella generously over the stuffed zucchinis, then finish with a dusting of Parmesan cheese. The mozzarella melts into a gooey layer while the Parmesan adds a nice salty crust. This cheesy topping is what makes the dish feel indulgent and comforting.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese layer covers filling evenly
Step 7
Place the stuffed zucchini boats on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper or foil for easy cleanup. Bake in the preheated oven for about 25-30 minutes. The zucchini should be tender but not mushy, and the cheese on top should be melted and golden brown. Keep an eye toward the end to avoid burning the cheese.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese is bubbly and golden, zucchini is soft when pierced with a fork
Step 8
Remove from oven and let the zucchini boats rest for 5 minutes before serving. This resting time helps the filling set slightly so it doesn’t spill when you cut into it. Plus, the flavors settle and meld together better after resting.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling is set but still warm and cheesy
Step 9
Garnish with freshly chopped parsley or your favorite herbs for a fresh pop of color and flavor. Serve warm with a simple side like a green salad or garlic bread. This final touch elevates the presentation and adds brightness to the rich, cheesy filling.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini boats look vibrant and inviting on the plate

Variations

Spicy Zucchini Boats

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or chopped jalapeños to the chicken mixture for a spicy kick. You can also swap mozzarella for pepper jack cheese to amp up the heat. Expect a lively, bold flavor that contrasts nicely with the mild zucchini.

Note: Watch the heat level carefully to avoid overpowering the dish. Baking time remains the same.

Vegetarian Zucchini Boats

Replace ground chicken with cooked quinoa or lentils and add sautéed mushrooms for a hearty vegetarian option. Use a little extra seasoning and maybe a splash of soy sauce for umami. The texture will be different—more grainy and earthy—but still satisfying.

Note: Cook filling ingredients thoroughly before stuffing. Baking time is unchanged.

Mediterranean Style

Mix in chopped sun-dried tomatoes, olives, and feta cheese into the filling. Use oregano and basil for seasoning. This variation adds tangy, briny notes and a fresh Mediterranean vibe.

Note: Feta doesn’t melt like mozzarella, so add it after baking or mix with mozzarella for best results.

Cheesy Ranch Zucchini Boats

Add ranch seasoning mix to the chicken and top with cheddar cheese instead of mozzarella. This gives the boats a creamy, tangy flavor loved by kids and adults alike.

Note: Cheddar melts differently—watch baking closely to avoid overbrowning.

Low-Carb Zucchini Boats with Cauliflower Rice

Add cooked cauliflower rice to the chicken filling to bulk it up and add texture without carbs. Season with garlic and herbs. This variation is great for keto or low-carb diets.

Note: Cauliflower rice cooks quickly; ensure it’s drained well to avoid watery filling.

Pro Tips

Don’t over-scoop the zucchini shells
Leaving about a 1/4-inch thick shell ensures the boats hold their shape during baking. Too thin and they’ll collapse or leak filling, too thick and the zucchini won’t get tender enough. The right thickness balances texture and structural integrity.
Cook onions and garlic gently before adding chicken
Softening onions slowly releases their natural sweetness and mellows garlic’s sharpness. This foundational flavor layer makes the filling more complex and prevents harsh, burnt notes that happen with high heat or rushing.
Use a mix of mozzarella and Parmesan cheeses
Mozzarella melts into a creamy, stretchy layer, while Parmesan crisps up and adds salty, nutty flavor. This combo creates a pleasing contrast in texture and taste that elevates the dish beyond a simple stuffed vegetable.
Let the stuffed boats rest after baking
Resting lets the filling firm up so it won’t spill out when serving. It also allows flavors to meld and the cheese to settle, improving both presentation and taste.
Season the filling generously but taste as you go
Balanced seasoning is key. Undersalted filling will taste bland, but too much salt can overwhelm the zucchini’s mildness. Tasting the cooked filling before stuffing helps you adjust seasoning perfectly.
Use fresh herbs for garnish
Adding fresh parsley or basil brightens the dish visually and flavor-wise. It adds a fresh, herbal note that contrasts nicely with the rich cheese and savory chicken.
Line your baking sheet with parchment or foil
This makes cleanup easier since some filling and cheese might bubble over. It also prevents sticking and burning on the pan, preserving the golden crust on the boats.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Scooping zucchini too thin or too thick

Why it happens People often either take out too much flesh, making the boats fragile, or leave too much, resulting in undercooked, tough zucchini. It’s easy to misjudge without experience.

Fix Aim for about 1/4 inch thickness of the shell. Use a small spoon and go slowly. If the shell feels flimsy, you can reinforce it by baking the empty boats for 5 minutes before filling.

Skipping the step of cooking the zucchini flesh with chicken

Why it happens To save time, some skip cooking the scooped zucchini with the filling. But raw zucchini releases water and lacks flavor.

Fix Always cook zucchini flesh with the chicken mixture so it softens and combines flavors. This prevents watery filling and improves taste.

Overcooking the boats until mushy

Why it happens Leaving the boats in the oven too long makes the zucchini soggy and the filling dry.

Fix Check at 25 minutes for tender zucchini that still holds shape. Remove as soon as cheese is golden and bubbling.

Using low-moisture cheeses only

Why it happens Some use only Parmesan or dry cheeses, missing the creamy melt of mozzarella.

Fix Use a combination of mozzarella for melt and Parmesan for flavor and crust. This balance enhances texture.

Not seasoning the filling enough

Why it happens Zucchini is mild, so under-seasoned filling tastes bland.

Fix Season generously with salt, pepper, and Italian herbs. Taste the cooked filling before stuffing to adjust.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Stuffed zucchini boats can safely sit out at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The zucchini may release some water when cold, so reheat gently to restore texture. Reheating in the oven helps maintain crispness better than microwave.

Freezer

Freeze leftover zucchini boats wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and foil for up to 2 months. To prevent freezer burn, remove as much air as possible. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat in a 350°F oven for 15-20 minutes until warmed through and cheese is bubbly. Adding a splash of water or broth before reheating helps prevent dryness. Avoid microwaving directly as it can make zucchini soggy.

Make Ahead

Prepare the filling and hollowed zucchini shells 1-2 days ahead. Store filling in the fridge and zucchini boats covered separately. Assemble and bake on the day of serving for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use ground turkey instead of ground chicken?
Yes, ground turkey works well as a substitute and has a similar mild flavor. Just be mindful that turkey breast can be leaner and may dry out faster, so cook gently and consider adding a little olive oil or moisture to the filling.
How do I prevent zucchini boats from getting soggy?
Scooping the zucchini carefully to leave a sturdy shell is key. Also, cooking the filling with the zucchini flesh helps reduce excess moisture. Baking at the right temperature and not overcooking ensures the zucchini stays tender but not mushy.
Can I make these zucchini boats vegan?
Absolutely! Swap ground chicken for cooked lentils or crumbled tofu, and replace cheese with vegan cheese alternatives or nutritional yeast. Adjust seasoning to taste, and bake as usual.
Is it okay to freeze the assembled zucchini boats before baking?
You can freeze them assembled, but the texture might suffer slightly. For best results, freeze filling and zucchini shells separately, then assemble and bake fresh. If freezing assembled, wrap tightly and thaw overnight before baking.
What side dishes go well with zucchini boats?
Simple green salads, garlic bread, or steamed vegetables complement zucchini boats nicely. You could also serve a grain like quinoa or rice if you want a heartier meal.
How do I know when the cheese is perfectly melted and golden?
Look for bubbly cheese that has started to turn a light golden brown, especially around the edges. This indicates it's melted through and has developed nice flavor without burning.
Can I prepare the filling in advance?
Yes, you can cook and season the filling a day ahead and refrigerate it. This actually helps flavors meld. Just fill the zucchini and bake when ready to serve.
What if I don’t have Italian seasoning?
You can mix dried basil, oregano, thyme, and rosemary to create your own blend. Even just basil and oregano alone work fine. Fresh herbs can be used too but add later to avoid burning.
