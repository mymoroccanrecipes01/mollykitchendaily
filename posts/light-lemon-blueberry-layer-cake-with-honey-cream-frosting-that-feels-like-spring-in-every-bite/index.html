Light and Airy Lemon Cake with Fresh Blueberries and Honey Cream Frosting

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 30 min cook 8 servings

This lemon cake is so light and fresh, bursting with juicy blueberries and topped with a honey-sweetened cream frosting that’s just dreamy. The honey adds a delicate floral hint that makes it perfect for spring afternoons or casual celebrations.

Why This Works

This cake’s success lies in balancing moisture, acidity, and texture to create a light, flavorful dessert. The buttermilk is key—it reacts with the baking soda to produce carbon dioxide, which helps the cake rise and stay tender. Buttermilk also adds a subtle tang that complements the lemon zest and juice, enhancing the bright citrus notes.

The gentle folding of blueberries preserves their shape and prevents the batter from turning purple, which keeps the cake visually appealing and adds bursts of fresh flavor. Overmixing the batter would develop gluten, making the cake dense and chewy, so folding just until combined is crucial.

Using oil instead of butter keeps the crumb moist without weighing it down, which is why this cake is lighter than traditional butter cakes. The honey in the frosting adds a floral sweetness that balances the tart lemon and fresh berries, and whipping the cream to soft peaks creates a luscious, airy topping that melts in your mouth.

Finally, letting the cake cool completely before frosting prevents the cream from melting and sliding off, ensuring a beautiful presentation and perfect texture contrast between cake and frosting. Each step is designed to maximize flavor and texture while keeping the process approachable for home bakers.

  • Look for fresh buttermilk with a tangy aroma and creamy texture. It’s essential for reacting with baking soda to help the cake rise and keep the crumb tender. If you don’t have buttermilk, you can make a substitute by adding a tablespoon of lemon juice or vinegar to a cup of milk and letting it sit for 10 minutes.
  • Choose plump, firm blueberries with a deep blue color and a slight bloom (the powdery natural coating). Avoid crushed or overly soft berries as they can bleed into the batter and affect texture. Gently fold them in to keep them whole and evenly distributed.
  • Use unwaxed lemons to get bright, clean zest and juice. The zest contains the essential oils that provide the intense lemon aroma, while the juice adds acidity and tang. Both are crucial for balancing the cake’s sweetness and enhancing flavor complexity.
  • Select a mild, floral honey like clover or wildflower to add subtle sweetness without overpowering. Honey also helps stabilize the whipped cream frosting slightly, giving it a smoother texture and delicate flavor.
  • Use cold, high-fat heavy cream (at least 36% fat) for whipping. The fat content is what allows it to whip into stable peaks, creating the light, airy frosting that complements the cake’s texture perfectly.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • Zest of 2 lemons
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup buttermilk, room temperature
  • 1/4 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 cup fresh blueberries
  • For the frosting:
  • 1 cup heavy cream, cold
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

Introduction

I first made this lemon blueberry cake a few springs ago when I wanted something light but still festive for a weekend get-together. The usual lemon cakes felt too dense or overly sweet, so I experimented with adding buttermilk and a touch of oil to keep it moist but airy. The real game-changer was folding fresh blueberries into the batter and topping the cake with a honey-sweetened whipped cream frosting instead of buttercream. That honey adds this subtle floral sweetness that perfectly balances the tart lemon. I’ve had a few attempts where the cake felt flat or the blueberries sank to the bottom, but I learned to gently fold them in and to not overmix the batter, which keeps the crumb tender and the berries evenly distributed. When it’s done right, the cake is light and fluffy with bursts of juicy blueberry and a creamy, slightly sweet frosting that’s not overpowering. It’s become my go-to for spring afternoon teas or casual celebrations because it feels special but isn’t fussy. Plus, it’s just the right amount of sweet with a fresh zing that’s hard to resist.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and flour an 8-inch round cake pan or line it with parchment paper. This ensures the cake doesn’t stick and comes out cleanly, preserving its delicate crumb. It’s important to prepare your pan well because a sticky cake can tear when you try to remove it, ruining the presentation.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Pan is evenly greased with no dry spots; parchment paper fits snugly
  2. Step 2

    In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Mixing these dry ingredients evenly helps the cake rise uniformly and prevents clumps of leavening agents that could cause uneven texture or bitter spots.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Dry ingredients are well combined with no visible clumps
  3. Step 3

    In a large bowl, combine sugar and lemon zest. Rub the zest into the sugar with your fingers until fragrant. This step releases the oils in the lemon zest, infusing the cake with bright citrus flavor that really shines through in every bite.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Sugar turns slightly moist and smells strongly of lemon
  4. Step 4

    Add eggs to the sugar mixture one at a time, beating well after each addition. The eggs provide structure and moisture, and beating them in properly ensures the batter will be smooth and well-aerated, which contributes to the cake’s lightness.

    ⏱ 4 minutes✓ Batter is smooth, glossy, and slightly thickened
  5. Step 5

    Mix in buttermilk, vegetable oil, lemon juice, and vanilla extract until just combined. The buttermilk adds tang and tenderness, while the oil keeps the cake moist without making it heavy. Mixing gently here avoids developing gluten, which would make the cake tough.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Wet ingredients are fully incorporated with no streaks
  6. Step 6

    Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture carefully using a spatula. Be gentle to keep the batter airy. Overmixing at this stage can lead to a dense, chewy cake instead of a tender crumb.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Batter is smooth with no visible flour, still light in texture
  7. Step 7

    Gently fold in the fresh blueberries, being careful not to crush them. This keeps the berries intact so they burst with juicy flavor during baking rather than bleeding into the batter and turning it purple.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Blueberries are evenly distributed without breaking
  8. Step 8

    Pour the batter into your prepared pan and smooth the top with a spatula. Bake for about 28-32 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Baking at the right temperature and time ensures the cake cooks through without drying out.

    ⏱ 28-32 minutes✓ Toothpick comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, cake springs back when lightly pressed
  9. Step 9

    Let the cake cool completely in the pan on a wire rack before frosting. Cooling prevents the frosting from melting and sliding off, and it allows the crumb to set for easier slicing.

    ⏱ 30 minutes✓ Cake is cool to the touch and no longer warm in the center
  10. Step 10

    For the frosting, whip cold heavy cream with honey and vanilla extract until soft peaks form. Whipping creates a light, airy texture that pairs perfectly with the lemon cake, and the honey adds a natural sweetness that’s less cloying than sugar alone.

    ⏱ 5-7 minutes✓ Cream holds soft peaks, is smooth and glossy
  11. Step 11

    Spread the honey whipped cream over the cooled cake evenly. You can add extra fresh blueberries on top for garnish if you like. This final step adds a creamy, fresh element that balances the tartness and makes the cake feel elegant and fresh.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Frosting is smooth, even, and holds shape

Variations

  • Gluten-Free Lemon Blueberry Cake: Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. The cake will be just as tender and flavorful, perfect for gluten-sensitive friends. Expect a slightly different crumb texture but still moist and airy.
    Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour that contains binding agents to mimic gluten’s structure and prevent crumbling.
  • Vegan Lemon Blueberry Cake: Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg), use coconut yogurt instead of buttermilk, and substitute oil with melted coconut oil. Use coconut cream whipped with maple syrup for frosting. The flavor stays bright and fresh with a plant-based twist.
    Flax eggs add moisture and binding but can slightly change texture, so don’t overmix.
  • Lemon Blueberry Bundt Cake: Bake this batter in a bundt pan for a show-stopping shape. The baking time will increase by about 10-15 minutes. This is great for larger gatherings or gifting.
    Grease the bundt pan thoroughly and dust with flour to prevent sticking due to the pan’s intricate design.
  • Honey-Lavender Lemon Cake: Add 1 tsp dried culinary lavender to the sugar and zest mixture for a subtle floral note that pairs wonderfully with the honey frosting. This elevates the cake for more special occasions.
    Use culinary-grade lavender and avoid overusing as it can become overpowering.
  • Lemon Blueberry Cake with Cream Cheese Frosting: Swap the whipped cream frosting for a tangy cream cheese frosting sweetened with honey. It adds richness and pairs perfectly with the lemon and berries for a more decadent dessert.
    Chill the frosting before spreading to prevent melting and sliding.

Pro Tips

  • Always use room temperature eggs and buttermilk for the batter. — Room temperature ingredients combine more easily, resulting in a smoother batter that traps air better. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, leading to a denser cake texture.
  • Gently fold in the blueberries at the very end to avoid breaking them. — Blueberries are delicate and can release juice if overmixed, which discolors the batter and affects texture. Folding carefully keeps the berries intact and distributes them evenly throughout the cake.
  • Do not overbeat the heavy cream when making the frosting. — Overwhipping cream causes it to turn grainy and eventually separate into butter and liquid. Stop whipping as soon as soft peaks form to get a smooth, airy frosting that melts nicely on the cake.
  • Use fresh lemon zest instead of dried or bottled lemon extract. — Fresh zest contains essential oils that give bright, natural lemon flavor. Extracts can taste artificial and won’t provide the same aromatic punch that fresh zest delivers.
  • Cool the cake completely before frosting. — Applying frosting to a warm cake can cause it to melt and slide off, ruining the presentation and texture. Cooling fully ensures the frosting holds its shape and the cake slices cleanly.
  • Line your cake pan with parchment paper for easy removal. — Parchment prevents sticking and helps the cake release cleanly, preserving the delicate crumb and making cleanup easier.
  • Use a spatula to gently fold ingredients instead of mixing vigorously. — Folding minimizes gluten development, which keeps the cake tender and prevents it from becoming tough or chewy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overmixing the batter
    Why: Many home cooks think mixing longer leads to a smoother batter, but overmixing develops gluten, which toughens the cake.
    Fix: Mix just until the dry ingredients are incorporated. Use gentle folding motions with a spatula, and stop as soon as the batter is uniform to keep the crumb light and tender.
  • Blueberries sinking to the bottom
    Why: Blueberries are denser than the batter and can sink if added too early or mixed too vigorously.
    Fix: Fold blueberries in last and gently. Tossing them in a little flour before adding can also help suspend them evenly in the batter.
  • Frosting melting or sliding off
    Why: Applying frosting to a warm cake or whipping cream too much can cause the frosting to lose structure.
    Fix: Cool the cake completely before frosting. Whip cream only to soft peaks and frost immediately to maintain a stable texture.
  • Cake drying out
    Why: Overbaking or using too much flour can dry the cake.
    Fix: Check the cake at the minimum baking time with a toothpick. Use proper measuring techniques for flour (spoon and level) to avoid packing too much in.
  • Using frozen blueberries without thawing
    Why: Frozen berries release excess water and can turn the batter soggy.
    Fix: Use fresh blueberries or if using frozen, thaw and drain them well before folding into the batter.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: This lemon blueberry cake can be kept at room temperature for up to 4 hours if covered loosely with a cake dome or plastic wrap. Longer exposure risks drying out the cake and softening the frosting too much.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The frosting may firm up and the cake will be denser when chilled. Let it sit at room temperature for 15 minutes before serving for best flavor and texture.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze the cake layers (unfrosted) wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and foil for up to 2 months. Thaw in the fridge overnight and bring to room temperature before frosting. Frosted cake does not freeze well due to the whipped cream.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Since the cake is best served fresh or at room temperature, avoid reheating. If desired, let refrigerated cake come to room temp naturally; microwaving may melt the frosting and dry the cake.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Bake the cake layers 1-2 days ahead, wrap them well, and refrigerate. Whip and spread the frosting the day you plan to serve for the freshest texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use frozen blueberries in this recipe?
Yes, but thaw and drain them well first to avoid excess moisture that can make the batter soggy and affect the cake’s texture. Tossing the berries in a little flour before folding them into the batter also helps keep them suspended evenly.
Why does my cake sometimes sink in the middle?
This usually happens if the cake is underbaked or the oven temperature is too low. Make sure your oven is fully preheated and test the cake with a toothpick at the minimum baking time. Avoid opening the oven door too early, which can cause temperature drops.
Can I substitute the heavy cream frosting with buttercream?
You can, but buttercream is much heavier and sweeter than whipped cream frosting. It will change the light texture and delicate flavor balance, making the cake richer but less airy.
How do I prevent blueberries from bleeding into the batter?
Use fresh, firm blueberries and fold them in gently at the end. Coating them lightly with flour before adding helps prevent them from sinking and bleeding color.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend that contains xanthan gum or another binder to mimic gluten’s structure. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious and tender.
Is there a vegan version of this cake?
Yes, replace eggs with flax eggs, use plant-based yogurt instead of buttermilk, and coconut oil instead of vegetable oil. For frosting, use coconut cream whipped with a little maple syrup. The flavor remains fresh with a plant-based twist.
How long does the whipped cream frosting last?
Whipped cream frosting is best enjoyed within 1-2 days when stored in the fridge. It can lose volume and become watery if kept longer, so plan to serve the cake soon after frosting.
Can I bake this cake in a bundt pan?
Yes, just increase the baking time by 10-15 minutes and grease the pan thoroughly. The texture will be the same, but the shape will be more dramatic and perfect for larger gatherings.
