Easy Sheet Pan Breakfast with Crispy Bacon and Roasted Potatoes

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 17, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This sheet pan breakfast brings together fluffy eggs, crispy bacon, and golden roasted potatoes all in one pan. It’s super easy to prep and perfect for feeding a crowd on busy mornings.

Introduction

I first discovered this sheet pan breakfast when I needed a quick way to feed a hungry family on a weekend morning without standing over the stove for ages. It’s such a lifesaver because everything cooks together on one pan — no juggling pots or pans. What I love most is how the bacon crisps up perfectly while the potatoes roast to a golden brown, and the eggs come out fluffy and just set. I’ve tried making this with scrambled eggs, but baking them on the pan gives a nicer texture and keeps cleanup easy. A little trick I use is to space the ingredients well so the heat circulates evenly, which helps everything cook at the same pace. The finished dish looks so inviting with crispy edges and soft centers, and it always disappears fast at my table. It’s honestly become my go-to for lazy weekend breakfasts or when friends come over for brunch. Plus, it’s easy to customize — swap the potatoes for sweet potatoes or add some veggies if you want. I hope you enjoy making this as much as I do!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 8 slices of bacon, raw
  • 4 large eggs, raw
  • 4 medium potatoes, diced
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). This temperature is key because it’s hot enough to crisp the bacon and roast the potatoes evenly without drying them out. Line a large sheet pan with foil or parchment paper for easy cleanup. Spread the diced potatoes on one side of the pan in a single layer, then drizzle with olive oil, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Toss them well to coat every piece, which helps them roast evenly and develop that beautiful golden crust. Avoid overcrowding the potatoes so they don’t steam and become soggy.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes prep
✓ Doneness: Potatoes look glossy with oil and evenly coated with seasoning.
Step 2
Place the sheet pan in the oven and roast the potatoes for 15 minutes. This initial roasting jump-starts the cooking process so they’ll be tender and crispy by the end. Meanwhile, arrange the bacon slices on the other side of the pan, making sure they don’t overlap. This spacing is important because overlapping bacon steams instead of crisps. After 15 minutes, flip the potatoes for even browning. If your oven allows, slide the bacon over to a hotter spot to help it crisp up faster.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes roasting
✓ Doneness: Potatoes are turning golden and bacon edges start curling and crisping.
Step 3
Carefully crack the eggs onto the empty spaces left on the sheet pan after the potatoes and bacon have started cooking. If you don’t have much room, use a second smaller pan or baking dish. Season the eggs lightly with salt and pepper. Return the pan to the oven and bake everything together for about 10 more minutes. This gentle heat cooks the eggs through without drying them out. Keep an eye on them — you want the whites set but the yolks still a bit runny unless you prefer fully cooked eggs.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes baking
✓ Doneness: Egg whites are opaque and firm; yolks slightly jiggle but hold shape.
Step 4
Remove the sheet pan from the oven and sprinkle chopped fresh parsley over everything for a pop of color and freshness. Let the breakfast rest for a minute or two before serving; this helps the eggs finish setting gently and makes handling easier. Use a spatula to lift portions of bacon, potatoes, and eggs onto plates. The potatoes should be crispy outside and tender inside, the bacon perfectly crunchy, and the eggs soft yet fully cooked. This balance of textures is what makes this sheet pan breakfast so satisfying.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes resting
✓ Doneness: Eggs slightly firm to the touch, bacon crispy, potatoes golden brown.
Step 5
If you want to add veggies, toss diced bell peppers or cherry tomatoes with the potatoes before roasting. This adds color and nutrients without extra effort. Just be mindful of moisture — tomatoes especially can soften quickly, so add them halfway through the roasting time or on the pan with the eggs to avoid sogginess.
⏱ Time: Optional step, add during potato prep
✓ Doneness: Veggies are tender but not mushy, maintaining some bite.
Step 6
For a spicier kick, sprinkle red pepper flakes over the potatoes before roasting or add a dash of hot sauce when serving. This simple tweak livens up the flavors and pairs well with the smoky bacon. I like to keep the base recipe mild for kids but add spice on the side for adults.
⏱ Time: Optional seasoning step
✓ Doneness: Spices evenly distributed and aroma noticeable but balanced.
Step 7
When selecting potatoes, go for waxy or all-purpose varieties like Yukon Gold or red potatoes. They hold their shape better during roasting and develop a nice crisp crust. Starchy potatoes like Russets can work but tend to get fluffier and may fall apart more easily. Cut potatoes into uniform pieces so they cook evenly. Uneven chunks cause some to burn while others stay undercooked.
⏱ Time: Prep during chopping
✓ Doneness: Potatoes cut evenly, about 1-inch cubes.
Step 8
If you want to make this recipe ahead, you can chop and season the potatoes the night before and store them in the fridge. Just toss them again with a little oil before roasting. Bacon can be laid out on the pan in the morning and eggs cracked just before baking. This saves time and keeps everything fresh.
⏱ Time: Make-ahead prep
✓ Doneness: Ingredients prepped but not cooked, stored properly.
Step 9
After baking, let leftovers cool completely before storing. Reheat gently in the oven or microwave with a splash of water to keep eggs moist. Avoid overheating or the eggs will get rubbery and potatoes dry. This breakfast reheats surprisingly well, making it great for busy mornings when you want a homemade meal fast.
⏱ Time: Reheating step
✓ Doneness: Food warmed through but still moist and tender.

Variations

Sweet Potato and Turkey Bacon Sheet Pan Breakfast

Swap regular potatoes for diced sweet potatoes and use turkey bacon for a leaner twist. The sweet potatoes add a natural sweetness that balances the smoky turkey bacon. Expect a slightly softer texture and vibrant color on the plate.

Note: Sweet potatoes roast faster than regular potatoes, so check for tenderness earlier to avoid overcooking.

Veggie-Packed Sheet Pan Breakfast

Add bell peppers, cherry tomatoes, and spinach to the potatoes for extra nutrients and color. This variation brings more freshness and a veggie boost without complicating the cooking process.

Note: Add delicate greens like spinach in the last 2 minutes of baking to prevent wilting.

Cheesy Bacon and Potato Sheet Pan

Sprinkle shredded cheddar or parmesan over the potatoes and bacon during the last 5 minutes of baking. The melted cheese adds richness and a crispy golden topping that’s irresistible.

Note: Keep an eye on cheese to prevent burning; remove pan once melted and lightly browned.

Spicy Chipotle Sheet Pan Breakfast

Mix chipotle powder or smoked paprika into the potato seasoning and serve with a side of salsa. This variation adds smoky heat that pairs beautifully with crispy bacon and eggs.

Note: Adjust spice level to taste; start with a small amount and add more if needed.

Herbed Sheet Pan Breakfast

Toss potatoes with fresh rosemary and thyme before roasting, and sprinkle chopped herbs over eggs after baking. This elevates the flavor with aromatic notes and freshness.

Note: Use fresh herbs sparingly to avoid overpowering the dish; dried herbs can be added earlier in roasting.

Pro Tips

Don’t overcrowd the sheet pan; leave space between ingredients.
Crowding the pan traps steam, which prevents crisping and results in soggy potatoes and bacon. Proper spacing allows hot air to circulate, ensuring even roasting and crispy textures.
Flip the potatoes halfway through roasting.
Turning the potatoes promotes even browning on all sides, preventing burnt edges and undercooked centers. It also helps develop that perfect crispy crust.
Use a rimmed baking sheet to catch rendered bacon fat.
A rimmed pan prevents fat from spilling into the oven, reducing smoke and mess. It also lets the bacon fat mingle with the potatoes, enhancing flavor without creating a greasy mess.
Add eggs last to prevent overcooking.
Eggs cook faster than potatoes and bacon, so adding them near the end ensures they don’t dry out or become rubbery. This timing keeps yolks tender and whites set just right.
Let the cooked breakfast rest a few minutes before serving.
Resting allows residual heat to finish cooking the eggs gently and helps the potatoes crisp up further. It also makes serving easier and safer.
Use parchment paper or foil for easy cleanup.
Lining the pan prevents sticking, makes washing up faster, and protects the pan from bacon grease, saving you time and effort.
Season potatoes well with salt and garlic powder.
Potatoes absorb seasoning better when coated in oil first. Garlic powder adds depth without burning, unlike fresh garlic, which can get bitter at high heat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overcrowding the pan

Why it happens It’s tempting to fit everything on one pan for convenience, but this traps moisture and prevents crisping.

Fix Use two pans if needed or roast in batches. Space ingredients evenly to allow air circulation, ensuring crispy potatoes and bacon.

Adding eggs too early

Why it happens Sometimes people crack eggs on the pan at the start to save time, but eggs cook faster than potatoes and bacon.

Fix Add eggs during the last 10 minutes of cooking so they set without becoming rubbery or overdone.

Using starchy potatoes

Why it happens Russets or similar potatoes can be easier to find but break down during roasting.

Fix Choose waxy potatoes like Yukon Gold for firmer texture and better roasting results.

Skipping the flip on potatoes

Why it happens Busy cooks sometimes forget to turn potatoes halfway, leading to uneven browning.

Fix Set a timer and flip potatoes gently with a spatula for consistent crispness.

Not preheating the oven

Why it happens Starting in a cold oven lengthens cooking time and affects texture.

Fix Always preheat to 400°F before placing the pan inside to ensure proper roasting.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep this breakfast at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean towel to avoid drying out. Beyond that, food safety risks increase.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The flavors mellow but the bacon may lose some crispness. Reheat gently in the oven or microwave with a splash of water to keep eggs moist.

Freezer

Freeze cooked breakfast in airtight containers or freezer bags for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating to maintain texture.

Reheating

Reheat in a 325°F oven for 10-15 minutes or microwave on medium power with a splash of water. Avoid high heat to prevent eggs from becoming rubbery and potatoes from drying out.

Make Ahead

Prep potatoes by dicing and seasoning 1-2 days ahead, stored in water to prevent browning. Assemble bacon and potatoes on the pan in the morning, then bake fresh. Eggs are best cracked just before baking to keep texture perfect.

FAQ

Can I use frozen potatoes for this recipe?
Frozen diced potatoes can work but usually release extra moisture, which can make them less crispy. If you use frozen, pat them dry well and reduce added oil slightly. You may also need to extend roasting time to achieve a golden crust.
How do I make sure the bacon gets crispy without burning?
Choose thick-cut bacon and space slices apart on the pan. Roast at 400°F and check after 15 minutes, flipping if needed. Keep an eye on bacon in the last 10 minutes to catch it at your preferred crispness without letting it burn.
Can I scramble the eggs instead of baking them on the sheet pan?
You can, but baking eggs on the pan keeps cleanup easier and the eggs more tender. Scrambled eggs require extra pans and can dry out if overcooked. If you prefer scrambled, cook separately on the stovetop and serve alongside.
Is it okay to use a different oil instead of olive oil?
Yes, vegetable or avocado oil works well because they have higher smoke points. Olive oil adds flavor but can smoke if oven gets too hot. Choose oils based on your flavor preference and heat tolerance.
How long can I store leftovers in the fridge?
Store leftovers in airtight containers for up to 3 days. The texture of eggs and bacon may soften over time, so reheat gently to preserve as much crispness and moisture as possible.
Can I add cheese to this recipe?
Absolutely! Sprinkle shredded cheese like cheddar or parmesan over the potatoes and bacon during the last 5 minutes of baking. Watch carefully to avoid burning the cheese.
What’s the best way to reheat this breakfast?
Reheat in a 325°F oven for 10-15 minutes or microwave on medium power with a splash of water to keep eggs moist. Avoid high heat to prevent rubbery eggs and dry potatoes.
Can I make this recipe gluten-free?
Yes, this recipe is naturally gluten-free as long as you use gluten-free seasonings and avoid cross-contamination. It’s a great option for gluten-sensitive eaters.
