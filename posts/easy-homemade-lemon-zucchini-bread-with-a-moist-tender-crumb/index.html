Moist Lemon Zucchini Bread with a Refreshing Citrus Twist

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 15, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon zucchini bread is so moist and tender, with fresh lemon zest brightening up the classic zucchini flavor. It’s great for breakfast or a light snack when you want something wholesome but tasty.

Introduction

I stumbled on this lemon zucchini bread recipe one spring when my garden zucchini was overflowing and I wanted something fresh and bright. Traditional zucchini bread is lovely, but adding lemon zest and juice totally elevates the flavor, making it feel lighter and more vibrant. What I love most about this version is how moist and tender it turns out—thanks to the yogurt and oil combination—and the subtle cinnamon that adds warmth without overpowering the citrus. The trick I learned is to squeeze the zucchini well to avoid a soggy loaf, but not so dry that it loses all moisture. Baking it low and slow helps develop a golden crust while keeping the inside soft. The finished bread is a beautiful balance of sweet, tangy, and earthy flavors with a texture that’s just right—not too dense, not crumbly. It’s perfect for breakfast with a cup of tea or as a snack to brighten your afternoon. I’ve made this for friends and family countless times, and it’s always a hit—sometimes even disappearing before it cools! If you’re looking for a refreshing twist on classic zucchini bread, this recipe is definitely worth trying.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup light brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/4 cup plain yogurt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Zest of 2 lemons
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • Optional: 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan or line it with parchment paper. This ensures the bread doesn’t stick and comes out easily after baking. It’s important to have the oven fully preheated so the bread bakes evenly from the start, developing a nice crust while keeping the interior moist.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven reaches steady 350°F and pan is evenly greased
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, and cinnamon. Mixing the dry ingredients first helps distribute the leavening agents evenly, which is key for a uniform rise and tender crumb. Avoid over-mixing later to keep the bread soft.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no lumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the granulated sugar, brown sugar, and eggs until smooth and creamy. This step incorporates air, which helps lighten the texture. Make sure the eggs are at room temperature for best results, as cold eggs can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly.
⏱ Time: 3-4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is pale, thick, and slightly fluffy
Step 4
Add the vegetable oil, yogurt, vanilla extract, lemon zest, and lemon juice to the sugar and egg mixture. Stir gently to combine. The yogurt adds moisture and a slight tang, which complements the lemon perfectly. The oil keeps the bread tender and prevents it from drying out during baking.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are fully incorporated with no streaks
Step 5
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture just until combined. Over-mixing here can develop gluten, making the bread tough. It’s okay if some flour streaks remain; they’ll disappear during baking. This gentle folding is what keeps the texture tender and soft.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick but smooth, with no large lumps
Step 6
Fold in the grated zucchini and nuts if using. Make sure the zucchini is squeezed dry to prevent excess moisture, which can cause a soggy bread. The zucchini adds natural moisture and a subtle earthiness that balances the lemon’s brightness.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and nuts are evenly distributed throughout the batter
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and develop a nice crust. Avoid tapping the pan too hard to prevent air bubbles from bursting and making the bread dense.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and reaches near the top of the pan
Step 8
Bake for 50-60 minutes until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Baking low and slow ensures the bread cooks through without drying out. If the top browns too quickly, tent loosely with foil to prevent burning.
⏱ Time: 50-60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust with clean toothpick test
Step 9
Let the bread cool in the pan for 15 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely. This prevents sogginess from steam trapped in the pan and helps the bread set properly. Slicing too soon can make it crumble or stick.
⏱ Time: 15-20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm and cool enough to slice without crumbling

Variations

Gluten-Free Lemon Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. Expect a slightly denser texture but still moist and flavorful. Make sure to use a blend designed for baking for best results.

Note: Gluten-free flours absorb liquid differently; you might need to adjust yogurt slightly to keep moisture balanced.

Vegan Lemon Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg) and use plant-based yogurt. The texture will be a bit firmer but still moist. Add a touch more lemon zest to brighten flavors.

Note: Flax eggs don’t provide as much leavening, so the bread may rise less and be denser.

Lemon Poppy Seed Zucchini Bread

Add 2 tablespoons of poppy seeds to the batter for a delightful crunch and visual appeal. The nutty flavor complements the lemon and zucchini beautifully, making this a fun twist.

Note: Poppy seeds don’t affect texture much but adding them requires gentle folding to distribute evenly.

Spiced Lemon Zucchini Bread

Increase cinnamon to 1 1/2 teaspoons and add 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg and ginger for a warmer spice profile. This variation is perfect for cooler months when you crave cozy flavors.

Note: Spices intensify during baking; don’t overdo it or they’ll overpower the lemon.

Lemon Zucchini Muffins

Divide the batter into a muffin tin lined with paper cups and bake for 20-25 minutes. Muffins bake faster and have more crust per bite, making them great for grab-and-go breakfasts.

Note: Watch closely to avoid overbaking; muffins dry out quickly.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the grated zucchini well to remove excess water.
Zucchini has high water content that can make the bread soggy if not drained properly. Squeezing out moisture ensures the bread bakes with the right texture and doesn’t collapse.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice for the best citrus flavor.
Lemon zest contains aromatic oils that add intense flavor, while fresh juice provides acidity to activate leavening. Bottled lemon juice lacks the brightness and can alter the taste.
Don’t overmix the batter once you combine wet and dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes the bread tough and chewy instead of soft and tender. Folding gently until just combined preserves a light crumb.
Bake the bread in the center of the oven for even heat distribution.
Ovens can have hot spots; placing the pan in the middle ensures uniform baking, preventing burnt edges or undercooked centers.
Cool the bread in the pan for 15 minutes before removing.
This helps the bread set and prevents it from breaking apart when you take it out. It also reduces moisture buildup on the bottom.
Use parchment paper to line your pan for easy removal.
Parchment prevents sticking and makes cleanup easier, especially with moist breads like this one.
Add nuts like walnuts or pecans for texture and flavor.
Nuts add crunch and a toasty flavor, balancing the soft, moist bread and bright lemon notes perfectly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing the zucchini enough

Why it happens Many assume shredded zucchini can be used as-is, but its high moisture content can ruin texture.

Fix After grating, place zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove excess water. This prevents soggy, dense bread.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens In a rush, it’s tempting to mix everything thoroughly, but this develops gluten.

Fix Fold ingredients gently until just combined. Streaks of flour are okay; they’ll bake out.

Using old or ineffective baking soda/powder

Why it happens Leavening agents lose potency over time, causing flat, dense bread.

Fix Test baking soda by adding a drop of vinegar; it should fizz. Replace old leavening agents regularly.

Baking at too high temperature

Why it happens High heat cooks the outside too fast, leaving the inside undercooked.

Fix Bake at 350°F as directed and tent with foil if browning too fast.

Cutting the bread before it cools

Why it happens Warm bread is fragile and can crumble or stick.

Fix Let it cool completely on a wire rack before slicing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cooled bread covered loosely with foil or in a bread box at room temperature for up to 2 days. Avoid plastic wrap at room temp as it traps moisture and makes the crust soggy.

Refrigerator

Keep in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap for up to 5 days. The bread may dry out slightly; warming before serving helps restore softness.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled loaf tightly in plastic wrap and then aluminum foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature wrapped to retain moisture.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in a toaster oven or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a small dab of butter or a light spray of water before reheating helps revive moisture. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

You can grate the zucchini and zest the lemon 1-2 days ahead and store separately in airtight containers. Mix the batter and bake fresh for best texture. Alternatively, bake and freeze the bread, then thaw and warm when needed.

FAQ

Can I use yellow squash instead of zucchini?
Yes, yellow squash works fine as a substitute. It has a milder flavor but similar moisture content, so be sure to squeeze out excess liquid just like with zucchini to avoid sogginess.
What if I don’t have plain yogurt?
You can substitute with sour cream or buttermilk in the same quantity. These provide similar moisture and acidity that help tenderize the bread and activate the baking soda.
Why is my bread dense or heavy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter, not squeezing zucchini properly, or using old leavening agents. Be gentle folding the batter and ensure your baking soda and powder are fresh for a good rise.
Can I make this bread gluten-free?
Yes, using a gluten-free flour blend designed for baking works well. You might need to slightly adjust the yogurt amount to keep the moisture balanced as gluten-free flours absorb liquids differently.
How do I store leftover bread?
Keep it in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Absolutely! Chopped walnuts, pecans, or chocolate chips make great additions. Fold them in gently in step 6 to distribute evenly without overmixing.
Why does my bread crack on top?
A cracked top is normal and often a sign of a well-risen loaf. It happens when the crust sets while the interior continues to expand.
How do I prevent the bread from drying out?
Don’t overbake; start checking at 50 minutes. Also, store properly in airtight containers and add a light wrap of foil or plastic to retain moisture.
