Soft and Chewy Vegan Pumpkin Spice Oatmeal Cookies for Cozy Fall Nights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
12 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These vegan pumpkin spice oatmeal cookies are the ultimate cozy treat. Soft and chewy with a perfect blend of warm spices and pumpkin, they’re my favorite for chilly evenings. Great with tea or coffee!

Introduction

I first made these vegan pumpkin spice oatmeal cookies on a chilly October afternoon when I wanted something comforting but dairy-free. I’ve tried a few pumpkin cookie recipes before, but they either came out too dry or cakey. This version nails the perfect soft and chewy texture every time — thanks to the combo of pumpkin puree and coconut oil. I also love how the warm spices blend beautifully with the oats, making them feel like a cozy hug in cookie form. One trick I learned is to let the dough rest for about 10 minutes before baking; it helps the oats absorb moisture, so the cookies hold together without being dense or crumbly. The finished cookies have a tender bite with a hint of spice and just the right sweetness. My family loves them for afternoon snacks, and they’re a hit with friends who don’t even realize they’re vegan! If you’re looking for a simple, wholesome fall treat that’s easy to whip up and full of flavor, you’ll want to keep this recipe handy all season long.

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 cookies, 12 servings

  • 1 cup canned pumpkin puree, well stirred
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/4 cup coconut oil, melted
  • 1/4 cup maple syrup
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups rolled oats
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans (optional)
  • 1/2 cup raisins or dairy-free chocolate chips (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment paper. This ensures your cookies bake evenly and don’t stick. Getting the oven fully preheated is key because it helps the cookies spread properly and develop a nice golden edge without drying out. Skipping this step can lead to uneven baking or flat, tough cookies.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven reaches 350°F and parchment paper is fully laid out smooth without wrinkles
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the pumpkin puree, brown sugar, melted coconut oil, maple syrup, and vanilla extract until smooth. This wet mixture forms the base of your cookies and helps keep them moist and tender. Make sure your coconut oil isn’t too hot or it can cook the sugar prematurely, affecting texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is uniform with no visible lumps or oil separation
Step 3
In a separate bowl, combine the rolled oats, flour, baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, and salt. Mixing the dry ingredients separately ensures even distribution of leavening agents and spices, so every cookie has balanced flavor and rise. Don’t skip sifting or thorough mixing to avoid clumps.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are evenly mixed with no clumps
Step 4
Gradually fold the dry ingredients into the wet pumpkin mixture using a spatula. Be gentle to keep the oats intact and avoid overmixing, which can make cookies tough. The dough should be sticky but hold together well. If too wet, add a tablespoon of flour at a time to reach the right consistency.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough is thick, sticky, and holds shape without running
Step 5
Stir in the chopped nuts and raisins or chocolate chips if using. These add texture and bursts of sweetness or crunch. Adding them last keeps them from getting crushed and ensures even distribution. Feel free to customize based on what you have on hand.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mix-ins are evenly scattered throughout the dough
Step 6
Let the dough rest for 10 minutes at room temperature. This resting time allows the oats to absorb moisture and flavors to meld, which helps the cookies hold together better and develop a chewy texture. Don’t skip this step or the cookies may crumble when baked.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough feels less sticky and more cohesive
Step 7
Scoop heaping tablespoons of dough onto the prepared baking sheet, spacing them about 2 inches apart. I like to use a cookie scoop for even sizing. Flatten them slightly with your fingers since the dough won’t spread much on its own. This helps them bake evenly and get that classic cookie shape.
⏱ Time: 8 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cookies are roughly the same size and flattened slightly
Step 8
Bake for 10-12 minutes, or until the edges are golden and the centers look set but still soft. Oven times vary, so start checking at 10 minutes to avoid overbaking. Overbaked cookies will be dry and crumbly rather than chewy. The smell of spices will be a good indicator they’re almost done.
⏱ Time: 10-12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges are lightly browned and centers are set but soft to touch
Step 9
Allow cookies to cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. This resting lets them firm up so they don’t break apart when moved. Patience here means you get perfectly chewy cookies instead of crumbling messes!
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cookies hold together when lifted and are warm but firm

Variations

Gluten-Free Version

Swap all-purpose flour with a gluten-free flour blend and use certified gluten-free oats. This keeps the cookies soft and chewy while making them safe for gluten-sensitive eaters. The texture will be similar but might be slightly more delicate.

Note: Gluten-free flour absorbs moisture differently, so you may need to adjust the dough consistency slightly by adding a teaspoon more flour or a splash of plant milk.

Add Vegan White Chocolate Chips

Mix in vegan white chocolate chips instead of regular chocolate for a sweet, creamy contrast to the warm spices. This adds a fun twist and extra richness to the cookies.

Note: White chocolate chips may melt faster; monitor baking time to prevent spreading too much.

Spiced Pumpkin and Cranberry Cookies

Add dried cranberries along with extra cinnamon and a pinch of cloves for a tart and spicy flavor combo. This variation is festive and perfect for holiday gatherings.

Note: Dried cranberries add chewiness and moisture, so reduce maple syrup slightly if dough feels too wet.

No-Oat Version

Replace oats with finely chopped nuts or seeds for a nutty, crunchy cookie. This changes the texture completely but still highlights the pumpkin spice flavor beautifully.

Note: Without oats, dough will be less chewy and more crumbly; chill dough before baking to help hold shape.

Pumpkin Spice Muffin Tops

Scoop dough onto a baking sheet and bake as individual muffin tops for a thicker, cakier treat. Great if you want a more substantial snack or breakfast bite.

Note: Increase baking time to 15-18 minutes and check for a golden top and springy center.

Pro Tips

Let the dough rest before baking.
Allowing the dough to rest for 10 minutes helps the oats soak up moisture, which improves the texture and prevents crumbling. Without resting, the cookies can be dry or fall apart because the oats haven’t absorbed enough liquid to bind the dough properly.
Use parchment paper or a silicone baking mat.
This prevents the cookies from sticking and promotes even baking by providing a consistent surface temperature. It also makes cleanup easier and helps the bottoms brown evenly without burning.
Don’t overmix the dough.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make cookies tough and dense instead of tender and chewy. Fold the ingredients gently just until combined to maintain a soft texture.
Measure flour accurately by spooning and leveling.
Scooping flour directly with a measuring cup can compact it, leading to too much flour and dry cookies. Lightly spoon flour into the cup and level with a knife for best results.
Use room temperature ingredients.
Room temperature pumpkin puree and melted coconut oil combine more smoothly, ensuring even mixing and consistent dough texture. Cold ingredients can cause lumps or uneven baking.
Customize mix-ins to your liking.
Adding nuts, raisins, or chocolate chips introduces texture and flavor variety. Make sure to fold them in gently to avoid breaking the dough’s structure.
Check cookies early to avoid overbaking.
Ovens vary, so start checking at 10 minutes to catch the perfect chewy texture. Overbaking dries them out and makes them crumbly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the dough resting time

Why it happens Many rush to bake immediately, thinking it saves time, unaware that oats need time to absorb moisture.

Fix Set a timer and let the dough rest for at least 10 minutes before baking. This step improves texture and helps the cookies hold together better.

Using pumpkin pie filling instead of puree

Why it happens Pumpkin pie filling is often mistaken for plain puree because it comes in similar cans.

Fix Always choose 100% pumpkin puree without added sugar or spices to control sweetness and flavor in your cookies.

Overmixing the dough

Why it happens Trying to fully combine ingredients can lead to overworked dough.

Fix Mix just until dry ingredients are incorporated to avoid tough cookies caused by gluten development.

Baking at too low or high temperature

Why it happens Incorrect oven settings or not preheating can cause uneven baking.

Fix Preheat oven fully to 350°F and use an oven thermometer if needed to maintain consistent temperature.

Not spacing cookies properly on the baking sheet

Why it happens Crowding cookies can cause them to bake together or bake unevenly.

Fix Leave at least 2 inches between cookies to allow spreading and even heat circulation.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store cooled cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep away from direct sunlight or heat sources to maintain softness. If they start to dry out, a quick zap in the microwave can soften them again.

Refrigerator

Cookies can be stored in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 7 days. Chilling may firm them up, so allow to come to room temperature or warm slightly before eating for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe bag for up to 3 months. Wrap well to avoid freezer burn. Thaw at room temperature or warm in the oven to refresh their soft texture.

Reheating

Reheat cookies in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 15-20 seconds with a damp paper towel to restore softness. Avoid overheating which can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can mix the dough and refrigerate it for up to 24 hours before baking. This actually improves flavor and texture. Just let the dough come to room temperature before scooping and baking as usual.

FAQ

Can I use fresh pumpkin instead of canned puree?
Yes, but fresh pumpkin needs to be cooked and pureed until smooth first. It’s less moist than canned, so you might need to add a tablespoon or two of plant milk to get the right dough consistency. Also, fresh pumpkin has a milder flavor, so spices might need adjusting.
Can I substitute coconut oil with another fat?
You can use vegan butter or neutral oil like sunflower or canola, but coconut oil adds a nice richness and helps the dough set properly. Substituting may slightly change texture and flavor, so expect a softer or less chewy cookie.
How do I make these cookies nut-free?
Simply omit the nuts or replace them with seeds like pumpkin or sunflower seeds. This keeps the texture interesting without allergy concerns. Just be sure to check all ingredients for cross-contamination if allergies are severe.
Why are my cookies too crumbly?
Common reasons include skipping the dough resting step, overbaking, or using too much flour. Make sure to let the dough rest so oats absorb moisture and watch baking time carefully. Also, measure flour accurately to avoid dryness.
Can I make these cookies gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use certified gluten-free oats and swap regular flour for a gluten-free blend. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious. Adjust moisture as needed since gluten-free flour can absorb liquids differently.
How long will these cookies keep fresh?
At room temperature, they stay fresh for about 3 days in an airtight container. Refrigerated, they last up to a week. For longer storage, freeze them for up to 3 months and thaw before eating.
Can I add chocolate chips to this recipe?
Yes, dairy-free chocolate chips are a great addition! Fold them in with the nuts or raisins. They add sweetness and richness that pairs wonderfully with pumpkin and spices.
What’s the best way to measure oats and flour?
For flour, spoon it into your measuring cup and level it off with a knife to avoid packing. For oats, lightly scoop and level as well. Accurate measuring helps maintain the right dough texture.
