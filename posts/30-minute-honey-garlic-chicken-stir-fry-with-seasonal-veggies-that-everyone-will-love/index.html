Easy 30-Minute Honey Garlic Chicken Stir Fry for Busy Nights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 27, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

A quick and tasty honey garlic chicken stir fry that’s perfect for busy weeknights. The sweet and savory sauce hugs tender chicken and crisp veggies. Ready in 30 minutes, it’s fresh, satisfying, and easy to whip up!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this honey garlic chicken stir fry when I was scrambling to make dinner after a long day. Honestly, it was a bit of a happy accident — I mixed honey and garlic into a quick sauce and tossed it with whatever veggies I had on hand. Since then, it’s become a staple in my kitchen because it’s fast, flavorful, and feels fresh without a ton of fuss. What I love about this version is how the honey balances the garlic’s punch, creating a sauce that clings perfectly to the chicken and veggies. The trick I learned is to slice the chicken thinly and cook it quickly over high heat so it stays juicy and tender. Plus, using a cornstarch slurry at the end gives the sauce a nice glossy finish that makes everything look restaurant-worthy. When it’s done, you get tender chicken, crisp veggies, and a sauce that’s both sweet and savory — it’s just the kind of comforting meal I crave on busy weeknights. I promise, once you try it, you’ll want to make it again and again!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 1 lb boneless skinless chicken breasts, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 1 red bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 1 cup broccoli florets
  • 1 medium carrot, julienned
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/4 cup honey
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Sesame seeds and sliced green onions for garnish (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by prepping all your ingredients. Thinly slice the chicken breasts against the grain to ensure tenderness. Also, slice the bell pepper, julienne the carrot, and chop the broccoli into bite-sized florets. This prep is key because stir frying moves fast — having everything ready means you won’t overcook anything. Avoid overcrowding your chopping board to keep cuts clean and even, which helps the chicken cook uniformly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken slices are even and thin, veggies are uniformly cut for quick cooking
Step 2
Heat 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. Once hot, add the chicken slices in a single layer. Let them sear without stirring for about 2 minutes to develop a light golden crust, then stir-fry until just cooked through, about 3-4 minutes total. Cooking the chicken quickly on high heat locks in juices and prevents toughness. Remove the chicken and set aside to keep it from overcooking.
⏱ Time: 6 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken is opaque with golden edges and no pink inside
Step 3
In the same pan, add the remaining 1 tablespoon of oil. Toss in the minced garlic and stir quickly for about 30 seconds to release its aroma but avoid burning it, which can turn bitter. Immediately add the sliced bell pepper, broccoli florets, and julienned carrot. Stir-fry the veggies for 4-5 minutes until they’re tender-crisp, bright in color, and still have a little snap. This quick cooking keeps them fresh and flavorful.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Veggies are vibrant and tender but still slightly crisp
Step 4
While the veggies cook, whisk together the honey, soy sauce, and rice vinegar in a small bowl. This combo balances sweet, salty, and tangy flavors that make the sauce so addictive. Set aside the cornstarch slurry separately; you’ll use it to thicken the sauce later. Mixing these ingredients ahead ensures a smooth sauce that coats everything evenly.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce is well combined and ready to pour
Step 5
Return the cooked chicken to the pan with the veggies. Pour the honey soy sauce mixture over the top and stir everything to combine. Let it simmer for 2 minutes so the flavors meld. This simmering step is crucial because it allows the sauce to penetrate the chicken and veggies, making every bite flavorful.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce bubbles gently and coats ingredients
Step 6
Give the cornstarch slurry a quick stir, then pour it into the pan while stirring constantly. This will thicken the sauce quickly, giving it a nice glossy sheen. Keep stirring for another minute until the sauce is thickened and clings beautifully to the chicken and veggies. Be careful not to add too much slurry at once to avoid a gluey texture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce is shiny, thickened, and coats the stir fry evenly
Step 7
Taste the stir fry and adjust seasoning with salt and pepper if needed. Sometimes the soy sauce adds enough saltiness, but a pinch can help balance flavors. Stir well after seasoning to distribute evenly. This final taste check ensures the dish is perfectly balanced before serving.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Balanced sweet and savory flavor with no overpowering saltiness
Step 8
Turn off the heat and sprinkle sesame seeds and sliced green onions over the stir fry for a fresh, nutty finish. These garnishes add texture and a pop of color that makes the dish look inviting and adds a subtle layer of flavor.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Dish looks vibrant and garnished attractively
Step 9
Serve the honey garlic chicken stir fry immediately over steamed rice or noodles. The contrast of the warm sauce with the fluffy rice is comforting and satisfying. Serving right away keeps the veggies crisp and the chicken tender, so try not to let it sit too long before eating.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Dish is hot, fresh, and ready to enjoy

Variations

Spicy Honey Garlic Chicken Stir Fry

Add a teaspoon of red pepper flakes or a drizzle of Sriracha to the sauce for a spicy kick. It pairs wonderfully with the sweet honey and savory garlic for those who like a little heat. The veggies and chicken remain the same, but the flavor profile is bolder and more exciting.

Note: Add the chili early with the garlic to infuse the oil, but adjust spice levels carefully to avoid overpowering the dish.

Honey Garlic Chicken Stir Fry with Cashews

Toss in a handful of toasted cashews at the end for added crunch and a nutty flavor. It adds texture contrast and makes the dish feel a bit more special. Cashews also complement the sweetness of the sauce nicely.

Note: Add cashews off heat to maintain their crunch and avoid burning.

Vegetarian Honey Garlic Stir Fry

Swap chicken for firm tofu or tempeh for a meatless version. Press tofu well to remove excess moisture and pan-fry until golden before adding the sauce. The honey garlic sauce works just as well with plant-based proteins.

Note: Adjust cooking time since tofu cooks differently; be gentle to avoid breaking pieces.

Honey Garlic Chicken Stir Fry with Noodles

Serve this stir fry over cooked rice noodles or toss the noodles in the pan with the sauce at the end for a one-pan meal. It’s a fun way to switch up the base and soak up all that delicious sauce.

Note: Add noodles last and toss gently to prevent them from breaking apart.

Low-Sodium Honey Garlic Chicken Stir Fry

Use low-sodium soy sauce and reduce added salt to make this recipe heart-healthier without sacrificing flavor. You can also add extra garlic and a splash of lemon juice for brightness to compensate for lower salt.

Note: Taste carefully and adjust acidity to balance the reduced saltiness.

Pro Tips

Slice chicken thinly and against the grain for maximum tenderness.
Slicing against the grain shortens muscle fibers, making the chicken easier to chew and juicier after cooking. Thin slices cook quickly and evenly, preventing toughness and dryness that often happen with thicker pieces.
Don’t overcrowd the pan during stir-frying.
Overcrowding traps moisture and causes steaming instead of searing. This prevents the Maillard browning reaction that adds flavor and texture. Cooking in batches or using a large pan ensures each ingredient gets that perfect sear.
Use a cornstarch slurry to thicken the sauce at the end.
Adding cornstarch mixed with water thickens the sauce smoothly and quickly without changing the flavor. Stirring constantly prevents lumps and helps the sauce cling to the chicken and veggies, making each bite flavorful and glossy.
Cook garlic briefly before adding veggies to avoid bitterness.
Garlic burns easily and becomes bitter if overcooked. Stir-frying it for just 30 seconds releases its aroma without burning, which keeps the sauce tasting fresh and balanced.
Prepare all ingredients before starting to cook.
Stir-frying happens fast, so having everything chopped and ready prevents overcooking and helps maintain the right texture and flavor balance.
Add veggies that cook at similar rates together.
Combining vegetables with similar cooking times ensures even doneness. Adding quick-cook veggies too early can result in mushiness, while slow-cooking ones added too late stay tough.
Adjust seasoning at the end after thickening the sauce.
The sauce’s flavor concentrates as it thickens, so seasoning before thickening might lead to over-salting. Tasting and adjusting last ensures perfect balance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Cutting chicken too thick or not against the grain.

Why it happens Many cooks slice chicken quickly without considering grain direction or thickness, leading to chewy or unevenly cooked meat.

Fix Take your time to slice thinly and against the grain. Use a sharp knife and steady hand to ensure even pieces that cook quickly and stay tender.

Overcrowding the pan.

Why it happens Trying to cook everything at once to save time is tempting but causes steaming rather than frying.

Fix Cook in batches if needed, giving ingredients space to sear. Use a large wok or skillet for best results.

Burning the garlic.

Why it happens Garlic cooks very fast and burns if left alone on high heat for too long.

Fix Add garlic last or stir it constantly for 30 seconds before adding veggies to prevent bitterness.

Skipping the cornstarch slurry.

Why it happens Some skip thickening to save time, resulting in watery sauce that doesn’t cling to food.

Fix Prepare and add the slurry slowly, stirring constantly until the sauce thickens to get that perfect coating.

Not tasting and adjusting seasoning at the end.

Why it happens Relying on initial measurements without tasting can lead to bland or overly salty dishes.

Fix Always taste after thickening and adjust salt, pepper, or acidity to balance the flavors.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This stir fry can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered. Beyond that, it’s best to refrigerate to avoid bacterial growth. Cover with a lid or foil to keep it fresh and prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The sauce may thicken and veggies soften when cold, so reheat gently. Adding a splash of water or broth while reheating helps restore the sauce’s consistency.

Freezer

Freeze in a freezer-safe container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating. Texture of veggies may soften after freezing but flavor remains good.

Reheating

Reheat in a skillet over medium heat, stirring occasionally and adding a little water or broth to loosen the sauce. Microwave works too but can make veggies soggy. Avoid overheating to keep chicken tender.

Make Ahead

You can prep and slice all ingredients 1-2 days ahead and store them separately. Mix the sauce in advance as well. Cook everything fresh the day of serving for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use chicken thighs instead of breasts?
Yes! Chicken thighs are a great option and tend to stay juicier. Just slice them thinly like breasts and adjust cooking time slightly—thighs may take a minute or two longer to cook through. The flavor will be richer, which many people love.
What can I substitute for honey?
Maple syrup or agave nectar can work as substitutes for honey, though they’ll alter the flavor slightly. Maple syrup adds a deeper, earthier sweetness. Avoid using granulated sugar unless dissolved fully, as it won’t caramelize the same way.
How do I keep the veggies crisp?
Cook veggies on high heat for a short time and avoid overcrowding the pan so they steam instead of fry. Adding them in stages based on cooking time helps too. Serve immediately to enjoy their fresh crunch.
Can I make this stir fry gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use tamari or a gluten-free soy sauce alternative. Double-check that your other ingredients, like rice vinegar, are gluten-free as well. This swap keeps the flavor profile very similar.
Why is my sauce too thin or too thick?
If your sauce is too thin, you might have added too little cornstarch slurry or not cooked it long enough. If it’s too thick or gluey, you may have added too much slurry at once. Add slurry gradually and stir constantly to get the perfect consistency.
Can I prepare this dish entirely in advance?
You can prep the ingredients and sauce ahead, but it’s best to cook the stir fry fresh to keep textures vibrant. If cooked in advance, veggies may become soggy and chicken less tender when reheated.
What’s the best oil to use for stir frying?
Use a neutral oil with a high smoke point like vegetable, canola, or peanut oil. Olive oil has a lower smoke point and can impart a stronger flavor, which might not suit this Asian-inspired dish.
How do I avoid burning the garlic?
Add garlic after the oil is hot and stir constantly for only about 30 seconds before adding other ingredients. Garlic burns quickly and turns bitter, so watch it closely and don’t let it sit alone on high heat.
