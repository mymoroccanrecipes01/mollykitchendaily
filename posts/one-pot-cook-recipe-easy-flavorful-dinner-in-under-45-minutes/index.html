Easy One-Pot Garlic Herb Chicken and Rice for Busy Weeknights

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 10, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This one-pot garlic herb chicken and rice is a lifesaver on hectic evenings. The garlic and fresh herbs build layers of flavor while everything cooks together, making dinner both quick and deeply satisfying.

Introduction

I stumbled on this one-pot garlic herb chicken and rice recipe on a particularly hectic weeknight when I wanted something fast but still comforting. What really hooked me was how simple it is to make without sacrificing flavor — the secret is in layering garlic and herbs right at the start. I used to rush through weeknight dinners, often ending up with bland meals or piles of dishes to clean. This recipe changed the game for me. The chicken stays juicy, the rice absorbs all those lovely garlicky, herby flavors, and it all cooks together in one pot, which means minimal cleanup. I also learned that sautéing the garlic and onions before adding the rice and broth gives the dish a deeper, richer taste. The finished meal smells incredible and looks rustic but inviting, perfect for any night you want to feel like you’ve made something special without the fuss. Honestly, it’s become a family favorite for those evenings when time is tight but you still want a wholesome, satisfying dinner.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 1 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into chunks
  • 1 cup long-grain white rice, rinsed
  • 3 cups low-sodium chicken broth
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 small onion, diced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)

How To Do It

Step 1
Heat the olive oil in a large deep skillet or pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook until softened and translucent, about 4-5 minutes. This step is crucial because gently cooking the onions releases their natural sweetness, which forms a flavorful base for the dish. Avoid browning them too much, as you want a mellow, aromatic start rather than bitterness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions look translucent and soft but not browned
Step 2
Add the minced garlic to the onions and sauté for about 1-2 minutes until fragrant. Garlic cooks quickly, so watch closely to prevent burning, which can cause a bitter flavor. This step layers in the aromatic garlic flavor that infuses the whole dish, making it feel homey and rich.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Garlic is fragrant and slightly golden but not dark brown
Step 3
Add the chicken pieces to the pot and season generously with salt, pepper, dried thyme, and oregano. Cook the chicken, stirring occasionally, until it’s lightly browned on the outside but not fully cooked through, about 5-7 minutes. Browning the chicken develops deeper flavor through the Maillard reaction, which adds complexity to the dish.
⏱ Time: 7 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken is golden on the edges but still slightly pink inside
Step 4
Pour in the rinsed rice and stir well to coat the grains in the oil and seasonings. This helps separate the rice and prevents it from becoming mushy. Toasting the rice slightly also adds a subtle nuttiness that enhances the final flavor.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice looks glossy and slightly toasted
Step 5
Slowly add the chicken broth, scraping the bottom of the pot to loosen any browned bits. These bits add a ton of flavor. Bring everything to a gentle boil, then reduce the heat to low and cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid. Cooking on low heat allows the rice to absorb the broth evenly without burning.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Broth is simmering gently, no vigorous boiling
Step 6
Let the dish simmer, covered, for 18-20 minutes without stirring. Resist the urge to lift the lid, as the steam is essential for cooking the rice perfectly. Stirring too soon can break up the rice and make it mushy. The chicken will finish cooking through in this time, becoming tender and juicy.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice is tender and liquid is fully absorbed
Step 7
Turn off the heat and let the pot sit, still covered, for another 5 minutes. This resting period allows the rice to steam gently and the flavors to meld. It also helps any residual moisture redistribute evenly, resulting in fluffier rice.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Steam rises when lid is lifted, rice looks fluffy
Step 8
Fluff the rice gently with a fork and taste for seasoning. Adjust salt and pepper as needed. This final seasoning step is important because rice can sometimes absorb salt unevenly during cooking.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice separates easily, flavors are balanced
Step 9
Garnish with freshly chopped parsley for a bright, fresh contrast and serve immediately. The fresh herbs add a pop of color and a mild freshness that cuts through the richness of the chicken and rice.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Dish looks vibrant with green garnish and smells fresh

Variations

Spicy One-Pot Chicken and Rice

Add 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper and a diced jalapeño with the onions to bring heat. Swap parsley for cilantro and squeeze fresh lime over the top for a zesty finish. Expect a lively, spicy flavor with the same comforting texture.

Note: Watch heat levels carefully when sautéing jalapeño to avoid burning; adjust cayenne to taste.

Creamy Mushroom and Herb Chicken Rice

Stir in 1 cup sliced mushrooms with the onions and finish with 1/4 cup cream or coconut milk before resting. This adds earthiness and richness, making the dish more indulgent and velvety.

Note: Cook mushrooms thoroughly to release moisture before adding rice to avoid watery texture.

Lemon and Dill Chicken Rice

Replace thyme and oregano with fresh dill and add zest and juice of one lemon at the end. This variation brightens the dish with citrus notes and herbal freshness, perfect for spring or summer meals.

Note: Add lemon juice after cooking to preserve its bright flavor and prevent bitterness.

Vegetable-Packed One-Pot Chicken Rice

Add diced carrots, peas, and bell peppers with the broth for extra color and nutrition. This makes the dish heartier and adds natural sweetness and crunch to balance the garlic and herbs.

Note: Adjust broth quantity slightly if adding large amounts of vegetables to ensure proper rice cooking.

Brown Rice Version

Use brown rice instead of white, increasing cooking time to 40-45 minutes and broth to 3 1/2 cups. This makes the dish nuttier and more fiber-rich, though texture will be chewier and less fluffy.

Note: Check liquid levels during cooking as brown rice absorbs more; add a splash more broth if needed.

Pro Tips

Sauté onions on medium heat until translucent, not browned.
Cooking onions gently releases their natural sweetness without creating bitterness. Browning too early can overpower the dish and mask the delicate herb flavors.
Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
Rinsing removes excess surface starch, which prevents the rice from becoming gummy or clumping together during cooking, ensuring fluffy grains.
Don’t stir the rice while it simmers.
Stirring releases starch and breaks the grains, causing mushiness. Leaving it untouched allows even cooking and proper absorption of the broth.
Use bone-in chicken thighs for extra flavor if you have time.
Bones release collagen and add richness to the broth, enhancing the overall depth of the dish. Just allow a bit more cooking time.
Let the dish rest covered off heat before serving.
This steaming step evens out moisture distribution, so the rice finishes cooking gently and the flavors meld perfectly.
Use fresh herbs for garnish instead of dried at the end.
Fresh herbs add brightness and color that dried herbs can’t match, balancing the richness and making the dish feel fresher.
Adjust seasoning after cooking, not before.
Rice absorbs salt unevenly, so it’s best to season at the end to avoid over or under-salting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the step of browning the chicken.

Why it happens People often want to save time or avoid extra dishes, but skipping browning loses flavor complexity.

Fix Take the extra 5-7 minutes to brown the chicken in the pot first. This builds a savory base through the Maillard reaction, making the final dish more flavorful and aromatic.

Stirring the rice during simmering.

Why it happens It’s tempting to check or prevent sticking, but stirring breaks grains and releases starch.

Fix Resist stirring until the cooking time is complete. If you worry about sticking, use a heavy-bottomed pot and medium-low heat to prevent burning.

Using quick-cooking or instant rice.

Why it happens Convenience can lead to choosing the wrong rice type, which cooks faster but doesn’t absorb liquid properly.

Fix Always use long-grain white rice for this recipe. It holds texture well and absorbs broth to create a creamy yet fluffy result.

Overcooking garlic until brown.

Why it happens Adding garlic too early or cooking on too high heat causes it to burn and taste bitter.

Fix Add garlic after onions soften and cook just until fragrant, about 1-2 minutes, stirring constantly.

Not letting the dish rest after cooking.

Why it happens Eagerness to eat leads to serving immediately, but the rice texture may be uneven.

Fix Turn off heat and keep the pot covered for 5 minutes. This gentle steaming evens moisture and improves texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This dish can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Beyond that, bacteria growth can increase, so refrigerate promptly. Cover with a lid or foil to keep it from drying out.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The rice may firm up and dry slightly when cold. Reheat gently with a splash of broth or water to restore moisture and fluffiness.

Freezer

Freeze in airtight containers or heavy-duty freezer bags for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat on the stovetop over low heat with a splash of broth or water, stirring occasionally until warmed through. Alternatively, microwave covered with a damp paper towel for 1-2 minutes, stirring halfway. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the chicken and sauté the aromatics 1-2 days ahead, storing them separately in the fridge. Add rice and broth just before cooking to maintain freshness. This saves prep time on busy nights.

FAQ

Can I use chicken breasts instead of thighs?
Yes, you can substitute chicken breasts, but they cook faster and can dry out easily. To keep them juicy, reduce simmering time slightly and avoid overcooking. Browning is still important to develop flavor.
What if I don’t have fresh parsley for garnish?
Dried parsley can be added during cooking but won’t provide the same fresh brightness. You can also use other fresh herbs like cilantro or chives to add a pop of color and flavor.
Can I make this recipe gluten-free?
Absolutely! This recipe is naturally gluten-free as long as you use gluten-free chicken broth. Always check labels to avoid hidden gluten in broth or seasoning blends.
How do I prevent the rice from sticking to the pot?
Use a heavy-bottomed pot and moderate heat to avoid scorching. Also, rinsing the rice removes excess starch which reduces stickiness. Don’t stir while cooking, and ensure there’s enough broth for the rice to absorb.
Can I add vegetables to this dish?
Yes, adding diced veggies like carrots, peas, or bell peppers works great. Add them with the broth so they cook evenly with the rice. Adjust broth quantity if adding a lot to maintain proper moisture balance.
What’s the best way to reheat leftovers?
Reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of broth or water to keep the rice moist. Microwaving covered also works well but stir halfway through. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out the chicken and rice.
Can I use brown rice instead of white rice?
Yes, but brown rice requires longer cooking time (around 40-45 minutes) and more liquid. Adjust broth amounts accordingly and check for tenderness before serving.
How do I make the dish spicier?
Add diced jalapeños or a pinch of cayenne pepper when sautéing onions and garlic. Start with small amounts and adjust to taste to avoid overpowering the dish.
