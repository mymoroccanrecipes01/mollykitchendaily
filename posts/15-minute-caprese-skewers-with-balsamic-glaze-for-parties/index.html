Fresh and Easy Caprese Skewers with Sweet Balsamic Glaze

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These Caprese skewers are fresh, simple, and bursting with flavor. The sweet balsamic glaze adds a lovely touch that makes them perfect for parties or quick snacks. Super easy to whip up anytime!

Introduction

I first made these Caprese skewers a few summers ago when I needed a quick appetizer for a last-minute get-together. Honestly, I wasn’t sure if anyone would notice such a simple snack, but they were a huge hit! What I love about this recipe is how fresh and vibrant each bite tastes, thanks to the combination of juicy cherry tomatoes, creamy mozzarella, and fragrant basil. The secret that really takes these skewers up a notch is the homemade balsamic glaze — it adds just the right amount of sweetness and tang without overpowering the fresh ingredients. I’ve tried store-bought glazes before, but making it yourself is surprisingly easy and so much better. These skewers come together in about 15 minutes, making them perfect for unexpected guests or when you just want something light and tasty. They look colorful and inviting on a platter, which always sparks compliments. Plus, they’re super versatile — great for summer parties, potlucks, or even as a snack when you’re craving something fresh and satisfying. I think you’ll really enjoy how effortlessly elegant they feel, even though they’re so simple to make. Just be sure to pick the freshest tomatoes and basil you can find, it makes all the difference!

What You'll Need

Approximately 20 skewers, 6 servings

  • 20 cherry tomatoes, ripe
  • 20 fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini), drained
  • 20 fresh basil leaves
  • 1/2 cup balsamic vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon honey or brown sugar
  • Wooden skewers, about 6 inches long

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by making the balsamic glaze, which is key to adding that sweet and tangy punch. Pour the balsamic vinegar into a small saucepan and add the honey or brown sugar. Heat it over medium heat, stirring occasionally until the mixture reduces by about half and becomes syrupy. This usually takes around 10 to 12 minutes. Be careful not to let it burn — you want a shiny, thick glaze that coats the back of a spoon. If it gets too thick, you can add a splash of water to loosen it. This step is crucial because the glaze balances the freshness of the tomatoes and mozzarella with a bit of sweetness.
⏱ Time: 12 minutes
✓ Doneness: The glaze should be thick enough to coat the back of a spoon and drizzle slowly without running off too quickly.
Step 2
While the glaze is reducing, rinse the cherry tomatoes and fresh basil leaves under cold water and pat them dry gently with a towel. This keeps everything fresh and prevents watery skewers. Also, drain the mozzarella balls well — excess moisture can make the skewers soggy and dilute the flavor. Taking a moment to properly prep these ingredients ensures each bite is perfectly balanced and not watery or bland.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: All ingredients should be dry to the touch and ready for assembly without excess liquid.
Step 3
Now, assemble the skewers. Take a wooden skewer and start by threading a cherry tomato, followed by a folded basil leaf, and then a mozzarella ball. Repeat this pattern until the skewer is full, usually 3 to 4 pieces per skewer. The basil leaf adds a fresh herbal note that complements the creamy mozzarella and juicy tomato beautifully. Be gentle while threading to avoid bruising the basil or squishing the tomatoes.
⏱ Time: 8 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers should hold together nicely with no ingredients slipping off or being crushed.
Step 4
Arrange the finished skewers neatly on a serving platter. Drizzle the balsamic glaze generously over the top just before serving. The glaze adds a glossy finish and a burst of flavor that ties all the fresh ingredients together. Avoid glazing too early, as the balsamic might make the basil wilt or the mozzarella soggy if left to sit too long.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers look glossy and appetizing, with the glaze evenly coating the ingredients without pooling.
Step 5
For an extra touch, sprinkle a pinch of flaky sea salt or cracked black pepper over the skewers. This enhances the flavors and adds a subtle crunch. It’s a small step that really makes the dish pop, especially if your tomatoes aren’t as sweet as you hoped. Season just before serving to keep the textures fresh.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Seasoning evenly distributed without overpowering the fresh ingredients.
Step 6
If you want to prep ahead, you can assemble the skewers without the glaze and cover them tightly with plastic wrap. Keep them refrigerated and add the balsamic drizzle just before serving. This helps maintain the fresh textures and prevents the basil from wilting. Planning ahead like this saves time on party day and keeps everything tasting crisp.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers are fully assembled, covered, and chilled without moisture buildup.
Step 7
When serving, offer extra balsamic glaze on the side for guests who want a little more sweetness or tang. Some people love an extra drizzle! Having the glaze on the side also lets you keep the skewers fresh longer if they’re sitting out for a while. Plus, it looks elegant and inviting.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Glaze is served in a small bowl or drizzle bottle alongside the platter.
Step 8
For a fun variation, try adding a small piece of prosciutto or a slice of avocado on each skewer. This adds richness and a different texture that pairs wonderfully with the fresh ingredients. Just be sure to add these extras right before serving to keep everything fresh and vibrant.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Additional ingredients are fresh and complement the classic flavors without overpowering.
Step 9
Finally, serve your Caprese skewers at room temperature or slightly chilled. They’re best enjoyed fresh but can be made a few hours ahead if properly stored. The combination of creamy mozzarella, sweet tomatoes, fresh basil, and tangy balsamic glaze is a guaranteed crowd-pleaser that feels fancy but is incredibly simple!
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Skewers are fresh, vibrant, and flavorful when served.

Variations

Prosciutto Caprese Skewers

Add a slice of prosciutto between the mozzarella and basil for a salty, savory twist. This adds richness and makes the skewers more substantial for a heartier appetizer.

Note: Add the prosciutto last to avoid tearing and serve immediately to keep the meat fresh.

Avocado Caprese Skewers

Add a small cube of ripe avocado for a creamy texture that complements the mozzarella. This variation adds healthy fats and a buttery flavor, perfect for summer gatherings.

Note: Add avocado just before serving to prevent browning and keep the skewers visually appealing.

Spicy Caprese Skewers

Sprinkle red pepper flakes over the skewers or add a small slice of jalapeño for a kick of heat. This gives a nice contrast to the sweet balsamic glaze and fresh ingredients.

Note: Adjust spice level to taste and warn guests if serving at parties.

Pesto Drizzled Caprese Skewers

Swap the balsamic glaze for a drizzle of fresh basil pesto. This adds a richer herbal flavor and a slightly oily texture that pairs beautifully with the mozzarella and tomatoes.

Note: Use a light drizzle to avoid overpowering the fresh flavors.

Grilled Caprese Skewers

Lightly grill the skewers for a couple of minutes on each side to add smoky char marks and deepen the tomato flavor. This variation adds warmth and complexity to the classic fresh appetizer.

Note: Use soaked wooden skewers and watch carefully to avoid burning the basil.

Pro Tips

Make the balsamic glaze ahead of time and store it in the fridge.
Cooling the glaze allows it to thicken further and makes it easier to drizzle. If you try to reduce it right before serving, it can be too hot and runny, which won’t coat the skewers properly. Having it ready also speeds up assembly.
Use wooden skewers soaked in water for 10 minutes before assembling.
Soaking wooden skewers prevents them from burning if you decide to grill or broil the skewers later. It also makes threading easier and helps the skewers hold together better without splitting or snapping.
Pat all ingredients dry before assembling skewers.
Excess moisture from tomatoes or mozzarella can dilute flavors and cause the balsamic glaze to slide off. Dry ingredients help the glaze stick better and keep the skewers fresh and vibrant longer.
Add the balsamic glaze just before serving.
Applying the glaze too early can cause the basil to wilt and the mozzarella to become soggy, which ruins the fresh texture and appearance. Drizzling right before serving maintains the crispness and visual appeal.
Use ripe cherry tomatoes instead of larger tomato slices.
Cherry tomatoes are the perfect bite-size and have a higher skin to flesh ratio, which adds a pleasant pop and texture contrast. Larger slices can be watery and harder to skewer neatly.
Try gently folding basil leaves instead of threading them flat.
Folding basil makes it easier to thread without tearing and adds visual layers on the skewer, enhancing both appearance and flavor distribution.
Serve skewers at room temperature for best flavor.
Cold mozzarella can taste bland, and chilled tomatoes lose some sweetness. Allowing the skewers to come to room temperature brings out the full depth of flavors.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using watery or underripe tomatoes

Why it happens Sometimes people grab tomatoes that look ripe but are actually watery or bland, thinking any tomato will do.

Fix Pick firm, ripe cherry tomatoes with vibrant color and a slight give when gently pressed. This ensures sweetness and prevents soggy skewers.

Skipping draining mozzarella

Why it happens Fresh mozzarella is often packed in water, and skipping this step can lead to soggy skewers.

Fix Drain mozzarella balls thoroughly and pat dry before assembling to avoid excess moisture diluting flavors and making the skewers soggy.

Adding balsamic glaze too early

Why it happens People often glaze the skewers too soon, thinking it will soak in better.

Fix Wait to drizzle the glaze just before serving to keep basil fresh and mozzarella from becoming soggy.

Bruising basil leaves when threading

Why it happens Forgetting to be gentle or threading too forcefully can tear basil leaves.

Fix Fold basil leaves gently and thread carefully to preserve their integrity and flavor.

Not reducing balsamic vinegar enough

Why it happens Rushing the glaze reduction results in a thin, watery balsamic that doesn’t coat well.

Fix Simmer the balsamic vinegar slowly until it thickens to a syrupy consistency, stirring occasionally to avoid burning.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These skewers are best served fresh but can be left at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely to prevent drying out. Avoid longer to maintain freshness and safety.

Refrigerator

Store assembled skewers without balsamic glaze in an airtight container for up to 24 hours. Add glaze just before serving to keep basil crisp and mozzarella fresh. Cold skewers may lose some flavor and texture but are still tasty.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as fresh tomatoes, basil, and mozzarella lose texture and flavor upon thawing. It’s best to prepare fresh or refrigerate briefly.

Reheating

Reheating is unnecessary and not advised. Serve at room temperature or chilled. If desired, allow refrigerated skewers to sit out 15 minutes before serving to enhance flavor.

Make Ahead

You can prep the balsamic glaze and assemble skewers up to 24 hours ahead (without glaze). Store covered in the fridge and drizzle glaze just before serving to keep everything fresh and vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use other types of tomatoes for these skewers?
You can use grape tomatoes or small plum tomatoes if cherry tomatoes aren’t available. Just make sure they’re ripe and firm. Larger tomato slices can be used but might be harder to skewer and change the bite-size nature of the appetizer.
How long does the balsamic glaze keep after making it?
The glaze can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks. Reheat gently before using to loosen the consistency if it thickens too much.
Can I make these skewers vegan?
Absolutely! Substitute the mozzarella balls with vegan cheese alternatives or marinated tofu cubes. The balsamic glaze and fresh basil still make for a delicious combination.
What if I don’t have balsamic vinegar? Can I skip the glaze?
While the glaze adds a lovely sweetness and tang, you can skip it and just drizzle some good olive oil and a sprinkle of salt and pepper. It won’t be the same but still tasty and fresh.
Is it okay to prepare these skewers a day ahead?
Yes, but keep them unglazed and covered tightly in the fridge. Add the balsamic glaze just before serving to keep the basil fresh and prevent sogginess.
Can I use dried basil instead of fresh?
Fresh basil is highly recommended for its flavor and aroma. Dried basil won’t provide the same freshness or texture and is not ideal for this recipe.
How can I prevent basil from wilting on the skewers?
Add basil leaves gently folded and drizzle the balsamic glaze only right before serving. Also, keep skewers refrigerated until serving to slow wilting.
What’s the best way to serve these at a party?
Arrange skewers on a large platter with extra balsamic glaze on the side. Keep them chilled until guests arrive and drizzle glaze just before serving for maximum freshness and flavor.
