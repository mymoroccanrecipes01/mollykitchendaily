Moist and Chocolatey Zucchini Bread You’ll Want Every Morning

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 9, 2026
15 min Prep Time
40 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This zucchini chocolate bread is a cozy, moist treat that blends tender zucchini with rich chocolate perfectly. It’s quick to make and great for breakfast or an afternoon snack. I love how every bite melts in your mouth!

Introduction

I first made this zucchini chocolate bread on a whim when I had some extra zucchini from my garden and a craving for chocolate. Honestly, I wasn’t sure how the veggies would work in a chocolate loaf, but the results blew me away. This version is extra moist thanks to squeezing the zucchini to just the right amount of moisture—not too wet—and folding in melty chocolate chips that make every bite feel indulgent. I’ve tried other versions before that turned out dry or crumbly, but this one hits the perfect balance of tender crumb and rich chocolate flavor. The trick I learned was not overmixing the batter, which keeps the texture light and soft. When it’s fresh from the oven, the aroma alone is enough to make you want to dive in. It’s great for breakfast with coffee or as an afternoon pick-me-up. Plus, it’s quick to whip up on a lazy weekend or when you need a cozy snack. I think you’ll love how the zucchini sneaks in moisture without overpowering the chocolate goodness.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini), squeezed to remove excess moisture
  • 1 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with butter or non-stick spray. This ensures your bread won’t stick and will come out cleanly, preserving the loaf’s shape. A properly greased pan also helps with even baking, preventing burnt edges. Avoid skipping this step as it can make removing the bread tricky and messy.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Loaf pan is evenly greased and oven is fully preheated
Step 2
In a large bowl, sift together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, baking powder, and salt. Sifting helps combine the dry ingredients evenly and aerates the flour mixture, which leads to a lighter texture in the final loaf. Make sure to mix gently but thoroughly so all leavening agents are distributed to avoid uneven rising.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no lumps or clumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk the vegetable oil and sugar until combined, then add the eggs one at a time, whisking well after each addition. Add the vanilla extract last. This method helps emulsify the fats and sugars, creating a smooth batter that traps air for a tender crumb. Be careful not to overbeat here; just mix until combined to avoid tough bread.
⏱ Time: 7 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are smooth and slightly thickened
Step 4
Grate the zucchini using the large holes of a box grater, then place the shreds in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze out excess moisture. Removing water is key because too much moisture can make the bread soggy and dense. You want the zucchini to add moisture and texture without turning the batter runny.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini shreds feel damp but not dripping wet
Step 5
Fold the dry ingredients gently into the wet ingredients in thirds, mixing just until combined after each addition. Overmixing develops gluten, which can toughen the bread, so be gentle and stop as soon as the flour disappears. This folding technique keeps the batter light and tender.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform with no visible flour streaks but still slightly lumpy
Step 6
Fold in the grated zucchini and chocolate chips carefully, distributing them evenly through the batter without overworking it. The zucchini adds moisture and subtle texture, while the chocolate chips melt into pockets of sweetness. Make sure the chips are fresh and not melted beforehand to avoid clumping.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and chocolate chips are evenly mixed without overmixing
Step 7
Pour the batter into your prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and rise nicely without cracking unevenly. Avoid tapping the pan too hard, which can deflate the air bubbles you just created.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread, surface smooth but not compressed
Step 8
Bake in the preheated oven for 40-45 minutes, checking at 40 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center. If it comes out with a few moist crumbs but no raw batter, it’s done. Baking too long can dry out the bread, while underbaking leaves it gummy inside.
⏱ Time: 40 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out mostly clean with few moist crumbs
Step 9
Let the bread cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely. This resting time lets the structure set so slices hold together well. Cutting too soon can cause crumbly slices and a gummy texture.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm to the touch and cools slightly on wire rack

Variations

Walnut Zucchini Chocolate Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped toasted walnuts to the batter for added crunch and nutty flavor. The walnuts complement the chocolate and zucchini beautifully, making the bread heartier and perfect for snack time.

Note: Fold in nuts gently after the zucchini and chocolate chips to avoid overmixing.

Spiced Zucchini Chocolate Bread

Incorporate 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg into the dry ingredients for a warm, cozy twist. This gives the bread a subtle spice that pairs wonderfully with the chocolate and zucchini.

Note: Add spices when mixing dry ingredients to ensure even flavor distribution.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Chocolate Bread

Substitute all-purpose flour with a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend. The texture will be slightly different but still moist and delicious. Perfect for those avoiding gluten.

Note: Check that your baking powder is gluten-free and do not overmix to maintain tenderness.

Vegan Zucchini Chocolate Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (2 tbsp ground flaxseed + 6 tbsp water) and use coconut oil instead of vegetable oil. Use dairy-free chocolate chips to keep it vegan. The bread remains moist and rich with these swaps.

Note: Allow flax eggs to gel for 5 minutes before mixing to mimic egg binding.

Banana Zucchini Chocolate Bread

Add one ripe mashed banana to the wet ingredients for extra natural sweetness and moisture. This variation makes the bread even softer with a slight fruity note.

Note: Reduce sugar by 1/4 cup to balance the banana’s sweetness.

Pro Tips

Squeeze the zucchini well to remove excess water before adding it to the batter.
Water content in zucchini varies and too much moisture can make the bread dense and soggy. By squeezing it, you concentrate the flavor and moisture without compromising the bread’s structure.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add the flour.
Overmixing develops gluten, which toughens the bread. Mixing just until combined keeps the crumb tender and soft, ensuring a moist texture.
Use fresh baking soda and baking powder for proper rise.
Leavening agents lose potency over time, which can cause the bread to be flat or dense. Testing freshness by adding a pinch to vinegar or hot water can help ensure good rise.
Let the bread cool completely on a wire rack before slicing.
Cooling allows the crumb to set and prevents the bread from crumbling or becoming gummy when cut. Patience here improves texture and presentation.
Add chocolate chips last and fold in gently.
Folding prevents the chips from melting prematurely or clumping, distributing them evenly for pockets of melted chocolate in every bite.
Use a loaf pan with straight sides for even baking.
Loaf shape affects baking time and crumb texture. Straight-sided pans promote even heat distribution and prevent uneven baking or domed tops.
Preheat your oven fully before baking.
An accurate oven temperature ensures proper leavening and baking. If the oven is too cool, the bread won’t rise well; too hot, and it may burn outside while raw inside.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture out of the zucchini

Why it happens People often assume zucchini will absorb moisture during baking, but too much water makes the bread soggy and prevents proper rise.

Fix Grate the zucchini finely, place it in a clean kitchen towel, and squeeze firmly to remove excess water before folding it into the batter.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid lumps, but this activates gluten and toughens the bread.

Fix Mix gently and only until the flour disappears. A few lumps are fine and will bake out.

Using expired leavening agents

Why it happens Old baking soda or powder loses effectiveness, often unnoticed until the bread fails to rise properly.

Fix Test freshness or replace baking soda/powder regularly to ensure good rise.

Slicing the bread while it’s still hot

Why it happens Impatience leads to crumbly, gummy slices since the structure hasn’t set.

Fix Let the bread cool completely on a wire rack before cutting for clean slices.

Using Dutch-processed cocoa powder instead of natural

Why it happens Dutch cocoa is less acidic and won’t react with baking soda, affecting rise and flavor.

Fix Use natural unsweetened cocoa powder for proper leavening and chocolate flavor.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the zucchini chocolate bread covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel at room temperature for up to 2 days. Avoid airtight containers at room temp as it can trap moisture and make the crust soggy.

Refrigerator

Keep the bread in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap in the fridge for up to 5 days. It may dry out slightly but can be refreshed by warming briefly in the microwave or oven.

Freezer

Wrap the loaf tightly in plastic wrap and then foil, or slice and freeze individual pieces in airtight bags for up to 3 months. Thaw slices at room temperature or warm gently before serving to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a 350°F oven for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a small pat of butter or a drizzle of milk before warming can enhance moisture and flavor. Avoid overheating or it can dry out.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze the zucchini a day ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix the dry ingredients in advance as well. Assemble the batter and bake on the day you plan to serve for freshest results.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this bread?
Yes, but you must thaw it completely and squeeze out all excess moisture before adding to the batter. Frozen zucchini tends to hold more water, so thorough draining is essential to avoid a soggy loaf.
What if I don’t have cocoa powder?
Cocoa powder is key for the chocolate flavor and acidity needed for rise, so substituting it isn’t recommended. If you want a plain zucchini bread, try a different recipe without chocolate.
How long does this bread keep fresh?
At room temperature, it stays fresh for about 2 days. Refrigerated, it lasts up to 5 days but may dry out slightly. Freezing is best for longer storage, up to 3 months.
Can I double this recipe?
Yes, you can double the ingredients and bake in two loaf pans or a larger pan. Just monitor baking time as it may increase slightly. Test doneness with a toothpick as usual.
Why is my bread dense or gummy?
Common causes include too much moisture from zucchini, overmixing the batter, or underbaking. Make sure to squeeze the zucchini well, mix gently, and bake until a toothpick comes out mostly clean.
Can I substitute oil with butter?
You can, but it may change the texture slightly, making the bread denser and less moist. If using butter, melt it first and cool before mixing for best results.
What’s the best way to grate zucchini?
Use the large holes on a box grater for tender strands that bake nicely into the bread. Grating too finely can release excess water, so avoid using a food processor for this step.
Can I add nuts or dried fruit?
Absolutely! Toasted walnuts, pecans, or dried cherries work well. Fold them in gently with the zucchini and chocolate chips to distribute evenly without overmixing.
