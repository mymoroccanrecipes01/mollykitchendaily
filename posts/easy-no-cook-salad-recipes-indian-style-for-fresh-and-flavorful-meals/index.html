Fresh and Flavorful No-Cook Indian Salad Perfect for Light Meals

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 9, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This no-cook Indian salad is a refreshing mix of crunchy veggies and vibrant spices that bring a burst of flavor without any fuss. I love it for quick lunches or when I want something light but satisfying.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon no-cook Indian salads during a summer visit to a friend’s home. The heat was relentless, and everyone was craving something fresh and light to eat. That’s when I realized how brilliant it is to combine crunchy veggies with vibrant Indian spices without turning on the stove. Since then, this salad has become my go-to when I want a quick, healthy meal that doesn’t skimp on flavor. What I love about this version is how the chaat masala and roasted cumin powder add a tangy, smoky depth that you don’t usually find in simple salads. Plus, the black salt gives it that unique savory note that just makes you want to keep eating. I usually toss it all together just before serving to keep the veggies crisp and fresh. It’s perfect for lunch or a light dinner, especially on warm days when you want to eat something refreshing but still satisfying. The best part? It’s ready in under 15 minutes, so you can whip it up anytime you need a quick bite. I think you’ll love how this salad balances simplicity with bold flavor — it’s like a little celebration in every bowl!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 2 cups chopped cucumber, fresh
  • 1 cup chopped tomatoes, ripe
  • 1 cup thinly sliced red onion
  • 1 cup grated carrot
  • 1/2 cup chopped fresh cilantro
  • 1 green chili, finely chopped (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon chaat masala
  • 1/2 teaspoon roasted cumin powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon black salt
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • Salt to taste

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing and chopping all your vegetables: cucumber, tomatoes, red onion, and carrot. Make sure to chop them into bite-sized pieces for easy eating. Washing thoroughly removes any dirt or residues, and chopping uniformly ensures every forkful has a balanced mix of flavors and textures. Avoid making the pieces too large, as that can make the salad harder to eat and less enjoyable.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Vegetables are clean, fresh-smelling, and chopped evenly into small pieces.
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, combine the chopped veggies with the finely chopped green chili and fresh cilantro. Adding the chili is optional but it brings a nice kick to the salad. Mixing these ingredients well helps distribute flavors evenly. If you add the chili, be sure to taste as you go to avoid overpowering the dish if you’re sensitive to heat.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ingredients are evenly mixed and the aroma of fresh herbs and chili is noticeable.
Step 3
Sprinkle the chaat masala, roasted cumin powder, black salt, and regular salt over the salad. These spices are the heart of the dish, giving it that unmistakable Indian flavor. Toss everything gently but thoroughly so the spices coat the veggies evenly. Don’t rush this step because uneven seasoning can cause some bites to be bland and others too salty or spicy.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Spices are evenly distributed and the salad has a light coating of seasoning.
Step 4
Squeeze the juice of one lemon over the salad and toss again. The lemon juice adds brightness and balances the spices with a fresh citrus note. Adding it last helps preserve the crisp texture of the vegetables and prevents them from getting soggy. If you add lemon juice too early, the salad might release water and become watery.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Salad looks fresh with a slight glossy coating from the lemon juice.
Step 5
Taste the salad and adjust salt or chili if needed. This final tasting step is crucial because spices can vary based on brand and freshness. Adjusting seasoning to your preference ensures the salad is perfectly balanced. Remember, it’s easier to add more salt or chili than to fix an overly spicy or salty salad.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced flavor with a hint of spice and citrus, no overpowering notes.
Step 6
Let the salad sit for 5-10 minutes before serving. This resting time allows the flavors to meld together without losing the fresh crunch of the veggies. It also helps the spices infuse the salad, enhancing the overall taste. Don’t let it sit too long or the salad will become soggy and less appetizing.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Aromatic and flavorful salad with vibrant colors and crisp texture.
Step 7
Serve the salad chilled or at room temperature as a light lunch or side dish. It pairs beautifully with grilled meats or rice dishes but also stands on its own for a quick snack. Chilling enhances the refreshing quality, especially on hot days. If serving later, cover and refrigerate immediately after resting.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad is fresh, vibrant, and inviting on the plate.
Step 8
If you want to add some crunch, sprinkle roasted peanuts or sev (crispy chickpea noodles) on top just before serving. This adds texture contrast and a nutty flavor that complements the spices perfectly. Adding toppings too early will cause them to lose their crunch.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Crunchy toppings remain crisp and fresh.
Step 9
Enjoy the salad immediately for the best texture and flavor. Leftovers can be refrigerated but the veggies might soften over time. If you make extra, keep the dressing and toppings separate and mix just before eating for maximum freshness.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad tastes fresh and vibrant when served.

Variations

Add Chickpeas for Protein Boost

Mix in a cup of cooked chickpeas to turn the salad into a more filling meal. The chickpeas add creaminess and protein, making it great for lunch or a light dinner. This boosts the nutritional value without changing the fresh, spicy character.

Note: Since chickpeas add moisture, toss them gently and serve immediately to prevent sogginess.

Spicy Mango Twist

Add diced raw mango and a pinch of red chili powder for a sweet and spicy twist. This variation adds a seasonal flavor and a tangy sweetness that contrasts beautifully with the spices.

Note: Use firm, unripe mango to keep the salad crunchy and fresh.

Cucumber Raita Style

Fold in a few tablespoons of plain yogurt for a creamy, cooling version of the salad. This makes it more like a raita but keeps the crunchy veggies and spices intact, perfect for pairing with spicy dishes.

Note: Add yogurt just before serving to maintain texture.

Herb-Heavy Salad

Increase the cilantro and add fresh mint leaves for an herbaceous, refreshing salad. This variation brightens the flavors and adds complexity, great for summer meals.

Note: Chop herbs finely and add last to preserve their vibrant flavor.

Roasted Peanut Garnish

Top the salad with roasted, salted peanuts for an extra crunch and nutty flavor. This adds texture contrast and a satisfying bite, making the salad more indulgent.

Note: Add peanuts just before serving to keep them crunchy.

Pro Tips

Use freshly ground spices for the best flavor.
Spices lose their potency quickly once ground. Freshly grinding cumin seeds or opening a new packet of chaat masala ensures the salad has the most vibrant aroma and taste. Stale spices can make your salad dull and flat.
Add lemon juice last to keep the veggies crisp.
Lemon juice’s acidity breaks down vegetable cell walls, causing sogginess if added too early. Adding it right before serving preserves the crunch and freshness, giving a better texture and mouthfeel.
Chop vegetables uniformly for even flavor distribution.
Uniform chopping makes sure every bite has a balanced mix of flavors and textures. Larger pieces may overpower or leave some bites bland, while too small pieces can become mushy quickly.
Let the salad rest for a few minutes after tossing spices.
Resting allows the spices to infuse the vegetables, mellowing harsh edges and blending flavors. Skipping this step can result in uneven seasoning and a less harmonious taste.
Use black salt for authentic Indian flavor.
Black salt’s unique sulfurous flavor is essential for recreating the signature taste of Indian street salads. Regular salt lacks this complexity and changes the overall flavor profile.
Serve the salad chilled for maximum refreshment.
Chilling enhances the crispness and makes the salad more satisfying, especially on hot days. Warm salad tends to taste flat and less appetizing.
Add crunchy toppings just before serving.
Toppings like roasted peanuts or sev add texture contrast but lose crunch quickly when mixed too early. Adding them last keeps the salad exciting and texturally interesting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Adding lemon juice too early

Why it happens Many cooks assume lemon juice can be mixed in at the start to marinate the salad, but this causes veggies to become soggy quickly.

Fix Add lemon juice right before serving to keep vegetables crisp. If prepping ahead, store veggies and lemon juice separately and combine just before eating.

Using regular salt instead of black salt

Why it happens Black salt is less common and some may not realize it’s a key ingredient for authentic flavor.

Fix Seek out black salt in Indian or specialty stores. Using it is essential to achieve that signature savory and slightly sulfurous taste.

Over-chopping vegetables

Why it happens In an effort to make the salad easy to eat, some chop veggies too finely, which leads to a mushy texture.

Fix Chop veggies into bite-sized pieces that retain crunch. This keeps the salad fresh and enjoyable.

Not resting the salad after seasoning

Why it happens Skipping the rest time to save time, but this results in uneven flavor and a less cohesive dish.

Fix Allow the salad to rest 5-10 minutes after tossing with spices to let flavors meld for the best taste.

Using bottled lemon juice

Why it happens Convenience leads some to use bottled juice, but it can taste artificial or harsh.

Fix Always use fresh lemon juice for a bright, natural flavor that balances the spices and veggies.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad is best eaten fresh. It can safely sit at room temperature for up to 1 hour covered but will lose crispness and freshness quickly.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 1 day. The salad will soften and lose some crunch. Keep lemon juice separate if storing longer to avoid sogginess.

Freezer

Not recommended to freeze this salad as the fresh vegetables will become mushy upon thawing.

Reheating

No reheating needed since it’s a fresh salad. Serve chilled or at room temperature for best flavor.

Make Ahead

Chop vegetables and prepare spices up to a day ahead, storing separately. Mix and add lemon juice just before serving to keep salad crisp and fresh.

FAQ

Can I make this salad ahead of time?
You can chop veggies and mix spices a few hours ahead, but add lemon juice and toss just before serving to keep the salad crisp. Storing the salad fully mixed will cause sogginess.
What can I substitute for black salt?
Black salt has a unique sulfurous flavor hard to replicate. If unavailable, use regular salt but the flavor won't be quite the same. Adding a bit of smoked paprika can mimic some earthiness, but it’s not a perfect substitute.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Absolutely! Bell peppers, radishes, or even finely chopped cabbage work well. Just keep the pieces bite-sized and fresh to maintain the salad’s crisp texture.
How spicy is this salad with the green chili?
The green chili adds a mild to moderate heat, depending on its size and type. You can omit it or reduce the amount if you prefer a milder salad.
Is this salad gluten-free and vegan?
Yes, this salad is naturally gluten-free and vegan as it contains only fresh vegetables and spices without any animal products or gluten ingredients.
Can I use bottled lemon juice?
While you can, fresh lemon juice is recommended for the best bright, natural flavor. Bottled juice can taste artificial and may affect the salad’s freshness.
What’s the best way to serve this salad?
Serve it chilled or at room temperature as a side dish or light lunch. It pairs well with grilled meats, rice dishes, or even on its own as a refreshing snack.
How do I keep the salad from becoming soggy?
Add lemon juice and any crunchy toppings right before serving. Also, avoid chopping veggies too small and store the salad in airtight containers if refrigerating.
