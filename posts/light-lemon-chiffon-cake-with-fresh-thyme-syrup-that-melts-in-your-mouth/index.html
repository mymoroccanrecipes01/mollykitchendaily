Light and Airy Lemon Chiffon Cake with a Refreshing Thyme Syrup Twist

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 11, 2026
20 min Prep Time
40 min Time
8 Servings
Medium Difficulty

This lemon chiffon cake is super light and fluffy with a delicate thyme-infused syrup that gives it a fresh herbal note. It’s perfect for spring afternoons or anytime you need a bright, uplifting dessert that’s not too heavy.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon lemon chiffon cake while browsing vintage cookbooks, and from the first bite, I was hooked. It’s one of those desserts that feels fancy but is surprisingly forgiving to bake. What sets this version apart is the thyme syrup — I love how the herbal notes balance the bright citrus, adding a subtle complexity you don’t usually find in chiffon cakes. Over the years, I’ve tweaked the technique to ensure the cake stays ultra-light and fluffy: the key is whipping the egg whites to soft peaks and folding them gently to keep all that air trapped inside. I remember one early attempt when I overmixed and ended up with a dense cake — lesson learned! The final cake is golden, tender, and melts in your mouth, with a moist crumb thanks to the syrup soak. It’s perfect for spring or any time you want a dessert that feels fresh and light without fuss. I usually serve it with a cup of tea or fresh berries, and it never fails to impress guests or lift my mood on a gray day.

What You'll Need

1 9-inch chiffon cake, 8 servings

  • 1 ½ cups cake flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 7 large eggs, separated
  • ½ cup vegetable oil
  • ¾ cup water
  • ¼ cup fresh lemon juice
  • 2 teaspoons lemon zest
  • 1 teaspoon cream of tartar
  • 1 tablespoon fresh thyme leaves
  • ½ cup water (for syrup)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 325°F (160°C). In a large bowl, sift together the cake flour, ½ cup of the sugar, baking powder, and salt. Sifting ensures the dry ingredients are light and well combined, which helps the cake rise evenly and have a tender crumb. Avoid skipping this step, or your cake might be dense or have lumps. Set this bowl aside for now.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined and light, with no clumps.
Step 2
In a separate bowl, whisk the egg yolks with the vegetable oil, water, lemon juice, and lemon zest until smooth. This mixture is the wet base that adds moisture and flavor. The acidity from the lemon juice will also react with the baking powder to give the cake its lift. Make sure the yolks are fully incorporated for a uniform batter.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, pale yellow, and slightly thickened.
Step 3
Combine the wet ingredients with the dry ingredients by gently folding them together until just combined. Be careful not to overmix here; overworking the batter can develop gluten, making the cake tough rather than tender and airy. The batter should be smooth but still light.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: No streaks of flour remain, and batter is smooth but not dense.
Step 4
In a clean, dry bowl, beat the egg whites with a mixer on medium speed until foamy. Add cream of tartar to stabilize the whites and help them hold volume. Gradually add the remaining ½ cup sugar while continuing to beat until stiff, glossy peaks form. This step is crucial to the cake’s light texture because the whipped egg whites trap air that expands during baking. Avoid any grease or yolk in the whites, or they won’t whip properly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Stiff, glossy peaks that hold shape without collapsing.
Step 5
Gently fold about one-third of the whipped egg whites into the batter to lighten it. Use a large spatula and a gentle folding motion to preserve as much air as possible. Then fold in the remaining whites carefully until just combined. This technique keeps the batter airy and ensures the cake rises beautifully and stays tender.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: No visible streaks of egg whites, batter looks light and fluffy.
Step 6
Pour the batter into an ungreased 9-inch chiffon cake pan. It’s important not to grease the pan so the batter can cling to the sides and rise properly. Smooth the top gently with a spatula. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any large air bubbles.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread with no large bubbles.
Step 7
Bake the cake for 35-40 minutes until a toothpick inserted near the center comes out clean and the cake springs back when lightly pressed. Avoid opening the oven door too early as this can cause the cake to collapse. The lower temperature and longer bake time ensure the cake cooks through without drying out.
⏱ Time: 35-40 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden top, toothpick clean, and springy texture.
Step 8
Immediately invert the pan onto a cooling rack or bottle neck to cool completely upside down. This prevents the cake from collapsing as it cools and maintains its height and airy texture. Let it cool for at least 1 hour before removing from the pan.
⏱ Time: 60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake is fully cooled and holds shape when removed.
Step 9
While the cake cools, prepare the thyme syrup by combining ½ cup water, ½ cup sugar, and fresh thyme leaves in a small saucepan. Bring to a simmer and stir until sugar dissolves. Let it steep off the heat for 10 minutes, then strain out the thyme. Brush the syrup generously over the cooled cake to add moisture and a subtle herbal flavor that complements the lemon beautifully.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Syrup is clear, fragrant with thyme, and absorbed into the cake.

Variations

Lavender Lemon Chiffon Cake

Swap fresh thyme for culinary lavender buds in the syrup for a floral twist. Lavender pairs beautifully with lemon for a delicate flavor that’s perfect for spring. The syrup infusion remains the same but steep the lavender gently to avoid bitterness.

Note: Use dried lavender buds sparingly and strain well to prevent overpowering the cake’s texture.

Orange Chiffon Cake with Rosemary Syrup

Replace lemon juice and zest with fresh orange for a sweeter citrus flavor, and infuse the syrup with fresh rosemary instead of thyme. This variation offers a warm, aromatic profile that’s great for fall.

Note: Orange juice is less acidic, so watch for slight differences in rise and adjust baking powder if needed.

Gluten-Free Lemon Chiffon Cake

Use a gluten-free flour blend designed for cakes instead of cake flour. The texture will be slightly different but still tender and moist. Be sure to fold whites carefully to maintain lightness.

Note: Gluten-free flours often require additional binding agents; consider adding a teaspoon of xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t include it.

Lemon Chiffon Cake with Coconut Syrup

Infuse the syrup with shredded coconut or coconut extract instead of thyme for a tropical flair. This pairs nicely with the citrus and adds a subtle sweetness and texture variation.

Note: Strain the syrup well if using shredded coconut to keep the cake texture smooth.

Vegan Lemon Chiffon Cake

Use aquafaba (chickpea water) whipped to stiff peaks instead of egg whites and replace egg yolks with a flaxseed mixture. The texture will be lighter but slightly different; the thyme syrup adds moisture and flavor.

Note: Whip aquafaba longer than egg whites to achieve similar volume and stability.

Pro Tips

Always separate eggs while cold but whip whites at room temperature.
Cold eggs separate more cleanly, reducing risk of yolk contamination in whites, which can ruin whipping. Room temperature whites whip to higher volume faster, trapping more air for a lighter cake.
Sift dry ingredients multiple times for extra lightness.
Sifting aerates the flour and evenly distributes leavening agents, preventing clumps and promoting uniform rise and tender texture.
Do not grease the chiffon cake pan.
The batter needs to cling to the pan walls to climb and rise properly. Greasing prevents this, causing the cake to fall and lose height.
Fold the egg whites gently using a spatula with a lifting and turning motion.
This technique preserves the trapped air bubbles in the whites, which are essential for the cake’s airy texture.
Cool the cake upside down in the pan.
Inverting prevents the cake from collapsing under its own weight as it cools, maintaining volume and height.
Use fresh lemon juice and zest for the best flavor.
Fresh lemon provides vibrant acidity and aromatic oils from the zest that bottled juice lacks, enhancing overall flavor.
Brush the thyme syrup generously after cooling.
Applying syrup while the cake is still slightly warm helps absorption, keeping the cake moist and infusing it with herbal notes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter after adding egg whites

Why it happens Many cooks get excited to finish the batter quickly and stir vigorously, not realizing this deflates the whipped whites.

Fix Use a gentle folding motion with a spatula, turning the bowl slowly and lifting the batter from the bottom. Stop folding as soon as no white streaks remain to keep the batter light.

Greasing the chiffon cake pan

Why it happens Habit from baking other cakes leads to greasing pans, but chiffon cakes rely on pan walls to support rise.

Fix Leave the pan ungreased so the batter can cling and climb the sides during baking, helping the cake maintain volume.

Not stabilizing egg whites with cream of tartar

Why it happens Some skip this ingredient thinking it’s optional, but it’s key for volume and stability.

Fix Always add cream of tartar when whipping whites; it strengthens protein bonds and keeps peaks firm.

Opening the oven door too early

Why it happens Impatience or curiosity during baking can cause temperature drops.

Fix Resist opening the door until at least 30 minutes into baking to avoid collapse from sudden temperature changes.

Removing cake from pan before cooling completely

Why it happens Eager to serve, some remove cake too soon, causing it to deflate.

Fix Cool upside down in the pan for at least an hour to set structure before unmolding.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The cake can be kept at room temperature for up to 1 day if covered tightly with plastic wrap or stored in an airtight cake dome to prevent drying out. Avoid direct sunlight or heat to maintain freshness.

Refrigerator

Store the cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The syrup helps keep it moist, but chilling may firm the texture slightly. Let it come to room temperature before serving for best flavor.

Freezer

Wrap the cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and bring to room temperature before serving. Syrup moisture helps protect from freezer burn.

Reheating

If you prefer warm cake, gently heat slices in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or warm in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes. Avoid overheating to prevent drying; add a light brush of syrup or lemon juice if needed.

Make Ahead

You can bake the cake and prepare the thyme syrup 1-2 days ahead. Store the cooled cake wrapped without syrup, then brush syrup on just before serving to keep it fresh and moist.

FAQ

Can I use all-purpose flour instead of cake flour?
You can substitute all-purpose flour, but the cake will be slightly denser and less tender. To mimic cake flour, remove 2 tablespoons of all-purpose flour per cup and replace with cornstarch, then sift well. This lowers the protein content, helping keep the cake light.
What if my egg whites don’t whip to stiff peaks?
Make sure your bowl and beaters are completely clean and dry, and free from any grease or yolk. Use room temperature egg whites for better volume. Adding cream of tartar stabilizes the whites, so don’t skip it. If whites still won’t whip, try a fresh batch of eggs.
How can I make the cake more lemony?
Add extra lemon zest or a teaspoon of lemon extract to the batter for a stronger citrus flavor. You can also increase the lemon juice in the syrup slightly. Just be cautious not to add too much liquid to avoid affecting the cake texture.
Why do I need to invert the cake while cooling?
Chiffon cakes are delicate and can collapse under their own weight as they cool. Cooling upside down uses gravity to keep the cake tall and airy by preventing shrinkage and deflation.
Can I make this cake gluten-free?
Yes! Use a gluten-free flour blend suitable for cakes, and consider adding xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t contain it. The texture may differ slightly but will still be tender and delicious.
Is it okay to use dried thyme instead of fresh?
Fresh thyme gives the best flavor and aroma for the syrup. If using dried thyme, reduce the amount by half since it’s more concentrated, and steep it gently to avoid bitterness.
Can I skip the thyme syrup?
You can, but the syrup adds moisture and a lovely herbal note that complements the lemon. Without it, the cake might be drier and less flavorful.
What’s the best way to store leftover cake?
Wrap leftovers tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container at room temperature for a day or refrigerate for up to 3 days. Bring to room temperature before eating for best texture.
