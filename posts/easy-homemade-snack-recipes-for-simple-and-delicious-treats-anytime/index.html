Easy Homemade Cinnamon Sugar Pretzels That Everyone Will Love

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 4, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These soft pretzels are a quick treat with a cozy cinnamon sugar twist. I love how the warm sugar coating melts just right after baking. Perfect for snack time or casual get-togethers!

Introduction

I first tried making soft pretzels years ago after craving that warm, doughy snack from the mall. My initial attempts were a bit tough and bland, but after some trial and error, I found a method that yields soft, chewy pretzels with a golden crust every time. What makes this recipe stand out is the quick baking soda bath—it’s the secret to that classic pretzel texture and color without needing complicated steps. Also, rolling the dough into ropes this way gives them that perfect shape, which is fun to do with family or friends. I like to sprinkle the warm pretzels with a cinnamon sugar mix right after brushing them with melted butter, which gives a cozy, sweet twist that feels special but is so simple. The finished pretzels are soft yet slightly chewy, with just enough cinnamon sweetness to make you want more. Honestly, these have become my go-to snack for when guests drop by or just when I want something comforting and homemade without a fuss.

What You'll Need

Approximately 8 pretzels, serves 8

  • 1 1/2 cups warm water (110°F)
  • 1 packet active dry yeast (2 1/4 tsp)
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 10 cups water
  • 2/3 cup baking soda
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tbsp ground cinnamon

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by activating the yeast: Pour warm water into a large bowl, sprinkle the yeast and sugar on top, then stir gently. Let it sit for about 5 minutes until it becomes foamy. This step is crucial because it confirms your yeast is alive and ready to help the dough rise. If it doesn’t foam, your yeast might be expired or your water temperature was off, so be sure the water is warm but not hot to avoid killing the yeast.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Foamy, bubbly surface indicating active yeast
Step 2
Add the flour and salt to the yeast mixture, then mix until it starts to come together. Knead the dough on a floured surface for about 8-10 minutes until it’s smooth and elastic. Kneading develops gluten, which gives the pretzels their chewy texture. Avoid adding too much flour, or your pretzels could end up dense.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, slightly tacky dough that springs back when poked
Step 3
Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl and cover it with a clean towel. Let it rise in a warm spot for about 1 hour until doubled in size. This rising period is key to creating that airy interior. Rushing this step can make the pretzels dense and heavy.
⏱ Time: 60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Doubled dough size with a soft, airy feel
Step 4
Preheat your oven to 450°F (230°C) and bring 10 cups of water with the baking soda to a boil in a large pot. The baking soda bath will give pretzels their signature chewy crust and deep brown color when baked. Don’t skip this step or your pretzels won’t have the authentic texture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rolling boil with baking soda dissolved
Step 5
While the water boils, punch down the dough and divide it into 8 equal pieces. Roll each piece into a long rope about 20 inches long, then twist into a pretzel shape. This shaping is part of the fun and gives pretzels their classic look. If the dough resists, let it rest a few minutes before shaping to relax the gluten.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly shaped pretzel ropes about 20 inches long
Step 6
Carefully dip each pretzel into the boiling baking soda water for about 30 seconds, then place it on a parchment-lined baking sheet. This bath firms up the dough exterior and sets the crust. Don’t leave them in too long or they’ll get soggy; 30 seconds is just right.
⏱ Time: 5-10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pretzels slightly puffed and wet but not falling apart
Step 7
Brush each pretzel with melted butter right after the baking soda bath. This helps the cinnamon sugar stick later and adds richness. Then sprinkle evenly with the cinnamon sugar mixture before baking. You want the sugar to lightly coat the pretzels without overwhelming them.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly coated pretzels with glossy butter and sugar
Step 8
Bake the pretzels in the preheated oven for 12-15 minutes until golden brown. Keep an eye on them during the last few minutes so they don’t burn. The baking soda crust will develop its color here, and the sugar will caramelize slightly for a perfect finish.
⏱ Time: 12-15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown pretzels with slightly crisp edges
Step 9
Remove the pretzels from the oven and brush again with melted butter while warm for extra shine and flavor. Let them cool for a few minutes before serving so the sugar sets slightly but the pretzels remain soft inside. This finishing touch elevates the flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Warm, shiny pretzels with a sweet cinnamon aroma

Variations

Savory Garlic Herb Pretzels

Skip the cinnamon sugar and brush pretzels with garlic butter mixed with herbs like rosemary and thyme before baking. Sprinkle with coarse sea salt for a savory twist that pairs well with dips.

Note: Reduce sugar in dough slightly and increase salt for balance. Garlic butter adds moisture and flavor but don’t overbrush or pretzels can become soggy.

Cheese-Stuffed Pretzels

Roll small cubes of your favorite cheese into the dough ropes before shaping. Bake as usual for warm, gooey cheese-filled pretzels that are perfect for parties.

Note: Use firmer cheeses like cheddar or mozzarella. Seal dough edges tightly to prevent cheese from leaking during boiling and baking.

Whole Wheat Pretzels

Replace half the all-purpose flour with whole wheat flour for a nuttier flavor and more fiber. The texture will be heartier but still soft and delicious.

Note: Whole wheat absorbs more water; you may need to add an extra tablespoon or two of warm water to achieve the right dough consistency.

Pretzel Bites with Honey Mustard Dip

Cut dough into bite-sized pieces instead of ropes and shape into small rounds. Serve with a homemade honey mustard sauce for dipping—a fun finger food variation.

Note: Shorter baking time (8-10 minutes) needed for smaller bites to avoid overbaking.

Vegan Cinnamon Sugar Pretzels

Use plant-based butter alternatives for brushing and replace honey with maple syrup if using as a dip. The dough remains the same, yielding soft, sweet vegan pretzels.

Note: Ensure vegan butter melts smoothly and use pure maple syrup for best flavor and consistency.

Pro Tips

Make sure your water is warm but not hot when activating yeast.
Water temperature around 110°F is ideal to wake up the yeast without killing it. Too hot water kills yeast cells, preventing dough from rising, while cold water slows activation, resulting in dense pretzels.
Don’t rush the dough rising step.
Allowing the dough to double in size ensures the yeast has produced enough gas bubbles, creating a light and airy texture inside. Skipping or shortening this leads to dense, heavy pretzels.
Use a large pot for the baking soda bath to prevent overcrowding.
Pretzels need room to float freely in the alkaline water for even crust development. Overcrowding causes uneven cooking and soggy spots.
Brush pretzels with butter immediately after the baking soda bath.
This step adds flavor and helps the cinnamon sugar stick better. If you wait too long, the dough surface dries and sugar won’t adhere evenly.
Roll dough ropes evenly to ensure uniform baking.
Uneven thickness causes thinner parts to overbake and thicker parts to remain undercooked. Consistent thickness ensures all pretzels bake evenly.
Use parchment paper for easy transfer and cleanup.
Parchment prevents sticking during baking and makes cleanup a breeze. It also helps pretzels bake evenly on the sheet.
Brush pretzels again with melted butter right out of the oven.
This final touch enhances shine and moisture, keeping pretzels soft and flavorful. Skipping it can result in a dull, dry crust.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using water that’s too hot or too cold for yeast activation

Why it happens Many assume any warm water is fine, but temperatures above 120°F kill yeast, and cold water slows fermentation, causing dough not to rise properly.

Fix Always use a thermometer or test water by touch—it should feel warm but not hot. Aim for about 110°F to ensure yeast activates fully for a good rise.

Skipping the baking soda bath

Why it happens Some think it’s an extra step and skip it to save time, not realizing it’s crucial for pretzel texture and crust development.

Fix Don’t skip this! Boil baking soda water and dip pretzels for 30 seconds each. This step sets the crust and creates that classic pretzel color.

Overcrowding the baking soda bath

Why it happens Trying to dip many pretzels at once to speed up the process can cause them to stick and cook unevenly.

Fix Dip pretzels one at a time or in small batches, giving each enough space to float freely for even coating and texture.

Not kneading dough enough

Why it happens In a rush, some skip thorough kneading, leading to poor gluten development and dense pretzels.

Fix Knead for at least 8-10 minutes until dough is smooth and elastic. Proper gluten structure is key to chewiness.

Applying cinnamon sugar before baking without butter

Why it happens Some sprinkle sugar directly on dry dough, causing it to fall off during baking.

Fix Brush pretzels with melted butter first to help cinnamon sugar stick and caramelize nicely during baking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Pretzels keep well at room temperature for up to 4 hours when stored in a paper bag to maintain crispness. Avoid plastic bags at room temp as moisture can make them soggy.

Refrigerator

Store pretzels in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Refrigeration may dry them slightly, so reheat gently before serving to restore softness.

Freezer

Freeze pretzels wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and foil for up to 2 months. Thaw at room temperature or overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat pretzels in a 350°F oven for 5-7 minutes to refresh crispness and warmth. Avoid microwaving as it can make them chewy or rubbery. Brush with a little butter before reheating for extra moisture.

Make Ahead

Prepare dough and shape pretzels up to 1 day ahead. Cover shaped pretzels with plastic wrap and refrigerate overnight, then proceed with the baking soda bath and baking the next day for fresher texture.

FAQ

Can I use instant yeast instead of active dry yeast?
Yes, you can substitute instant yeast but reduce the amount slightly (about 25% less). Instant yeast can be mixed directly with flour without proofing, which can save time. However, proofing active dry yeast as described ensures it’s alive and helps catch any issues early.
What if my pretzels come out too hard or tough?
This usually means the dough was over-kneaded, overbaked, or the water temperature was too high for yeast activation. Make sure to knead just until elastic and watch the baking time closely. Also, brushing with butter after baking helps keep them soft.
Can I make these gluten-free?
Gluten-free pretzels are tricky because gluten provides elasticity and chewiness. You’d need a specialized gluten-free flour blend with xanthan gum and possibly adjust hydration. This recipe isn’t optimized for gluten-free flours, so results may vary.
Why do I need to boil pretzels in baking soda water?
Boiling in baking soda water raises the dough’s pH, which triggers the Maillard browning reaction on the crust during baking. This gives pretzels their characteristic color, flavor, and chewy crust. Skipping this step results in pale, bread-like pretzels.
Can I shape the pretzels differently or make pretzel buns?
Absolutely! You can shape pretzels into knots, sticks, or even buns. Just adjust baking time slightly depending on size and thickness to ensure even baking without overcooking.
How should I store leftover pretzels?
Store pretzels in an airtight container at room temperature for up to a day or refrigerate for a few days. Reheat in the oven before eating to restore softness. Avoid plastic bags at room temp to prevent sogginess.
Is there a substitute for butter in this recipe?
You can use margarine or a plant-based butter substitute if you prefer. Keep in mind that butter adds flavor and richness, so substitutes may alter the taste slightly. Melt substitutes gently to avoid separation.
Can I add toppings other than cinnamon sugar?
Yes! Pretzels are versatile—try coarse salt for classic pretzels, sesame seeds, poppy seeds, or even grated cheese. Brush with butter first so toppings stick well before baking.
