Moist Banana Zucchini Bread That Feels Like a Cozy Hug

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 21, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This banana zucchini bread is my go-to when I want something soft and naturally sweet. The zucchini keeps it tender without being too veggie-forward. Great for breakfast or a mid-afternoon treat!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this banana zucchini bread recipe a few years ago when I had a bunch of overripe bananas and some zucchini from my garden that I wasn’t sure how to use. I was skeptical about adding zucchini to banana bread, but it turned out to be a total game-changer. The zucchini adds moisture and tenderness without making the bread taste like vegetables, which I think is the magic here. What makes this version better than others I’ve tried is the balance of spices and the careful squeezing out of the zucchini to avoid sogginess. I also love mixing in a bit of brown sugar for a deeper sweetness and texture. When the loaf comes out of the oven, it has this beautiful golden crust and a soft, almost melt-in-your-mouth crumb. It’s perfect for breakfast with a smear of butter or as a cozy afternoon snack with tea. Honestly, I’ve baked this loaf multiple times and it never disappoints. Just a heads up: don’t skip squeezing the zucchini—that’s my secret to keeping the bread moist but not wet. If you’re on the fence about adding veggies to your baked goods, give this one a shot; it’s a subtle, delicious way to sneak them in!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup ripe mashed bananas (about 2-3 bananas)
  • 1 cup finely shredded zucchini, squeezed of excess moisture
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan or line it with parchment paper. This step ensures your bread won’t stick and will bake evenly. Using a parchment liner makes cleanup easier and helps you lift the loaf out cleanly once baked.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Loaf pan is evenly greased or lined with parchment paper, ready for batter
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg. Mixing the dry ingredients separately helps distribute the leavening agents and spices evenly throughout the batter, so every bite is flavorful and the bread rises properly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no clumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the sugars and oil together until combined. Then add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. Stir in the vanilla extract. This method ensures a smooth, cohesive wet mixture that will help the bread stay moist and tender.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are smooth and slightly thickened
Step 4
Fold in the mashed bananas and shredded zucchini into the wet mixture. Make sure the zucchini is squeezed to remove excess moisture before adding to avoid a soggy loaf. The bananas add natural sweetness and moisture, while the zucchini keeps it tender without a veggie taste.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bananas and zucchini are evenly incorporated with no large chunks
Step 5
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, folding gently until just combined. Be careful not to overmix here to keep the bread light and tender. Overworking the batter develops gluten which can make the bread tough.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: No visible streaks of flour remain but batter is not overworked
Step 6
If using, fold in the chopped walnuts gently at this point. Nuts add a nice crunch and contrast to the soft crumb. You can omit them if you prefer a nut-free loaf or swap for chocolate chips for a sweeter twist.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Nuts are evenly distributed without overmixing batter
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and rise uniformly. Avoid tapping the pan too hard, which can cause air bubbles to collapse.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly fills the pan with a smooth surface
Step 8
Bake for 50 to 60 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs. The baking time can vary depending on your oven, so start checking around 50 minutes. Avoid opening the oven door frequently, as this can cause the bread to sink.
⏱ Time: 50 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean or with moist crumbs
Step 9
Let the bread cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring it to a wire rack to cool completely. This rest period allows the structure to set so the bread slices cleanly without crumbling. Cooling fully also helps the flavors mellow and develop.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is firm enough to handle but not hot, cooling on rack

Variations

Chocolate Chip Banana Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup of chocolate chips to the batter for a sweeter, more indulgent loaf. The chocolate melts into pockets of gooey sweetness that complement the natural flavors. Perfect for a treat!

Note: Fold in chocolate chips gently at the end to avoid melting them into the batter before baking.

Gluten-Free Banana Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour with a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This version is great for gluten-sensitive folks but may be a bit denser and more delicate.

Note: Adjust baking time slightly and avoid overmixing to keep crumb tender.

Spiced Walnut Banana Zucchini Bread

Increase cinnamon to 1 1/2 teaspoons and add 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves or allspice. Fold in chopped walnuts for crunch and extra warmth. This variation feels cozy and festive.

Note: Toast walnuts lightly before adding for deeper flavor.

Vegan Banana Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a neutral oil. The texture is slightly different but still moist and flavorful.

Note: Allow flax eggs to gel for 5 minutes before mixing into wet ingredients.

Lemon Zucchini Banana Bread

Add zest of one lemon and 2 tablespoons lemon juice to the batter for a fresh, tangy twist. This brightens the flavors and pairs well with a light glaze.

Note: Reduce cinnamon slightly to avoid overpowering the citrus notes.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the shredded zucchini before adding it to the batter.
Removing excess water from zucchini prevents the batter from becoming too wet, which would make the bread dense or soggy. This step ensures the zucchini contributes moisture without compromising the bread's structure.
Use ripe bananas with brown spots for best flavor and sweetness.
Ripe bananas have higher sugar content and more moisture, which naturally sweetens and tenderizes the bread. Using underripe bananas results in a bland and dry loaf.
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture gently and just until combined.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes the bread tough and chewy. Gentle folding keeps the crumb light and tender, perfect for quick breads.
Let the bread cool completely before slicing.
Cooling allows the bread’s structure to set, preventing it from crumbling when cut. It also helps flavors to meld and improves texture.
Add a mix of granulated and brown sugar for depth of flavor.
Brown sugar adds moisture and a subtle molasses flavor that enhances the bread’s taste and keeps it moist longer than using white sugar alone.
Use vegetable oil instead of butter for a moister crumb.
Oil remains liquid at room temperature, keeping the bread soft and tender, whereas butter solidifies and can make the crumb firmer.
Check the bread starting at 50 minutes to avoid overbaking.
Ovens vary, and overbaking dries out the bread. A toothpick test ensures it’s perfectly baked and moist inside.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing the zucchini enough

Why it happens Many assume shredded zucchini can be added as is, but its high water content can make the batter too wet.

Fix Use a clean towel or cheesecloth to squeeze out as much moisture as possible before folding zucchini into the batter. This keeps the bread from becoming soggy.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid lumps, but overmixing develops gluten leading to toughness.

Fix Mix just until the flour disappears. Some small lumps are okay and will disappear during baking.

Using underripe bananas

Why it happens Unripe bananas lack sweetness and moisture, leading to less flavorful bread.

Fix Use bananas with brown spots or that are very soft. You can even freeze ripe bananas to use later.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens High heat can cause the crust to brown too fast while the inside stays undercooked.

Fix Bake at 350°F (175°C) and check doneness with a toothpick after 50 minutes.

Not letting the bread cool before slicing

Why it happens Cutting hot bread causes it to crumble and lose shape.

Fix Allow the bread to cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then on a rack until completely cool before slicing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the banana zucchini bread covered loosely with foil or in a bread box at room temperature for up to 2 days. This keeps it soft but avoid leaving it uncovered to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up and lose some moisture but tastes great toasted or warmed slightly before serving.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled loaf tightly in plastic wrap and foil, then freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Reheat slices in a toaster or oven to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven or regular oven at 300°F for 5–7 minutes to refresh moisture and soften the crumb. Avoid microwaving too long as it can make the bread rubbery.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it refrigerated in the mixing bowl covered with plastic wrap. Stir gently before pouring into the pan and bake as usual. This helps save time on busy mornings.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
Frozen zucchini can be used if fully thawed and thoroughly squeezed to remove all moisture. Excess water can make the bread soggy, so drying is crucial. Fresh zucchini is ideal for best texture.
What if I don’t have brown sugar?
You can substitute brown sugar with an equal amount of white sugar plus a teaspoon of molasses if you have it. Otherwise, white sugar alone works but the bread may be less moist and flavorful.
How do I know when the bread is done baking?
Insert a toothpick or skewer into the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs (not wet batter), the bread is done. If it’s wet, bake for a few more minutes and check again.
Can I make this bread nut-free?
Absolutely! Just omit the walnuts or any nuts. The bread will still be moist and delicious without them.
Why does my bread sometimes come out dense?
Dense bread often results from overmixing the batter or using underripe bananas. Mix just until combined and ensure bananas are ripe for best rise and texture.
Can I use whole wheat flour instead of all-purpose?
You can substitute up to half the flour with whole wheat, but the texture will be denser and the flavor earthier. You may need to add a bit more moisture or reduce baking time slightly.
How do I store leftover banana zucchini bread?
Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for 2 days or refrigerate up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Is it okay to add chocolate chips or other mix-ins?
Yes! Chocolate chips, dried fruit, or nuts can be folded in at the end. Just don’t add too much or it can affect the bread’s structure and baking time.
