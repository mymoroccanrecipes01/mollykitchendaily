Quick and Tangy Honey Mustard Chicken with Steamed Green Beans

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 30 min cook 4 servings

This honey mustard chicken is a speedy weeknight winner—juicy, tangy, and sweet all at once. The steamed green beans add a fresh crunch. Great for busy nights when you want something tasty but fuss-free.

Why This Works

One of the key reasons this honey mustard chicken turns out so juicy and flavorful is the balance between the sweet honey and sharp Dijon mustard. Honey adds moisture and a natural sweetness that caramelizes slightly during cooking, creating a beautiful glaze on the chicken. Dijon mustard contributes tang and a subtle heat that cuts through the sweetness, making the sauce vibrant rather than cloying.

Cooking the chicken over medium heat and searing it first locks in the juices by forming a crust through the Maillard reaction—this is the chemical browning that adds depth of flavor and a satisfying texture. If the heat is too high or the chicken moves too soon, it won’t brown properly, resulting in a less flavorful, drier piece of meat.

Simmering the chicken in the sauce with the lid on at low heat allows the flavors to meld and the sauce to thicken gently without burning. This slow cooking step ensures the chicken absorbs the honey mustard flavors deeply while staying tender. The sauce's viscosity helps it cling to the chicken, enhancing every bite.

Steaming the green beans briefly keeps them crisp and brightly colored. Overcooking would cause them to turn dull and mushy, losing that fresh contrast to the rich chicken. Tossing them with butter adds a luscious mouthfeel and rounds out the overall texture of the meal.

  • Look for fresh, boneless skinless chicken breasts that are plump and pale pink with no off odors. Even thickness helps ensure even cooking, so if pieces are uneven, consider pounding them gently to uniform thickness. This prevents dry edges and undercooked centers.
  • Use a mild, runny honey for easy mixing and glazing. Raw or local honey can add unique floral notes, but avoid crystallized honey as it’s harder to incorporate smoothly. Honey acts both as a sweetener and a glaze, caramelizing during cooking to create a shiny sauce.
  • Choose a classic Dijon mustard with a smooth texture and balanced acidity. It’s milder than spicy brown mustard, which keeps the sauce bright without overwhelming heat. Dijon mustard emulsifies well with honey, creating a smooth sauce consistency.
  • Extra virgin olive oil works well here for its fruity flavor and healthy fats. It tolerates medium heat needed for searing without burning quickly, unlike butter alone. The oil helps create a non-stick surface and promotes even browning of the chicken.
  • Fresh green beans should snap easily and have vibrant green color with no brown spots. Freshness is key for that crisp texture after steaming. Trim the ends just before cooking to preserve freshness and prevent fibrous bites.

Ingredients

  • 4 boneless skinless chicken breasts, patted dry
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1/4 cup honey
  • 1/4 cup Dijon mustard
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 1 pound fresh green beans, trimmed
  • 1 tablespoon butter (optional, for green beans)

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this honey mustard chicken recipe on a hectic weeknight when I needed something fast but satisfying. What I love about this version is how the sauce is super simple—just honey, Dijon mustard, and garlic—but packs so much flavor without any fuss. I used to overcook chicken breasts, which made them dry and tough, but the trick I learned here is to cook them over medium heat and baste with the sauce, keeping them juicy and tender every time. Plus, steaming fresh green beans alongside adds a crisp, vibrant contrast that brightens the whole plate. The finished dish has this lovely balance of sweet, tangy, and savory notes, with a glossy sauce that clings to the tender chicken. It’s perfect for those nights when you want a home-cooked meal without spending hours in the kitchen. I think you’ll find it’s a total winner for quick dinners or even casual weekend meals.

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    Start by seasoning your chicken breasts on both sides with salt and freshly ground black pepper. This simple seasoning step is crucial because it enhances the natural flavor of the chicken without overpowering the honey mustard sauce. Make sure to pat the chicken dry with paper towels before seasoning—this helps the chicken brown beautifully instead of steaming. Overcrowding the pan can cause the meat to steam rather than sear, so use a pan that's big enough to give each piece some space.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Chicken breasts look golden brown on the outside with a slight crust
  2. Step 2

    Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Once hot, add the chicken breasts. Cook them undisturbed for about 5-6 minutes on one side until they develop a golden brown crust. Resist the urge to move them early—this helps lock in juices and creates a nice sear. Flip the chicken carefully using tongs to avoid tearing the meat. If you rush this step, the chicken won’t brown properly, and the texture will suffer.

    ⏱ 6 minutes✓ Chicken easily releases from the pan and has a golden crust
  3. Step 3

    While the chicken cooks, whisk together the honey, Dijon mustard, and minced garlic in a small bowl. This simple sauce combines sweet and tangy flavors that balance each other perfectly. The garlic adds a subtle kick without being overpowering. Mixing the sauce ahead ensures it’s ready to add at just the right moment.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Sauce is smooth and well combined with no lumps
  4. Step 4

    Flip the chicken breasts to the other side and cook for another 5 minutes. Then reduce the heat to low and pour the honey mustard sauce over the chicken. Spoon some of the sauce over the top to coat well. Cover the skillet with a lid and let the chicken simmer gently in the sauce. This slow simmer allows the sauce to thicken slightly and soak into the chicken, making it juicy and flavorful.

    ⏱ 7 minutes✓ Sauce thickens slightly and chicken is cooked through with no pink inside
  5. Step 5

    While the chicken simmers, bring a pot of salted water to a boil for the green beans. Add the trimmed green beans and cook them for 3-4 minutes until just tender but still crisp. This quick blanching keeps them bright green and fresh-tasting. Avoid overcooking because mushy beans won’t provide the fresh crunch that contrasts nicely with the tender chicken.

    ⏱ 4 minutes✓ Green beans are bright green and slightly crisp when bitten
  6. Step 6

    Drain the green beans and transfer them to a bowl. If you like, toss them with a tablespoon of butter and a pinch of salt while still hot. The butter adds a silky richness that complements the tangy chicken sauce beautifully. This step is optional but highly recommended for extra flavor and mouthfeel.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Green beans glisten slightly with butter and smell fragrant
  7. Step 7

    Check the chicken breasts for doneness by slicing into the thickest part—juices should run clear, and the meat should be opaque. If not fully cooked, cover and simmer a few more minutes. Avoid overcooking as chicken can dry out quickly. The sauce should be slightly thickened and glossy at this point, clinging to the chicken.

    ⏱ 2-3 minutes✓ Chicken is opaque inside and sauce coats the meat well
  8. Step 8

    Remove the chicken from heat and let it rest covered for 5 minutes. Resting allows the juices to redistribute inside the meat, making each bite juicy and tender. Serving immediately without resting can cause the juices to run out, leaving the chicken drier than desired.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Chicken feels firm but still moist when pressed gently
  9. Step 9

    Serve the honey mustard chicken breasts hot with a side of steamed green beans. Spoon any remaining sauce from the pan over the chicken for extra flavor. This dish pairs well with rice, mashed potatoes, or a simple salad for a complete meal. Enjoy the balance of sweet, tangy, and savory flavors with a fresh crunch.

    ⏱ Immediate✓ Dish looks glossy and colorful, and aroma is inviting

Variations

  • Spicy Honey Mustard Chicken: Add a teaspoon of cayenne pepper or a splash of hot sauce to the honey mustard sauce for a spicy kick. This variation adds warmth without overpowering the sweet and tangy balance. Serve with steamed green beans or roasted veggies to complement the heat.
    Adjust spice level carefully to maintain sauce balance; too much heat may mask the honey’s sweetness.
  • Honey Mustard Chicken Thighs: Swap chicken breasts for boneless skinless thighs for a juicier, more flavorful option. Thighs are more forgiving if slightly overcooked and have a richer taste. Cooking time may increase by a few minutes to ensure thorough cooking.
    Monitor internal temperature closely; thighs should reach 165°F for safety but remain tender.
  • Garlic Herb Honey Mustard Chicken: Incorporate fresh herbs like thyme or rosemary into the sauce for an aromatic twist. The herbs add depth and complexity that pairs wonderfully with the honey and mustard. This is perfect for a slightly more sophisticated flavor profile.
    Add herbs towards the end of cooking to preserve their fresh flavor.
  • Honey Mustard Chicken with Roasted Vegetables: Instead of steamed green beans, serve the chicken with a medley of roasted vegetables like carrots, bell peppers, and Brussels sprouts. Roasting adds caramelized sweetness and a hearty texture that complements the tender chicken.
    Roast vegetables at 425°F for 20-25 minutes until tender and caramelized.
  • Gluten-Free Honey Mustard Chicken: Ensure all ingredients, especially mustard and honey, are certified gluten-free. This recipe naturally contains no gluten, making it a safe and tasty option for gluten-sensitive eaters without any modifications.
    Always double-check labels to avoid hidden gluten in condiments.

Pro Tips

  • Pat the chicken dry before seasoning and cooking. — Removing excess moisture from the chicken surface helps it brown properly instead of steaming. This promotes the Maillard reaction, which adds flavor and texture. Wet chicken tends to release water during cooking, preventing a nice crust from forming.
  • Don’t rush flipping the chicken while searing. — Allowing the chicken to cook undisturbed for several minutes develops a golden crust and locks in juices. Moving it too soon can cause sticking and tearing, resulting in uneven cooking and a less appealing texture.
  • Simmer the chicken covered in sauce over low heat. — Covering traps steam which helps cook the chicken evenly and keeps it moist. The gentle simmer thickens the sauce gradually, allowing flavors to meld without burning or drying out the meat.
  • Blanch green beans quickly to retain color and crunch. — Short cooking time preserves the beans’ bright green color and crisp texture. Overcooking causes them to become dull and mushy, which reduces the fresh contrast to the rich chicken.
  • Rest the chicken after cooking before slicing. — Resting allows juices to redistribute inside the meat, preventing them from spilling out when cut. This keeps the chicken moist and tender rather than dry and stringy.
  • Use a heavy-bottomed skillet for even heat distribution. — A thick pan maintains steady heat, avoiding hot spots that can burn the sauce or unevenly cook the chicken. This ensures consistent browning and simmering.
  • Add fresh garlic to the sauce for aromatic depth. — Fresh garlic lends a subtle pungency that enhances the honey mustard flavor without overpowering it. Garlic powder or pre-minced garlic won’t provide the same fresh, vibrant taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the chicken breasts
    Why: Many cooks rush the cooking process or don’t monitor temperature closely, causing the chicken to dry out and become tough.
    Fix: Cook chicken over medium heat and check doneness by slicing the thickest part. Remove from heat as soon as juices run clear and meat is opaque. Rest the chicken for 5 minutes before slicing to lock in moisture.
  • Not drying chicken before cooking
    Why: Skipping this step is common when in a hurry, but wet chicken steams instead of browning.
    Fix: Pat each chicken breast dry with paper towels before seasoning. This helps develop a golden crust and better flavor through the Maillard reaction.
  • Overcooking green beans until mushy
    Why: Green beans cook quickly, and many leave them in boiling water too long or don’t check texture.
    Fix: Boil green beans for just 3-4 minutes until bright green and tender-crisp. Immediately drain and toss with butter or ice water to stop cooking.
  • Pouring sauce too early or too late
    Why: Adding sauce before searing can prevent browning; adding too late means the chicken won’t absorb flavors.
    Fix: Sear chicken first, then pour sauce over and simmer gently. This sequence ensures a flavorful glaze and tender meat.
  • Using low-quality mustard or honey
    Why: Some use generic condiments that lack flavor depth, resulting in a flat sauce.
    Fix: Choose a good quality Dijon mustard and mild, runny honey for best taste and sauce consistency.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: This dish can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours when covered loosely to avoid drying out. Beyond that, bacteria growth risks increase, so refrigerate promptly.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The sauce may thicken and chicken may firm up when cold; reheat gently before serving to restore moisture and flavor.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freeze cooked chicken and sauce in a freezer-safe container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.
  • ♨️ Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop over low heat, adding a splash of water or broth to loosen the sauce if it thickened. Avoid high heat to prevent drying out the chicken. Microwave reheating works but do in short bursts to maintain juiciness.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: You can prepare the honey mustard sauce and trim green beans up to 1 day ahead. Cook the chicken fresh for best texture, but sauce can be made in advance and heated with the chicken.

FAQ

Can I use chicken thighs instead of breasts?
Absolutely! Chicken thighs are juicier and more forgiving if slightly overcooked. Just increase cooking time by a few minutes until they reach an internal temperature of 165°F. The sauce works beautifully with thighs, offering a richer flavor.
How do I know when the chicken is fully cooked?
The best way is to slice into the thickest part to check that the meat is opaque with no pink inside and that juices run clear. Using a meat thermometer is even better—aim for 165°F internal temperature to ensure safety and juiciness.
Can I make this recipe gluten-free?
Yes! This recipe is naturally gluten-free as long as you use gluten-free Dijon mustard and honey. Always check labels on condiments to avoid hidden gluten, especially in store-bought sauces or mustards.
What can I serve with this honey mustard chicken?
It pairs well with steamed green beans (as suggested), rice, mashed potatoes, or a crisp salad. Roasted vegetables also work great if you want a heartier side. Choose sides that complement the tangy, sweet flavors without overpowering them.
How can I make the sauce spicier?
Add a pinch of cayenne pepper, chili flakes, or a dash of hot sauce to the honey mustard mixture before cooking. Start with a small amount and adjust to your taste to keep the balance between sweet and tangy intact.
Is it okay to cook the chicken in the oven instead of stovetop?
Yes, you can bake the chicken at 375°F for about 25-30 minutes, then brush the honey mustard sauce on during the last 10 minutes to caramelize. However, stovetop cooking allows better control over browning and sauce reduction.
Can I prepare this recipe ahead of time?
You can make the sauce and prep the green beans a day ahead. For best texture, cook the chicken fresh. If needed, you can cook the chicken in advance and gently reheat it with the sauce, but fresh cooking yields the juiciest results.
What if my sauce is too thick or too thin?
If the sauce is too thick, add a splash of water or chicken broth to loosen it while reheating. If it’s too thin, simmer uncovered for a few more minutes to reduce and thicken. The sauce should coat the chicken nicely without being runny.
