Creamy Garlic Salmon Made Easy in the Air Fryer

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 28, 2026
15 min Prep Time
15 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This salmon recipe cooks quickly in the air fryer, locking in juicy flavor and topped with a garlicky cream sauce that’s just dreamy. Perfect for a cozy weeknight dinner when you want something simple but special.

Introduction

I first tried this creamy garlic salmon recipe when I was desperate for a quick dinner that felt a bit fancy. Using the air fryer was a game changer—it cooks the salmon perfectly juicy without drying it out. What really makes this recipe stand out is the garlicky cream sauce that’s so easy to whip up while the salmon cooks. I used to overcook salmon all the time, but the air fryer’s quick, hot air circulation keeps the texture tender and flaky every time. Plus, the lemon zest in the sauce adds a bright freshness that balances the richness beautifully. This dish always feels like a treat, yet it’s totally doable on a weeknight. I love serving it with simple sides like steamed veggies or a light salad. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes I keep coming back to when I want something comforting but don’t want to spend hours in the kitchen. The key is timing the sauce just right so it’s warm and velvety when you’re ready to eat. I hope you enjoy making it as much as I do!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 salmon fillets, skin-on, about 6 ounces each
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 teaspoon lemon zest
  • 1 tablespoon fresh parsley, chopped

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by prepping your salmon fillets—pat them dry with paper towels to ensure the olive oil and seasonings stick well. Rub each fillet with olive oil, then sprinkle salt and pepper evenly on both sides. This helps the salmon develop a nice crust in the air fryer and enhances flavor. Avoid skipping the drying step, or you might end up with soggy skin instead of crispy. Set the fillets aside while you prepare the sauce.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salmon skin looks dry and slightly shiny from the oil
Step 2
Preheat your air fryer to 400°F (200°C) for about 3 minutes. This step ensures the salmon cooks evenly and quickly, locking in moisture. Place the salmon fillets skin-side down in a single layer in the air fryer basket. Don’t overcrowd them, or they won’t cook properly. Cooking at this high heat will create a crispy skin and tender flesh.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer is hot and ready, salmon fillets fit comfortably
Step 3
Cook the salmon at 400°F for 10-12 minutes, depending on thickness. You’re aiming for the flesh to be opaque and flake easily with a fork but still moist inside. Overcooking will dry it out, so check a minute or two before the end if your fillets are on the thinner side. The air fryer’s circulating heat cooks the salmon fast while keeping it juicy.
⏱ Time: 10-12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salmon flesh is opaque and flakes gently when pressed
Step 4
While the salmon cooks, melt the butter in a small saucepan over medium heat. Add the minced garlic and sauté gently for 1-2 minutes until fragrant but not browned. This slow cooking brings out the garlic’s sweetness without bitterness, which is key for a smooth sauce.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Garlic is soft and aromatic, not browned or burnt
Step 5
Pour in the heavy cream and stir to combine with the garlic butter. Lower the heat and let it simmer gently for 3-4 minutes to thicken slightly. Stir frequently to prevent the cream from scorching. This step creates a silky sauce that clings to the salmon beautifully.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce has thickened enough to coat the back of a spoon
Step 6
Add the lemon zest and chopped parsley to the cream sauce. Stir well and season with a pinch of salt and pepper to taste. The lemon zest adds brightness that cuts through the richness, while parsley brings freshness and color.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce looks vibrant and fragrant with herbs and lemon
Step 7
Once the salmon is done, carefully transfer the fillets to serving plates. Spoon the warm garlic cream sauce generously over each piece. The contrast of the crispy skin with the smooth sauce is what makes this dish special.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salmon is plated with sauce glossy and evenly coated
Step 8
Serve immediately with your favorite sides like steamed asparagus, rice, or a fresh green salad. The sauce is best enjoyed warm, so timing your sides to finish at the same time is ideal. Leftover sauce can be refrigerated and gently reheated.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Entire meal is warm and ready to enjoy
Step 9
Clean your air fryer basket promptly after use to prevent buildup of oils and food residue. This keeps your appliance working efficiently and your salmon tasting fresh every time. A quick wipe with a damp cloth after it cools is usually enough.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer basket is clean and dry

Variations

Spicy Creamy Salmon

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or a dash of hot sauce to the cream sauce for a spicy kick. This variation adds warmth and depth without overpowering the salmon’s delicate flavor.

Note: Be careful not to add too much heat at once; start small and adjust to taste to keep the sauce balanced.

Dill and Caper Cream Sauce

Swap parsley for fresh dill and stir in a tablespoon of chopped capers to the sauce. This gives a tangy, herbaceous twist that pairs beautifully with salmon’s richness.

Note: Add capers at the end of sauce cooking to keep their texture and briny flavor intact.

Lemon Butter Salmon

Skip the cream and make a simple lemon butter sauce by melting butter with lemon juice and garlic. This lighter version is bright and buttery, perfect for warmer months.

Note: Reduce cooking time slightly as the sauce is thinner and won’t need simmering.

Herb-Crusted Salmon

Before air frying, coat salmon with a mixture of breadcrumbs, Parmesan, and chopped herbs for a crispy, flavorful crust. The cream sauce complements the crunchy texture nicely.

Note: Press the herb mixture firmly onto the salmon skin side before cooking to help it adhere.

Coconut Cream Salmon

Replace heavy cream with coconut milk and add a teaspoon of grated ginger to the sauce for a tropical twist. This variation creates a subtly sweet, exotic flavor profile.

Note: Simmer gently to thicken coconut milk without separating.

Pro Tips

Pat the salmon dry before seasoning.
Removing excess moisture from the salmon surface helps the olive oil and seasonings adhere better and promotes crispier skin. If the fillet is damp, it steams rather than crisps, resulting in a less appealing texture.
Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket.
Leaving space around each fillet allows hot air to circulate evenly, ensuring consistent cooking and browning. Overcrowding traps moisture and causes uneven results, with some pieces undercooked or soggy.
Simmer the cream sauce gently.
High heat can cause cream to curdle or scorch, ruining the sauce’s smooth texture. A gentle simmer thickens the sauce slowly and blends the garlic flavor without bitterness.
Use fresh lemon zest, not juice.
Zest contains fragrant oils that brighten the sauce without adding acidity or thinning it. Juice can separate the cream and change the sauce’s texture.
Check salmon doneness early.
Air fryer cooking times vary based on fillet thickness. Checking a minute or two before the timer prevents overcooking, keeping the salmon moist and tender.
Serve sauce immediately over hot salmon.
Warm sauce clings better and enhances the salmon’s flavor. Letting it cool makes it thicken too much and lose the luxurious mouthfeel.
Clean the air fryer basket after each use.
Residual oils and food particles can burn and create unpleasant flavors or smoke during future cooking. Keeping the basket clean preserves the appliance and your food’s taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overcooking the salmon.

Why it happens Many cooks set the timer too long or don’t check for doneness early, assuming thicker fillets need more time. This leads to dry, tough salmon.

Fix Start checking the salmon 1-2 minutes before the recommended cooking time by gently flaking the flesh with a fork. Remove it from the air fryer as soon as it flakes easily but is still moist inside.

Skipping the preheat step.

Why it happens It’s tempting to just put the salmon in the air fryer without preheating to save time, but this affects cooking quality.

Fix Always preheat the air fryer to the correct temperature so the salmon cooks evenly and the skin crisps up immediately, preventing sogginess.

Using garlic that’s too finely minced or cooked too fast.

Why it happens Rushing the garlic cooking or mincing it too fine can cause it to burn quickly, resulting in bitter flavors.

Fix Mince garlic moderately fine and cook it gently in butter over medium-low heat until fragrant, not browned. Stir constantly to avoid burning.

Not drying the salmon before oiling.

Why it happens Moisture on the salmon surface prevents oil and seasoning from sticking and creates steam, which softens the skin.

Fix Pat salmon dry thoroughly with paper towels before brushing with olive oil and seasoning to ensure crispy skin.

Adding lemon juice instead of zest to the cream sauce.

Why it happens Some cooks think lemon juice will add the same brightness, but it alters the sauce’s texture and can cause curdling.

Fix Use finely grated lemon zest to add citrus aroma without affecting the sauce’s creaminess or stability.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep cooked salmon at room temperature for up to 2 hours covered loosely to avoid drying out. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigeration is recommended.

Refrigerator

Store salmon and sauce separately in airtight containers for up to 3 days. The sauce may thicken when chilled; reheat gently with a splash of cream or water to loosen it. Salmon can be served cold in salads but is best warmed.

Freezer

Freeze cooked salmon fillets wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and foil or in freezer bags for up to 2 months. Freeze sauce separately in airtight containers. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat salmon in a low oven (275°F) wrapped in foil for 10-15 minutes to preserve moisture. Warm sauce gently on the stovetop over low heat, stirring and adding a little cream if needed. Avoid microwave reheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

Prepare the garlic cream sauce a day ahead and store chilled. On serving day, reheat sauce gently while cooking salmon fresh in the air fryer. This saves time and keeps textures fresh.

FAQ

Can I use frozen salmon for this recipe?
Yes, but make sure to thaw the salmon completely and pat it dry before cooking. Cooking from frozen will result in uneven cooking and less crispy skin. Thawing overnight in the fridge is best.
What if I don’t have an air fryer?
You can cook the salmon under a broiler or on a stovetop skillet. For broiling, place the salmon skin-side up on a foil-lined tray and cook 8-10 minutes until cooked through. For skillet, cook skin-side down over medium-high heat to crisp the skin, then flip and finish cooking.
Can I substitute heavy cream with milk or yogurt?
Heavy cream is best for the sauce’s richness and texture. Milk is too thin and may separate, while yogurt can curdle when heated. If you want a lighter sauce, use half-and-half but watch the heat carefully.
How do I know when salmon is fully cooked?
The flesh should be opaque and flake easily with a fork but still feel moist. Overcooked salmon becomes dry and chalky. Using a food thermometer, aim for an internal temperature of 125-130°F for medium doneness.
Can I make this recipe spicy?
Absolutely! Add red pepper flakes or a dash of hot sauce to the cream sauce during cooking for a gentle heat. Adjust the spice level to your preference.
What sides go well with this salmon?
Steamed or roasted vegetables, rice, quinoa, or a fresh green salad all pair beautifully. The creamy sauce makes simple sides feel elevated.
Is it better to cook salmon skin-side up or down in the air fryer?
Cook skin-side down first to crisp the skin, then flip if you like. For this recipe, skin-side down the entire time works well to keep skin crispy and protect the fish from drying out.
Can I prepare the sauce ahead of time?
Yes, you can make the garlic cream sauce 1-2 days ahead and store it in the fridge. Reheat gently before serving. Just be sure to stir frequently to keep it smooth.
