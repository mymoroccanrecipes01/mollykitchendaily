Moist and Fudgy Zucchini Brownies That Sneak in Veggies Deliciously

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 29, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These zucchini brownies are shockingly fudgy and moist thanks to the shredded zucchini. They sneak in veggies without losing that classic chocolatey goodness. Great for afternoon snacks or dessert!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon zucchini brownies when I was trying to find a way to add veggies into my family's favorite treats without them noticing. Honestly, I was skeptical at first—how could zucchini possibly make brownies taste better? Turns out, the shredded zucchini not only keeps these brownies incredibly moist but also adds a subtle earthiness that perfectly complements the chocolate. What makes this recipe stand out is the balance between fudginess and a tender crumb, thanks to squeezing out the zucchini moisture and using melted butter. I’ve made plenty of brownies that came out dry or cakey, but this one nails that soft, chewy texture every time. Plus, the optional chocolate chips on top add just the right touch of melty sweetness. These brownies are my go-to for afternoon snacks or casual dessert nights when I want something comforting but a little healthier. They look like classic brownies, taste like chocolate heaven, and secretly pack in a serving of veggies—what’s not to love?

What You'll Need

Approximately 8 brownies, 8 servings

  • 1 cup shredded zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/3 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by shredding the zucchini finely and then squeezing out as much moisture as you can using a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth. This step is crucial because excess water can make your brownies soggy and prevent them from setting properly. Taking the time to wring out the zucchini ensures the right fudgy texture instead of a mushy mess. You’ll notice the zucchini loses volume and feels drier, which is exactly what you want for a balanced batter.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet when squeezed tightly.
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the melted butter and sugar until smooth and glossy. Adding the butter while warm helps dissolve the sugar and creates a silky base for the batter. Then, beat in the eggs one at a time, mixing well after each addition. This builds structure and ensures your brownies rise just enough while staying dense and fudgy. Stir in the vanilla extract to enhance the chocolate flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, slightly thickened, and shiny with no lumps.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, sift together the flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt. Sifting helps remove lumps and aerates the mixture, which promotes even mixing and a tender crumb. Slowly fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture using a spatula, just until combined. Overmixing at this stage can develop gluten and make the brownies tough, so be gentle and stop as soon as there are no streaks of flour.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform in color and texture, thick but spreadable.
Step 4
Fold the squeezed zucchini into the batter carefully. You want the zucchini evenly distributed but not overworked. This step adds moisture and subtle veggie flavor without overpowering the chocolate. If you’re adding chocolate chips, fold them in now for extra pockets of melty goodness throughout the brownies.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and chips are mixed evenly without clumps.
Step 5
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease or line an 8x8 inch baking pan with parchment paper. Pour the batter into the pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. This ensures even baking and a nice flat surface for cutting. Don’t overfill; the batter should be about an inch thick to bake evenly without drying out.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread in the pan with no thick or thin spots.
Step 6
Bake the brownies in the center rack for 25-30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out with moist crumbs but not wet batter. This timing keeps the brownies fudgy inside while fully cooked. Baking too long will dry them out, so keep an eye on them near the end.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted comes out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.
Step 7
Once baked, remove the brownies from the oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling allows the brownies to set and makes cutting easier. If you try to cut them warm, they might fall apart or be too gooey. Patience here rewards you with clean, fudgy squares.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Brownies feel firm to the touch and hold shape when sliced.
Step 8
Use a sharp knife to cut the brownies into 8 squares. For clean cuts, wipe the knife between slices to avoid smearing the chocolate. These brownies hold their shape well when cooled and sliced carefully. Serve immediately or store for later to enjoy that fudgy texture at its best.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Clean, even brownie squares with visible chocolate chips if added.
Step 9
Optional: Warm brownies slightly in the microwave or oven before serving for that fresh-baked feel. Add a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a drizzle of chocolate sauce for an extra indulgent treat. These brownies taste amazing on their own but also pair well with simple toppings.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Brownies are warm but not melted or overly soft.

Variations

Gluten-Free Zucchini Brownies

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend to make these brownies suitable for gluten-sensitive diets. The texture might be slightly different—often a bit more crumbly—but still moist and fudgy thanks to the zucchini. Add a teaspoon of xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t contain it for better structure.

Note: Mix the gluten-free flour blend thoroughly and monitor baking time, as gluten-free batters can brown faster.

Nutty Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans to the batter for a wonderful crunch that contrasts the fudgy texture. Nuts also add a rich, toasty flavor that complements the chocolate and zucchini beautifully. Toast nuts lightly beforehand for extra depth.

Note: Fold nuts in at the last step before pouring batter into the pan to ensure even distribution.

Spiced Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg to the dry ingredients for a warm, cozy twist. These spices enhance the chocolate flavor and add complexity, making the brownies perfect for fall or winter treats.

Note: Adjust spices to taste but don’t overdo it, as they can overpower the chocolate.

Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use coconut oil instead of butter. The texture will be slightly denser but still moist thanks to the zucchini. These brownies are a great plant-based option for chocolate lovers.

Note: Mix flax eggs well and allow them to gel before adding to the batter for proper binding.

Peanut Butter Swirl Zucchini Brownies

Drop dollops of peanut butter on top of the batter before baking and swirl gently with a knife. This adds a rich, nutty layer that pairs perfectly with chocolate and zucchini, creating a visually stunning and delicious dessert.

Note: Swirl lightly to avoid mixing completely, preserving the marbled effect.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from zucchini before adding it to the batter.
Removing excess water prevents the brownies from becoming soggy and ensures they bake up with the right fudgy texture. Too much moisture dilutes the batter and can cause underbaking or a gummy crumb.
Use melted butter instead of softened for better incorporation with sugar.
Melted butter coats the sugar granules and flour proteins evenly, resulting in a smooth, glossy batter that bakes into a moist and fudgy texture. Softened butter can trap air and produce a cakier brownie.
Don’t overmix once you add the dry ingredients to avoid tough brownies.
Overmixing activates gluten in the flour, which creates a chewy or tough texture that’s not ideal for fudgy brownies. Folding gently until just combined keeps the crumb tender.
Allow brownies to cool fully before cutting for clean slices.
Cooling lets the brownies set and firm up, preventing them from crumbling or falling apart when sliced. Warm brownies are too soft and can be messy to cut.
Add chocolate chips for extra melty pockets of chocolate in the brownies.
Chocolate chips melt during baking and provide delightful bursts of rich chocolate flavor and texture contrast, enhancing the overall eating experience.
Use parchment paper to line your baking pan for easy removal.
Parchment paper prevents sticking and allows you to lift the entire batch out at once, making cutting and serving much easier without breaking the brownies.
Check doneness with a toothpick inserted in the center at 25 minutes.
This helps avoid overbaking, which dries out the brownies. The toothpick should come out with moist crumbs but not wet batter, indicating perfect fudginess.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume shredded zucchini can be added directly, but it contains a lot of water that can ruin brownie texture.

Fix Use a clean towel or cheesecloth to press and squeeze the zucchini firmly until it stops dripping. This keeps the batter balanced and prevents sogginess.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens Eager cooks want to combine everything quickly, but mixing too much develops gluten.

Fix Fold the dry ingredients gently and stop as soon as no flour streaks remain to keep brownies tender.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Some ovens run hot or cooks increase temp to speed baking, but this dries brownies out.

Fix Stick to 350°F and check early for doneness to maintain moistness.

Cutting brownies before they cool

Why it happens Impatience leads to cutting warm brownies that crumble easily.

Fix Let brownies cool completely on a rack before slicing for neat pieces.

Using low-quality cocoa powder

Why it happens Buying cheap cocoa can result in weak chocolate flavor.

Fix Invest in a good unsweetened cocoa for rich taste and proper color.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Brownies can be kept at room temperature in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Make sure to cover well to prevent them from drying out. They’ll stay soft but are best eaten within this timeframe for optimal freshness.

Refrigerator

Store brownies in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. They may firm up and become denser when chilled but will still taste delicious. Reheat briefly before serving to soften if desired.

Freezer

Freeze brownies wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and placed in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a few hours or overnight in the fridge. Reheat gently to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm brownies in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk or water before reheating can help maintain moisture. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it refrigerated. Bring it to room temperature before baking. Brownies themselves also freeze well, so bake in advance and thaw as needed for quick treats.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for these brownies?
Yes, you can use frozen zucchini, but be sure to thaw it completely and squeeze out as much moisture as possible. Frozen zucchini tends to be more watery, so thorough draining is essential to avoid soggy brownies.
What if I don’t have cocoa powder? Can I use melted chocolate?
You can substitute melted chocolate, but reduce the butter slightly since chocolate contains fat. Melt the chocolate gently and fold it into the wet ingredients. This will make the brownies richer but may alter texture slightly.
How do I know when the brownies are done without overbaking?
Start checking around 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center. It should come out with moist crumbs but no wet batter. Overbaking dries brownies out, so err on the side of slightly underbaked.
Can I make these brownies dairy-free?
Yes! Swap butter for coconut oil or a vegan butter alternative. The texture might be a bit different but still moist and fudgy thanks to the zucchini. Use dairy-free chocolate chips if adding.
Will these brownies taste like zucchini?
Not really. The zucchini adds moisture and a subtle earthiness but is masked by the rich chocolate flavor. Most people won’t guess there are veggies inside!
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Absolutely! Chopped nuts, chocolate chips, or even dried fruit work well. Fold them in gently at the end to keep the batter balanced.
Why do my brownies come out cakey instead of fudgy?
This can happen if you use too much flour, overmix the batter, or bake too long. Measure ingredients carefully and fold gently to maintain fudgy texture.
How should I store leftover brownies?
Keep brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days, or refrigerate for up to 5 days. Freeze for longer storage. Let them come to room temp or warm slightly before serving.
