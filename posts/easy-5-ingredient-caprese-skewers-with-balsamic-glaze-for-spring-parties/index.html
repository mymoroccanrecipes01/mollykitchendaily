Fresh and Easy Caprese Skewers with Balsamic Glaze for Spring Gatherings

Molly Kitchen Daily Published on May 29, 2026
15 min prep 0 min cook 6 servings

These Caprese skewers are such a simple, fresh appetizer that’s bursting with flavor. Juicy cherry tomatoes, soft mozzarella, and a sweet-tangy balsamic glaze come together perfectly. Ideal for spring parties or casual get-togethers!

Why This Works

The success of these Caprese skewers lies in the balance of fresh, high-quality ingredients combined with a simple yet transformative technique: the balsamic glaze reduction. When balsamic vinegar is reduced, its water content evaporates, concentrating the sugars and acids to create a syrupy glaze that coats each bite with intense flavor. This reduction also softens the vinegar’s sharpness, making it sweet and tangy, which contrasts beautifully with the creamy mozzarella and juicy tomatoes.

Fresh mozzarella balls have a delicate texture that can easily become watery if not handled carefully. Draining and drying them before assembly ensures they maintain their shape and don’t dilute the flavors. The basil leaves add an aromatic freshness due to their essential oils, which are released gently by slight bruising when threading onto the skewers, enhancing the overall sensory experience.

The assembly order is intentional to keep the basil visible and prevent it from wilting too quickly. Placing it between the tomato and cheese protects it from direct contact with the glaze until serving, preserving its vibrant green color and fresh flavor. Lastly, seasoning with salt and pepper just before serving brightens the flavors and balances the sweetness of the glaze, making each skewer a harmonious bite.

These details come together to elevate what might seem like a simple appetizer into a fresh, flavorful, and visually appealing dish perfect for any occasion.

  • When choosing balsamic vinegar for the glaze, opt for a good-quality traditional or aged balsamic. These have a richer, more complex flavor that thickens nicely when reduced. Avoid cheap, watery vinegars as they won't reduce well and may taste harsh. The vinegar’s natural sugars caramelize during reduction, giving the glaze its characteristic sweetness and glossy texture.
  • Look for bocconcini or ciliegine varieties of fresh mozzarella. They have a soft, moist texture that melts slightly on the tongue but holds shape well when threaded on skewers. Avoid pre-shredded or processed mozzarella as it lacks moisture and creaminess, which are key for this recipe’s luscious mouthfeel.
  • Select firm, ripe cherry tomatoes with bright skin and no blemishes. Their natural juiciness and mild sweetness are essential to balance the acidity of the balsamic glaze. Overripe tomatoes can be too soft and watery, which affects the texture and presentation of the skewers.
  • Fresh basil should be vibrant green without any brown spots or wilting. The essential oils in fresh basil provide bright, aromatic notes that elevate the dish. Washing and drying the leaves carefully prevent excess moisture from making the skewers soggy. Large leaves can be folded or torn to fit the skewers better.
  • Adding a natural sweetener like honey or brown sugar to the balsamic vinegar helps balance its acidity and aids caramelization during reduction. This creates a glaze that’s both sweet and tangy, enhancing the overall flavor profile. Substitute maple syrup if desired for a different sweetness note.

Ingredients

  • 1 pint cherry tomatoes, washed and dried
  • 8 oz fresh mozzarella balls (bocconcini), drained
  • Fresh basil leaves, about 24 leaves
  • 1/2 cup balsamic vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons honey or brown sugar
  • Salt to taste
  • Black pepper to taste
  • 24 small wooden skewers or toothpicks

Introduction

I first made these Caprese skewers at a spring picnic a few years ago, and they instantly became a favorite in my circle. What I love about this recipe is how effortlessly it combines fresh, wholesome ingredients into a bite-sized treat that everyone loves. Unlike the traditional Caprese salad, putting everything on skewers makes it super portable and perfect for gatherings. The trick I learned is making a quick balsamic glaze by simmering vinegar with a touch of honey; it gives a sweet tang that perfectly complements the creamy mozzarella and juicy tomatoes. Plus, adding fresh basil leaves between each tomato and cheese ball adds an aromatic freshness that really elevates the flavor. These skewers look vibrant and inviting when arranged on a platter, making them a beautiful addition to any party spread. They’re so quick to assemble that I often throw them together last minute when friends drop by. The finished bites are juicy, creamy, and just a little sweet from the glaze—so satisfying and light at the same time. Honestly, once you try these, you’ll want to keep them on your go-to list for every spring or summer get-together!

How To Do It

  1. Step 1

    To make the balsamic glaze, pour 1/2 cup of balsamic vinegar into a small saucepan. Add 2 tablespoons of honey or brown sugar to balance the acidity with a gentle sweetness. Bring the mixture to a simmer over medium heat, stirring occasionally to dissolve the sweetener. Let it reduce slowly until it thickens to a syrupy consistency, about 10-12 minutes. This slow reduction concentrates the flavors, creating a glaze that clings beautifully to the skewers. Be careful not to let it burn; it should coat the back of a spoon. Once done, remove from heat and let cool. This glaze is the flavor star of the dish, adding a tangy-sweet punch that ties all the ingredients together.

    ⏱ 12 minutes✓ The glaze should be thick enough to coat the back of a spoon and drip slowly.
  2. Step 2

    While the glaze cools, prepare the ingredients for skewering. Pat the cherry tomatoes dry to avoid excess moisture, which can make the skewers soggy. Drain the fresh mozzarella balls well to prevent watery bites. Rinse and gently dry the fresh basil leaves, selecting smaller ones that fit nicely on the skewers. This prep ensures each bite has balanced texture and flavor. Using a small skewer or toothpick, thread one cherry tomato, one basil leaf folded if large, and one mozzarella ball onto each skewer. Repeat until all ingredients are used. This assembly order helps keep the basil fresh and visible, adding a pop of green and a burst of herbal flavor in every bite.

    ⏱ 10 minutes✓ Each skewer should hold together firmly with no slipping ingredients.
  3. Step 3

    Arrange the finished skewers on a serving platter in a single layer for a beautiful presentation. Drizzle the cooled balsamic glaze generously over the skewers right before serving. This timing keeps the glaze glossy and prevents the basil and cheese from becoming soggy. If you drizzle too early, the glaze can soak in and make the cheese watery. For an extra touch, sprinkle a little freshly ground black pepper and a pinch of salt over the skewers to enhance the flavors. The seasoning helps balance the sweetness of the glaze and brightens the overall taste.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Skewers should look shiny with glaze and seasoned evenly.
  4. Step 4

    If you want to prep ahead, you can assemble the skewers without the glaze and store them in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 hours. Just wait to drizzle the balsamic glaze until serving time to keep everything fresh and vibrant. This makes it easy to prepare for parties in advance without any loss of texture or flavor. Remember to cover the container tightly to prevent the basil from wilting or the mozzarella from drying out.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Skewers remain firm and basil leaves stay fresh-looking.
  5. Step 5

    For a slight twist, you can add a small piece of prosciutto or a green olive on each skewer for extra flavor and texture. This variation adds a salty, savory note that pairs wonderfully with the other ingredients. If you choose to include these, thread them on after the mozzarella ball before the basil leaf. This keeps the flavors balanced and visually appealing. Be mindful of the skewer length to accommodate the extra ingredients comfortably without crowding.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Skewers with extras hold together well and look balanced.
  6. Step 6

    Serve the Caprese skewers chilled or at room temperature. If refrigerated, take them out about 15 minutes before serving to let the flavors open up. Cold cheese can mute flavors, so this resting time helps the mozzarella soften slightly and the basil aroma to come through. Arrange the skewers on a nice platter and provide extra balsamic glaze on the side for guests who want a little more tang.

    ⏱ 15 minutes resting✓ Skewers taste fresh and the glaze is smooth and glossy.
  7. Step 7

    When selecting your cherry tomatoes, look for firm, bright red ones with no wrinkles or soft spots. Fresh tomatoes provide the best sweetness and juiciness, which is key for this recipe’s fresh flavor profile. If tomatoes are underripe or mealy, the overall taste will suffer. Similarly, choose high-quality fresh mozzarella labeled bocconcini or ciliegine (small balls) for the best creamy texture. Avoid shredded or pre-sliced cheese as it won’t hold up well on skewers or provide the same mouthfeel.

    ⏱ 5 minutes✓ Ingredients are fresh, firm, and visually vibrant.
  8. Step 8

    If you want to make the glaze extra rich, consider adding a tiny pinch of salt or a splash of good olive oil after it’s reduced and cooled. This adds depth and a slight sheen that makes the glaze even more appetizing. Just be careful not to add too much oil or it will thin the glaze. A little goes a long way in balancing the acidity with richness.

    ⏱ 2 minutes✓ Glaze is glossy and balanced in flavor.
  9. Step 9

    Finally, garnish the platter with a few extra basil leaves or a sprinkle of freshly cracked black pepper right before serving for a pretty finish. This adds an inviting aroma and enhances the visual appeal. Presentation counts, especially for appetizers, and a few thoughtful touches make the dish feel special and homemade.

    ⏱ 3 minutes✓ Platter looks fresh, colorful, and inviting.

Variations

  • Add Prosciutto for a Salty Twist: Include thin slices of prosciutto on each skewer for a salty, savory contrast to the fresh ingredients. This variation adds richness and makes the skewers more filling, great for cocktail parties or as a light appetizer.
    Thread prosciutto after the mozzarella and before the basil to keep flavors balanced and maintain skewer stability.
  • Use Pesto Instead of Basil Leaves: Swap the fresh basil leaves with a small dollop of pesto on each mozzarella ball. This adds an herby, garlicky flavor and a creamy texture that’s different but equally delicious.
    Add pesto right before serving to prevent the skewers from becoming soggy.
  • Make It Vegan with Plant-Based Cheese: Replace mozzarella with creamy vegan cheese alternatives and omit honey in the glaze for a fully vegan version. Use maple syrup or agave as a sweetener. The flavor will be slightly different but still fresh and satisfying.
    Choose firm vegan cheeses that hold their shape well on skewers to mimic mozzarella’s texture.
  • Add Grilled Zucchini or Eggplant Slices: Thread thin, grilled slices of zucchini or eggplant onto the skewers for a smoky flavor addition. This turns the appetizer into a more substantial bite and adds a bit of earthiness.
    Cool grilled vegetables completely before assembling to avoid wilting basil or melting cheese.
  • Use Different Cherry Tomato Colors: Mix red, yellow, and orange cherry tomatoes for a colorful, festive look that brightens any table. The slight flavor differences add complexity and visual interest.
    Select tomatoes that are similarly ripe to ensure consistent texture across colors.

Pro Tips

  • Dry your ingredients thoroughly before assembling the skewers. — Excess moisture from wet tomatoes or mozzarella can dilute the balsamic glaze and make the skewers soggy. Thorough drying preserves the texture and ensures the glaze sticks properly for a beautiful, flavorful finish.
  • Simmer the balsamic glaze slowly and watch carefully. — Reducing balsamic vinegar too quickly or at too high heat can burn it, resulting in a bitter taste. A gentle simmer allows the sugars to caramelize evenly, producing a sweet and balanced glaze that enhances the dish.
  • Assemble the skewers shortly before serving and add glaze last. — Adding the balsamic glaze too early can make the basil wilt and the cheese soggy. Drizzling it just before serving keeps the ingredients fresh and visually appealing, preserving the crisp textures and vibrant colors.
  • Use small wooden skewers or toothpicks for easy eating. — Smaller skewers make the appetizer more manageable and elegant for guests to handle. They also help keep the ingredients securely together without overcrowding, which ensures balanced bites.
  • Add a pinch of freshly ground black pepper just before serving. — Pepper adds a subtle heat and depth that balances the sweetness of the glaze and the creaminess of the cheese, enhancing the overall flavor complexity of the skewers.
  • Choose ripe but firm cherry tomatoes for the best texture. — Overripe tomatoes can become mushy and watery, which negatively affects the texture and presentation. Firm tomatoes provide a satisfying bite and maintain their shape on the skewer.
  • If you want a thicker glaze, continue simmering until it coats the back of a spoon. — A thicker glaze clings better to the skewers and prevents pooling on the platter. It intensifies the flavor and adds a glossy finish that looks appetizing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using watery or underripe mozzarella.
    Why: People often buy any mozzarella without considering texture or moisture content, assuming all are the same.
    Fix: Choose fresh bocconcini or ciliegine mozzarella and drain it well before use. Avoid shredded or low-moisture mozzarella as they won’t provide the creamy texture needed.
  • Adding balsamic glaze too early.
    Why: Eager cooks want to finish the dish quickly and drizzle the glaze during assembly.
    Fix: Wait to add glaze until just before serving to prevent soggy cheese and wilted basil. This keeps flavors fresh and textures intact.
  • Not reducing balsamic vinegar enough.
    Why: Impatience or misunderstanding how reduction works leads to a thin, watery glaze.
    Fix: Simmer vinegar gently until thick and syrupy, about 10-12 minutes. This concentrates flavors and creates the perfect glaze consistency.
  • Skipping drying the tomatoes and mozzarella.
    Why: Assuming rinsed ingredients are dry enough can cause excess moisture.
    Fix: Pat all ingredients dry with paper towels before assembling to avoid watery skewers.
  • Using large basil leaves without folding.
    Why: Not considering skewer size leads to oversized leaves that tear or look messy.
    Fix: Fold or tear large basil leaves to fit neatly between tomato and cheese for a tidy presentation.

Storage & Tips

  • 🌡️ Room Temperature: Caprese skewers can safely sit out for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with a clean cloth. Avoid leaving them longer to prevent cheese spoilage and basil wilting.
  • ❄️ Refrigerator: Store assembled skewers without glaze in an airtight container for up to 4 hours. Add the balsamic glaze right before serving to maintain freshness. The texture of basil and mozzarella is best when served chilled but not cold.
  • 🧊 Freezer: Freezing is not recommended as fresh mozzarella and basil lose texture and flavor. Tomatoes can become mushy, so best to prepare fresh.
  • ♨️ Reheating: No reheating needed. Serve chilled or at room temperature. If refrigerated, let sit 15 minutes before serving to soften mozzarella and release aromas.
  • 📅 Make Ahead: Prepare the balsamic glaze and assemble skewers up to 4 hours ahead without the glaze. Store in airtight containers in the fridge. Drizzle glaze and season just before serving for best results.

FAQ

Can I make the balsamic glaze ahead of time?
Yes! You can make the balsamic glaze a day ahead and store it in the fridge in a sealed container. Before using, bring it to room temperature or warm slightly to loosen it up. This saves time on serving day and the flavor often deepens overnight.
What can I substitute for fresh mozzarella?
If fresh mozzarella isn’t available, look for a mild, creamy cheese like burrata or even small bocconcini made from cow’s milk. Avoid pre-shredded cheeses as they don’t provide the same texture or flavor. For a vegan option, use firm plant-based cheese alternatives.
How do I keep the basil from wilting?
Dry the basil leaves completely before assembling and add them between the tomato and cheese to protect them. Also, add the balsamic glaze just before serving to avoid sogginess. Store the skewers chilled and avoid leaving them out too long.
Can I use regular vinegar instead of balsamic?
Regular vinegar won’t work well because it lacks the sweetness and depth of balsamic. The glaze relies on balsamic’s natural sugars and flavor complexity, so substituting will result in a less balanced taste.
What’s the best way to serve these skewers at a party?
Arrange them on a large platter with extra balsamic glaze on the side for guests to drizzle as they like. Keep the skewers chilled until about 15 minutes before serving to bring out the flavors and soften the cheese slightly.
Can these skewers be made gluten-free?
Absolutely! All the ingredients are naturally gluten-free. Just ensure the balsamic vinegar and any additional seasonings are certified gluten-free to avoid cross-contamination.
How long do leftovers keep in the fridge?
Leftover skewers without glaze will keep well for up to 4 hours refrigerated. After that, the basil may wilt and mozzarella texture can change. It’s best to enjoy them fresh for optimal flavor and appearance.
Is it okay to use larger skewers or toothpicks?
You can use larger skewers if you prefer, but keep in mind that smaller ones make the bites easier to eat and look neater. If using bigger skewers, space out ingredients to maintain balance and presentation.
