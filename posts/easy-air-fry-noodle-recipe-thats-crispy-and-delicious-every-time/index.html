Crispy Air-Fried Noodles with Soy and Sesame Flavor - Snack or Side Dish

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 29, 2026
10 min Prep Time
15 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These air-fried noodles come out perfectly crispy without the hassle of deep frying. Tossed quickly in soy sauce and sesame oil for a flavorful crunch. Great for snacking or as a crunchy side anytime!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this air-fried noodle recipe when I wanted a crunchy snack without the mess of deep frying. Traditional deep-fried noodles can be delicious but often leave your kitchen smelling oily and require lots of clean-up. This method is a game-changer! By tossing the noodles in soy sauce and toasted sesame oil before air-frying, you get this amazing flavor infused into every crispy bite. The trick I learned is to make sure the noodles are well coated but not soggy, and spreading them evenly in the air fryer basket so they crisp up uniformly. The end result is golden, crunchy noodles that are addictive and perfect for snacking or as a fun side dish with Asian meals. Plus, it only takes about 25 minutes total, so it’s great when you want something quick but tasty. I love serving these at casual get-togethers or whenever I need a salty, crunchy fix. The aroma of toasted sesame oil roasting in the air fryer fills the kitchen and makes it impossible to wait until they’re done!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings as a snack or side

  • 150 grams dried thin egg noodles
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  • 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Pinch of black pepper
  • Optional: sliced green onions for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by boiling the dried egg noodles in salted water according to package instructions, usually around 3-4 minutes until just tender but still firm (al dente). This step is crucial because overcooked noodles will become mushy and won’t crisp up well. Once boiled, drain thoroughly and spread them out on a clean kitchen towel or paper towels to remove excess moisture. Patting dry helps the noodles crisp better during air frying, so don’t skip this.
⏱ Time: 6 minutes
✓ Doneness: Noodles should be firm to the bite and dry on the surface before seasoning.
Step 2
In a large bowl, combine soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, vegetable oil, garlic powder, and black pepper. Toss the drained noodles gently but thoroughly to coat each strand evenly. The soy and sesame oil add deep umami and nutty flavor that infuses during air frying. Be careful not to add too much liquid or the noodles will clump and steam instead of crisping.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Noodles look glossy but not wet or sticky; they should separate easily.
Step 3
Preheat your air fryer to 375°F (190°C) for about 3 minutes. This ensures the noodles start crisping immediately when placed inside. Preheating is important because cold air fryers can cause uneven cooking and soggy spots.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer display shows 375°F and is ready for cooking.
Step 4
Spread the coated noodles evenly in a single layer in the air fryer basket. Avoid overcrowding as this will trap steam and prevent crispiness. You might need to cook in batches depending on your air fryer size. Even spacing allows hot air to circulate freely, crisping every noodle strand.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Noodles are spread flat with no clumps or overlapping.
Step 5
Cook the noodles at 375°F for 8-10 minutes, shaking the basket gently every 3 minutes to turn the noodles. This shaking helps them brown evenly and prevents burning in spots. Keep an eye on them the last few minutes because they can go from perfectly crispy to burnt quickly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Noodles are golden brown and crispy with a toasted aroma.
Step 6
Once crispy, transfer the noodles to a plate lined with paper towels to absorb any excess oil. Let them cool slightly—they'll crisp up even more as they cool. This resting step is important to lock in that crunch and avoid sogginess.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Noodles feel dry and crisp when touched, no oily residue.
Step 7
Optional: Garnish with sliced green onions or a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds for an extra fresh and nutty touch. This adds some color and a mild crunch contrast that pairs well with the noodles’ texture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Green onions look fresh and vibrant, sesame seeds lightly toasted.
Step 8
Serve immediately as a crunchy snack or alongside your favorite Asian dishes. These noodles are best enjoyed fresh to keep their crisp texture. If serving later, store in an airtight container and re-crisp in the air fryer before serving.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Noodles remain crunchy and flavorful when served.
Step 9
Clean your air fryer basket promptly after use to prevent oil residue buildup, which can affect future cooking and flavor. A quick wipe with warm soapy water usually does the trick.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Basket is clean, dry, and ready for next use.

Variations

Spicy Chili Air-Fried Noodles

Add a teaspoon of chili flakes or a dash of chili oil when tossing the noodles before air frying. This gives the noodles a pleasant heat that pairs well with the savory soy and sesame flavors. Expect a slightly fiery crunch that’s perfect for spice lovers.

Note: Chili flakes can burn if too much is added; moderate the amount and shake the basket often to avoid bitterness.

Sweet and Spicy Honey Soy Noodles

Mix in a teaspoon of honey with the soy sauce and sesame oil for a sweet glaze that caramelizes beautifully during air frying. This variation balances savory, sweet, and nutty notes for a complex flavor profile.

Note: Honey can cause faster browning; monitor the noodles closely to prevent burning.

Herb-Infused Crispy Noodles

Add dried herbs like thyme or rosemary to the seasoning mix before air frying. The herbs add an aromatic twist that works wonderfully as a side for grilled meats or roasted veggies.

Note: Herbs dry out and can become brittle; add them sparingly to avoid overpowering.

Gluten-Free Rice Noodle Version

Use thin rice noodles instead of egg noodles for a gluten-free option. Cook and dry them similarly, then toss with gluten-free tamari and toasted sesame oil. The texture will be a bit lighter and more delicate but still crispy.

Note: Rice noodles are more fragile; handle gently and reduce air frying time slightly to prevent burning.

Peanut Butter Sesame Noodles

Stir a tablespoon of smooth peanut butter into the soy and sesame oil mixture for a rich, creamy coating before air frying. This gives the noodles a luscious nutty flavor and a slightly different texture.

Note: Peanut butter can clump; whisk it well with oils and soy sauce for an even coating.

Pro Tips

Pat the noodles very dry after boiling to ensure crispiness.
Water on the noodle surface causes steaming in the air fryer, resulting in soggy, limp noodles instead of crisp ones. Removing moisture lets the hot circulating air do its job, creating that crunchy texture.
Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket; cook in batches if needed.
Overcrowding traps steam and unevenly distributes heat, which prevents the noodles from crisping uniformly. Spreading them in a single layer ensures hot air reaches every strand.
Shake the basket every few minutes during cooking.
This redistributes the noodles so they brown evenly and prevents burning in one spot. It also helps release any trapped steam, maintaining crispiness.
Use toasted sesame oil instead of regular sesame oil for deeper flavor.
Toasted sesame oil has a rich, nutty aroma that intensifies during air frying, adding complexity to the noodles. Regular sesame oil is milder and won’t provide the same depth.
Preheat the air fryer before adding noodles.
Starting with a hot air fryer jumpstarts the Maillard reaction and prevents the noodles from absorbing oil or moisture before crisping. This leads to a better texture.
Avoid adding too much soy sauce to prevent sogginess.
Soy sauce is liquid and can weigh the noodles down if overused, making them less crispy. A light coating is enough to impart flavor without compromising texture.
Let the noodles cool on paper towels after air frying.
Cooling allows residual heat to finish crisping and the paper towel absorbs excess oil, resulting in a lighter, crunchier snack.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the drying step after boiling noodles

Why it happens Many assume draining is enough, but surface moisture remains and causes steaming during air frying.

Fix After draining, spread noodles on towels and gently pat dry. This extra step removes water that would otherwise prevent crisping.

Using too much soy sauce, making noodles soggy

Why it happens Soy sauce is liquid, so over-applying leads to clumping and sogginess.

Fix Measure carefully and toss lightly to coat noodles just enough for flavor without excess moisture.

Overcrowding the air fryer basket

Why it happens Trying to cook all at once to save time, unaware it traps steam.

Fix Cook in batches if needed, spreading noodles in a single layer for even crisping.

Not shaking the basket during cooking

Why it happens Forgetting or rushing the process leads to uneven browning.

Fix Set a timer to shake the basket every 3 minutes; this ensures uniform cooking.

Using fresh garlic instead of garlic powder

Why it happens Fresh garlic burns quickly at high heat, causing bitterness.

Fix Use garlic powder for even, mild seasoning that withstands air fryer temperatures.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These air-fried noodles can be kept at room temperature for up to 4 hours in a covered container. Beyond that, they tend to lose their crispness and become stale or soggy due to moisture in the air.

Refrigerator

Store leftover noodles in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. Note that refrigeration softens the noodles, so reheat them in the air fryer or oven to restore crispiness before serving.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as noodles lose their texture and become mushy after thawing. If necessary, freeze in an airtight container for up to 1 month and re-crisp thoroughly in the air fryer upon thawing.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 350°F (175°C) for 3-5 minutes to bring back the crunch. Avoid microwaving as it makes the noodles soggy. Adding a light spray of oil before reheating can help restore crispness.

Make Ahead

You can boil and dry the noodles a day ahead, then toss with the seasoning just before air frying. Store the dried noodles in an airtight container at room temperature. Cook fresh in the air fryer for best results.

FAQ

Can I use fresh noodles instead of dried for this recipe?
Fresh noodles have higher moisture content and a softer texture, which makes them less suitable for air frying crispy. If you use fresh noodles, they may become mushy or won’t crisp evenly. It’s best to stick with dried thin egg noodles for this recipe.
What if I don't have an air fryer? Can I bake these noodles instead?
Yes, you can bake the seasoned noodles on a parchment-lined baking sheet at 400°F (200°C) for 10-15 minutes, stirring halfway through. Keep a close eye to prevent burning. The texture will be similar but might not be as evenly crisp as air frying.
How do I prevent the noodles from sticking together after tossing with sauce?
Make sure to toss gently and evenly with just enough soy sauce and oil to coat. Pat the noodles dry after boiling to reduce moisture. Also, spread them in a single layer when air frying to avoid clumping.
Can I add other seasonings or spices?
Absolutely! Feel free to experiment with chili flakes, garlic powder, five-spice powder, or even a sprinkle of sugar for sweetness. Just keep in mind that some spices may burn quickly, so adjust cooking time and monitor closely.
Why are my noodles soggy after air frying?
Sogginess usually results from excess moisture—either from not drying the noodles properly after boiling or from too much soy sauce. Also, overcrowding the air fryer traps steam, preventing crisping. Make sure to dry noodles well and cook in batches if needed.
Is it okay to use low-sodium soy sauce?
Yes, low-sodium soy sauce works fine and can be a healthier option. Keep in mind it may affect the saltiness of the final dish, so adjust seasoning accordingly if you prefer a saltier taste.
Can I make this recipe vegan?
Yes, this recipe is naturally vegan as long as you use egg-free noodles (like rice noodles) and check that your soy sauce is vegan-friendly. The flavor and crispiness remain great with these swaps.
How long do these noodles stay crispy after cooking?
They’re crispiest right after cooking and start to soften within a couple of hours due to humidity. To enjoy maximum crunch, serve immediately or reheat briefly in the air fryer before serving.
