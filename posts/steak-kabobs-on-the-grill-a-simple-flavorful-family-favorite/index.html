Juicy Marinated Steak Kabobs with Colorful Veggies for Perfect Summer Grilling

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 16, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These steak kabobs are my go-to for easy grilling nights. The marinade keeps the meat tender and juicy, while bell peppers and onions add a fresh crunch. Great for weekend dinners or summer cookouts!

Introduction

I first made these steak kabobs years ago when I wanted a simple, no-fuss dinner that still felt special. After a few tries, I found the perfect marinade combo that keeps the steak super juicy and tender without overpowering it. The trick I learned is soaking the wooden skewers beforehand to prevent burning, plus cutting the veggies and meat into even pieces so everything cooks evenly. I love how the colorful bell peppers and onions add a fresh crunch and a pop of sweetness, balancing out the savory meat. These kabobs always turn out beautifully charred on the outside but juicy inside, making them a hit at family dinners and summer cookouts alike. Honestly, one time I rushed the marinating and ended up with tougher meat — lesson learned! Now I always let it soak for at least 2 hours, and it makes all the difference. If you’re looking for a simple grilled meal that’s packed with flavor and easy to customize, this recipe’s got you covered.

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 kabobs, 4 servings

  • 1.5 pounds sirloin steak, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 1 red bell pepper, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 1 green bell pepper, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 1 large red onion, cut into wedges
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Wooden skewers, soaked in water for 30 minutes

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by soaking your wooden skewers in water for at least 30 minutes before grilling. This is crucial to prevent the skewers from burning on the grill. While they soak, cut the sirloin steak into 1-inch cubes, making sure the pieces are uniform to ensure even cooking. Also, chop the bell peppers and onion into similar-sized chunks. Uniform sizes help everything cook at the same rate, so you avoid undercooked veggies or overdone meat.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers feel saturated with water and veggies and meat pieces look evenly sized
Step 2
Prepare your marinade by whisking together olive oil, soy sauce, Worcestershire sauce, minced garlic, dried oregano, salt, and pepper in a bowl. The olive oil helps keep the steak moist, while the soy and Worcestershire add depth of flavor and umami. Garlic and oregano give it a nice aromatic punch. Whisking ensures the flavors blend well and the oil emulsifies slightly, helping to coat the meat evenly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Marinade is well combined and slightly thickened
Step 3
Add the steak cubes to the marinade, stirring gently to coat all pieces thoroughly. Cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, ideally 4. Marinating tenderizes the meat and infuses it with flavor. Rushing this step can result in less juicy, less flavorful kabobs, so patience is key. If you're short on time, even 30 minutes helps, but longer is better.
⏱ Time: 2-4 hours
✓ Doneness: Steak pieces look glossy and slightly swollen from marinade absorption
Step 4
Preheat your grill to medium-high heat, around 400°F. This temperature is perfect for searing the meat quickly and locking in juices without drying it out. Make sure the grill grates are clean and well-oiled to prevent sticking. If the grill is too hot, the outside will burn before the inside cooks; too low, and you won't get that nice char.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Grill grates are hot and slightly smoking, ready for kabobs
Step 5
Thread the marinated steak, bell peppers, and onion wedges onto the soaked skewers, alternating pieces. This layering not only looks great but ensures balanced flavors and textures in every bite. Don't pack the pieces too tightly; leave a little space so heat can circulate and cook the meat and veggies evenly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skewers are evenly loaded with alternating meat and veggies, spaced slightly
Step 6
Place the kabobs on the grill and cook for about 12-15 minutes, turning every 3-4 minutes. This frequent turning promotes even cooking and prevents burning on one side. Watch for grill marks and a nice crust on the steak. Avoid pressing down on the meat as it cooks, or you’ll lose those precious juices.
⏱ Time: 12-15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Steak has a caramelized crust with grill marks and veggies are tender but firm
Step 7
Check the doneness by cutting into a piece of steak; it should be cooked through but still juicy inside. If you prefer medium-rare, aim for an internal temperature of 130-135°F. Use a meat thermometer if you have one. Remember, the steak will continue to cook slightly after removing from the grill.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Steak is firm but yields slightly to touch, with pink center for medium-rare
Step 8
Remove the kabobs from the grill and let them rest for about 5 minutes on a plate. Resting allows the juices to redistribute throughout the meat, making each bite tender and flavorful. Cutting into the steak too soon will cause those juices to spill out, resulting in drier meat.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Kabobs look rested and juices settle without pooling
Step 9
Serve your steak kabobs hot with your favorite sides like rice, salad, or grilled bread. The combination of juicy steak and crisp veggies makes for an easy, satisfying meal. Garnish with fresh herbs if you like for a pop of color and extra flavor.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Kabobs look vibrant and ready to enjoy

Variations

Spicy Chipotle Steak Kabobs

Add 1 teaspoon chipotle powder and a splash of lime juice to the marinade for a smoky, spicy kick. The lime brightens the flavors while chipotle adds warmth. Expect a bolder, slightly smoky taste with a hint of heat.

Note: Spicy powders can burn easily, so watch grilling times closely to prevent bitterness.

Mediterranean Herb Kabobs

Swap oregano for fresh rosemary and thyme, and add lemon zest to the marinade. Use cherry tomatoes and zucchini as veggies. This brings fresh, herbaceous notes and a lighter feel perfect for Mediterranean-inspired meals.

Note: Fresh herbs are delicate, so add them to the marinade just before grilling to preserve flavor.

Asian-Style Steak Kabobs

Replace Worcestershire with hoisin sauce and add grated ginger and sesame oil to the marinade. Serve with a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds and scallions for an Asian flair with sweet and savory layers.

Note: Sesame oil has a low smoke point; use sparingly and add mostly after cooking.

Vegetarian Kabobs

Skip the steak and use firm tofu or portobello mushrooms marinated in the same sauce. Grill along with the peppers and onions for a satisfying vegetarian option with similar smoky flavor.

Note: Tofu should be pressed to remove moisture for better grilling texture.

Honey Garlic Steak Kabobs

Add 1 tablespoon honey to the marinade for a touch of sweetness that caramelizes beautifully on the grill. Garlic remains prominent, balancing sweet and savory flavors perfectly.

Note: Honey can burn easily; keep an eye on kabobs and turn frequently to avoid charring.

Pro Tips

Always soak wooden skewers before grilling.
Soaking prevents the skewers from catching fire or burning, which can impart a bitter taste and cause flare-ups. Wet skewers also help hold the kabobs together securely during cooking.
Cut meat and veggies into uniform sizes.
Uniform pieces cook evenly, preventing some chunks from being undercooked while others dry out. This also ensures a balanced bite with meat and vegetables.
Don’t rush the marinating time.
Allowing the steak to marinate for at least 2 hours lets the flavors penetrate and tenderizes the meat. Short marinating times result in less flavorful and tougher kabobs.
Preheat the grill to medium-high heat before cooking.
Proper heat level ensures a good sear, caramelizing the meat and locking in juices. Too low heat means no crust; too high risks burning.
Turn kabobs frequently while grilling.
Flipping every few minutes promotes even cooking and prevents one side from burning or drying out.
Let kabobs rest for 5 minutes after grilling.
Resting redistributes juices within the meat, making it more tender and juicy when you bite in.
Use a meat thermometer to check doneness.
This helps avoid overcooking or undercooking. Aim for 130-135°F for medium-rare steak, adjusting for your preference.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the soak for wooden skewers

Why it happens People often overlook soaking because it seems like an unnecessary step or they forget.

Fix Always soak skewers for at least 30 minutes in water before grilling. This prevents them from burning and protects the flavor of your kabobs.

Cutting uneven pieces

Why it happens In a rush, it's tempting to cut chunks irregularly, thinking it won’t matter.

Fix Take your time to cut steak and veggies into uniform 1-inch pieces so they cook evenly and look appealing.

Marinating for too short a time

Why it happens Impatience or last-minute cooking plans lead to rushing the marinade step.

Fix Plan ahead and marinate for at least 2 hours. This tenderizes the meat and infuses flavor deeply.

Cooking on too high heat

Why it happens People think higher heat means faster cooking and better sear.

Fix Keep grill at medium-high heat to get a good crust without burning. Too hot and kabobs burn outside but stay raw inside.

Not letting kabobs rest

Why it happens Eager to eat, many skip resting and cut immediately off the grill.

Fix Rest kabobs for 5 minutes to let juices redistribute. This prevents dry, tough meat.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Steak kabobs should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Cover with foil or place in a covered dish if serving buffet-style to keep them safe and moist.

Refrigerator

Store cooked kabobs in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. They may dry out slightly when cold, so reheat gently or serve at room temperature for best texture.

Freezer

Kabobs can be frozen for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly in plastic wrap and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw in the fridge overnight before reheating to maintain texture.

Reheating

Reheat kabobs on a stovetop skillet over medium heat or in the oven at 350°F for 10-15 minutes. Add a splash of water or broth to keep meat moist. Avoid microwave reheating which can dry out the steak.

Make Ahead

Marinate the steak up to 24 hours ahead and keep refrigerated. You can also pre-cut veggies and thread kabobs a few hours before grilling, storing them covered in the fridge until ready to cook.

FAQ

Can I use other cuts of steak for these kabobs?
Yes, but some cuts work better than others. Sirloin is ideal for its balance of tenderness and flavor. Flank or skirt steak can also be used but benefit from longer marinating and should be sliced thinly against the grain. Avoid very lean or tough cuts as they can dry out or be chewy.
What if I don’t have Worcestershire sauce?
You can substitute with a mix of soy sauce and a splash of balsamic vinegar or a little fish sauce for umami depth. The flavor won’t be exactly the same but will still add great savory notes to the marinade.
How long should I marinate the steak?
Marinate for at least 2 hours to allow flavors to penetrate and tenderize the meat. For best results, marinate up to 4 hours. Shorter times will still add flavor but won’t tenderize as much.
Can I grill these kabobs indoors?
Yes, you can use a grill pan or broiler indoors. Make sure to preheat well and turn frequently to mimic outdoor grilling. Watch for smoke and ventilate your kitchen properly.
How do I prevent the kabobs from drying out?
Key factors are proper marinating, not overcooking, and letting kabobs rest after grilling. Also, avoid pressing down on the meat while cooking to keep juices inside.
What sides go best with steak kabobs?
Rice, grilled vegetables, fresh salads, or warm pita bread all pair wonderfully. Choose sides that complement the smoky, savory flavors without overpowering them.
Can I prepare kabobs ahead of time?
Yes, you can marinate the steak and pre-cut the veggies up to 24 hours before grilling. Thread the kabobs a few hours before cooking and keep them refrigerated covered until ready to grill.
Is it better to use wooden or metal skewers?
Wooden skewers are traditional and soak up marinade moisture, but must be soaked before grilling. Metal skewers conduct heat and can cook the meat from the inside, which some prefer for faster cooking. Both work well depending on personal preference.
