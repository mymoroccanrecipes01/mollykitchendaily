Sweet and Moist Zucchini Cake with Honey and Warm Spices

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on August 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
45 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This zucchini cake is a delightful way to sneak veggies into dessert without sacrificing flavor. The honey adds natural sweetness, while cinnamon and nutmeg bring cozy warmth. Great for afternoon tea or family snacks!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon zucchini cake years ago when I was trying to find ways to sneak more veggies into my family's diet without them noticing. Honestly, I was skeptical at first—dessert and veggies? But this recipe quickly won me over. What makes this version stand out is the use of real honey instead of refined sugar, which adds a lovely depth of flavor and natural sweetness. Plus, the warm spices like cinnamon, nutmeg, and a hint of cloves bring a cozy, comforting vibe that feels just right for cooler afternoons. I also learned that squeezing out the excess moisture from the shredded zucchini is key; it keeps the cake from getting soggy but still maintains that moist, tender crumb everyone loves. When it comes out of the oven, it’s golden, fragrant, and perfectly spiced — the kind of cake that makes you want a second slice. It’s great for a simple family snack or even a casual dessert when friends come over. I hope you enjoy it as much as we do!

What You'll Need

One 9-inch cake, 8 servings

  • 2 cups shredded zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/2 cup honey
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by shredding the zucchini finely. Then, place it in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step is crucial because zucchini holds a lot of water, and if you don’t remove the excess, your cake will turn out soggy and dense instead of light and moist. Taking the time to squeeze thoroughly ensures the right texture and helps the cake hold together well.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves. These dry ingredients create the flavor base and leavening structure. Mixing them well ensures even distribution of the spices and raising agents, which means your cake will rise evenly and have consistent flavor throughout.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is uniform with no clumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, beat the eggs lightly. Add the honey, brown sugar, vegetable oil, and vanilla extract, whisking until the mixture is smooth and glossy. Combining the wet ingredients well at this stage helps to incorporate air and ensures the sugar dissolves properly, which contributes to a tender crumb and moist texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is homogeneous and slightly thickened
Step 4
Slowly add the wet ingredients to the dry ingredients, stirring gently with a spatula or wooden spoon. Mix just until combined—overmixing can develop gluten and make the cake tough. The batter should be thick but pourable, with no large pockets of flour or streaks.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no dry spots
Step 5
Fold the shredded zucchini and chopped walnuts (if using) into the batter. Folding rather than stirring vigorously keeps the texture light and prevents overworking the batter. The zucchini adds moisture and subtle veggie flavor, while walnuts contribute crunch and nuttiness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and nuts are evenly distributed
Step 6
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease or line a 9-inch cake pan with parchment paper to prevent sticking. The even heat of 350°F is perfect for this cake to cook through without drying out or browning too quickly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes (including preheating)
✓ Doneness: Oven fully preheated and pan prepared
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared pan, smoothing the top with a spatula. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any air bubbles, which helps create an even texture. This step makes sure the cake bakes uniformly and looks appealing.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread with no air pockets
Step 8
Bake for 40 to 45 minutes, checking for doneness by inserting a toothpick in the center. If it comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs, the cake is done. Avoid opening the oven too frequently to keep the temperature steady and prevent the cake from collapsing.
⏱ Time: 40-45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean
Step 9
Let the cake cool in the pan for about 15 minutes before transferring it to a wire rack. Cooling in the pan first helps it set, making it easier to remove without breaking. Once on the rack, it cools completely and firms up for clean slicing.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake pulls away from pan edges slightly

Variations

Chocolate Chip Zucchini Cake

Add 1/2 cup of semisweet chocolate chips to the batter for a fun twist. The chocolate adds bursts of sweetness and pairs wonderfully with the warm spices. Expect a slightly richer, more indulgent cake.

Note: Fold in chocolate chips gently at the end to prevent sinking. Bake as usual, checking for doneness carefully as chocolate can affect baking time slightly.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Cake

Replace all-purpose flour with a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This adaptation maintains a tender crumb while accommodating gluten sensitivities. The texture might be a bit denser but still moist.

Note: Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend and avoid overmixing to keep the cake from becoming crumbly.

Vegan Zucchini Cake

Swap eggs for flax eggs (1 tbsp flaxseed meal + 3 tbsp water per egg) and use maple syrup instead of honey. Use coconut oil or a neutral oil. This version is moist and flavorful but slightly denser.

Note: Let the flax eggs sit for 5 minutes to thicken before mixing. Baking time may increase by 5-10 minutes.

Lemon-Spiced Zucchini Cake

Add the zest of one lemon and replace cloves with a teaspoon of ground ginger for a fresh citrusy twist. The lemon zest brightens the warm spices, offering a refreshing flavor profile.

Note: Be careful not to add too much zest to avoid bitterness. Bake as directed.

Zucchini Carrot Cake

Mix equal parts shredded zucchini and carrot for a colorful, nutrient-packed cake. The carrot adds natural sweetness and a slightly firmer texture, complementing the zucchini’s moisture.

Note: Squeeze moisture from both veggies thoroughly. The cake may need an extra 5 minutes baking time.

Pro Tips

Squeeze zucchini thoroughly using a clean towel or cheesecloth.
Removing excess water prevents a soggy cake and helps the crumb set properly. If you skip this, the batter will be too wet, causing the cake to collapse or bake unevenly.
Use room temperature eggs and oil for easier mixing.
Ingredients at room temperature combine more easily, creating a smooth batter that traps air for a light texture. Cold ingredients can cause uneven mixing and denser results.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients combine.
Overmixing develops gluten, leading to a tough and chewy cake. Stir just until no flour pockets remain to keep the texture tender.
Let the cake cool in the pan before removing.
Cooling allows the cake to firm up and reduces the chance of crumbling or breaking apart when you transfer it to a wire rack.
Add chopped nuts last and fold gently.
Folding preserves the light texture and prevents nuts from sinking or creating dense pockets within the cake.
Use fresh spices for best flavor.
Old spices lose their potency and aroma, resulting in a bland cake. Freshly ground spices enhance the warm, inviting flavor profile.
Check doneness with a toothpick after 40 minutes.
Baking times vary with oven differences. Testing prevents overbaking, which dries out the cake, or underbaking, which leaves it gooey inside.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not removing enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume shredded zucchini is dry enough, but it actually holds a lot of water that can ruin texture.

Fix Use a clean towel or cheesecloth to squeeze out excess liquid thoroughly until the zucchini feels just damp, not wet. This prevents soggy cake and ensures proper crumb.

Overmixing batter after adding flour

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix until perfectly smooth, but overmixing develops gluten, making the cake tough.

Fix Mix only until the flour disappears and the batter looks uniform. Stop as soon as you see no dry spots.

Using too much honey or sugar

Why it happens Adding extra sweetener to taste can backfire, causing the cake to be overly moist or dense.

Fix Follow the recipe’s sweetener amounts closely to maintain balance. The zucchini and honey combo naturally sweetens the cake enough.

Not preheating the oven fully

Why it happens Putting the cake in a cold or warm oven affects rising and texture.

Fix Always preheat to 350°F (175°C) before baking to ensure even cooking and proper leavening.

Skipping the cooling step before slicing

Why it happens Cutting hot cake causes it to crumble or fall apart.

Fix Allow the cake to cool in the pan for 15 minutes, then on a rack until completely cool before slicing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cake covered at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it in an airtight container or wrapped tightly with plastic wrap to prevent it from drying out. This cake stays moist but will become firmer over time.

Refrigerator

Keep the cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The flavors actually deepen when chilled, but the texture will be slightly denser. Serve cold or bring to room temperature before eating.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil, or place in a freezer-safe container. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and warm gently in the oven to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a 300°F oven for 10-15 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a small pat of butter or a splash of milk before warming can enhance moisture. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

You can shred zucchini and squeeze the moisture out 1-2 days ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix the dry ingredients separately as well. Combine everything and bake on the day you plan to serve for freshest results.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this cake?
Yes, but be sure to thaw the zucchini completely and squeeze out all excess moisture before using. Frozen zucchini tends to hold even more water, so thorough drying is essential to avoid a soggy cake.
How can I make this cake less sweet?
Reduce the honey and brown sugar by about 1/4 cup combined, but keep in mind that honey helps retain moisture. If you reduce too much, the cake might be drier and less tender.
Can I substitute the vegetable oil with butter?
Yes, melted butter works well and adds richness. However, oil keeps the cake moister and more tender. Butter may result in a slightly denser texture.
How do I know when the cake is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick or skewer into the center of the cake. It should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs attached. If it’s wet batter, it needs more time.
Can I make muffins instead of a cake?
Absolutely! Use the same batter, but reduce baking time to about 20-25 minutes. Check doneness with a toothpick as muffins bake faster due to smaller size.
Is it okay to use a different sweetener like maple syrup?
Maple syrup can substitute honey but will change the flavor slightly. Since maple syrup is more liquid, reduce other liquids slightly to keep the batter balanced.
How should I store leftover cake?
Wrap leftovers tightly or store in an airtight container in the fridge. This keeps the cake moist and fresh for up to 5 days. Reheat gently before serving.
