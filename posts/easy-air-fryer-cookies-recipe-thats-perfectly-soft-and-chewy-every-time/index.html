Soft and Chewy Air Fryer Chocolate Chip Cookies You’ll Love

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 18, 2026
15 min Prep Time
10 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These air fryer cookies are soft and chewy with just a hint of crisp on the edges. The air fryer makes baking quick and easy without heating up the kitchen. Perfect for a last-minute sweet treat!

Introduction

I first tried making cookies in my air fryer on a whim during a hot summer when turning on the oven was just not an option. Honestly, I wasn’t expecting much, but the results totally surprised me! These cookies come out soft and chewy with just the right crisp edges, and the air fryer bakes them so quickly that they’re ready before you know it. What I love about this method is how little heat it generates in the kitchen — perfect for those days when you want a treat but don’t want to sweat it out. The secret is in shaping the dough into small, evenly sized balls and giving them enough space to spread without overcrowding the basket. I’ve tweaked this recipe a few times to get that perfect balance of gooey center and crispy edges, and it’s become my go-to whenever I need a quick cookie fix. Plus, you only need one bowl, so cleanup is a breeze. The finished cookies have a warm, buttery aroma and melt in your mouth, making them an instant crowd-pleaser. If you love classic chocolate chip cookies but want a faster, cooler way to bake, this recipe is for you!

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 cookies, 12 servings

  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups semi-sweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by creaming the softened butter with both the brown and granulated sugars in a large mixing bowl. Use a hand mixer or stand mixer on medium speed until the mixture is light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. This step is crucial because it incorporates air into the batter, which helps the cookies rise and gives them a tender texture. Be careful not to overmix, or the cookies might turn out cakey rather than chewy.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: The mixture should look pale and fluffy, with no visible sugar granules.
Step 2
Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition, then mix in the vanilla extract. This helps to evenly distribute the eggs and flavor throughout the dough. If you rush this step, the dough might separate or not bind properly, affecting the texture of your cookies.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: The batter should be smooth and glossy with no streaks of egg.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together the all-purpose flour, baking soda, and salt. This ensures even distribution of the leavening agent and salt, which is important for consistent rising and balanced flavor in the cookies.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: The dry ingredients should be evenly mixed with no clumps.
Step 4
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients, mixing on low speed just until combined. Avoid overmixing here; overworked dough can lead to tough cookies. The dough should look thick and slightly sticky.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: No visible streaks of flour remain, and the dough holds together well.
Step 5
Fold in the chocolate chips gently with a spatula. This step distributes the chips evenly without breaking them up or overworking the dough, which helps keep the texture light and the chips intact.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly spread throughout the dough.
Step 6
Preheat your air fryer to 350°F (175°C) for about 3 minutes. Preheating ensures the cookies start baking immediately and helps them spread properly, creating that perfect balance of chewy center and crisp edges.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Air fryer is at a steady 350°F, ready for baking.
Step 7
Scoop the dough into 1 1/2 tablespoon-sized balls and place them in the air fryer basket, leaving at least 2 inches between each cookie to allow for spreading. Overcrowding will cause uneven baking and misshapen cookies.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dough balls are evenly spaced and not touching.
Step 8
Bake the cookies in the air fryer at 350°F for 8-10 minutes. Check around 8 minutes to avoid overbaking. The cookies should look set but still soft in the center; they will firm up as they cool. If you bake too long, they’ll lose that chewy texture.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Edges are lightly golden, and centers look slightly underbaked but not raw.
Step 9
Remove the cookies carefully and let them cool on a wire rack for at least 10 minutes. This resting time allows the cookies to finish setting and develop their final texture. Resist the urge to eat them right away – fresh out of the air fryer, they’re fragile and can fall apart.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Cookies are firm enough to lift without breaking but still soft inside.

Variations

Oatmeal Chocolate Chip Cookies

Swap 1 cup of flour for old-fashioned oats for a chewy, hearty twist. The oats add texture and a nutty flavor that pairs beautifully with chocolate chips.

Note: Oatmeal dough may spread a bit differently; chill dough slightly longer to maintain shape.

Peanut Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

Add 1/2 cup creamy peanut butter to the wet ingredients for a rich, nutty flavor. This variation is perfect if you love the classic combo of peanut butter and chocolate.

Note: Peanut butter makes the dough denser; reduce butter by 2 tablespoons to balance moisture.

Double Chocolate Cookies

Add 1/4 cup cocoa powder to the dry ingredients and use white chocolate chips instead of semi-sweet for a decadent, chocolatey cookie with a twist.

Note: Cocoa powder absorbs moisture; add a tablespoon of milk if dough feels too dry.

Walnut Chocolate Chip Cookies

Fold in 1 cup chopped walnuts along with chocolate chips for added crunch and a slightly earthy flavor that complements the sweet dough.

Note: Chop nuts evenly to ensure consistent texture and prevent large chunks from disrupting baking.

Vegan Air Fryer Cookies

Use vegan butter and a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water) in place of eggs. These cookies come out just as chewy and delicious without dairy or eggs.

Note: Flax eggs require a few minutes to thicken before mixing; dough may be slightly softer and benefit from chilling.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature ingredients for even mixing.
Room temperature butter and eggs blend more easily with sugars and dry ingredients, resulting in a smooth dough that traps air well. Cold ingredients can cause uneven mixing and dense cookies.
Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket.
Cookies need space to spread and for hot air to circulate evenly. Overcrowding leads to uneven cooking and soggy spots where cookies touch.
Chill the dough briefly if it’s too soft.
If your dough is too sticky, chilling it for 15-20 minutes firms it up, making it easier to scoop and helping the cookies hold their shape during baking.
Check cookies early to prevent overbaking.
Air fryers can vary in heat intensity. Starting to check a minute or two before the suggested time prevents dry, hard cookies and preserves chewiness.
Use parchment paper or a silicone mat in the air fryer basket.
This prevents sticking and makes cleanup easier. Just make sure to cut the paper to fit and avoid blocking air vents.
Let cookies cool completely before removing from basket.
Cookies are fragile right out of the air fryer and can break easily. Cooling lets them firm up and develop the right texture.
Experiment with mix-ins like nuts or different chocolate chips.
Adding variety keeps the recipe fresh and lets you customize flavors. Just keep the total mix-in amount similar to maintain dough consistency.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the dough

Why it happens Many cooks think mixing longer makes dough better, but overmixing develops gluten too much, leading to tough, cakey cookies.

Fix Mix just until ingredients are combined and no dry flour remains. Stop as soon as the dough looks uniform to keep cookies tender.

Using melted butter instead of softened

Why it happens Some assume melted butter speeds mixing, but it prevents proper creaming, reducing air incorporation that makes cookies fluffy.

Fix Use butter softened to room temperature—soft enough to press but not oily. Cream with sugars for several minutes before adding eggs.

Placing dough balls too close together

Why it happens In a hurry, it’s tempting to fit more dough in, but this causes cookies to merge and bake unevenly.

Fix Space dough balls at least 2 inches apart. Bake in batches if needed to ensure even airflow and perfect shape.

Not preheating the air fryer

Why it happens Skipping preheat can cause uneven cooking and poor cookie spread since the initial heat jump is missing.

Fix Always preheat the air fryer to 350°F for a few minutes before baking to ensure consistent temperature.

Removing cookies too soon

Why it happens Impatience leads to fragile cookies that break apart when moved from basket.

Fix Allow cookies to cool in the basket for at least 10 minutes before transferring to a cooling rack.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight and heat to maintain softness. If they start to harden, a quick zap in the microwave for 10 seconds can soften them.

Refrigerator

Cookies can be stored in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. They may firm up when cold, so bring to room temperature or warm gently before serving to restore chewiness.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe bag or container for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for about an hour or warm in a low oven for a few minutes.

Reheating

Reheat cookies in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 10-15 seconds with a damp paper towel to keep them moist. Avoid overheating or they’ll become dry and crumbly.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the dough 1-2 days ahead and keep it tightly wrapped in the fridge. When ready to bake, let it warm slightly for easier scooping. This makes last-minute cookie baking super quick and hassle-free.

FAQ

Can I use frozen cookie dough in the air fryer?
Yes! You can bake frozen dough balls directly in the air fryer. Just add 2-3 extra minutes to the baking time and check frequently to avoid overbaking. This is a great way to enjoy fresh cookies anytime.
What if my cookies come out too hard or dry?
Hard cookies usually mean they were baked too long or at too high a temperature. Try reducing bake time by a minute or two and check early. Also, ensure you’re using the right amount of butter and sugars to keep them moist.
Can I use gluten-free flour instead of all-purpose?
You can substitute gluten-free flour blends, but results may vary. Use a blend that includes xanthan gum for structure. The cookies might be slightly more crumbly but still tasty.
How do I prevent cookies from sticking to the air fryer basket?
Line the basket with parchment paper or a silicone mat cut to size. Avoid covering the entire basket to allow air flow. Lightly greasing the basket also helps prevent sticking.
Can I make larger cookies in the air fryer?
Larger cookies will need longer bake times and may not cook evenly. It's best to keep dough balls small for uniform baking and texture. If you want bigger cookies, bake in batches or adjust time carefully.
Why are my cookies not spreading enough?
If cookies don’t spread, the dough may be too cold or stiff. Let dough soften slightly before baking. Also, check your baking soda's freshness since old leavening agents affect spread.
Is it okay to add nuts or other mix-ins?
Absolutely! Add-ins like nuts, dried fruit, or different chocolate chips work great. Just keep the total volume similar so the dough consistency remains balanced.
How do I store leftover cookies to keep them fresh?
Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for a couple of days. For longer storage, refrigerate or freeze. Adding a slice of bread in the container helps keep them soft.
