Classic Southern Pickled Cucumbers and Onions in Vinegar

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 15, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This tangy cucumber and onion salad is a Southern staple with a perfect crisp bite. The secret? Just the right soak time in vinegar to keep it fresh and zingy. Ideal for summer meals or casual gatherings.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this Southern classic during a summer family reunion years ago, and honestly, it stole my heart with its simplicity and bright flavor. What makes this recipe stand out for me is the balance between the tangy vinegar and the fresh crunch of the cucumbers and onions. I’ve tried versions that soak the veggies too long, making them limp, but the trick I learned is to soak just long enough to get that zing without losing the crunch. The sugar and salt create a perfect brine that enhances the natural flavors without overpowering them. It’s refreshingly light, which makes it a great side for heavy summer dishes or barbecues. Plus, it’s super easy to whip up ahead of time, and the flavors only get better after a few hours chilling in the fridge. When you serve it, expect a crisp, tangy bite with subtle sweetness and just a hint of spice if you add the pepper flakes. It’s one of those recipes I keep coming back to, especially when I want to impress friends with something homemade but fuss-free. I hope you enjoy making and sharing this as much as I do!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, serves 6

  • 3 medium cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 large white onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 cup white vinegar
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black peppercorns (optional)
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing the cucumbers and onions. Use a sharp knife or a mandoline to get even slices about 1/8 inch thick. This ensures they soak evenly and maintain a nice texture. Avoid slicing too thick or the veggies won’t absorb the flavors as well. Place the slices in a large bowl to prepare for the brine.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly thin, translucent slices with no jagged edges
Step 2
In a saucepan, combine the white vinegar, water, sugar, salt, and optional peppercorns and red pepper flakes. Heat over medium until the sugar and salt dissolve completely, stirring occasionally. This hot brine helps infuse the cucumbers and onions with flavor quickly. Don’t boil vigorously—just a gentle simmer is enough.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Clear liquid with fully dissolved sugar and salt, simmering gently
Step 3
Pour the warm brine over the sliced cucumbers and onions in the bowl. Use a spoon to press the veggies down slightly so they’re fully submerged. This step is critical because full contact with the brine guarantees even pickling. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap or transfer everything to a clean jar with a lid.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Vegetables fully immersed in liquid without floating
Step 4
Let the mixture sit at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow the flavors to meld. This soaking time is the sweet spot—too short and the veggies won’t absorb enough tang, too long and they’ll get soggy. You can stir once or twice during this period to redistribute the brine.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Veggies slightly softened but still crisp and vibrant in color
Step 5
After soaking, transfer the pickles to the refrigerator to chill for at least 2 hours before serving. Cooling helps the flavors deepen and the texture stay crisp. If you can wait overnight, even better! The salad tastes best cold and fresh.
⏱ Time: 120 minutes or overnight
✓ Doneness: Chilled, tangy cucumbers and onions with a firm crunch
Step 6
Before serving, give the pickled cucumbers and onions a gentle stir to mix any settled spices. Taste and adjust seasoning if needed—sometimes a pinch more salt or sugar can balance the flavor just right. Serve chilled as a refreshing side with grilled meats or sandwiches.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced tanginess with a fresh, crisp texture
Step 7
If you want a spicier kick, add a few fresh sliced jalapeños or extra red pepper flakes when making the brine. This adds a nice heat contrast to the vinegar’s acidity. Just be cautious not to overpower the delicate cucumber flavor.
⏱ Time: Optional, 5 minutes prep
✓ Doneness: Slight spicy aroma and taste without masking the veggies
Step 8
For an extra crunch, try adding a handful of thinly sliced radishes or bell peppers along with the cucumbers and onions. They soak up the brine similarly and add color and texture variety to the salad.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes prep
✓ Doneness: Bright, crunchy slices mixed evenly with the cucumbers and onions
Step 9
Always use a clean container for pickling and store leftovers in the fridge. This prevents unwanted bacteria growth and keeps your pickles fresh longer. Enjoy within a week for best texture and flavor.
⏱ Time: Ongoing storage care
✓ Doneness: Fresh, crisp pickles without off smells or sliminess

Variations

Spicy Jalapeño Pickled Cucumbers and Onions

Add sliced fresh jalapeños or increase red pepper flakes for a spicy twist. This variation adds a lively heat that plays beautifully against the tangy vinegar and sweet sugar. Great for those who like a little kick in their sides.

Note: Be mindful of the spice level—start small and taste as you go. The heat intensifies over time, so adjust soaking accordingly.

Sweet and Tangy Bread and Butter Style

Increase sugar slightly and add mustard seeds and celery seeds to create a classic bread-and-butter pickle flavor. This gives the salad a sweeter, more complex profile while keeping the crunch intact.

Note: Mustard and celery seeds release flavor best when added to hot brine; be sure to simmer briefly before pouring.

Herbed Pickled Cucumbers and Onions

Add fresh dill, thyme, or rosemary sprigs to the brine for an herbal aroma and flavor. This variation pairs wonderfully with grilled fish or chicken for a fresh, garden-inspired taste.

Note: Use fresh herbs and add them after heating the brine to preserve their bright flavors.

Quick Refrigerator Pickled Cucumbers Only

Omit the onions and make a quick cucumber-only pickle with the same brine. It’s a faster, simpler side that highlights the cucumber’s fresh crunch and tangy bite.

Note: Without onions, soaking time can be slightly reduced as cucumbers pickle faster alone.

Asian-Inspired Pickled Cucumbers and Onions

Swap white vinegar for rice vinegar and add a splash of soy sauce and a pinch of sesame seeds for an Asian flavor profile. This twist adds umami and nuttiness to the crisp pickles.

Note: Reduce salt slightly due to soy sauce’s saltiness, and add ingredients after heating the brine to preserve delicate flavors.

Pro Tips

Slice the veggies as thin and evenly as possible for best flavor absorption.
Uniform thin slices soak up the brine evenly, ensuring consistent tang and texture throughout. Thick or uneven slices can lead to some pieces being under-flavored or too soft.
Use a clean glass jar or non-reactive container for soaking and storage.
Metal or dirty containers can react with the vinegar or harbor bacteria, affecting flavor and safety. Glass or food-safe plastic keeps the pickles fresh and uncontaminated.
Don’t skip the rest time at room temperature before refrigerating.
This allows the veggies to start absorbing the brine gently, which enhances flavor and texture. Putting them directly in the fridge slows this process and can result in a less vibrant taste.
Adjust sugar and salt amounts according to your taste preferences.
Some people prefer a sweeter or saltier pickle. Tasting the brine before pouring lets you customize the balance to your liking without compromising the crisp texture.
Add spices like peppercorns or red pepper flakes for extra aroma and heat.
These ingredients infuse the brine with subtle complexity that makes the pickles more interesting. They’re optional but elevate the flavor profile nicely.
Keep pickled veggies refrigerated and consume within a week.
The quick-pickling process doesn’t preserve indefinitely like canned pickles. Refrigeration slows spoilage and maintains crunch, but long storage will degrade texture and flavor.
Use a mandoline slicer if you want perfectly thin, uniform slices quickly.
A mandoline ensures consistent thickness, improving soak time and presentation. It’s faster and safer than trying to slice very thinly by hand.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Soaking the veggies too long and ending with soggy pickles

Why it happens Many cooks assume longer soaking means more flavor, but cucumbers and onions lose their crispness if left too long in vinegar.

Fix Stick to the recommended soaking time of about 30 minutes at room temperature. If you prefer stronger flavor, chill overnight but don’t extend the initial soak. This keeps the perfect crunch.

Using thick, uneven slices that don’t pickle evenly

Why it happens Without uniform slicing, some pieces absorb too much brine while others stay bland or tough.

Fix Slice as evenly and thinly as possible, ideally 1/8 inch thick. A mandoline slicer helps achieve this consistency for even pickling.

Skipping heating the brine and just mixing ingredients cold

Why it happens Some skip this thinking it saves time, but cold brine dissolves sugar and salt poorly and slows flavor infusion.

Fix Heat the vinegar, water, sugar, and salt until dissolved and warm before pouring over veggies. This step is key for balanced flavor and texture.

Using iodized or table salt which causes cloudy brine

Why it happens Table salt often contains additives that react with vinegar and cloud the liquid, affecting appearance and sometimes taste.

Fix Use kosher or pickling salt without additives to keep the brine clear and flavor pure.

Not fully submerging the cucumbers and onions in brine

Why it happens Veggies exposed to air won’t pickle evenly and can spoil faster.

Fix Press slices down with a plate or use a jar with a tight lid to keep everything submerged under the brine.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Pickled cucumbers and onions can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered, but it’s best to refrigerate promptly to maintain crispness and prevent spoilage.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to one week. The pickles will stay crisp initially but may soften slightly after several days. Serve cold or at room temperature.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as it breaks down the cucumbers’ texture, resulting in mushy pickles upon thawing.

Reheating

No reheating is needed or recommended; these pickles are best served chilled or at room temperature to preserve their refreshing crunch.

Make Ahead

You can slice the cucumbers and onions and prepare the brine up to 1 day ahead. Combine and soak just before serving to keep the veggies crisp and fresh.

FAQ

Can I use other types of vinegar for this recipe?
Yes, you can substitute white vinegar with apple cider vinegar or rice vinegar, but keep in mind these will alter the flavor profile slightly. Apple cider vinegar adds a fruity note, while rice vinegar is milder and sweeter. Always use vinegar with at least 5% acidity for safe pickling.
How long can I store these pickled cucumbers and onions?
When stored properly in the fridge in an airtight container, they last about one week. Beyond that, the texture starts to soften and the flavor can become overly sour. For best taste and crunch, consume within this timeframe.
What if I don’t have a mandoline slicer?
No worries! Use a sharp knife and slice as thinly and evenly as possible. Take your time and steady your hand. The key is consistent thickness to ensure even pickling and texture.
Can I make this recipe vegan or gluten-free?
Absolutely! The basic recipe is naturally vegan and gluten-free. Just make sure any optional ingredients like soy sauce (if used in variations) are gluten-free versions.
Why are my pickles sometimes soggy?
Soggy pickles usually result from over-soaking or using cucumbers that are too watery or old. To prevent this, slice thinly, soak for the recommended time, and always use fresh, firm cucumbers.
Can I add other vegetables to this recipe?
Yes! Radishes, bell peppers, or even thinly sliced carrots work well. Just adjust soaking time slightly as some veggies pickle faster or slower than cucumbers and onions.
Do I have to heat the brine?
Heating the brine dissolves sugar and salt quickly and helps infuse flavors faster. Cold brine works but takes much longer and may not dissolve ingredients fully, affecting taste and texture.
Is it safe to leave the pickles at room temperature overnight?
No, for food safety, after the initial 30-minute soak at room temperature, you should refrigerate the pickles. Leaving them out too long can lead to spoilage and unsafe bacteria growth.
