Creamy Watermelon Coconut Popsicles That Beat the Heat Perfectly

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 25, 2026
15 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These popsicles are a summer lifesaver—fresh watermelon meets smooth coconut milk for a creamy, refreshing bite. I love how easy they come together, perfect for backyard hangs or cooling off on hot afternoons!

Introduction

I've always loved watermelon in the summer, but I wanted to find a way to enjoy it that's a bit more special than just sliced fruit. That's when I stumbled upon the idea of blending it with coconut milk to make creamy popsicles. The first time I tried this, I was a bit skeptical — watermelon is mostly water, so I wondered if it would freeze well or just turn icy. But blending it with coconut milk gave it this dreamy, smooth texture that’s just so satisfying. Plus, adding a little lime juice brightens the flavor and balances the sweetness perfectly. I usually whip these up on a hot afternoon when my family is craving something cool but not overly sugary. They’re also perfect for kids because they’re naturally sweet and creamy without any artificial ingredients. What I love about this recipe is how simple it is — just blend and freeze — but the result feels like a fancy treat. It’s a refreshing way to beat the heat and sneak in some fruit and healthy fats. If you’ve struggled with icy popsicles before, give this one a try; the coconut milk really makes all the difference in texture and richness.

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 popsicles

  • 4 cups fresh watermelon chunks, seeds removed
  • 1 cup full-fat coconut milk
  • 2 tablespoons honey or maple syrup (optional)
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lime juice
  • Pinch of salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by cutting fresh watermelon into chunks, making sure to remove all the seeds. This step is important because seeds can get stuck in the blender and also affect the texture of your popsicles. Using ripe watermelon ensures maximum sweetness and juiciness, which means you might not even need extra sweetener. Be gentle when cutting to avoid bruising the fruit, which could alter the taste slightly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Watermelon chunks should be bright red, firm, and seedless.
Step 2
Place the watermelon chunks in a blender along with full-fat coconut milk, honey or maple syrup (if using), fresh lime juice, and a pinch of salt. The coconut milk adds creaminess and richness, while the lime juice brightens the flavor and the salt enhances the sweetness. Blend everything until completely smooth. If you rush the blending, you might end up with a grainy texture or bits of watermelon pulp, so blend until silky.
⏱ Time: 3-5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture should be smooth, creamy, and uniform in color.
Step 3
Taste the blended mixture and adjust sweetness or lime juice if needed. This is your chance to balance the flavors before freezing. If it tastes flat, a little more lime or salt can really lift it. If it’s not sweet enough, add a bit more honey or maple syrup. Remember, flavors will dull slightly once frozen, so err on the side of a little extra zing.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced sweet-tart flavor that tastes lively and fresh.
Step 4
Pour the mixture evenly into popsicle molds, leaving a little space at the top for expansion as they freeze. Insert sticks and place the molds on a flat surface in the freezer. It’s important to freeze on a level surface to keep the popsicles shaped nicely. Avoid opening the freezer repeatedly during freezing to prevent ice crystals from forming.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes to fill molds
✓ Doneness: Molds filled without spilling and sticks inserted firmly.
Step 5
Freeze the popsicles for at least 6 hours, preferably overnight. This slow freezing allows the creamy texture to set properly. If you try to eat them too soon, they will be too soft and watery. Patience here is key to get that perfect creamy consistency.
⏱ Time: 6+ hours
✓ Doneness: Popsicles are solid and firm to the touch.
Step 6
When ready to serve, run the popsicle molds under warm water for a few seconds. This loosens them from the mold without melting the popsicle. Avoid using hot water as it can cause melting and dripping. Gently pull the popsicles out and enjoy immediately for the best texture and flavor.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Popsicles release easily from molds without sticking.
Step 7
If you want to add a fun twist, sprinkle finely chopped fresh mint or a few small chunks of watermelon into the molds before pouring the mixture. This adds texture and a pop of flavor. Just make sure the pieces aren’t too large or the popsicles won’t unmold cleanly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly distributed add-ins that don’t interfere with freezing.
Step 8
For an adult version, try adding a splash of rum or tequila to the mixture before freezing. Keep the alcohol content low so it still freezes properly. The alcohol adds a subtle kick and makes these popsicles perfect for summer parties.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Alcohol mixed well without separation or curdling.
Step 9
Store any leftover popsicles in an airtight container or freezer bag to prevent freezer burn and absorption of other odors. Make sure they’re fully frozen before transferring to storage. When stored properly, they’ll keep their creamy texture and fresh taste for up to 2 weeks.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Popsicles remain firm and flavorful after storage.

Variations

Mint-Infused Watermelon Popsicles

Add fresh mint leaves to the blender or sprinkle chopped mint into the molds before freezing. The mint adds a refreshing herbal note that pairs beautifully with watermelon and coconut. Expect a slightly more complex flavor and a subtle cooling sensation.

Note: Chop mint finely to avoid large bits. Freeze as usual; mint won’t affect texture but adds a fresh aroma.

Spicy Watermelon Coconut Popsicles

For a fun twist, add a pinch of cayenne pepper or chopped fresh chili to the mixture before freezing. This gives the popsicles a subtle spicy kick that contrasts nicely with the sweet and creamy base. Great for adventurous eaters!

Note: Start with a small amount of spice and adjust to taste. Too much can overpower the delicate watermelon flavor.

Berry Watermelon Coconut Popsicles

Blend in a handful of fresh or frozen berries like strawberries or raspberries for added color and flavor complexity. Berries add antioxidants and a slight tartness, making these popsicles even more refreshing and nutritious.

Note: If using frozen berries, thaw slightly before blending for smoother texture.

Alcohol-Infused Adult Popsicles

Add a splash of rum, tequila, or vodka to the mixture before freezing for a boozy summer treat. Keep alcohol content low (about 1-2 tablespoons) to ensure popsicles still freeze solid but have a pleasant kick.

Note: Too much alcohol prevents freezing. Adjust quantity carefully and freeze overnight.

Vegan Sweetener-Free Popsicles

Omit the honey or maple syrup entirely for a naturally sweetened vegan option. Using extra ripe watermelon and good coconut milk ensures enough sweetness. Perfect for those avoiding added sugars.

Note: Taste mixture before freezing to confirm sweetness; adjust lime juice or salt as needed.

Pro Tips

Use ripe watermelon for natural sweetness.
Ripe watermelon ensures the popsicles are naturally sweet and juicy, which means you can reduce or skip added sugars. Less added sugar makes for a healthier treat and a cleaner flavor. If your watermelon isn’t very sweet, add a little honey or maple syrup to boost the taste without overpowering the natural fruit flavor.
Blend until completely smooth for best texture.
Blending thoroughly breaks down the watermelon fibers and fully incorporates the coconut milk, preventing icy chunks. A smooth blend means the fat and water molecules are evenly distributed, which helps achieve that creamy mouthfeel instead of a grainy or icy texture.
Freeze popsicles on a flat surface to keep shape.
Freezing molds on a level surface prevents the mixture from pooling on one side or creating uneven shapes. This ensures popsicles set uniformly and unmold easily. Uneven freezing can cause cracks or weak spots that break when you try to remove them.
Add lime juice to brighten the flavor.
Acidic ingredients like lime juice balance the sweetness and enhance the overall flavor profile. It prevents the popsicles from tasting flat or overly sweet. The acidity also helps bring out the freshness of the watermelon and coconut milk, making the popsicles taste more vibrant.
Don’t rush the freezing process.
Slow, consistent freezing helps prevent large ice crystals from forming, which can ruin the creamy texture. If you try to eat the popsicles too soon, they will be watery and too soft. Leaving them to freeze overnight ensures the mixture sets perfectly and maintains its creamy consistency.
Run molds under warm water to unmold easily.
Warm water loosens the popsicles from the mold without melting them. This prevents the popsicles from breaking or sticking, which can happen if you try to pull them out cold. Avoid hot water as it melts the edges and causes drips.
Use full-fat coconut milk for richness.
The fat in full-fat coconut milk is what gives these popsicles their creamy texture. Using light or diluted coconut milk results in icy, less satisfying popsicles. The fat also helps stabilize the mixture during freezing and improves mouthfeel.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using watery or underripe watermelon

Why it happens Sometimes people grab any watermelon without checking ripeness, thinking it’ll be fine. Underripe or watery watermelon lacks sweetness and flavor, which leads to bland popsicles.

Fix Always pick watermelon that’s heavy for its size and has a deep red flesh. Taste a small piece before blending. If it’s bland, add a bit more sweetener or try a different melon. This ensures your popsicles are flavorful and naturally sweet.

Not blending long enough

Why it happens Rushing the blending process can leave chunks of fruit or unmixed coconut milk, causing grainy texture and uneven freezing.

Fix Blend until the mixture is completely smooth and uniform in color. Use a high-speed blender if possible. This improves texture and prevents icy bits from forming.

Skipping the lime juice or salt

Why it happens Some might think these ingredients aren’t necessary in a sweet recipe, but they’re key for flavor balance.

Fix Don’t skip the lime juice and pinch of salt. They brighten and enhance the overall flavor, preventing the popsicles from tasting flat or dull.

Filling popsicle molds too full

Why it happens Water expands when it freezes, so filling molds to the brim can cause spills or broken popsicles.

Fix Leave about 1/2 inch of space at the top of each mold to allow for expansion. This helps the popsicles freeze evenly and unmold easily.

Eating popsicles too soon

Why it happens Impatience leads to eating popsicles before they’re fully frozen, resulting in watery texture and less flavor.

Fix Freeze for at least 6 hours, preferably overnight. This ensures a firm, creamy texture and full flavor development.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These popsicles should not be left at room temperature for more than 15 minutes as they will quickly melt. Keep them covered in the freezer to avoid absorbing odors or freezer burn.

Refrigerator

Popsicles don’t store well in the refrigerator once frozen; they’ll melt. Only store in the fridge if thawing briefly before serving. Best served frozen.

Freezer

Store popsicles in an airtight container or freezer-safe bag for up to 2 weeks. Wrap molds or popsicles tightly to prevent freezer burn and maintain flavor. Avoid stacking heavy items on top to keep shape intact.

Reheating

No reheating needed—serve straight from the freezer. To unmold easily, run molds under warm water for a few seconds before removing popsicles.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the blended mixture 1-2 days ahead and keep it covered in the refrigerator. Stir well before pouring into molds and freezing. This saves time on the day you want to serve them.

FAQ

Can I use light coconut milk instead of full-fat?
Light coconut milk is much thinner and contains less fat, which means your popsicles will be icy instead of creamy. For the best texture and richness, full-fat coconut milk is recommended. If you only have light coconut milk, consider adding a bit of coconut cream or another fat source.
What if I don’t have popsicle molds?
No popsicle molds? No problem! You can use small paper cups or ice cube trays and insert wooden sticks or spoons once the mixture starts to freeze. Just make sure to cover the containers to avoid freezer odors.
Can I make these popsicles without added sweetener?
Absolutely! If your watermelon is very ripe and sweet, you might not need extra sugar. Taste the mixture before freezing and adjust lime juice or salt to balance flavors. The popsicles will still be delicious and naturally sweet.
How long do these popsicles last in the freezer?
Stored properly in an airtight container, these popsicles keep well for up to 2 weeks. Beyond that, they may develop freezer burn or lose flavor and texture quality.
Can I add other fruits to the recipe?
Yes! Berries, mango, or pineapple can be blended in for different flavors. If using frozen fruits, thaw slightly before blending for a smooth texture. Adjust sweeteners as needed based on fruit sweetness.
Why do my popsicles sometimes turn icy?
Icy popsicles usually result from too little fat or sugar, or from rushing the freezing process. Fat and sugar lower the freezing point and help prevent large ice crystals. Also, freezing slowly helps maintain creamy texture.
Can I make these popsicles vegan?
Yes, this recipe is naturally vegan if you use maple syrup or omit the sweetener. Just ensure your coconut milk is plant-based, which it always is. It’s a great dairy-free summer treat!
What’s the best way to unmold popsicles without breaking?
Run the molds under warm water for 10-20 seconds to loosen the popsicles. Don’t use hot water as it melts the edges. Gently pull on the sticks while holding the mold to release the popsicles cleanly.
