Juicy Peach Crisp with a Perfectly Crunchy Oat Topping

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 17, 2026
15 min Prep Time
40 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This peach crisp is pure comfort—sweet, juicy peaches with a cinnamon-spiced oat topping that’s just the right crunch. I love making it for late summer dinners or chilly fall nights when you want something warm and cozy.

Introduction

I first made this peach crisp a few summers ago when I had a basket of peaches that were just begging to be baked. It quickly became a favorite in my family because it’s so simple yet incredibly satisfying. What I love most is how the topping gets perfectly crunchy thanks to the oats and brown sugar, while the peaches inside turn tender and juicy. I’ve tried a few versions over the years, and adding a pinch of cinnamon in the crumble really takes the flavor up a notch without overpowering the fruit. The trick I learned is to toss the peaches with lemon juice and vanilla before baking—it brightens their natural sweetness and keeps them from turning mushy. When it’s fresh out of the oven, you get this wonderful contrast between the soft, syrupy peaches and that crunchy, buttery topping. It’s my go-to dessert for warm summer evenings but also makes a cozy treat on cooler fall nights. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes that feels like a hug in a bowl, and I hope you’ll find it as comforting as I do.

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 servings

  • 6 cups fresh peaches, peeled and sliced
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup old-fashioned oats
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C). While it’s warming up, peel and slice your peaches into even slices about 1/2 inch thick. This ensures they cook evenly without turning into mush. Toss the peaches gently in a large bowl with the granulated sugar, lemon juice, and vanilla extract. The sugar sweetens them, while the lemon juice adds brightness and helps preserve their color during baking. Mixing everything well is key so that each slice is coated evenly, which results in consistent flavor and texture in the finished dish.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches are evenly coated and look glossy but not mushy
Step 2
In a separate bowl, combine the oats, flour, brown sugar, cinnamon, and salt. Stir these dry ingredients together until well mixed. Then pour in the melted butter and use a fork or your fingers to mix until the mixture clumps together into coarse crumbs. This crumbly texture is what will bake into a crunchy topping that contrasts beautifully with the soft peaches. Don’t overmix here; you want some larger oat clusters to stay intact for texture. If you rush this step or add too much butter, the topping can become greasy or too dense.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Crumb topping holds together loosely with visible oat clusters
Step 3
Transfer the peach mixture into a greased 9x13-inch baking dish, spreading it out evenly. Then sprinkle the oat crumble topping evenly over the peaches, covering the fruit completely. This even layering is important because it ensures every bite has that perfect balance of juicy peaches and crunchy topping. If the topping is uneven, some parts might burn while others stay soft. Smooth the topping lightly with your hands or a spoon to help it bake uniformly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peach layer is fully covered with crumb mixture, smooth and even
Step 4
Place the baking dish in the preheated oven and bake for about 35-40 minutes. You’re looking for the topping to turn golden brown and the peach juices to bubble up around the edges. This bubbling indicates the fruit is releasing its natural sugars and the filling is thickening nicely. If you open the oven too often, the temperature drops and can affect the texture, so try to keep the door closed until close to the end of baking.
⏱ Time: 35-40 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping is golden and crisp, peach filling bubbly and thickened
Step 5
Once baked, remove the crisp from the oven and let it cool for at least 15 minutes before serving. Cooling helps the filling set a bit so it’s not too runny when you scoop it out. It also allows the flavors to meld and the topping to crisp up further as it cools. If you try to serve it piping hot, the filling might be too liquid and the topping less crunchy. Patience here really pays off!
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling thickened, topping crisp but not burnt
Step 6
Serve your peach crisp warm, ideally with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream. The cold creaminess pairs perfectly with the warm, spiced peaches and crunchy topping, creating a delightful contrast of textures and temperatures. If you want to make it a bit fancier, sprinkle a pinch of extra cinnamon on top just before serving. This final touch enhances the aroma and flavor beautifully.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Crisp served warm with melting ice cream or cream on top
Step 7
If you have leftovers, cover the crisp tightly with plastic wrap or foil and store it in the fridge. To reheat, warm it in the oven at 325°F (160°C) for 10-15 minutes to restore crispiness. Avoid microwaving if possible, as it can make the topping soggy. Reheating in the oven brings back that fresh-baked texture and flavor much better.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes prep + reheating time
✓ Doneness: Crisp warmed through with topping crunchy again
Step 8
Want to make this gluten-free? Simply swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend and ensure your oats are certified gluten-free. The texture might be slightly different but still delicious. Just mix the topping gently to keep some oat clusters intact for crunch.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping crumbly and golden, no difference in baking time
Step 9
For an extra flavor boost, try adding a handful of chopped nuts like pecans or walnuts to the crumble topping. They toast up nicely in the oven and add a lovely nutty crunch. Mix them in with the oats and other dry ingredients before adding the butter. This little addition makes the crisp feel a bit more special without much extra effort.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Nuts toasted golden and crunchy in the topping

Variations

Mixed Berry Peach Crisp

Add 1 cup of fresh or frozen berries (blueberries, raspberries) to the peaches for a tangy twist. The berries add color and a slight tartness that complements the sweet peaches and cinnamon topping.

Note: If using frozen berries, thaw and drain excess liquid to avoid a soggy filling.

Vegan Peach Crisp

Replace butter with coconut oil or vegan butter and use maple syrup instead of brown sugar for a plant-based version. The coconut oil adds a subtle tropical note that pairs well with peaches.

Note: Use firm coconut oil to mimic butter’s texture; slightly adjust baking time as needed.

Nutty Crunch Peach Crisp

Stir 1/2 cup chopped pecans or walnuts into the oat topping for extra crunch and a nutty flavor that complements the cinnamon and peaches beautifully.

Note: Toast nuts lightly before mixing into topping for enhanced flavor.

Spiced Autumn Peach Crisp

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg and 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves to the crumble for a warm, cozy spice blend perfect for fall gatherings.

Note: Adjust spice amounts carefully; these are potent and can overpower if overused.

Gluten-Free Peach Crisp

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free flour blend and ensure oats are certified gluten-free. The topping still crisps nicely and the peaches remain tender and juicy.

Note: Watch baking time as gluten-free flours sometimes brown faster.

Pro Tips

Toss peaches with lemon juice right after slicing.
The lemon juice prevents the peaches from oxidizing and turning brown, keeping the filling bright and appetizing. It also adds a subtle tartness that balances the sweetness and enhances the overall flavor.
Don’t overmix the crumble topping once you add the butter.
Overmixing breaks down the oats and flour too much, resulting in a dense or greasy topping. Mixing just until clumps form preserves the texture that crisps up beautifully during baking.
Let the crisp cool for at least 15 minutes before serving.
Cooling allows the juices to thicken and the topping to set, preventing a soggy or runny dessert. This step makes scooping easier and improves the eating experience.
Use old-fashioned rolled oats for the topping.
Rolled oats provide the perfect chewy texture and hold their shape when baked. Instant oats tend to get mushy, and steel-cut oats are too hard and require longer cooking.
Add a pinch of salt to the crumble topping.
Salt enhances the sweetness and balances flavors, making the topping more complex and satisfying. Even a small amount makes a noticeable difference in taste.
Bake in a glass or ceramic dish for even heat distribution.
These materials retain heat well and help the topping brown evenly without burning. Metal pans can sometimes cook too quickly on the edges.
Serve with vanilla ice cream or whipped cream.
The cold creaminess contrasts perfectly with the warm, spiced peaches and crunchy topping, elevating the dessert and adding another layer of flavor and texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using underripe or overripe peaches

Why it happens Sometimes cooks grab peaches that are too firm and tart or too mushy, hoping the baking will fix it.

Fix Choose peaches that are ripe but still firm to the touch. If peaches are underripe, add a little extra sugar; if overripe, use them quickly and handle gently to avoid mushy filling.

Skipping the lemon juice toss

Why it happens It’s easy to forget this small step, thinking it’s unnecessary.

Fix Always toss sliced peaches with lemon juice to prevent browning and add brightness. It’s a simple step that improves color and flavor dramatically.

Overmixing the crumble topping

Why it happens In a rush, some mix too vigorously trying to combine everything thoroughly.

Fix Mix just until the butter coats the dry ingredients and the mixture clumps. Overmixing breaks down oats and ruins texture.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Some try to speed up baking by turning the oven hotter.

Fix Stick to 350°F; higher temps burn the topping before the peaches cook through. Slow, even baking produces the best texture and flavor.

Serving immediately out of the oven

Why it happens It’s tempting to dig in while still hot.

Fix Let the crisp cool at least 15 minutes so the filling thickens and topping crisps up. This makes serving cleaner and tastier.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The peach crisp can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Cover it with foil or plastic wrap to prevent drying out or contamination.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The topping may soften slightly, but reheating in the oven restores some crunch. Cold crisp can also be served as a chilled dessert.

Freezer

Freeze the peach crisp for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly with plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat in a preheated oven at 325°F (160°C) for 10-15 minutes until warmed through. Add a splash of water or juice if filling seems dry. Avoid microwaving to keep topping crisp.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the peach mixture and crumble topping separately 1-2 days ahead, storing each in the fridge. Assemble and bake fresh on the day you plan to serve to keep the topping crunchy.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches instead of fresh?
Yes, but thaw and drain frozen peaches well before using to avoid excess water making the crisp soggy. You may also want to reduce added sugar slightly since frozen peaches can be sweeter.
How do I prevent the topping from getting soggy?
Make sure the topping is evenly spread and contains enough butter to crisp up. Avoid stirring the crisp once baked and let it cool to allow the topping to set. Reheating in the oven also helps maintain crunch.
Can I make this peach crisp vegan?
Absolutely! Replace butter with coconut oil or vegan butter alternatives and use maple syrup or coconut sugar instead of brown sugar. The texture and flavor remain delicious.
What if I don’t have old-fashioned oats?
Quick oats can be used but will produce a softer topping. Steel-cut oats aren’t recommended as they won’t soften properly during baking and can be tough.
How long can I store leftovers?
Store leftovers covered in the fridge for up to 4 days. The crisp topping may soften but reheating in the oven restores some crunch.
Can I add nuts to the topping?
Yes! Chopped pecans or walnuts add great flavor and crunch. Toast them lightly before mixing into the topping for the best taste.
What’s the best way to peel peaches quickly?
Score a small 'X' on the bottom of each peach and blanch in boiling water for 30 seconds, then plunge into ice water. The skins will loosen and peel off easily.
Can I make this gluten-free?
Yes, use a gluten-free flour blend and certified gluten-free oats. Baking time stays about the same, but keep an eye on browning.
