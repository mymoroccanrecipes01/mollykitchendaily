Easy One-Dish Air Fryer Chicken with Crispy Veggies

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on July 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This one-dish air fryer chicken is a total game changer—crispy on the outside, juicy inside, with tender veggies cooked right alongside. I love how quick it comes together, perfect for busy weeknights when you want dinner done fast but tasty.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this one-dish air fryer chicken recipe when I was craving a simple dinner that didn’t require standing over the stove for ages. I remember trying to roast chicken and veggies together in the oven, but often ended up with unevenly cooked food or mushy veggies. Using the air fryer changed the game for me—everything cooks evenly with a crispy finish, and cleanup is a breeze. What I love about this version is the combo of bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs for juicy meat and crispy skin, paired with a mix of tender baby potatoes, carrots, and broccoli that roast beautifully in the hot air. The secret is patting the chicken dry and seasoning it well, plus adding lemon slices for a fresh zing that brightens the whole dish. It’s a recipe that feels comforting but isn’t heavy, perfect for any night of the week. The finished meal looks vibrant with golden chicken and colorful veggies, making it as pleasing to the eye as it is to the palate. Honestly, I’ve made this a dozen times now, and it never disappoints—quick, tasty, and fuss-free.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs, patted dry
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • 2 cups baby potatoes, halved
  • 1 cup baby carrots
  • 1 cup broccoli florets
  • 1 tablespoon fresh rosemary, chopped
  • 1 lemon, sliced thin

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by patting the chicken thighs dry with paper towels—this step is crucial because dry skin crisps up better in the air fryer. Then, rub the olive oil all over the chicken pieces and season generously with smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. The oil helps the spices stick and promotes even browning. Avoid skipping the paprika as it adds a smoky depth that makes the chicken irresistible.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken skin looks dry before cooking and feels slightly tacky after seasoning
Step 2
Arrange the baby potatoes, carrots, and broccoli in a bowl. Toss them with a bit of olive oil, salt, pepper, and the chopped rosemary. The olive oil helps the veggies roast evenly and develop a nice caramelized surface in the air fryer. Don’t overcrowd the basket later to ensure proper air circulation for crispiness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Vegetables are evenly coated with oil and herbs, no clumps
Step 3
Place the seasoned chicken thighs skin-side down in the air fryer basket first. This initial positioning helps render the fat and crisps the skin better. Cook at 380°F (193°C) for 12 minutes before flipping. Resist the urge to open the basket too often, as it lowers the temperature and slows crisping.
⏱ Time: 12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken pieces are golden brown on the bottom and release easily when flipped
Step 4
Flip the chicken thighs skin-side up and add the tossed vegetables around the chicken in the basket. Add lemon slices on top for a subtle citrus aroma. Cook for another 15-18 minutes at the same temperature. The veggies will roast in the chicken drippings, soaking up flavor while crisping up nicely.
⏱ Time: 15-18 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken skin is crispy and golden; vegetables are tender and lightly browned
Step 5
Check the internal temperature of the chicken with a meat thermometer; it should read 165°F (74°C). This ensures the chicken is fully cooked but still juicy inside. If needed, cook an extra 2-3 minutes but watch closely to avoid drying out the meat.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes if needed
✓ Doneness: Thermometer reads 165°F; juices run clear when pierced
Step 6
Once cooked, let the chicken rest for 5 minutes before serving. Resting allows the juices to redistribute, keeping the meat moist. Meanwhile, you can toss the veggies gently to coat them in the lemony pan juices left in the basket for extra flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken juices no longer run clear immediately; veggies glisten with pan juices
Step 7
Serve the chicken thighs alongside the roasted veggies and lemon slices. The contrast of crispy skin, juicy meat, and tender, flavorful vegetables makes every bite satisfying. A simple green salad or crusty bread pairs well if you want to round out the meal.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dish is plated attractively and ready to enjoy
Step 8
Clean your air fryer basket promptly after it cools down to avoid stuck-on bits. A quick soak and wipe with a non-abrasive sponge will keep your air fryer in great shape for the next use.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Basket is clean and free of residue
Step 9
Store any leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge. Reheat gently in the air fryer or oven to help crisp the skin back up instead of the microwave, which can make it soggy.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Leftovers reheat evenly with crispy skin restored

Variations

Spicy Cajun Air Fryer Chicken

Swap the smoked paprika and garlic powder for Cajun seasoning and a pinch of cayenne pepper to add a spicy kick. The veggies can be switched to bell peppers and zucchini for a colorful twist. Expect a bolder, smoky heat with the same crispy texture.

Note: Adjust seasoning quantities carefully since Cajun blends can be saltier; also, cayenne can burn if cooked too long, so add it with other spices initially.

Mediterranean Style with Olives and Feta

Add pitted olives and crumbled feta cheese to the veggies before cooking. Use oregano instead of rosemary and sprinkle fresh parsley after cooking. This variation brings bright, tangy Mediterranean flavors with a creamy contrast from the cheese.

Note: Add feta towards the end or after cooking to prevent it from melting completely and losing texture.

Honey Mustard Glazed Chicken

Brush the chicken with a mixture of honey, Dijon mustard, and a splash of apple cider vinegar before air frying. This glaze caramelizes beautifully, giving a sweet and tangy crust. Serve with roasted sweet potatoes and green beans for a comforting meal.

Note: Apply glaze halfway through cooking to prevent burning from the sugars in honey.

Herb Butter Infused Chicken

Add small pats of herb butter (butter mixed with garlic and parsley) on top of the chicken before cooking. This adds richness and a fragrant herb aroma. Use fingerling potatoes and asparagus for a fresh spring feel.

Note: Butter melts quickly; add it just before starting the air fryer to maximize flavor without burning.

Vegan Air Fryer Roasted Vegetables

Skip the chicken and double up on hearty veggies like cauliflower, sweet potatoes, and Brussels sprouts. Toss with olive oil, garlic powder, smoked paprika, and fresh thyme. This makes a delicious, crispy vegan meal that still feels substantial.

Note: Cook veggies at 375°F for 20-25 minutes, shaking basket halfway to avoid burning delicate pieces.

Pro Tips

Always pat chicken dry before seasoning.
Removing moisture from the skin ensures it crisps up during air frying instead of steaming. Moist skin traps steam which prevents browning and results in soggy texture, so this simple step is vital for that coveted crispy skin.
Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket.
Proper air circulation is crucial to even cooking and crisping. Overcrowding traps moisture and heat unevenly, causing some pieces to steam and others to burn. Give each piece enough space to allow hot air to circulate freely.
Flip the chicken halfway through cooking.
Starting skin-side down helps render fat and begin browning, then flipping skin-side up finishes crisping the skin and cooks the meat evenly. Skipping the flip can lead to uneven texture and undercooked areas.
Use a meat thermometer to check doneness.
Chicken thighs should reach 165°F to be safe and juicy. Guesswork risks undercooking or drying out. A thermometer ensures perfect results every time without cutting into the meat and losing juices.
Add lemon slices during the last cooking stage.
Lemon adds a fresh citrus aroma and brightens the dish without overpowering it. Adding too early can burn the lemon, so timing is key to extract the best flavor.
Rest the chicken before serving.
Resting lets the juices redistribute throughout the meat, preventing them from spilling out immediately when cut. This keeps the chicken moist and tender, improving overall eating experience.
Reheat leftovers in the air fryer instead of microwave.
Microwaving can make the skin soggy and veggies mushy. The air fryer restores crispiness and heats evenly, preserving texture and flavor better for leftovers.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not drying the chicken skin before cooking

Why it happens People often skip this to save time or forget, assuming seasoning alone is enough. Moist skin traps steam, preventing crisping.

Fix Pat the chicken skin thoroughly with paper towels until it feels dry to the touch. This simple step allows the skin to crisp up in the air fryer instead of steaming.

Overcrowding the air fryer basket

Why it happens Trying to cook everything at once to save time or because of limited basket size. This blocks airflow and leads to uneven cooking.

Fix Cook in batches if needed, leaving space between pieces for hot air to circulate properly, ensuring even crisping and roasting.

Skipping the flip halfway through cooking

Why it happens Some assume one side cooking is enough or forget to flip. This results in uneven browning and texture.

Fix Flip chicken skin-side up halfway through cooking to allow both sides to brown properly and cook evenly.

Not using a meat thermometer

Why it happens Relying on appearance or timing alone can be misleading. Undercooked chicken is unsafe and overcooked is dry.

Fix Use a thermometer to verify the internal temperature is 165°F for safe, juicy chicken every time.

Adding lemon too early

Why it happens Trying to infuse flavor from the start but lemon slices burn at high heat.

Fix Add lemon slices in the last 10 minutes of cooking to get fresh flavor without bitterness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This dish can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate promptly. Cover loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel to keep moisture balanced.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The chicken skin may soften when cold; reheat in the air fryer or oven to restore crispness. Veggies hold up well but may lose some texture.

Freezer

Freeze cooked chicken and veggies in a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating. Texture may be slightly softer after freezing.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 350°F for 5-7 minutes to revive crispy skin and warm veggies evenly. Avoid microwaving as it can make the skin soggy and veggies mushy. If needed, add a splash of water when reheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

You can prep and season the chicken and veggies a day ahead, storing them separately in the fridge. Assemble and cook just before serving for maximum freshness and crispness.

FAQ

Can I use boneless chicken breasts instead of thighs?
You can substitute boneless chicken breasts, but keep in mind they cook faster and can dry out more easily. Reduce cooking time and monitor closely, flipping halfway. The skin won't crisp since breasts usually come skinless, so the texture will be different.
What if I don't have an air fryer?
You can roast this dish in a conventional oven at 425°F (220°C) on a baking sheet. Cook chicken for about 25-30 minutes and roast veggies alongside, tossing halfway. The texture won’t be quite the same, but it’s still delicious.
How do I make this recipe spicier?
Add cayenne pepper or chili powder to the spice mix, or sprinkle red pepper flakes on the veggies. Adjust to your heat preference gradually to avoid overpowering the dish.
Can I use frozen vegetables?
Frozen veggies tend to release more moisture and may get soggy, so it’s best to use fresh. If using frozen, pat them dry well and reduce cooking time slightly to avoid overcooking.
Why is my chicken skin not crispy?
This usually happens if the chicken skin wasn’t dried well before cooking or the basket was overcrowded. Make sure to pat skin dry and give pieces enough space for air circulation.
Can I marinate the chicken overnight?
Yes, marinating overnight can add flavor, but avoid adding too much oil or acidic ingredients that might prevent skin crisping. Pat the chicken dry before air frying regardless.
How do I know when the veggies are done?
They should be tender when pierced with a fork and have a slight caramelized color. Cooking times vary by vegetable type, so check periodically and shake the basket for even cooking.
Is it safe to cook lemon slices in the air fryer?
Yes, adding lemon slices during the last 10-15 minutes adds flavor without burning them. Too early and they might char, resulting in bitterness.
