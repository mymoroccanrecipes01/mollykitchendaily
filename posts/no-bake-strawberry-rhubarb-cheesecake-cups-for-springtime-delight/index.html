No-Bake Rhubarb Strawberry Cheesecake Cups That Taste Like Spring

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on May 13, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These no-bake cheesecake cups combine tangy rhubarb and sweet strawberries in a creamy, light dessert. No oven needed—perfect for a fresh spring treat or easy entertaining when you want something simple but impressive.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature cream cheese for a smooth filling.
Cold cream cheese is stiff and difficult to blend smoothly, often resulting in lumps. Letting it soften at room temperature ensures it mixes evenly with sugar and vanilla, creating a creamy base that folds well with whipped cream. This step is essential for a smooth, lump-free cheesecake texture.
Chill your mixing bowl and beaters before whipping cream.
Cold equipment helps heavy cream whip faster and hold its structure longer. Warm bowls can cause the cream to whip slowly or not reach stiff peaks, affecting the lightness and stability of the filling. This simple step improves the texture and consistency of your cheesecake filling.
Fold whipped cream gently to keep the filling airy.
Folding preserves the air whipped into the cream, keeping the filling light and fluffy. Stirring or beating vigorously will deflate the cream, resulting in a denser, heavier cheesecake. Use a spatula and gentle motions to combine ingredients without losing volume.
Don’t skip refrigerating the assembled cups for at least 2 hours.
Chilling allows the filling to set and develop the perfect creamy yet firm texture. Without adequate refrigeration, the filling will be too soft and may not hold its shape. This step also deepens the flavors and makes slicing or scooping easier.
Press graham cracker crumbs lightly but firmly for a stable base.
Light pressing compacts the crumbs so they hold together and provide a crunchy contrast to the creamy filling. Pressing too hard can make the base overly dense and hard to eat, while too loose results in crumbs falling apart when served.
Simmer rhubarb just until softened but still slightly chunky.
Cooking rhubarb gently softens its tartness and breaks down fibers without turning it into mush. This preserves a fresh texture and bright flavor that balances the sweet, creamy filling perfectly.
Slice strawberries right before assembling to keep them fresh.
Slicing strawberries too early causes them to release moisture and lose their vibrant color and texture. Freshly sliced strawberries add a juicy, bright topping that complements the rhubarb and filling beautifully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using cold cream cheese straight from the fridge

Why it happens Many people forget to soften cream cheese beforehand, assuming it can be mixed cold like other ingredients.

Fix Plan ahead and let cream cheese sit at room temperature for at least 30 minutes before mixing. If you’re short on time, soften gently in the microwave in 10-second bursts, stirring in between, but avoid melting it. This ensures a smooth, lump-free filling.

Overwhipping the heavy cream

Why it happens It’s easy to get carried away when whipping cream, thinking stiffer peaks are better.

Fix Stop whipping as soon as stiff peaks form—when the cream holds its shape but is still smooth. Overwhipped cream becomes grainy and can separate into butter and buttermilk, ruining the filling’s texture.

Not folding whipped cream gently

Why it happens Some cooks stir or beat the cream into the cheese mixture, which deflates the air whipped in.

Fix Use a large spatula and fold the cream in with gentle, sweeping motions. Turn the bowl as you fold to combine evenly without losing volume.

Skipping the refrigeration time

Why it happens Impatience or lack of planning can lead to serving immediately.

Fix Refrigerate the assembled cups for at least two hours to allow the filling to set properly. This step is crucial for texture and flavor development.

Overcooking the rhubarb compote

Why it happens Trying to speed up cooking or leaving it unattended can cause mushy rhubarb.

Fix Cook rhubarb on medium heat, stirring occasionally, and remove from heat as soon as it softens but still holds small chunks. This keeps a fresh texture and prevents loss of flavor.

Variations

Lemon Zest Rhubarb Strawberry Cups

Add a teaspoon of fresh lemon zest to the cream cheese mixture for a bright citrus twist. The lemon enhances the tartness of rhubarb and adds a refreshing aroma. This variation pairs well with a sprinkle of crushed pistachios on top for extra crunch.

Note: Incorporate the lemon zest into the cream cheese mixture before folding in whipped cream to distribute flavor evenly.

Ginger-Spiced Rhubarb Strawberry Cheesecake

Stir a pinch of ground ginger into the rhubarb compote while cooking for a warm, spicy note that complements the fruit’s tartness. This adds complexity and a subtle warmth to the dessert without overpowering the fresh flavors.

Note: Add ginger early in the cooking process to allow its flavor to infuse the compote fully.

Vegan No-Bake Cheesecake Cups

Use vegan cream cheese and coconut cream whipped until fluffy in place of dairy. Sweeten with maple syrup instead of powdered sugar. The rhubarb compote stays the same. Expect a slightly different texture—less tangy but still creamy and delicious.

Note: Chill coconut cream overnight and whip just the solid part for best results; folding technique remains the same.

Crunchy Nut Crust Variation

Replace graham cracker crumbs with finely chopped toasted almonds or pecans mixed with a bit of melted coconut oil for a nutty, crunchy base. This adds richness and a toasty flavor that pairs wonderfully with the fruit topping.

Note: Press the nut mixture firmly into the cups to form a stable crust that contrasts the creamy filling.

Mixed Berry Rhubarb Cups

Add blueberries and raspberries to the strawberry topping for a colorful berry medley. This variation enhances the fruity complexity and visual appeal, making the dessert even more vibrant and fresh.

Note: Gently toss all berries together just before topping to prevent them from becoming mushy.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These cheesecake cups should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours as they contain dairy and fresh fruit which can spoil quickly. Keep them covered loosely if serving soon.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container or cover cups tightly with plastic wrap in the fridge for up to 3 days. The filling may firm up further, and the fruit topping can lose some freshness but will still taste great. Serve chilled for best texture.

Freezer

You can freeze the cheesecake cups for up to 1 month. Wrap each cup tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe container to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before serving. The texture may change slightly, becoming less creamy but still enjoyable.

Reheating

No reheating is recommended for these no-bake cheesecake cups. Serve cold straight from the fridge for the best flavor and texture. If needed, let sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes before eating to soften slightly.

Make Ahead

Prepare the rhubarb compote and crust layers 1-2 days ahead and keep refrigerated separately. Assemble the filling and layer the cups the day of serving to maintain freshness and texture. This saves time while ensuring the dessert looks and tastes its best.

FAQ

Can I use frozen rhubarb for the compote?
Yes, frozen rhubarb works well if fresh isn’t available. Just thaw it completely and drain excess liquid before cooking. Because frozen rhubarb releases more water, you might need to cook it a bit longer to reach the right consistency. This prevents your compote from being too watery and keeps the right texture.
How long can I store the cheesecake cups in the fridge?
They keep well for up to 3 days refrigerated in airtight containers. After that, the filling may start to lose its texture and the fresh fruit can become soggy or mushy. For best taste and appearance, enjoy within the first couple of days.
Can I substitute cream cheese with Greek yogurt?
Greek yogurt can be used for a lighter version, but it will change the texture and flavor significantly. The filling will be less creamy and more tangy, and it won’t set as firmly. If you try this, use thick Greek yogurt and consider adding gelatin or agar to help it hold shape.
What if my whipped cream is overwhipped?
If whipped cream becomes grainy or starts separating, it’s overwhipped. Unfortunately, it’s difficult to fix completely, but you can try adding a little fresh cream and gently folding it in to smooth the texture. For best results, whip cream carefully and stop as soon as stiff peaks form.
Can I prepare these cups without the graham cracker crust?
You can skip the crust, but the texture contrast will be lost and the dessert might feel less substantial. If omitting, consider adding a few crushed nuts or granola on top for crunch. The crust provides a nice base that balances the creamy filling and fruity topping.
How do I prevent the rhubarb compote from being too tart?
Cooking rhubarb with enough sugar helps mellow its tartness, but if you prefer sweeter, add a bit more sugar or a splash of honey. Also, cooking the compote until it softens but still holds texture balances the flavors. Taste as you go and adjust sweetness accordingly.
Is it possible to make this recipe vegan?
Yes, by replacing cream cheese with a plant-based alternative and using coconut cream whipped to stiff peaks instead of heavy cream, you can make a vegan version. Sweeten with maple syrup or powdered sugar alternatives. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious.
Can I assemble these cups ahead of time for a party?
Yes, you can assemble the cups 1-2 days ahead. Keep them covered and refrigerated. The filling may firm up more, and fruit might soften a bit, but the flavors will meld nicely. For freshest fruit appearance, add sliced strawberries just before serving if possible.

What You'll Need

4 individual cheesecake cups

  • 1 cup fresh rhubarb, chopped
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 cup fresh strawberries, sliced
  • 8 oz cream cheese, softened
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup heavy cream, cold
  • 8 small graham cracker crumbs or digestive biscuits
  • Fresh mint leaves for garnish (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by making the rhubarb compote. In a small saucepan, combine the chopped rhubarb and granulated sugar. Cook over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until the rhubarb softens and breaks down but still has some texture, about 8-10 minutes. This step is crucial because it softens the rhubarb and mellows its tartness while creating a naturally sweet sauce. Avoid letting it cook too long or it will become mushy and lose its fresh flavor.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: The rhubarb should look soft and glossy but still hold some small chunks.
Step 2
While the rhubarb cools, prepare the strawberry topping by slicing the fresh strawberries thinly. This adds a fresh, juicy contrast to the creamy filling. Set aside and keep refrigerated until assembly. Don't slice too early to avoid sogginess and loss of vibrant color.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Strawberries should be evenly sliced, bright red, and firm.
Step 3
In a large mixing bowl, beat the softened cream cheese with powdered sugar and vanilla extract until smooth and creamy. This mixture forms the base of your cheesecake filling and needs to be lump-free for the best texture. Use room temperature cream cheese to avoid clumps and ensure smooth blending.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, creamy, and no lumps remain.
Step 4
In a separate chilled bowl, whip the heavy cream until stiff peaks form. This step is essential to give the filling its light, airy texture. Be careful not to overwhip or the cream will become grainy and separate. The cold cream whips better and holds its structure longer.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Whipped cream holds stiff peaks that don’t fold back when the whisk is lifted.
Step 5
Gently fold the whipped cream into the cream cheese mixture using a spatula. Folding rather than stirring preserves the airiness of the whipped cream, ensuring a light cheesecake filling. Mix just until combined—overmixing will deflate the texture and make it dense.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Filling is smooth and fluffy with no visible streaks of cream or cheese.
Step 6
To assemble, start by spooning a layer of graham cracker crumbs into the bottom of each serving cup to create a crunchy base. This adds texture contrast and mimics the crust of a traditional cheesecake. Press lightly to compact but don’t crush completely.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Crumbs form an even, slightly compacted layer at the bottom of each cup.
Step 7
Add a generous layer of the cheesecake filling over the crumb base in each cup, smoothing the tops with the back of a spoon. This layer should be thick enough to hold its shape but still soft and creamy. Fill about two-thirds full to leave room for the fruit topping.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Filling layers are even, smooth, and fill the cups about two-thirds.
Step 8
Top each cup with a spoonful of the rhubarb compote followed by fresh strawberry slices. This layering balances tart and sweet flavors and adds a fresh, colorful finish. Distribute the fruit evenly for the best presentation and flavor balance.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Fruit topping looks fresh, vibrant, and evenly distributed.
Step 9
Refrigerate the assembled cups for at least 2 hours to allow the filling to set properly. Chilling is key to firming up the cheesecake texture since this is a no-bake dessert. Avoid skipping this step or the filling will be too soft and runny.
⏱ Time: 120
✓ Doneness: Filling feels firm to the touch but still creamy and sliceable.
