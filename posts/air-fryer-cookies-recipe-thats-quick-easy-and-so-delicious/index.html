Soft and Chewy Air Fryer Chocolate Chip Cookies You’ll Love

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 18, 2026
15 min Prep Time
10 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These air fryer cookies are my go-to when I want fresh, soft, and chewy treats fast without turning on the oven. The secret is a quick air-frying trick that gives perfect texture every time. Great for an afternoon snack or last-minute dessert!

Introduction

I first tried making cookies in the air fryer on a whim when I didn’t want to heat up my kitchen during a hot summer day. Honestly, I was skeptical at first—would they really taste as good as oven-baked? But the results totally surprised me. These cookies come out soft and chewy every time, with a little crisp on the edges that’s just perfect. What makes this recipe different is a quick air-frying trick I learned that keeps the cookies from drying out or burning. Plus, the air fryer cooks the cookies so evenly and fast, you get fresh-baked goodness in under 15 minutes. I’ve made these for last-minute guests and even packed some for road trips because they hold up so well. The texture is just right, thanks to a balance of brown sugar and granulated sugar and the perfect amount of butter. If you’re looking for an easy, fuss-free way to satisfy your cookie cravings without firing up the oven, this is it. Trust me, once you try this method, you’ll be hooked like I am!

What You'll Need

About 16 cookies, 8 servings

  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 cups semisweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by creaming the softened butter with both sugars until the mixture is light and fluffy. This usually takes about 3-4 minutes with a hand mixer on medium speed. Creaming incorporates air into the batter, which helps the cookies rise and creates a tender texture. Don’t rush this step—if the butter isn’t fully softened or you don’t cream long enough, the cookies can turn out dense and flat.
⏱ Time: 4
✓ Doneness: The mixture should look pale and fluffy, and when you lift the beaters, the batter should fall in thick ribbons.
Step 2
Beat in the eggs one at a time, followed by the vanilla extract. Mixing in the eggs slowly helps the batter emulsify properly, which means the fat and water combine smoothly for a better texture. Adding vanilla at this stage infuses the dough with that classic warm flavor. Be careful not to overmix here, or the dough can become tough.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: The batter will look smooth and slightly glossy after adding the eggs and vanilla.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together the all-purpose flour, baking soda, and salt. This ensures the leavening agent and salt are evenly distributed to prevent uneven rising or salty bites. Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture in three parts, mixing on low speed just until combined each time. Overmixing at this stage develops gluten, which can make cookies chewy but also tough, so stop as soon as no flour streaks remain.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: The dough should be thick and uniform without visible flour patches.
Step 4
Fold in the chocolate chips carefully with a spatula. Folding rather than mixing preserves the dough’s airiness and prevents chocolate chips from breaking. Distribute them evenly for every bite to have that perfect chocolate hit. Use good quality chips for richer flavor.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly scattered throughout the dough without clumps.
Step 5
Preheat your air fryer to 320°F (160°C). Preheating is essential because it helps the cookies start baking immediately, producing a nice rise and preventing them from spreading too thin. If your air fryer doesn’t have a preheat function, run it empty for 3-5 minutes at temperature.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Air fryer display shows it has reached 320°F and is ready for cooking.
Step 6
Line the air fryer basket with parchment paper or a silicone liner to prevent sticking. Scoop tablespoon-sized balls of dough onto the liner, spacing them at least 2 inches apart as the cookies will spread slightly. Don’t overcrowd the basket, or the cookies won’t cook evenly and might stick together.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dough balls are spaced evenly with room to expand during cooking.
Step 7
Air fry the cookies at 320°F for 8-10 minutes. Keep an eye on them starting at 8 minutes because air fryers vary. The cookies should be golden around the edges but still soft in the center. The hot circulating air cooks them quickly and gives a lightly crisp exterior while keeping the inside chewy.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Edges are golden brown, and center looks set but slightly soft.
Step 8
Remove the cookies from the air fryer and let them cool on a wire rack for at least 5 minutes. They will firm up as they cool but stay soft and chewy inside. Cooling is important because hot cookies are fragile and can break apart if moved too soon.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Cookies hold their shape and feel slightly firm to the touch after cooling.
Step 9
Repeat the process with remaining dough, allowing the air fryer basket to cool briefly between batches if needed. This prevents overheating and ensures each batch cooks evenly. Store cooled cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days or freeze for longer storage.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Each batch maintains consistent texture and color without burning.

Variations

Double Chocolate Chip Cookies

Add 1/4 cup cocoa powder to the flour mixture and swap half the chocolate chips for white chocolate chips. This gives a rich, fudgy flavor with bursts of sweet white chocolate. The dough will be darker and slightly thicker.

Note: Add cocoa powder with flour and reduce flour by same amount to keep dough consistency.

Oatmeal Chocolate Chip Cookies

Replace 1 cup of flour with old-fashioned rolled oats and add 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon. This adds chewiness and a warm spice note. The cookies will be heartier and a bit thicker.

Note: Mix oats with dry ingredients to ensure even distribution.

Peanut Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

Substitute 1/2 cup butter with creamy peanut butter for a nutty twist. The cookies will be softer with a rich peanut flavor that pairs perfectly with chocolate chips.

Note: Reduce sugar slightly if your peanut butter is sweetened.

Salted Caramel Chocolate Chip Cookies

Add caramel bits to the dough and sprinkle flaky sea salt on top before air frying. This sweet-salty combo makes the cookies extra indulgent and gooey in the center.

Note: Press caramel bits gently into dough balls to prevent melting out too much.

Vegan Air Fryer Cookies

Use vegan butter and flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg). Swap chocolate chips for dairy-free versions. These cookies stay soft and delicious without animal products.

Note: Let flax eggs thicken fully before mixing into dough for best texture.

Pro Tips

Make sure your butter is just softened, not melted.
Softened butter creams properly with sugar to trap air needed for rise and tenderness. Melted butter creates greasy, flat cookies because it lacks the structure to hold air.
Don’t overmix the dough once you add flour.
Overmixing develops gluten, which can make cookies tough and chewy in a bad way. Mix just until combined to keep them tender and soft.
Preheat the air fryer before baking.
Starting with a hot air fryer ensures the cookies set quickly and spread less, producing a better texture and even browning.
Use parchment paper or silicone liners in the air fryer basket.
This prevents sticking and makes cleanup easier. It also helps cookies bake evenly without direct contact with hot metal.
Space cookie dough balls well apart.
Cookies spread during baking, so leaving room prevents them from melting into each other and ensures even cooking.
Cool cookies fully before storing.
Cooling allows moisture to redistribute, preventing soggy bottoms and helping cookies keep their chewy texture when stored.
Check cookies a minute or two before the timer ends.
Air fryer models vary; checking early helps avoid overbaking, which dries out cookies and ruins their soft center.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using melted butter instead of softened butter

Why it happens Many assume melted butter is easier to mix or speeds up preparation, but it prevents proper creaming which is vital for texture.

Fix Always let butter soften at room temperature until it yields to gentle pressure but isn’t oily or warm. This traps air with sugar, giving rise and tenderness.

Overmixing the dough after adding flour

Why it happens In a rush, people often mix vigorously to combine ingredients, not realizing it develops gluten and toughens cookies.

Fix Mix just until no flour streaks remain. Use a spatula to fold in flour gently to keep dough tender.

Skipping air fryer preheating

Why it happens Some think preheating wastes time or electricity, but it’s crucial for even cooking.

Fix Always preheat the air fryer for 3-5 minutes at baking temperature to ensure cookies cook evenly and set quickly.

Crowding the air fryer basket

Why it happens Trying to bake too many cookies at once can save time but leads to uneven cooking and sticking.

Fix Bake in batches with at least 2 inches between dough balls for best results.

Removing cookies too soon from the air fryer

Why it happens Impatience or fear of overcooking often leads people to take cookies out before edges set.

Fix Look for golden edges and slightly soft centers. Cookies will firm up as they cool, so don’t overbake.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat sources to prevent melting chocolate chips. They maintain softness best when stored with a slice of bread inside the container to keep moisture balanced.

Refrigerator

Cookies can be refrigerated for up to 5 days in an airtight container. Chilling may firm them up, so warm slightly in the microwave for 10-15 seconds before eating to restore softness. Refrigeration is good for longer storage but can dry cookies if left too long.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a parchment-lined tray, then transfer to a freezer-safe bag or container for up to 3 months. Wrap tightly to avoid freezer burn. Thaw at room temperature for 30 minutes or warm gently in the microwave for a few seconds to refresh texture.

Reheating

Reheat cookies in a 300°F oven for 5 minutes or microwave for 10-15 seconds with a damp paper towel nearby to keep them moist. Avoid overheating which makes cookies hard and dry. Adding a tiny splash of water to the container before microwaving helps maintain softness.

Make Ahead

You can mix the dough 1-2 days ahead and store it tightly wrapped in the fridge. When ready, scoop and air fry as usual. Dough can also be frozen in balls for up to 1 month; just thaw in the fridge overnight before baking. This makes last-minute cookie preparation a breeze.

FAQ

Can I use frozen cookie dough in the air fryer?
Yes! You can air fry frozen dough balls directly, but increase the cooking time by 2-3 minutes and check frequently to avoid burning. The texture might be slightly different, but still delicious.
What if my cookies are too flat or spread too much?
This usually means your butter was too soft or melted, or you didn’t chill the dough enough. Also, overcrowding the air fryer basket can cause spreading. Use softened butter, chill dough briefly if needed, and space cookies well.
Can I substitute gluten-free flour?
You can try a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend, but texture may vary. These blends often lack gluten’s elasticity, so cookies might be more crumbly. Adding a bit of xanthan gum can help improve structure.
Is it necessary to preheat the air fryer?
Preheating ensures the cookies start baking immediately, which helps them set properly and prevents excessive spreading. Skipping preheat can result in flatter, less evenly cooked cookies.
How do I keep cookies soft longer?
Store cookies in airtight containers with a slice of bread or a damp paper towel to maintain moisture. Avoid refrigeration unless you plan to reheat before eating, as cold can dry them out.
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Absolutely! Chopped walnuts, pecans, or dried fruit work great. Fold them in gently with chocolate chips. Keep in mind that nuts may alter baking time slightly, so watch your cookies closely.
Why do some cookies come out dry or crumbly?
Overbaking is the main cause of dry cookies, especially in an air fryer. Also, too much flour or not enough fat can dry them out. Follow timing carefully and measure ingredients precisely.
Can I make bigger or smaller cookies?
Yes, but adjust cooking time accordingly. Larger cookies need a few extra minutes; smaller ones will bake faster. Always check for doneness early to avoid overbaking.
