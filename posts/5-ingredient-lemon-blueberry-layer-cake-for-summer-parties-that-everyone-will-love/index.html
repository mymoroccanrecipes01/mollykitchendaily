Fresh Lemon Blueberry Cake with a Light, Zesty Twist

Molly Kitchen Daily
Published on June 3, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon blueberry cake is my go-to for summer gatherings. It's super light, bursting with juicy blueberries and a bright lemon zing. Super easy with just five ingredients—perfect for casual weekend treats!

Introduction

I first made this lemon blueberry cake last summer when I wanted a dessert that felt light and fresh but still satisfying. Most lemon cakes I tried were either too dense or overly sweet, so I played around with the ingredients until I found a balance that works perfectly. The trick I learned was folding the blueberries in gently at the end so they don’t burst and turn the batter purple, keeping the cake beautifully speckled instead. Also, using fresh lemon zest and juice instead of extract gives it a bright, authentic zing that just wakes up your taste buds. When baked, the cake is moist with a tender crumb, and the blueberries add juicy pops of sweetness. It’s become a favorite for backyard parties and weekend brunches in my house, especially because it’s quick and uses only a handful of ingredients. I think you’ll love how fresh and easy it is to whip up anytime you want a summery treat.

What You'll Need

One 9-inch round cake, 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup whole milk
  • Zest and juice of 1 large lemon
  • 1 1/2 cups fresh blueberries, rinsed and patted dry

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9-inch round cake pan with butter or non-stick spray, then dust it lightly with flour to prevent sticking. This step ensures your cake releases easily after baking and that it cooks evenly. Skipping this can lead to a cake that sticks or bakes unevenly, so don’t rush it.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan feels evenly coated with no bare spots and a light flour dusting remains
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking powder, and a pinch of salt. Sifting aerates the flour, which helps the cake stay light and tender. If you skip sifting, you might get lumps or a denser texture, so take a minute here.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined and fluffy
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the sugar and melted butter together until smooth and slightly creamy. This step helps dissolve the sugar and distributes the fat evenly, which contributes to the cake’s moist texture. If the butter is too hot, it can cook the eggs in the next step, so make sure it's cooled.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks smooth with no lumps and a pale yellow color
Step 4
Add eggs one at a time to the butter-sugar mixture, beating well after each addition. This incorporates air for a lighter cake and ensures the eggs are fully mixed in. Rushing this step can cause uneven texture or curdling.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth, glossy, and slightly thickened
Step 5
Stir in the lemon zest and juice along with the milk. The fresh lemon juice adds brightness and moisture, while the zest gives concentrated citrus flavor. Use fresh lemons for the best taste; bottled juice won't have the same zing.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly mixed with visible lemon zest strands
Step 6
Gradually fold in the dry ingredients to the wet mixture using a spatula. Folding gently preserves the air bubbles in the batter, keeping the cake light. Overmixing will develop gluten and make the cake tough, so stop as soon as the flour disappears.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and uniform with no streaks of flour
Step 7
Gently fold in the fresh blueberries, being careful not to crush them. This keeps the berries intact and prevents the batter from turning purple, which helps the cake look more appetizing. If you use frozen berries, thaw and drain them first to avoid excess moisture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Blueberries are evenly distributed without broken skins
Step 8
Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top with a spatula. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any large air bubbles. This gives the cake an even surface and prevents large holes inside.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and smooth in the pan
Step 9
Bake the cake in the preheated oven for about 30-35 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Avoid opening the oven door too early to prevent the cake from sinking. The golden edges and slight spring back when touched signal doneness.
⏱ Time: 30-35 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick comes out clean and cake springs back lightly when pressed
Step 10
Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling allows the cake to set and prevents it from becoming soggy. Don’t skip this or try to frost it while warm.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cake is cool to the touch and holds its shape when moved

Variations

Gluten-Free Lemon Blueberry Cake

Swap the all-purpose flour for a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend. The cake remains moist and flavorful, though the crumb may be slightly denser. Add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t include it to improve texture.

Note: Gluten-free flours behave differently, so avoid overmixing and ensure proper leavening for rise.

Lemon Blueberry Bundt Cake with Glaze

Bake the batter in a bundt pan for a prettier presentation. Once cooled, drizzle with a simple lemon glaze made from powdered sugar and lemon juice for extra zing and sweetness.

Note: Increase baking time by 5-10 minutes and check doneness with a toothpick due to thicker batter depth.

Vegan Lemon Blueberry Cake

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use plant-based milk and vegan butter substitutes. The cake will be slightly less fluffy but still delicious and moist.

Note: Flax eggs add density, so fold batter gently and consider adding a bit more baking powder.

Lemon Blueberry Muffins

Divide the batter into a muffin tin lined with paper cups for individual servings. Bake about 20-25 minutes. Perfect for grab-and-go breakfasts or snacks with the same fresh flavors.

Note: Watch closely to avoid overbaking, which can dry out the smaller portions.

Lemon Blueberry Cake with Almond Flour

Substitute 1/3 of the flour with almond flour for a nutty flavor and moister crumb. This variation adds richness and a slight chewiness that complements the blueberries.

Note: Almond flour adds fat and moisture, so reduce butter slightly if needed to balance texture.

Pro Tips

Always sift your dry ingredients for a lighter cake.
Sifting aerates the flour and baking powder, breaking up lumps and ensuring even distribution. This results in a tender crumb and proper rise. Skipping sifting can cause uneven texture and dense spots.
Fold in blueberries gently at the end.
Blueberries are delicate and can burst easily, staining the batter and altering texture. Gently folding them preserves their shape and ensures pretty specks of fruit instead of purple streaks.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice for true brightness.
Fresh lemon zest contains essential oils that provide intense citrus aroma and flavor, while fresh juice adds acidity for balance. Bottled lemon juice lacks this complexity and can make the cake taste flat.
Cool the cake completely before slicing.
Cooling allows the cake's structure to set and prevents crumbling or sogginess. Cutting too soon can cause the cake to fall apart or be gummy.
Tap the pan before baking to release large air bubbles.
This step prevents large holes or tunnels inside the cake by allowing trapped air to escape, resulting in a more even crumb.
Don’t overmix the batter once flour is added.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes cakes tough and chewy. Mix just until combined for a tender texture.
Use room temperature eggs and milk.
Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or not emulsify properly, affecting texture and rise.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using frozen blueberries without thawing

Why it happens Many assume frozen berries can be added directly to save time, but frozen berries release extra moisture when baking.

Fix Always thaw and drain frozen blueberries thoroughly before folding them into the batter to avoid soggy cake and uneven texture.

Skipping sifting of dry ingredients

Why it happens Sifting is often overlooked as an unnecessary step to save time.

Fix Take a moment to sift flour and baking powder together to ensure even distribution and prevent lumps, which helps the cake rise and stay tender.

Overmixing after adding flour

Why it happens In a rush, cooks often mix vigorously to combine ingredients quickly.

Fix Mix just until the flour disappears to avoid toughening the cake by gluten overdevelopment.

Not cooling cake before slicing

Why it happens Impatience leads to cutting the cake while still warm.

Fix Allow the cake to cool completely on a wire rack so it firms up and slices cleanly without crumbling.

Using bottled lemon juice

Why it happens Convenience or running out of fresh lemons causes this substitution.

Fix Use fresh lemon juice and zest for authentic flavor and proper acidity balance that impacts texture and rise.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cake covered loosely with a cake dome or foil at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat to prevent drying out. The cake stays moist and fresh for casual serving.

Refrigerator

Place the cake in an airtight container or cover tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for up to 5 days. The texture firms up slightly when cold, so bring to room temperature before serving for best flavor.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Refresh by warming slightly in a low oven before serving.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 15-20 seconds. Avoid overheating to prevent drying. Adding a tiny splash of lemon juice or butter before warming can restore moisture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it refrigerated in an airtight container. Stir gently before baking. Alternatively, bake the cake and freeze it fully, then thaw and glaze or decorate on the day of serving.

FAQ

Can I use frozen blueberries instead of fresh?
Yes, but thaw and drain them completely before folding into the batter to avoid excess moisture making the cake soggy. Using frozen berries straight from the freezer can cause the batter to turn purple and affect texture.
What if I don’t have fresh lemons for zest and juice?
Fresh lemon zest and juice are key for bright flavor and acidity. Bottled lemon juice lacks the fresh aroma and can make the cake taste flat. If you must, add a little lemon extract and reduce sugar slightly, but the result won’t be quite the same.
How do I know when the cake is done baking?
Insert a toothpick into the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. The cake’s edges will pull slightly away from the pan and the top will spring back lightly when pressed.
Can I make this cake dairy-free?
Yes! Substitute the milk with any plant-based milk like almond or oat milk, and use vegan butter or coconut oil instead of butter. The texture might be slightly different but still delicious and moist.
Why is my cake dense or heavy?
Common reasons include overmixing the batter after adding flour, expired baking powder, or not sifting the dry ingredients. Overmixing develops gluten, making the cake tough. Always mix gently and check your leavening agents.
Can I double this recipe for a larger cake?
Absolutely! Use a larger pan or two pans and adjust baking time accordingly, checking doneness with a toothpick. Larger cakes usually need more time, so start checking at the original baking time and add increments as needed.
What’s the best way to store leftovers?
Keep the cake in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Can I add frosting or glaze to this cake?
Yes, a simple lemon glaze or cream cheese frosting pairs beautifully. Just make sure the cake is fully cooled before applying to prevent melting or sliding.
